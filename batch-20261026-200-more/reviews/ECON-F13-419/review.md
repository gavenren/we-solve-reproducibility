# Three response observations identify both adjustment speeds up to their unavoidable swap

The manuscript proves that retail responses cannot distinguish the inventory replacement speed λ from the pricing adjustment speed α. That symmetry is the only ambiguity within its positive-pass-through two-stage model: three exact response levels identify their unordered pair and η, even without observing the long run.

Normalize the retail step response by the known border log-price change, writing r_h=z_h/a and r_(−1)=0. Define the first three impulse increments d₀=r₀, d₁=r₁−r₀ and d₂=r₂−r₁. With u=1−λ and v=1−α, the source's convolution formula gives
d₀=ηαλ,
d₁=d₀(u+v),
d₂=d₀(u²+uv+v²).
When η>0, α,λ>0 imply d₀>0, so these ratios are well defined.

Put s=d₁/d₀ and p=s²−d₂/d₀. Then s=u+v and p=uv. Thus u and v are precisely the roots of t²−st+p=0. The unordered adjustment-speed set is {1−u,1−v}, and
η=d₀/[(1−u)(1−v)]=d₀/(1−s+p).
Both speeds are strictly positive in the maintained model, so the denominator is positive. This proves that no additional parameter ambiguity remains after interchanging the two stages.

The formulas handle equal speeds through a repeated quadratic root; no division by u−v is used. If one speed is instantaneous, one root is zero and p=0. If both are instantaneous, s=p=0, the response is r_h=η from date zero, and η=d₀. If η=0 instead, every observed response is zero and neither speed is identified, exactly as the source notes.

A sharp finite-path model check follows. Candidate observations with d₀>0 fit some admissible first-three-response model exactly if and only if the polynomial has real roots in [0,1). Indeed necessity was just derived, while sufficiency follows by taking those roots as u,v and setting η=d₀/(1−s+p). The resulting α,λ∈(0,1] and η>0 reproduce all three increments. A negative discriminant, a root outside [0,1), or a nonpositive d₀ refutes this positive-pass-through model at the exact population level. With noisy data these are specification restrictions, not mechanical rejection rules without sampling analysis.

For additional horizons there is an overidentifying restriction:
d_h=s d_(h−1)−p d_(h−2), for h≥2.
To prove it, the convolution polynomial H_h=Σ_(j=0)^h u^jv^(h−j) obeys H_h=(u+v)H_(h−1)−uvH_(h−2); cancellation of the two sums leaves exactly the first. Multiplication by d₀ gives the recursion. Therefore all later response increments are determined by the identified first three values. Conversely a full path satisfying this recursion with the initial increments and admissible roots is generated by the two-stage model.

For the manuscript's example r₀=1/8, r₁=9/32, r₂=55/128, the increments are 16/128,20/128,19/128. Hence s=5/4, p=3/8 and roots {1/2,3/4}, recovering speeds {1/2,1/4} and η=1.

This is finite-population-path identification inside the declared geometric log-index model. It does not label which speed belongs to inventories; the source's additional cost measurement is still needed for that. Nor does it impose physical inventory accounting, estimate welfare incidence, or solve noisy short-panel identification and inference.
