# An imperfect reset still identifies the memory label and its own efficacy

The source's successful reset removes the circumstance channel completely. Full removal is stronger than necessary in its two-exponential model. An unknown but strictly positive proportional attenuation identifies both the memory label and the reset's efficacy from aligned response profiles.

Maintain the unreset causal profile
\[
\psi_h=\alpha r^h+\beta s^h,\qquad
\alpha,\beta>0,\quad r\ne s,\quad0<r,s<1.
\]
Suppose a randomized reset leaves the memory response unchanged, multiplies the circumstance amplitude by \(1-\eta\), and changes neither decay rate, where the unknown common efficacy satisfies \(0<\eta\le1\). An exposure-independent direct reset effect may still cancel within reset-cell exposure contrasts, as in the source. The reset profile is
\[
\chi_h=\alpha r^h+(1-\eta)\beta s^h.
\]
These are maintained channel restrictions, not consequences of randomization alone.

Four unreset population responses identify the two unordered rates and aligned amplitudes by the source's theorem. Call the roots \(r_1,r_2\) and corresponding positive amplitudes \(a_1,a_2\), without yet assigning mechanism labels. Two reset responses identify its amplitudes on those same roots:
\[
b_1=\frac{\chi_1-r_2\chi_0}{r_1-r_2},\qquad
b_2=\chi_0-b_1.
\]
Exactly one amplitude is unchanged and the other strictly reduced:
\[
b_{\mathrm{memory}}=a_{\mathrm{memory}},\qquad
0\le b_{\mathrm{circumstance}}<a_{\mathrm{circumstance}}.
\]
Thus the unchanged amplitude labels the memory root, and
\[
\eta=1-\frac{b_{\mathrm{circumstance}}}
{a_{\mathrm{circumstance}}}.
\]
No prior knowledge of the efficacy is needed.

The proof is linear inversion followed by the strict efficacy restriction. Distinct roots make the two-by-two amplitude system nonsingular. The true memory amplitude remains exactly its positive unreset value; the other decreases by the positive amount \(\eta\beta\). Therefore the two roles cannot both satisfy the same pattern, establishing unique labeling. Conversely every inferred parameter tuple satisfying those amplitude restrictions reproduces the two observed profiles under the declared partial-reset model. Later reset horizons must obey \(b_1r_1^h+b_2r_2^h\), providing additional restrictions rather than new freely adjustable amplitudes.

An even simpler check uses the difference profile:
\[
\psi_h-\chi_h=\eta\beta s^h.
\]
Because \(\eta\beta>0\), its first two levels identify the circumstance rate as \((\psi_1-\chi_1)/(\psi_0-\chi_0)\). It must equal one of the unreset roots; the other is memory. Comparing the difference amplitude to the aligned unreset amplitude yields the same efficacy formula. This derivation independently confirms the amplitude-label argument.

For example, unreset amplitudes \(a_1=2,a_2=3\) at rates \(1/5,4/5\), with reset amplitudes \(b_1=2,b_2=3/2\), identify the first rate as memory and efficacy one half. The reset is imperfect, yet mechanism labels are point identified in the restricted class.

If both amplitudes are unchanged, efficacy zero is compatible but the labels remain unresolved; this boundary is excluded by the strict assumption. If both change, or an amplitude increases, the single-channel proportional-attenuation model is rejected. Horizon-dependent attenuation can imitate a changed decay rate and invalidates the identification. Exact equalities here are population statements; noisy profiles need joint uncertainty and separation conditions, especially near equal roots or tiny efficacy.
