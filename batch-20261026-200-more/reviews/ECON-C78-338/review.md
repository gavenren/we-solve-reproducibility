# A conflict-graph characterization for correlated two-applicant lotteries

The source proves hardness for explicit correlated profile support and tractability for independent lotteries on disconnected two-applicant components. Correlated uncertainty also has a useful exact structural characterization in that component graph. It yields a polynomial subclass different from applicant independence.

Keep the disjoint union of K_{2,2} acceptability components, and restrict each support profile within every component to one of the manuscript's three types: force matching bit 0, force bit 1, or permit either. Let the explicit support have profiles k=1,…,K with nonnegative rational probabilities p_k summing to one. Each complete global matching is a bit vector z over the components. The deterministic factorization proved in the source says profile k accepts z exactly when all its forced coordinates agree with z.

Build a graph G on support profiles. Join k and l by an edge if some component forces opposite bits in these two profiles. Assign vertex weight p_k. Then the maximal popularity probability equals the maximum total weight of an independent set of G.

For the first direction, the set of profiles accepting a fixed bit vector contains no conflicting pair, hence is independent. Its weight is precisely that matching's popularity probability. For the reverse direction, take any independent set S. At each coordinate, all forced bits among profiles in S agree; otherwise two of those profiles would be adjacent. Assign that common bit when present and any bit when absent. Every profile in S then accepts the resulting matching, so its popularity probability is at least the weight of S. Combining the inequalities proves equality of optima. Extra accepted profiles cause no problem.

Incomplete global matchings remain irrelevant because the source's component argument makes them unpopular at every support profile. Zero-weight support vertices can be dropped. This characterization is an exact finite reduction, not a distributional independence claim.

It also identifies a tractable correlated subclass. Suppose G is bipartite, with parts L and R. The complement of an independent set is a vertex cover and the weights sum to one, so the optimum is one minus the minimum weighted vertex-cover cost. To compute that cost directly, build a source-to-L arc of capacity p_k for each left vertex, an R-to-sink arc of capacity p_l for each right vertex, and an L-to-R arc of capacity M>1 for every conflict edge. A cut of capacity at most one exists, so a minimum cut cannot sever an M arc. Its finite-capacity cut arcs select left vertices on the sink side and right vertices on the source side. They cover every conflict edge. Conversely every cover gives such a cut with exactly its weight, after assigning sides accordingly. Thus minimum cut and minimum cover costs coincide.

This supplies a polynomial optimization method for an explicitly checkable support condition even when applicant preferences are highly correlated. The graph can be built by checking every profile pair over every component. The min-cut construction uses rational capacities of polynomial encoding length. After obtaining the complement independent set, the coordinate assignment above reconstructs a maximizing matching; if it accepted more positive-weight profiles, that would contradict optimality of the independent-set weight.

The representation is expressive: every finite simple graph can occur as a conflict graph. Introduce a separate component for each graph edge. In the profile corresponding to one endpoint force 0 in that component, in the other endpoint profile force 1, and in all other profiles use the permissive type. Two profiles conflict exactly when they were endpoints of an edge. This is a constructive expressive-power statement, not an invocation of any unverified approximation-hardness theorem.

The result supplements both source regimes: independence across applicants is sufficient for product optimization, while bipartite support conflicts are a separate sufficient condition permitting correlations. General graph acceptability, arbitrary component profile types, compact independent lotteries and tie refinements remain outside this extension. No external complexity theorem or novelty claim is needed for the equivalence and flow argument.
