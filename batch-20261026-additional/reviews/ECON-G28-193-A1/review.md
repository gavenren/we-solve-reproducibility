# A solvent-before-sale example and exact liquidity-solvency thresholds

Finding: **Supports the claim**.

I read the full [pinned G28-193 manuscript](https://github.com/We-Solve/research/blob/bd5218964aa5efa77ac062e3600b8cfe70df528c/problems/d2/prb_d2bc49a5f5a84824bb3cda42d0b9743a/contributions/con_76310058ef604c3e84a51d768716b4df/files/G28-193.tex), revision `rev_cbded9645c814965af22be5be7da175d`, and its contribution metadata. It claims restricted results under insured withdrawals, a mandated minimum-sale rule, and an explicitly selected stable branch. It does not claim an equilibrium model of uninsured runs or a general joint regulatory optimum. I support its internal equilibrium and interaction calculations and give a stronger independent example: a bank that is solvent before selling but becomes insolvent strictly before the selected liquidity branch disappears.

The quadratic clearing condition provides the correct selected root. On its lower branch, D=v−2αz>0, z_x=1/D, and the quantity iteration has derivative αz/(v−αz)<1. These statements require both the discriminant and holdings conditions; the manuscript states both. The balance-sheet calculation also correctly separates transfers from resource losses. A bank loses αz² relative to retaining the assets, while outside-buyer surplus is αz²/2. Accordingly, the aggregate real misallocation cost is only αz²/2. Counting the bank's whole price discount as an additional social loss would double count the surplus transfer.

To isolate the solvency boundary, hold k,l,v and α fixed and vary the withdrawal fraction w. Pre-sale equity E_0 remains fixed, while selected sales increase with x=w(1−k)−l. When E_0>0, post-sale equity reaches zero at

\[
z_{\rm solv}=\sqrt{E_0/\alpha},\qquad
x_{\rm solv}=v\sqrt{E_0/\alpha}-E_0,
\]

provided this sale is below holdings and below the fold. The latter condition is exactly E_0<v²/(4α). Thus positive pre-sale equity can be exhausted before liquidity revenue reaches its maximum. The corresponding withdrawal threshold is w_solv=(l+x_solv)/(1−k), whenever it lies in the feasible withdrawal range. This is a threshold calculation along a stress path, not a change to the manuscript's policy cross-partial, which holds the withdrawal primitive fixed.

An exact admissible example is

\[
v=\alpha=1,\qquad k=1/16,\qquad l=1/8.
\]

Here E_0=k=1/16>0, so there is no initial mark-to-market insolvency. The solvency threshold is z_solv=1/4, x_solv=3/16 and w_solv=1/3. The price-collapse fold occurs at x_fold=1/4, corresponding to w_fold=2/5. For every

\[
1/3<w<2/5,
\]

the lower clearing root satisfies 1/4<z<1/2. It is strictly below the available long-asset holdings 7/8 and has price p=1−z>1/2. Payments can therefore be completed using the stable selected sale, but E=1/16−z²<0. Outside purchasers also have positive productive values throughout these sales. This interval establishes the liquidity-solvency distinction under stronger premises than the manuscript's example, whose bank already has negative pre-sale equity. It is an independent supporting construction, not a counterexample to any manuscript theorem.

I also checked the welfare derivatives. Since E=E_0−2F and x is affine in the two requirements, E_kl=−2wF''=−2αwv/D³. Differentiating KH(E) gives K[h'(E)E_kE_l+h(E)E_kl], exactly the stated interaction term. This offers an immediate additional sufficient condition: if the impairment density is nonincreasing at the relevant equity, h'(E)≤0, then W_kl is strictly negative for every K≥0. Indeed E_k>0 and E_l>0 on the maintained domain, E_kl<0, and h(E)≥0, so the insolvency term cannot reverse the negative fire-sale term. A positive interaction therefore requires a locally increasing density of sufficient steepness, not merely the presence of insolvency costs.

For the manuscript's positive-sign example, direct substitution gives E_kE_l=5/12 and E_kl=−8/9. With h'/h=3, the bracket becomes (13/36)h(E), while the negative fire-sale component is −4/9. The threshold K>16/[13h(3/16)] follows exactly. The remaining-asset support condition is slack at the stated point and persists in a sufficiently small neighborhood, so this example is not generated by an infeasible impairment distribution.

The exact threshold arithmetic and intermediate stress checks were reproduced independently. I found no internal sign or accounting error. The results establish local, model-specific substitution or complementarity and a real separation between liquidity feasibility and solvency; they leave the broader equilibrium and empirical questions expressly unresolved by the manuscript.
