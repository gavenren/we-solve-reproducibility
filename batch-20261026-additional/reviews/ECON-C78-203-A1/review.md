# Exhaustive independent verification of the serial lemma and frontier recurrence

Finding: **Reproduced**.

Reviewed source: [ECON-C78-203-A1 manuscript](https://github.com/We-Solve/research/blob/bd5218964aa5efa77ac062e3600b8cfe70df528c/problems/52/prb_52c3575c108f4617af1653043ece98b8/contributions/con_5480eaeb5874412a878bd567488e43b0/files/C78-203.tex), revision `rev_4dded74e5016462384b5830220c86b77` at pinned repository commit `bd5218964aa5efa77ac062e3600b8cfe70df528c`.

I reproduced the claimed serial characterization and the frontier dynamic program by a separate exhaustive checker, and checked why the state compression is valid for arbitrary instance size. The metadata describes an unresolved exploration, and the manuscript explicitly assumes a strict common priority on the partner side and a bound on the acceptability frontier. My finding concerns that restricted exact algorithm, not a new approximation ratio for unrestricted master-list instances.

The serial lemma depends on strict priority in both directions of the proof. Fix an allegedly stable matching, remove the partners of earlier agents, and consider agent \(u_i\). Any remaining acceptable partner is unmatched or matched to a later agent, so that partner strictly prefers \(u_i\) to its current status. If \(u_i\) is unmatched despite such a partner, or matched below its best available tie, both endpoints strictly prefer the deviation. Thus every stable matching must take a member of the best available tie whenever one exists. Conversely, in a serial output an unused pair cannot block: the partner either has an earlier, strictly preferred agent, or was available when \(u_i\) chose something weakly better. Indifference at the agent endpoint is sufficient to prevent a weak-stability blocking pair.

This proof also shows exactly why a stronger-looking greedy conclusion would fail. Serial choices inside a tie can consume a partner needed by a later agent, even though every such choice preserves stability. Optimizing cardinality therefore requires retaining alternatives rather than simply imposing arbitrary tie-breaking once.

The dynamic program retains only occupied partners whose last incident agent is still in the future. A rigorous state-equivalence check is as follows. Consider two serial prefixes with the same occupied subset of \(F_i\), possibly different numbers of past matches. Every partner that can appear in a later neighborhood is either in that frontier or has not appeared yet. In the latter case it is certainly free. Partners that have disappeared permanently cannot affect any future best available tie. Therefore the two prefixes admit exactly the same continuations, including the same incremental rewards. Keeping only the better prefix label cannot discard an optimal completion. This establishes the dominance step needed when transitions merge; merely observing that past partners are forgotten would not by itself justify maximizing the stored label.

I implemented two independent descriptions. The first enumerates every feasible partial injective matching, then tests the definition of a mutually strict blocking pair directly. The second generates serial outcomes. A third routine evaluates the frontier recurrence. For each of three named partners, a row can omit that partner or rank it in an arbitrary ordered tie partition. There are exactly 26 distinct rows on three partners, including the empty row. Combining these independently for three agents gives \(26^3=17{,}576\) instances. This covers every mutually acceptable three-by-three instance with arbitrary weak preferences on the agent side and the fixed strict common priority on the partner side; labels make the construction exhaustive without random sampling.

For every instance, the set of directly stable matchings equaled the set of serial outputs. In addition, the frontier result equaled the exhaustive optimum for the lexicographic objective consisting of cardinality first and a signed integer edge reward second. The signed secondary rewards exercise the claimed label extension without changing stability transitions. The checker reported zero serial-set mismatches and zero frontier-objective mismatches. The largest difference between cardinalities of stable matchings in this finite class was one, confirming that serial stability does not make arbitrary tie-breaking cardinality-optimal.

The executable checker is saved separately as \`checker.py\`. Its result is finite evidence, not a substitute for the state-equivalence proof above. In particular it says nothing about a possible bug that first appears beyond three agents, whereas the proof explains why the recurrence generalizes.

The stated complexity is also appropriately qualified in the manuscript. There are at most \(2^k\) occupied-frontier states. Per state and agent, scanning the neighborhood and emitting all members of the best available tie takes \(O(1+|\Gamma_i|)\) work if masks and projection operations have the specified word cost. Summation gives \(O(2^k(n+|E|))\) elementary word operations. For arbitrary-sized masks, additional bit-operation costs must be retained; the source already says so. Consequently \(k=O(\log(n+|W|))\) still gives polynomial time with ordinary bit operations.

No result of this check extends the serial lemma to weak priorities on both sides. Nor does the finite test control the frontier when many partners remain potentially relevant across the same cut. Those are explicitly excluded by the manuscript. Within the stated restriction, the independent combinatorial proof and exhaustive reproduction support the algorithm.
