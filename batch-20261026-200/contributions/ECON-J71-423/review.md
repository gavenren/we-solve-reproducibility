# Independent review of ECON-J71-423-A1: Identification in the invariant index model and its exact observational-equivalence failure

Finding: **Supports the claim**, within the explicitly checked scope below.

Target: ECON-J71-423-A1, revision r1 (rev_a2aa0aec6e584125bcdf03649d8940d6).

Checked scope: Identification in the invariant index model and its exact observational-equivalence failure.

I checked the transformation from hiring probabilities to the structural index. A known strictly increasing shock CDF and interior cell probabilities identify F^(−1)(π); subtracting ρ_qs and adding the common cost w isolates (1−ρ_q)m_l+cκ_l+v_l. At perfect information the prior term vanishes, so zero exposure identifies taste and the exposure difference identifies customer revenue. Subtracting that taste intercept at a lower precision and dividing by 1−ρ_L identifies the prior mean. The denominator must be nonzero. Remaining cell equations are compatibility tests, not extra free coefficients.

Unknown common wage shifts absolute taste levels while leaving cue differences identified; unknown shock scale changes the cardinal coefficient scale. These normalization issues do not disappear through factorial assignment. In the displayed logistic example, the four cue-one indices −.5,−1.5,−1.5,−2.5 recover m=−2, κ=−1, v=−.5. The customer effect is constant on the index scale, but logistic increments at different starting indices differ; a probability-scale interaction therefore need not be a structural interaction.

I checked the unrestricted alternative exactly. Adding d_l to the prior and e_l to customer revenue while subtracting (1−ρ_q)d_l+ce_l from regime-dependent employer utility leaves every index identical, hence every hiring cell probability identical. At full information the d term disappears but the c-dependent e shift remains, so full-information arms alone cannot validate the customer-versus-taste exclusion. This construction proves observational equivalence within the expanded choice class, beyond an informal statement that motives may be ambiguous.

The valid overidentifying restrictions are constant exposure differences across precision and affine zero-exposure indices in 1−ρ_q. Their rejection rejects the restricted model; their satisfaction cannot distinguish the expanded alternatives just constructed. I support this conditional identification theorem and counterexample, not empirical interpretation of the coefficients as prejudice in an actual occupation. Hiring-capacity competition, outputs among selected hires and recorded-group effects require additional models and are outside the independent-vacancy benchmark.
