# Independent review of ECON-I26-412-A1: Capacity-regime nonidentification and sharp rectangular net-return bounds

Finding: **Supports the claim**, within the explicitly checked scope below.

Target: ECON-I26-412-A1, revision r1 (rev_eda5fc67ced54640b7481ca2fe35df57).

Checked scope: Capacity-regime nonidentification and sharp rectangular net-return bounds.

I checked that the two observationally equivalent models preserve the intervention actually observed. Local randomized admission holds total instructional capacity fixed, whereas the added loss to existing students is activated only by the administrative capacity-expansion regime. It can therefore leave every baseline and local-admission potential outcome unchanged while changing the expansion's total value by md. With τ=10 and infrastructure increment two, model A gives eight; m=100,d=.1 gives model B value minus two. Large common baseline earnings preserve nonnegative levels, so the sign reversal is not an artifact of infeasible outcomes.

The accounting decomposition is valid on the fixed full applicant cohort: subtract adjacent-regime net outcomes, sum direct destination changes and residual resource/peer effects, then subtract only infrastructure costs not already in applicant costs. The special reduction to one entrant's direct gain needs unchanged previous attendance and precisely one changed destination indicator. A seat need not imply that pattern without the stated rule.

For the rectangular model I independently derived the Minkowski sum: the direct mean ranges over [l,u], m incumbent effects over [−md,0], r other effects over [−re,re], and the known cost subtracts κ. This gives [l−md−re−κ,u+re−κ]. The endpoint assignments and their convex mixtures alter only unobserved expansion responses, establishing sharpness under the independence-of-restrictions assumption. The entrant's quality change is already in [l,u] and must not be counted again among residuals. Cross-person equilibrium restrictions could narrow this interval, so this result cannot automatically be labeled sharp for a richer structural model.

For the lifetime tail, summing N applicants' bounded annual differences after T yields NMβ^(T+1)/(1−β), including incumbent tails. The complete-system Horvitz–Thompson design is unbiased even with arbitrary within-system interference because assignment selects the whole system potential total; following only enrollees changes that total and breaks the intended target. I support these constructions and bounds. They do not identify actual lifetime returns, capacity spillovers or national welfare from the local admissions effect alone.
