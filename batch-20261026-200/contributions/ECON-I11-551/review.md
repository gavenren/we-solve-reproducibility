# Review: coding costs, welfare fees and claims-only ambiguity

**Finding:** Supports the checked claims. **Checked scope:** provider implementation, unconstrained welfare-optimal variable fees and claims-only observational equivalence in ECON-I11-551-A1, revision r1.

I independently differentiated the provider objective. The Hessian is diagonal with entries $-(c_a+\alpha),-(c_q+\alpha),-c_k$, all strictly negative. Its nonnegative linear coefficients yield the stated nonnegative actions even at $\alpha=s=b=0$. The fixed payment changes participation but not effort; minimizing it gives $\max(0,\bar U-R)$. Thus the budget test is both necessary and sufficient for some nonnegative fixed payment, rather than only a sufficient financing condition.

For the treatment fee, differentiating social welfare after substitution gives
\[
W_s'=\frac{\theta c_a(1-\alpha)-(1+c_a)s}{(c_a+\alpha)^2}-s/c_k.
\]
Solving this equation produces the displayed formula. Its second derivative is strictly negative, so the stationary point is the global fee optimum. If $\alpha<1$ it is positive; then the first-order equation requires treatment below its clinical first-best. At $\alpha=1$, both variable rewards are zero and first-best behavior is already implemented. As $c_k\to\infty$, coding distortion vanishes and the fee approaches the clinical implementation value.

The claims-only example checks exactly. With $\alpha=0,\theta=1$, both cost pairs $(1,1)$ and $(2,2/3)$ generate billed slope two. They generate actual treatment slopes one and one-half, respectively. The optimal fees are $1/3$ and $2/9$, so identical complete billed-volume response functions do not identify the welfare-optimal fee. An independently audited treatment slope separates these two models.

A boundary limitation is important and is stated correctly: an unconstrained optimum failing the expenditure test is not a feasible recommendation. Compactness of a bounded fee menu proves existence if feasible, but does not solve the binding-budget optimum. Likewise the enforceable all-patient access obligation removes selection only within this benchmark.

No error was found in these claims. The review does not certify altruism, verifiable quality, universal access enforcement, empirical clinical-production parameters or a general health-payment policy. Payment is treated as a transfer under equal welfare weights; replacing real cost with reimbursement would change the objective.
