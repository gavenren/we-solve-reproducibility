# The full all-decompositions Murre package for smooth projective toric varieties

Claim type: **Partial result**.

Every smooth projective complex toric variety satisfies the full A–D package **for every Chow–Künneth decomposition**, with
\[
 F^0\mathrm{CH}^j(X)_{\mathbb Q}=\mathrm{CH}^j(X)_{\mathbb Q},
 \qquad F^r\mathrm{CH}^j(X)_{\mathbb Q}=0\quad(r\ge1).
\]
The useful point is that the projectors themselves are unique as Chow correspondences in this class. Thus the universal quantifier is proved, rather than replaced by a preferred toric splitting.

We use the established cycle-class theorem for smooth projective toric varieties and their products: odd rational cohomology vanishes and
\[
 \mathrm{CH}^j(X)_{\mathbb Q}\cong H^{2j}(X,\mathbb Q),\qquad
 \mathrm{CH}^d(X\times X)_{\mathbb Q}\cong H^{2d}(X\times X,\mathbb Q). \tag{1}
\]
One source route is Totaro [1, Theorem3]: toric varieties are linear schemes, their rational Chow groups identify with the lowest weight part of Borel–Moore homology, and smooth proper homology is pure. Odd vanishing also follows from the usual affine-cell filtration of a smooth projective toric variety. The latter can be obtained from a generic one-parameter subgroup: on each smooth fixed-point affine chart, the attracting set sets negative-weight coordinates to zero and is an affine space; an equivariant projective embedding orders these attracting sets into a closed filtration. Cellular localization then gives only even homology. The same facts apply to$X\times X$, again smooth projective toric. These are classical inputs, not new toric computations.

Here is a complete abstract deduction from(1). Choose a basis $a_{j,1},\ldots,a_{j,b_j}$ of $\mathrm{CH}^j(X)_{\mathbb Q}$ and its dual basis $b_{j,1},\ldots,b_{j,b_j}\in\mathrm{CH}^{d-j}(X)_{\mathbb Q}$ for the perfect intersection pairing. Such a dual exists by Poincaré duality and(1). Define
\[
 p_{2j}=\sum_\nu b_{j,\nu}\times a_{j,\nu}\in\mathrm{CH}^d(X\times X)_{\mathbb Q},
 \qquad p_{2j+1}=0.
\]
With the prompt’s pull–intersect–push action, $p_{2j}$ acts on $\mathrm{CH}^j$ by the identity, and on every other Chow codimension by zero: only a first-factor intersection of total codimension$d$ has a degree that survives pushforward. In cohomology it projects onto$H^{2j}$. The cohomology classes therefore satisfy orthogonality, idempotence and sum-to-diagonal. All these identities lift to actual Chow identities because the cycle map on$\mathrm{CH}^d(X^2)$ is injective. This constructs A in precisely the correspondence convention of the prompt.

If$(q_i)$ is **any** other decomposition satisfying A, $q_i-p_i$ acts trivially on every cohomology group. Künneth and Poincaré duality identify a degree-zero correspondence’s full cohomology class with those graded actions, so$\operatorname{cl}(q_i-p_i)=0$. Injectivity in(1) gives$q_i=p_i$ in the Chow group. No nilpotence assumption or conjugacy-up-to-isomorphism argument is needed.

It follows that on$\mathrm{CH}^j$ the only nonzero projector is$p_{2j}$, acting as identity. Every index$i<j$ or$i>2j$ therefore acts as zero, proving the prompt’s strong B. For$r\ge1$ the displayed intersection defining$F^r$ includes$\ker(p_{2j})_*=0$, so it is zero. For$r=0$ the intersection is empty, hence the whole group. This proves C, including all$r$, by actual equality of the unique systems. Finally the homologically trivial subgroup is zero by(1), so D holds. The case$j=0$ and the endpoint$r>2j$ obey the same argument.

Full prior A2 gives a conditional nilpotent-conjugacy criterion and a matrix obstruction to deriving C from nilpotence alone; A1 is a definition/source record, and A3 summarizes unverified general$p_g=0$ claims. This report gives an unconditional unbounded-dimensional geometric class in which injectivity on the square removes that obstruction completely. It is credited classical geometry with a full all-decompositions deduction, not a new general Murre theorem.
