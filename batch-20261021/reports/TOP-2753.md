# Rational second homology does not provide a finite presentation of Torelli

**Problem:** TOP-2753

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** Precisely reports Minahan–Putman's 2025 preprint: rational H₂ is finite-dimensional for g≥5 and algebraic for g≥6, with the closed-surface H² calculation at g≥6. Explains exactly which finite-presentation obstruction this removes and which integral and relation-theoretic gaps remain.

A recent substantial result changes one natural strategy for the prompt, but does not supply a presentation. Minahan–Putman's checked 2025 preprint proves that
\[
 \dim_{\mathbb Q}H_2(\mathcal I_g;\mathbb Q)<\infty\quad(g\geq5),
\]
and that this homology is an algebraic \(\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}\)-representation for \(g\geq6\) (Theorem B). Thus infinite-dimensional rational second homology cannot obstruct finite presentation in that range.

For closed surfaces, their Theorem A calculates the degree-two rational representation at \(g\geq6\):
\[
 H^2(\mathcal I_g;\mathbb Q)\cong
 V_{(1,1)}\oplus V_{(1,1,1,1)}
 \oplus V_{(2,2,1,1)}\oplus V_{(1,1,1,1,1,1)} .
\]
Here \(V_\lambda\) is the irreducible algebraic symplectic representation indexed by the partition \(\lambda\). The theorem also says the cohomology is spanned by degree-one cup products. This is a rational representation calculation; it is not a finite collection of group relators.

The finite-presentation obstruction itself can be checked independently. If a group \(G\) has a finite presentation, its presentation complex \(K\) is a finite 2-complex. Attach cells in dimensions at least three to make a \(K(G,1)\). No 1- or 2-cells are added, so \(H_2(G;\mathbb Z)\) is a quotient of \(H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\). The latter is a subgroup of the finitely generated free group \(C_2(K;\mathbb Z)\), hence finitely generated. Consequently finite presentation forces integral \(H_2\) to be finitely generated and, after tensoring with \(\mathbb Q\), forces finite rational dimension.

The converse implication is not supplied by this argument or the preprint. Rational coefficients forget all torsion and need not control integral finite generation; even integral \(H_2\) finite generation would not by itself exhibit or prove completeness of a finite relator set. The preprint explicitly leaves integral \(H_2\) finite generation open in Remark 1.1.

The proof's main work is a representation-stability argument on homology of Torelli subgroups. It controls a cokernel from curve stabilizers, constructs generators through equivariant homology, and identifies relations forcing a finite-dimensional algebraic quotient. These are homological presentations of representations, rather than a presentation of the entire group. Confusing those two uses of “generators and relations” would falsely answer the prompt.

Accordingly this report records a substantial, version-specific obstruction-removal theorem. It neither resolves \(g=3,4\) by a low-genus substitute nor claims finite presentability for \(g\geq5\); all genera requested in the prompt still require an actual complete group-presentation argument or a different obstruction beyond the theorem reported here.
