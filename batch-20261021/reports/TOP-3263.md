# A complete additive proof for arbitrary prime cyclic connection sets

**Claim type: Literature report.** Let \(p\) be prime and let \(\varnothing\ne S\subseteq\mathbb F_p\setminus\{0\}\), \(|S|=r\). The digraph with arcs \(x\to x+s\), \(s\in S\), contains a simple directed cycle of length at most \(\lceil p/r\rceil\). Opposite arcs are allowed. This is a classical special case of Hamidoune's much broader Cayley-digraph theorem, recorded with proof in Nathanson [1, Theorem7]. The point here is a self-contained proof for **every** connection set on a prime cyclic group, beyond the interval connection sets in the earlier attempt.

First prove the required additive inequality
\[
 |A+B|\ge\min(p,|A|+|B|-1)
 \tag{1}
\]
for nonempty subsets of \(\mathbb F_p\). If \(|A|+|B|>p\), then \(A\) and \(z-B\) intersect for every \(z\), so \(A+B=\mathbb F_p\). Otherwise put \(a=|A|,b=|B|\), and suppose \(|A+B|\le a+b-2\). Choose a set \(C\) of size \(a+b-2<p\) containing \(A+B\). The polynomial
\[
 P(X,Y)=\prod_{c\in C}(X+Y-c)
\]
vanishes on \(A\times B\), while its coefficient of \(X^{a-1}Y^{b-1}\) is \(\binom{a+b-2}{a-1}\ne0\) in \(\mathbb F_p\).

For completeness the grid coefficient identity used here is
\[
 [X^{a-1}Y^{b-1}]P
 =\sum_{u\in A,v\in B}
 \frac{P(u,v)}
 {\prod_{u'\in A-\{u\}}(u-u')\prod_{v'\in B-\{v\}}(v-v')},
 \qquad \deg P\le a+b-2.
\]
To verify it, use linearity and check each monomial \(X^iY^j\). The one-variable Lagrange identity gives sum0 for exponent less than \(a-1\), and sum1 for exponent \(a-1\), with the analogous statement for \(B\). Unless \(i=a-1,j=b-1\), the total-degree restriction forces at least one exponent to be below its threshold. Thus both sides agree on every admissible monomial. The zero evaluations of \(P\) contradict its nonzero coefficient, proving (1).

Set \(A=S\cup\{0\}\), and put \(h=\lceil p/r\rceil\). Iterating (1) gives
\[
 |(h-1)A|\ge \min\{p,1+(h-1)r\}.
\]
If a positive sum of at most \(h\) elements of \(S\) were never zero, then \((h-1)A\) would be disjoint from \(-S\): an element common to the two sets would express \(-s\) as at most \(h-1\) nonzero summands after the zeros are removed. Therefore
\[
 p\ge |(h-1)A|+r\ge1+hr,
\]
where the alternative minimum value \(p\) is already impossible because \(r>0\). But \(hr\ge p\), a contradiction. There are consequently \(s_1,\ldots,s_\ell\in S\), \(1\le\ell\le h\), with sum0.

The partial sums give a positive-length closed directed walk. Choose a shortest positive zero-sum sublist of consecutive steps in this walk. No two internal partial sums can coincide, since deleting the segment between them would give a shorter positive closed subwalk. Thus it is a simple cycle, of length at most \(h\). Since \(0\notin S\), its length is at least2. This also treats \(p=2,r=1\).

This proof uses primality at the coefficient step and does not silently extend (1) to composite cyclic groups. Hamidoune's all-finite-group theorem uses a different additive result, not this prime-field argument. In particular, this is neither a proof for arbitrary digraphs nor a consequence of replacing an arbitrary digraph by a Cayley graph.

The retained second attempt covers directed interval circulants, reductions and small degree regimes; it does not give this arbitrary-set proof. The other earlier contribution is an externally hosted claimed solution; it is not a premise of this report, and no judgment about its complete general argument is made here. No execution is claimed.
