# Verified derangement-product families and the exact primitive reduction

Claim type: **Literature report**.

The universal assertion in the prompt is stronger than the sufficiently-large-order theorem of Larsen–Shalev–Tiep. Combining precise primary results does, however, settle several entire families and many imprimitive actions. This report states that coverage with exact quantifiers, proves the transport to finer actions, and gives a direct quantitative certificate for two-factor coverage.

The primary source boundaries are as follows.

* Larsen–Shalev–Tiep[1, Theorem10.2] prove $G=D^2$ once the order of the finite simple group is sufficiently large. The threshold is absolute and the statement applies to every transitive action; it is not a lower bound on the permutation degree alone. Their Theorem11.2 proves this for all $A_n$, $n\ge5$, with arbitrary transitive action.
* Burness–Fusari[2, Theorem9(i)] establish the conclusion for all alternating, sporadic and rank-one simple groups, with arbitrary transitive action. Their stronger class-product conclusion has an explicit $L_2(7),S_4$ exception, but $D^2=G$ holds there too.
* Their Theorem9(ii) establishes $D^2=G$ for simple primitive actions with soluble point stabilizer. No sufficiently-large-order assumption remains in this subclass.

These are credited theorems, not new proofs of classification-dependent results. Here is the full reduction keeping the action dependence. For a proper subgroup $H<G$,
\[
 D_H=G\setminus\bigcup_{x\in G}H^x. \tag{1}
\]
Indeed $g$ fixes a coset if and only if a conjugate of$g$ lies in$H$. If$H\le M<G$, then $D_M\subseteq D_H$, so $D_M^2=G$ implies $D_H^2=G$. Every proper subgroup lies in a maximal subgroup. For a nonabelian simple$G$, every proper-subgroup coset action is faithful because its kernel is a proper normal subgroup. Thus proving the assertion in all primitive actions is equivalent to proving it in all transitive actions.

In particular the soluble-stabilizer theorem gives a larger explicitly described class than its primitive wording: **every**$G/H$ with$H$ contained in a soluble maximal subgroup works. There is no need for$H$ to be maximal or for$G/H$ to be primitive. Conversely, a soluble$H$ contained only in nonsoluble maximal subgroups is not covered by this deduction. Solubility of$H$ alone is not silently used to choose a soluble overgroup.

The identity must be included. The derangement set is inverse-closed, since an element and its inverse have exactly the same fixed points. It is nonempty: if all group elements fixed a point in a transitive action of degree$m>1$, the sum of fixed-point counts would be at least$|G|+m-1$, contradicting the orbit-counting total$|G|$. Hence for$d\in D$, $1=dd^{-1}\in D^2$. This explains why a theorem covering$G\setminus\{1\}$ by products of derangement classes is enough.

A useful stronger quantitative condition is
\[
 |D|>|G|/2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \#\{(a,b)\in D^2:ab=g\}\ge2|D|-|G|>0\quad(g\in G). \tag{2}
\]
For fixed$g$, possible$a$ are the intersection$D\cap gD^{-1}$, whose size is at least$2|D|-|G|$ by inclusion-exclusion. Then$b=a^{-1}g$ is determined. Thus(2) certifies the **same two-factor** conclusion for every target; generation of$G$ by$D$ would be a weaker statement and does not replace it. The proportion$89/325$ from a related conjecture is below$1/2$, so that lower bound alone cannot justify(2).
