# Sharp involutory twisted-power gap and the abelian norm calculation

Claim type: **Partial result**.

The literal prompt asks for two ways to deduce a gap for the noncommutative twisted norm. We establish the sharp base constant
\[
c_2=\frac34,
\]
and compute the corresponding restricted constant, for every fixed positive integer $n$, when the underlying group is abelian:
\[
c_n^{\mathrm{ab}}=\frac12.
\]
The supremum defining the latter still excludes pairs for which the norm equation is an identity. These are uniform exact bounds, rather than checks of a bounded list of groups. They do not prove either of the prompt's general implications for composite $n$.

For a finite group $K$ and automorphism $\alpha$, write
\[
I(\alpha)=\{x\in K:\alpha(x)=x^{-1}\}.
\]
We first give a complete density argument, without a classification of finite groups or automorphisms.

**Four-translate lemma.** If $|I(\alpha)|>3|K|/4$, then $K$ is abelian and $\alpha$ is inversion on all of $K$.

Let $g,h\in K$ be arbitrary and put $I=I(\alpha)$. The four subsets
\[
I,\quad g^{-1}I,\quad h^{-1}I,\quad (gh)^{-1}I
\]
have a common element $t$: otherwise their four complements would cover $K$, although the sum of their cardinalities is less than $|K|$. Thus $t,gt,ht,ght\in I$. From the first two membership conditions,
\[
\alpha(g)t^{-1}=\alpha(gt)=(gt)^{-1}=t^{-1}g^{-1},
\qquad \alpha(g)=t^{-1}g^{-1}t.
\]
Likewise $\alpha(h)=t^{-1}h^{-1}t$. Multiplicativity and the condition on $ght$ now give
\[
t^{-1}g^{-1}h^{-1}=\alpha(g)\alpha(h)\alpha(t)
=\alpha(ght)=t^{-1}h^{-1}g^{-1}.
\]
Therefore $g$ and $h$ commute. Since they were arbitrary, $K$ is abelian. On an abelian group the map $x\mapsto x\alpha(x)$ is a homomorphism, and $I$ is its kernel. A proper subgroup of a finite group has index at least two. Since $|I|>|K|/2$, this kernel is all of $K$, proving the second conclusion.

For an automorphism with $\alpha^2=1$, the prompt's twisted equation is precisely
\[
x\alpha(x)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\in I(\alpha).
\]
The lemma proves that every admissible nonidentity equation has solution proportion at most $3/4$. To obtain equality, take the dihedral group
\[
D_8=\langle r,s:r^4=s^2=1,\ srs=r^{-1}\rangle
\]
and $\alpha=\mathrm{id}$. Its square-one elements are $1,r^2$ and the four reflections $r^js$, $0\le j<4$. The other two elements are $r,r^3$, both of order four. Thus exactly six of eight elements solve the equation, and it is not an identity. This proves the claimed exact value of $c_2$.

Now let $A$ be any finite abelian group, written additively, and let $\alpha^n=1$. Define the endomorphism
\[
N=1+\alpha+\cdots+\alpha^{n-1}:A\longrightarrow A.
\]
Commutativity is what permits this to be an endomorphism. The solution set is $\ker N$, so the homomorphism theorem gives the exact formula
\[
\frac{|X_{n,\alpha}(A)|}{|A|}=\frac1{|N(A)|}.
\]
Consequently every nonidentity equation has proportion at most $1/2$. This formula also identifies the obstruction to carrying the argument directly to nonabelian groups: there the ordered twisted product is generally not a homomorphism, so its fibers need not be cosets of one kernel.

The bound is sharp for **each fixed** $n$, including even $n$. Write $n=2^a u$ with $u$ odd, choose $A=\mathbb Z/2^{a+1}\mathbb Z$, and use $\alpha=\mathrm{id}$. Multiplication by $n$ has image of order two, since $\gcd(n,2^{a+1})=2^a$. Its kernel therefore has index two and the equation is nonidentity. This establishes $c_n^{\mathrm{ab}}=1/2$, with no dependence on $|A|$.

The three-quarter inversion threshold is classical; Bors, Proposition 2.1, reviews it using centralizers. The proof above is an explicit four-translate derivation specialized to the prompt's constant, followed by the sharp abelian calculation for every parameter. No novelty claim is made for the threshold or the mechanism. The remaining noncommutative implications in parts (a) and (b) are outside this result.
