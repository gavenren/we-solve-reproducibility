# Integral K5 injectivity in all equicharacteristics, and the ramification boundary

Claim type: **Literature report**.

For every **equicharacteristic** Noetherian regular local domain$A$, the integral map $K_5(A)\to K_5(F)$ is injective, with no finite-type or dimension bound. This is a direct consequence of Panin’s published integral Gersten theorem [1, TheoremA]. It includes arbitrary regular local rings containing fields, not only localizations of smooth affine varieties. It does not include the ramified mixed-characteristic case singled out in the prompt.

The exact source theorem states that for an equicharacteristic regular local ring the Gersten complex
\[
 0\longrightarrow K_q(A)\longrightarrow K_q(F)
 \longrightarrow\bigoplus_{\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak p=1}K_{q-1}(\kappa(\mathfrak p))
 \longrightarrow\bigoplus_{\operatorname{ht}\mathfrak p=2}K_{q-2}(\kappa(\mathfrak p))\longrightarrow\cdots
\]
is exact. Here $q=5$ gives the requested injectivity at the first arrow. These are integral Quillen groups; no tensoring with$\mathbb Q$ and no prime inversion appears. The primary paper explains that it combines Quillen’s geometric case with Popescu/Grothendieck approximation. We credit that deep passage to arbitrary equicharacteristic rings rather than asserting that every such ring is itself smooth finite type.

There is a useful exact description of what remains to be excluded in other characteristics. For a nonzero $s\in A$, let $K(A\text{ on }V(s))$ be the K-theory of perfect complexes acyclic on$A[1/s]$. The localization sequence gives
\[
 K_5(A\text{ on }V(s))\longrightarrow K_5(A)
 \longrightarrow K_5(A[1/s]). \tag{1}
\]
Since $F$ is the filtered union of the rings$A[1/s]$ and algebraic K-theory commutes with filtered localization,
\[
 \ker(K_5(A)\to K_5(F))
 =\bigcup_{s\ne0}\operatorname{im}\bigl(K_5(A\text{ on }V(s))\to K_5(A)\bigr). \tag{2}
\]
To see the equality rather than only containment, a class dying in the filtered colimit dies at one finite localization; exactness of(1) then supplies its supported antecedent. Conversely any such supported image vanishes in$F$. The union is directed: the support for$s$ and that for$t$ are both contained in$V(st)$. This does not say that every supported K-group vanishes; it is its **pushforward image** that must vanish. Panin’s theorem proves that vanishing for the entire union in equicharacteristic.

The earlier A1 claims a nonzero kernel class for
\[
 A=(V[x,y]/(5+x^4+y^4))_{(5,x,y)},
\]
where$V$ is the integer ring of an unramified extension of$\mathbb Q_5$. We do not verify its claimed K-class or its detection argument. We can, however, check exactly why this local model would not contradict the theorem just used. In the regular local ring $R=V[x,y]_{(5,x,y)}$ of dimension3, the element $f=5+x^4+y^4$ has nonzero linear term$5$ modulo the square of the maximal ideal. It is a non-zero-divisor, so$R/(f)$ has dimension2. Its maximal ideal is generated by the images of$x,y$, since$5=-x^4-y^4$, and therefore it is regular local of dimension2. In particular it is a domain. The coefficient map$V\to A$ is injective: the polynomial is monic in$x$, so the quotient before localization is free as a$V[y]$-module, and the resulting regular local ring has characteristic0 (equivalently$f$ is not associated to$5$ in$R$). Its residue characteristic is5 and $5\in\mathfrak m^4\subset\mathfrak m^2$. Thus this is genuinely ramified mixed characteristic, beyond the equicharacteristic theorem.

Regularity of that ring alone supplies no nonzero K-class. Formula(2) separates the two tasks an actual counterexample would need: a supported image and a proof that it survives integrally in$K_5(A)$. Neither is established by citing the earlier summary. The literal all-characteristic prompt is consequently not claimed solved or disproved here; this contribution specifies the full established equicharacteristic theorem and the exact support/ramification boundary.
