# A fully Galois-equivariant restriction bijection with arbitrary nonabelian Sylow complement

Claim type: **Partial result**.

For every finite semidirect product $G=A\rtimes P$ with$A$ abelian of order prime to$p$ and$P$ an **arbitrary**$p$-group, restriction itself gives a bijection
\[
 \operatorname{Irr}_{p'}(G)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Irr}_{p'}(N_G(P)). \tag{1}
\]
It commutes with the full Galois group of$\mathbb Q(\zeta_{|G|})$, and hence with the specified$\mathcal H_p$. The earlier A1 required$P$ abelian; the proof below removes that restriction and handles higher-dimensional inertia representations. This is a known-type explicit class, not a general McKay–Navarro theorem or a blockwise claim.

Write$A$ additively, let$e=\exp A$, choose an integer$b$ with$b|P|\equiv1\pmod e$, and put
\[
 T(a)=b\sum_{u\in P}u(a),\quad C=C_A(P),\quad B=[A,P].
\]
Averaging is valid even when$P$ is nonabelian: left multiplication permutes its elements. Thus$T(A)\subset C$ and$T|_C=1$. It kills$B$. In$A/B$, every$u$ acts trivially, so$T(a)\equiv b|P|a\equiv a$. Therefore$\ker T=B$ and$A=C\oplus B$. It follows that restriction induces
\[
 \widehat A^P\cong\widehat C,\qquad
 \lambda\mapsto\lambda|_C,
 \quad\eta\mapsto\eta\circ T. \tag{2}
\]
These are actual group characters, not averages of possibly nonlinear characters.

We next identify all prime-to-$p$ irreducibles. In an irreducible representation$V$ of$G$, decompose into weight spaces for the abelian group$A$. The occurring weights form one$P$-orbit, since the sum over any orbit is$G$-invariant. Choose a weight$\lambda$ and its stabilizer$P_\lambda$. Its weight space$W$ is an irreducible representation of$P_\lambda$: a proper invariant subspace would generate, by its distinct weight translates, a proper$G$-subrepresentation. Conversely the translates of$W$ span$V$ and give
\[
 \dim V=[P:P_\lambda]\dim W. \tag{3}
\]
No common eigenvector for a nonabelian inertia group has been assumed.

Every irreducible complex degree of a finite$p$-group is a power of$p$. One standard short proof uses the general divisibility$\chi(1)\mid |H|$: class sums act on an irreducible by the algebraic integers $|K|\chi(g)/\chi(1)$, and character values are algebraic integers, so orthogonality expresses $|H|/\chi(1)$ as a sum of algebraic integers; it is rational and hence an integer. Apply this with$H=P_\lambda$. Thus both factors in(3) are$p$-powers. Degree prime to$p$ forces$P_\lambda=P$ and$\dim W=1$. Conversely every pair
\[
 \lambda\in\widehat A^P,
 \qquad\mu\in\operatorname{Irr}_1(P).
\]

This pair defines the linear character$\chi_{\lambda,\mu}(au)=\lambda(a)\mu(u)$ of$G$. The weight argument proves these are exactly$\operatorname{Irr}_{p'}(G)$, with distinct pairs giving distinct restrictions to$A,P$.

An element$a\in A$ normalizes$P$ precisely when it centralizes$P$: its commutators with$P$ lie in$A$, and normalizing would put them in$P$ as well; the intersection is1. Since$P$ already normalizes itself,
\[
 N_G(P)=C\times P.
\]
This direct product need not be abelian. Its prime-to-$p$ irreducibles are exactly$\eta\otimes\mu$ with$\eta\in\widehat C$ and$\mu\in\operatorname{Irr}_1(P)$, again by the$p$-group degree fact. Under these labels restriction in(1) is exactly$(\lambda,\mu)\mapsto(\lambda|_C,\mu)$, a bijection by(2).

For every cyclotomic Galois automorphism$\sigma$ and every$n\in N_G(P)$, one has $(\chi|_N)^\sigma(n)=\sigma(\chi(n))=(\chi^\sigma)|_N(n)$. Therefore this **single** restriction map is equivariant under the full group, not merely separately compatible choices for individual automorphisms. All values on the normalizer lie in the same cyclotomic field because$|N|$ divides$|G|$.

For an explicit nonabelian family, let$P=D_8$, let$A=C_3$ and let a nontrivial quotient$D_8\twoheadrightarrow C_2$ act by inversion on$C_3$. Here$C_A(P)=1$, the Sylow normalizer is the proper subgroup$P$, and exactly$|P/P'|=4$ characters on either side have odd degree. The theorem covers arbitrary larger nonabelian$p$-groups and coprime abelian modules, not just this example. The source [1] fixes the global$\mathcal H_p$ formulation; its all-group prime-two theorem is context, not an input to this all-prime class proof.
