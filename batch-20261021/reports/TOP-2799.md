# The exact orientable two-cusp minimum and orientation-cover bookkeeping

**Problem:** TOP-2799

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** Complete published classification of the orientable n=2 class in part (d), with an orientation-double-cover lower bound when a nonorientable manifold has 2t+k=2. No minimum for the other classes or cusp counts is asserted.

One of the requested minima has a sharp published answer:
\[
 v(\{\text{orientable, exactly two cusps}\})=v_8,
 \qquad
 v_8=4\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}{(-1)^j\over(2j+1)^2}.
\]
Here \(v_8\) is the volume of a regular ideal octahedron in curvature \(-1\). Agol's Theorem 3.6 says that the only minimizers, up to unoriented isometry, are the Whitehead-link complement and the \((-2,3,8)\)-pretzel-link complement (the Whitehead sister). This answers part (d) at \(n=2\), including its equality classification.

The mechanism explains why this is more than a numerical census observation. Agol first proves that an essential two-sided surface avoiding a specified cusp can be changed by annular compressions until its complementary guts have negative Euler characteristic. The existence of an appropriate surface in the two-cusp case is supplied by the essential-surface theorem used in his proof. The credited guts-volume and geodesic-boundary estimates then give
\[
 \operatorname{vol}(M)\geq
 \operatorname{vol}(\operatorname{Guts}(X))
 \geq -v_8\chi(\operatorname{Guts}(X))\geq v_8 .
\]
The equality analysis forces the regular-octahedron situation; Agol's face-gluing classification with two cusps leaves exactly the two manifolds above. Their octahedral decompositions realize \(v_8\). These deep geometric and equality inputs are reported from the cited theorem, not inferred from a finite list of software examples.

The wording about “orientable cusps” in part (c) deserves separate bookkeeping. Suppose a nonorientable \(M\) has \(t\) torus cusps and \(k\) Klein-bottle cusps. Its connected orientation double cover \(\widetilde M\) has
\[
 2t+k\ \text{torus cusps},\qquad
 \operatorname{vol}(\widetilde M)=2\operatorname{vol}(M).
\]
Indeed the orientation character restricts trivially to a torus-cusp group, giving two lifted cusps. Its restriction to a Klein-bottle-cusp group is nontrivial, giving one cusp whose cross-section is the orientation double cover of the Klein bottle. Consequently, if \(2t+k=2\), Agol's theorem proves \(\operatorname{vol}(M)\geq v_8/2\). Equality would require the orientation cover to be one of the two classified complements; this calculation alone does not produce a free orientation-reversing involution or prove sharpness in any nonorientable class.

In particular one cannot apply the two-cusp theorem merely because a manifold has two torus cusps while ignoring additional Klein-bottle cusps. Closed nonorientable minima, all the other cusp counts, and the two possible readings of part (c) require additional theorems and are not settled here.
