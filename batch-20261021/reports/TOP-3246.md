# Girth-five squares and a transfer to total list coloring

**Claim type: Literature report.** Bonamy, Cranston and Postle prove that there is an absolute constant \(C\) such that every planar graph of girth at least5 and maximum degree \(\Delta\ge C\) has a square that is \((\Delta+2)\)-choosable, and even \((\Delta+2)\)-paintable [1]. We credit this theorem rather than supply its discharging proof. We then check its implication for the literal bound and prove a subdivision-to-total-graph transfer. No numerical value of \(C\) is assumed.

A graph is \(k\)-choosable if every assignment of lists of at least \(k\) colors to the vertices permits a proper list coloring. This implies ordinary \(k\)-colorability by choosing one common list. Paintability is stronger still, but no online strategy is reconstructed here. The girth hypothesis excludes triangles and4-cycles; forests have infinite girth by convention. There is no restriction on order or on connectivity.

Increasing \(C\) if necessary to be at least8, the theorem gives
\[
 \chi(G^2)\le\Delta+2\le\lfloor3\Delta/2\rfloor+1
 \quad(\Delta\ge C).
\]
Indeed the second inequality is equivalent to \(\Delta+1\le\lfloor3\Delta/2\rfloor\), valid for every integer \(\Delta\ge2\). Thus the large-degree girth-five class meets the prompt's high-degree target with a substantially stronger list-coloring conclusion. This does not settle the small-degree branches of the statement, nor planar graphs with triangles or4-cycles.

For a different consequence, let \(H\) be any finite simple planar graph and subdivide **each** edge once, obtaining \(S(H)\). Its vertices are the original vertices \(V(H)\) and one new vertex \(x_e\) per edge \(e\). This is planar; each simple cycle of \(S(H)\) alternates original and subdivision vertices and corresponds to a simple cycle of \(H\), so its length is at least6. If \(\Delta(H)\ge2\), then \(\Delta(S(H))=\Delta(H)\).

We claim the exact graph identity
\[
 S(H)^2\cong T(H),
 \tag{1}
\]
where the total graph \(T(H)\) has one vertex for every vertex and edge of \(H\), and adjacency means adjacent vertices, incident edges, or an incident vertex-edge pair. Identify \(x_e\) with \(e\). Two original vertices have distance2 in \(S(H)\) exactly when they are adjacent in \(H\). Two subdivision vertices \(x_e,x_f\) have distance2 exactly when the corresponding edges share an endpoint. An original vertex and \(x_e\) are at distance1 exactly when incident. If they are nonincident, no length2 path joins them, because \(S(H)\) is bipartite and they lie on opposite sides. These exhaust the cases and prove (1), including disconnected components.

Apply the source theorem to \(S(H)\). For every planar \(H\) with \(\Delta(H)\ge\max(C,2)\), the total graph is \((\Delta(H)+2)\)-choosable. Equivalently, arbitrary lists of that size on **both vertices and edges** of \(H\) permit a total coloring: adjacent vertices, incident edges and an edge's endpoints all receive distinct colors. The conclusion even transfers paintability through (1). This argument requires no artificial global palette and no compatibility hypothesis on lists.

The previous attempt treats forests exactly, subcubic planar squares via a different theorem, and lower-bound configurations with many short cycles. It does not establish the high-degree girth-five list/online result or identity(1) and its total-list consequence. The latter identity also prevents the mistaken claim that subdivision squares contain only a copy of \(H\): they contain the entire total graph.

The source theorem is a deep external input. Its arXivv2 abstract and publication record were checked; full proof access was unavailable through the attempted versioned PDF route. The transfer and numerical comparison are fully proved above. No finite computation or explicit threshold verification is claimed.
