# A quantitative, proof-level upper bound for the flip graph

For the prompt's graph $\mathcal A_n$ of triangulations of a convex labelled $n$-gon, the complete proof of Addario-Berry, Reed, Scott and Wood, arXiv:1811.08972v2, yields
\[
\chi(\mathcal A_n)\le11556\lceil\log_3 n\rceil\qquad(n\ge3).
\]
The result is theirs. This report identifies an explicit bound and the separation certificates in the revised proof, rather than treating a logarithmic abstract as a proof of the opposite, lower-bound question. The earlier archive attempt studies independent pentagon products and mentions the abstract upper bound; its product-colouring argument is not repeated here.

The proof framework is as follows. Associate to a triangulation a vector of five colours. For every flip, prove that at least one coordinate changes. If the respective palettes have sizes $b_1,\ldots,b_5$, the vector is a proper colouring with at most their product many colours. This implication is immediate from the definition of an edge and does not require any coordinate by itself to colour the whole graph.

Here are the precise source ingredients. The authors assign an edge of endpoints$U,V$ its scale $\lceil\log_\alpha|U-V|\rceil$, with $\alpha\ge3$, and classify triangles according to the equalities among their edge scales. The first coordinate colours their four triangle-type counts using a fixed auxiliary graph of maximum degree at most320, hence321 colours. Proposition2 covers flips changing this coordinate. Proposition4 supplies a scale-sensitive coordinate with palette size $3\lceil\log_\alpha n\rceil$. Proposition5 handles three remaining local configurations through two parity coordinates. Proposition7 deals with the final two configurations using colours of bounded-size reduced triangulations obtained from monochromatic-scale components of the dual tree. That last palette has size $\chi(\mathcal A_{2\alpha-1})$. These propositions and their exhaustion of flip configurations are the substantive imported theorem; they are not inferred from a few tested triangulations.

Multiplying the palettes actually defined before Proposition4 gives the following safe bound:
\[
\chi(\mathcal A_n)\le321\,[3\lceil\log_\alpha n\rceil]\,2\,2\,
\chi(\mathcal A_{2\alpha-1}).
\]
Taking $\alpha=3$ reduces the small graph to the pentagon $\mathcal A_5=C_5$, whose chromatic number is three. The coefficient is therefore $321\cdot3\cdot2\cdot2\cdot3=11556$. The p11 final display instead places the factor3 inside the ceiling, yielding a smaller count that does not follow from the stated modulus. We retain the proven palette size and do not infer that smaller rounding. This extraction keeps the polygon-size indexing of the prompt: the associahedron's polytope dimension is$n-3$, not$n$.

The upper bound gives no evidence for an increasing lower bound. In particular an unbounded palette in one supplied algorithm does not force the optimal chromatic number to be unbounded. For a quantitative consistency check, if $\chi(\mathcal A_n)\ge q$, the display implies
\[
n\ge3^{q/11556-1},
\]
because $\lceil\log_3 n\rceil\le\log_3 n+1$. This is only a necessary size condition for a hypothetical large-chromatic example. It supplies neither such an example nor a fixed-colour colouring for all$n$.

The contribution is thus a precisely scoped credited literature report with a checked numerical palette bound and explicit dependencies. The conjecture in the literal prompt asks whether $\sup_n\chi(\mathcal A_n)$ is infinite; that statement remains unproved here.
