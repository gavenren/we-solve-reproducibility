# An upper certificate that records the concurrency obstruction

Let$B$ be the total number of bounded edges of the line arrangement, and let$E_{00}$ count those bounded edges whose two endpoints each lie on exactly two arrangement lines. For every arrangement, including parallel and concurrent lines,
\[
3T\le2B-E_{00}.
\]
In general position with$n\ge2$, $B=E_{00}=n(n-2)$, recovering the classical$T\le\lfloor n(n-2)/3\rfloor$. The displayed incidence certificate is the claim for degenerate arrangements; no unjustified assertion that$E_{00}=B$ survives concurrency is used.

An open side of a cell cannot contain an intersection with another arrangement line: that line crosses the side line and subdivides a neighborhood into additional sectors. Hence every bounded side along$\ell$ is the segment between two consecutive$p_j,p_{j+1}$. It can support at most one triangle on either side of$\ell$, so it is used at most twice.

If both endpoints are ordinary, a triangle on either side must use the same unique other line through each endpoint. Those two other lines have at most one intersection, so they cannot supply third vertices on both sides. Such an edge is used at most once. All other bounded edges are used at most twice. Summing counts every triangular cell exactly three times and proves the certificate.

This also yields a directly computable formula. If$\ell$ has$k_\ell$ distinct finite intersections, it contributes$\max(0,k_\ell-1)$ to$B$. Check consecutive endpoint multiplicities to compute$E_{00}$. For rational line coefficients, all intersections and coincidences are exact rational determinant tests; sorting on each line and recording multiplicities uses polynomial time in the full encoded input length. A floating near-concurrency tolerance is unnecessary and could change the certificate.

Here is an exact reason the simple proof cannot be transferred unmodified. Use four lines$x=0$, $y=0$, $y=1-x$, $y=x-1$. The two triangle cells with vertices$(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)$ and$(0,0),(1,0),(0,-1)$ share the segment from$(0,0)$ to$(1,0)$. One endpoint is ordinary and the other is triple. Along$y=0$ there are two triangle incidences, exceeding the naive simple per-line bound$n-2$ only after adding more such examples, but already disproving the claimed one-triangle-per-bounded-edge premise. Requiring both endpoints to be concurrent would also be false in this example. The certificate subtracts only edges with two ordinary endpoints.

For all concurrent lines,$B=0$, so the certificate correctly gives zero. For$n=1,2$, again$B=0$. More generally it improves the crude$T\le2B/3$ by the exact ordinary-edge count. It does not determine$T(n)$ or prove that the general-position numerical bound is sharp or universal through this method.
