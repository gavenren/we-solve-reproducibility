# Exact recognition and cone tests in the simplicial subclass

Restrict$\mathcal P^d$ to simplicial$d$-polytopes, with fixed$d\ge2$. The classical Billera–Lee–Stanley$g$-theorem gives a binary-polynomial decision algorithm for its face vectors. It also permits exact rational tests for the supporting-hyperplane and interior-ray questions for this subclass's cone. This goes beyond the earlier attempt's complete unrestricted dimension-three calculation without claiming an unrestricted answer in dimensions four or higher.

Put$f_{-1}=1$ and transform the candidate$f$-vector by
\[
\sum_{i=0}^d h_i t^{d-i}
=\sum_{i=0}^d f_{i-1}(t-1)^{d-i}.
\]
Let$r=\lfloor d/2\rfloor$, $g_0=1$ and$g_i=h_i-h_{i-1}$ for$1\le i\le r$. The theorem says precisely: $h_0=1$, $h_i=h_{d-i}$, and$(g_0,\ldots,g_r)$ is an$M$-sequence. The last condition means nonnegative integer entries and
\[
g_{i+1}\le g_i^{\langle i\rangle}\quad(1\le i<r).
\]
For the unique Macaulay expansion$g_i=\binom{a_i}{i}+\binom{a_{i-1}}{i-1}+\cdots+\binom{a_j}{j}$ with strictly decreasing upper indices, replace every term$\binom{a_\ell}{\ell}$ by$\binom{a_\ell+1}{\ell+1}$ to obtain$g_i^{\langle i\rangle}$. Zero raises to zero. There is no extra upper bound on$g_1$.

With fixed$d$, the linear transform has fixed integer coefficients. Each expansion has at most$i$ terms, found greedily by binary search for the largest$a$ with$\binom ai$ no larger than the current remainder. Upper indices have$O(B)$ bits for a$B$-bit input; binomial evaluation uses at most$d$ factors. Thus all searches and comparisons have polynomial bit cost in$B$. This is a decision algorithm; constructing a realizing polytope may require exponentially many faces relative to$B$ and is not included in the running-time claim.

For the cone, the simplex has$g_1=\cdots=g_r=0$. The linear image of every difference$f(P)-f(\sigma^d)$ is therefore the nonnegative vector$(g_1,\ldots,g_r)$. Its conic hull$C$ has closure exactly$\mathbb R_{\ge0}^r$. To see the reverse inclusion, fix$j\le r$ and take the$M$-sequence
\[
g_i=\binom{t+i-1}{i}\ (1\le i\le j),\qquad g_i=0\ (i>j).
\]
It is realized by the$g$-theorem. Divide by$g_j$ and let$t\to\infty$; lower coordinates tend to zero and the limit is the$j$th coordinate ray. Hence every coordinate ray lies in$\overline C$, proving the claim.

Transform a rational hyperplane functional through the simplex into coefficients$c_1,\ldots,c_r$ in these$g$ coordinates. It supports the cone in one of its closed halfspaces exactly when all$c_i\ge0$ or all$c_i\le0$. Necessity follows by testing the limiting coordinate rays; sufficiency follows from nonnegativity of every generator. The zero functional merely contains the whole affine span. A hyperplane contains a relative-interior ray exactly when its coefficients have both signs, or are all zero: a strictly positive vector can have zero dot product precisely in those cases. Here$\operatorname{ri}C=\operatorname{ri}\overline C=\mathbb R_{>0}^r$, the standard relative-interior identity for convex sets.

Closure is used only for these support/interior tests, not for exact membership. For example an isolated positive$g_2$ with$g_1=0$ lies in the closed orthant but is not a realizable$M$-sequence. The full nonsimplicial cone and face-vector set need not equal this subclass and are not characterized here.
