# A uniform split-prime obstruction and the precise exceptional alternatives

Claim type: **Literature report**.

The literal prompt concerns the order of discriminant $-4n$, including nonmaximal orders. Kani's Corollary23 leaves precisely three alternatives above1848: no extra number; one extra squarefree number congruent to1mod4; or two extra numbers $n_*,4n_*$, with $n_*$ squarefree and congruent to2mod4. Under GRH only the first remains. It is incorrect to replace the unconditional statement by “at most one extra idoneal integer”: the possible squarefree number and its fourfold multiple correspond to different orders.

We give a complete uniform exclusion argument, credited to the classical reduced-form criterion and Kani's Proposition25, and derive an explicit simultaneous residue bound. It applies to unbounded integers, not merely to another finite search.

**Split-prime obstruction.** If $p$ is an odd prime, $n>p^2$, and
\[
\left(\frac{-n}{p}\right)=1,
\tag{1}
\]
then $n$ is not idoneal.

Indeed choose an integer$r$ with
\[
0<r<p/2,\qquad r^2\equiv-n\pmod p.
\]
This is possible by choosing the smaller of two nonzero square roots. Put$c=(n+r^2)/p$. Then
\[
q(X,Y)=pX^2+2rXY+cY^2
\]
has discriminant$-4n$, is positive definite, and is primitive since$p\nmid2r$. Moreover
\[
0<2r<p<c,
\]
because$n>p^2$. Thus$q$ and its inverse form$(p,-2r,c)$ lie in the strict interior of the reduced domain and are distinct reduced representatives. The uniqueness theorem for primitive properly reduced forms implies that their proper classes are different. Hence the class of$q$ is not its own inverse, so the proper ideal class group of the specified order has an element not of order dividing2. This proves the obstruction without replacing the order by its maximal order.

For example, taking$p=3$ excludes **every**$n\equiv2\pmod3$ with$n>9$, by the explicit form
\[
(3,2,(n+1)/3).
\]
The general statement supplies a whole family of such obstructions. A splitting prime of the associated quadratic field must also be prime to its discriminant for(1); primes dividing$n$ have symbol0 and are not excluded by this argument.

**A simultaneous counting consequence.** Fix distinct odd primes$p_1,\ldots,p_t$, let
\[
P=\prod_{i=1}^t p_i,\qquad
R=\prod_{i=1}^t\frac{p_i+1}{2},
\qquad B=\max_i p_i^2.
\]
Every idoneal$n>B$ belongs to exactly$R$ permitted residue classes modulo$P$: at each prime the value$-n$ must be either zero or a quadratic nonresidue. There is one zero residue and$(p_i-1)/2$ nonresidues. The Chinese remainder theorem multiplies these independent counts.

Consequently, if$I(X)$ is the number of idoneal positive integers at most$X$, the explicit bound
\[
I(X)\le B+R\left(\left\lfloor\frac XP\right\rfloor+1\right)
\tag{2}
\]
holds for every$X\ge1$. The bound is valid even when very crude for small$X$; the first term covers all integers up to$B$. No unperformed computation of reduced forms is hidden in(2).

For fixed$t$, divide(2) by$X$ and let$X$ tend to infinity:
\[
\limsup_{X\to\infty}\frac{I(X)}X
 \le\prod_{i=1}^t\frac{p_i+1}{2p_i}.
\]
Each factor is at most$2/3$. Taking arbitrarily many distinct odd primes proves density zero using only the explicit local obstruction. This is weaker than the known finite-list alternatives, and is not advertised as an improvement to them. Its value is a direct unbounded residue certificate, with its constants and threshold visible.

The strict threshold in(1) matters. One cannot simply enlarge it to include all primes with$p^2$ slightly larger than$n$. Kani's Remark26 identifies idoneal examples of the form$n=p^2-1$ that invalidate a stronger historical test. Our construction then has$c=p$, placing the form on the ambiguous boundary instead of the strict interior. That is exactly where the inverse-class obstruction disappears.

The earlier contribution, read fully, develops a complete fixed-discriminant enumeration and reports a search through5000. Here the added content is the exact exceptional-order classification from a fully accessible primary source, a uniform explicit nonambiguous class for a whole family of residue conditions, and the all-size CRT bound. Neither density zero nor arbitrarily many residue filters excludes a final exceptional integer: the permitted residue classes never disappear merely by counting. We do not prove completeness of the65-element list unconditionally, rerun historical large computations, or claim an effective universal cutoff.
