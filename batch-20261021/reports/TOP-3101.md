# A quantitative prismatoid subclass and its correct height optimization

For a prismatoid with convex top$A$ and base$B$ in parallel planes, restrict here to the source's triangular lateral-face setting, so no added diagonal is needed as a cut. Write$z$ for the plane separation,$P_A$ for the top perimeter,$A'$ for its orthogonal projection to the base plane,$d_{AB}=\operatorname{diam}(A'\cup B)$, and$\Delta_B=\pi-\max_i\angle B(b_i)>0$. Bian–Demaine–Madhukara's Theorem1.2 proves a nonoverlapping petal edge unfolding whenever
\[
z\ge\frac{3\pi P_A+4d_{AB}}{2\Delta_B}.
\]
This is a genuine infinite subclass of the original-metric problem. The theorem is credited; the correction below makes explicit why its stated threshold follows despite two algebraic slips in the proof text.

The proof obtains two sufficient height requirements, depending on$0<\ell<1$:
\[
z\ge\frac{2d_{AB}}{\Delta_B\ell},
\qquad
z\ge\frac{3\pi P_A}{2\Delta_B(1-\ell)}.
\]
Both must hold. Put$a=2d_{AB}/\Delta_B$ and$b=3\pi P_A/(2\Delta_B)$. Their joint requirement is$z\ge\max(a/\ell,b/(1-\ell))$, not their minimum. For any$\ell$ this maximum is at least$a+b$: otherwise$a<z\ell$ and$b<z(1-\ell)$ would imply$a+b<z$. Equality is attained at
\[
\ell=\frac{a}{a+b}=\frac{4d_{AB}}{4d_{AB}+3\pi P_A}.
\]
Thus the optimized sufficient bound is exactly the displayed theorem threshold. The proof's final printed 'min' must be 'max', and its balancing denominator$4d+4\pi P_A$ must be$4d+3\pi P_A$. Correcting these retains the theorem rather than weakening a simultaneous requirement to one alternative.

The geometric source argument confines each unfolded face chain, including the top when attached, inside the disjoint exterior-angle sectors of the base. Its two estimates bound respectively the initial face angle and the total angle subtended by top-edge chains. Those geometric lemmas are imported; they are not inferred merely from the scalar optimization.

A different neighboring theorem, Ghomi's Theorem1.1 and Corollary1.2, says that any convex polyhedron with a monotone spanning edge tree becomes edge-unfoldable after a sufficiently large affine stretch in a direction with distinct vertex heights. The normalized map is$(x,y,z)\mapsto(x/\lambda,y/\lambda,z)$. It preserves combinatorics but changes intrinsic face lengths and angles; applying the inverse affine map to its planar net generally is not an isometric development of the original surface. Therefore the affine theorem cannot close the literal conjecture for the original$P$. It does rule out a purely combinatorial obstruction.
