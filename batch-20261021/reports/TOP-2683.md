# Alexander obstructions to the all-colour and HOMFLYPT unknot identities

**Problem:** TOP-2683

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** Garoufalidis–Lê's analytic MMR theorem implies that for every knot with ΔK≠1, all sufficiently large normalized colours have Jn(K;q)≠1; even an unbounded subsequence of identically trivial colours forces ΔK=1. The HOMFLYPT identity likewise forces ΔK=1 by skein specialization. This does not decide ordinary Jones detection or rule out Alexander-trivial candidates.

There is a universal restriction on candidates for part (b), stronger than merely requiring that some colour distinguish a given knot:
\[
 \Delta_K\ne1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \exists N_K\ \forall n\ge N_K,\ J_n(K;q)\ne1 .
\]
The Alexander polynomial is symmetrized and normalized by \(\Delta_K(1)=1\). The coloured Jones normalization is the reduced zero-framed convention of the prompt.

The substantial input is Garoufalidis–Lê's Theorem 1: for a knot-dependent complex neighborhood \(U_K\) of zero,
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}J_n(K;e^{z/n})=\frac1{\Delta_K(e^z)}
 \qquad(z\in U_K).
\]
This is an analytic theorem, with compact-uniform convergence, rather than a formal substitution at a large angle.

Suppose \(\Delta_K\ne1\). Since \(\Delta_K(e^z)\) is analytic and equals one at zero, it cannot equal one throughout a neighborhood unless the Laurent polynomial \(\Delta_K-1\) is identically zero. Choose a sufficiently small \(z_0\in U_K\) with \(\Delta_K(e^{z_0})\ne0,1\). Put \(c=1/\Delta_K(e^{z_0})\ne1\). For all sufficiently large \(n\), convergence gives
\[
 |J_n(K;e^{z_0/n})-c|<|c-1|/2.
\]
Therefore this evaluation is not one, and its polynomial cannot be identically one. This proves the displayed eventual statement.

The same proof shows that \(J_n(K;q)=1\) for an unbounded subsequence of integers \(n\) already forces \(\Delta_K=1\): along that subsequence the analytic limit is one for every small \(z\), and the identity theorem finishes the argument. An arbitrary finite collection of colours does not supply an unbounded subsequence. No effective value of \(N_K\) is extracted here from the asymptotic theorem.

Part (c) has a parallel obstruction without coloured asymptotics. Setting \(a=1\) in the prompt's HOMFLYPT skein relation gives
\[
 P_{L_+}(1,z)-P_{L_-}(1,z)=zP_{L_0}(1,z),\qquad P_U(1,z)=1.
\]
These determine the Conway polynomial \(\nabla_L(z)\): the skein recursion changes crossings to obtain a descending unlink, with the crossingless initial values fixed by the unknot normalization and split-link skein relation. Thus \(P_K(1,z)=\nabla_K(z)\), and
\(\Delta_K(t)=\nabla_K(t^{1/2}-t^{-1/2})\).
Consequently \(P_K(a,z)=1\) implies \(\Delta_K(t)=1\).

These deductions exclude every knot with nontrivial Alexander polynomial from (b) and (c). They do not exclude Alexander-trivial knots, and they do not assert that such knots have either polynomial identity. Nor does the coloured limit prove the ordinary Jones assertion in (a), where a single colour is prescribed. Homological categorifications that detect the unknot would be additional data, not consequences of equality of their Euler-characteristic polynomials; no such replacement is made.
