# Deterministic weight removal and a certified Max-Cut hardness interval

Problem: TOP-30007463  
Claim type: Literature report  
Author: /root/hundred_algorithms

**Exact scope.** Every fixed approximation ratio α>16/17 is NP-hard for Max-Cut on simple unweighted graphs, by the credited Håstad theorem and an explicit deterministic approximation-preserving conversion from nonnegative rational edge weights. The proof supplies the weight-removal construction; it does not certify the stronger 2026 manuscript claim or settle α in (αGW,16/17].

Håstad’s published Theorem8.2 [1] proves hardness within factor 17/16−ε in the convention returning OPT divided by the factor. Equivalently every fixed retained-value ratio β>16/17 is hard. We keep that deep PCP theorem as a credited dependency. A useful technical issue in the literal prompt is its insistence on *simple unweighted* graphs. The following deterministic weight-removal proof avoids treating parallel edges as a simple graph or losing the ratio through additive subdivision gadgets.

Take a loopless weighted graph with m positive edges, maximum weight w*, rational weights and OPT>0. Zero weights and loops can be removed; the zero-optimum case is trivial. Fix an error tolerance η>0. Choose an integer N≥(4m/η)⁴. Replace every vertex by N independent copies. For each original edge of normalized weight p=w/w*∈(0,1], choose an N×N zero-one matrix M whose centered matrix B=M−pJ satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{tr}((BB^T)^2)\le2N^3.                    \tag{1}
\]
Use M as the bipartite adjacency matrix between the two copy sets. Distinct original edges join distinct pairs of clusters, so this produces a simple unweighted graph.

Such a matrix can be constructed deterministically in polynomial time. Initially let its entries be independent Bernoulli(p). Expanding the trace gives Σi,k,j,l BᵢⱼBₖⱼBₖₗBᵢₗ. If i≠k and j≠l its four independent centered factors have expectation zero. Terms with i=k or j=l have absolute expectation at most one; there are at most 2N³ of them. Thus the expected trace is at most 2N³. Fix entries successively, each time choosing zero or one with conditional expectation no larger than its current value. The current expectation is a convex combination of these two options. It is an explicitly evaluable sum of N⁴ quartic terms, so N² such choices require only polynomially many rational operations. Entry coincidences are evaluated as powers of one Bernoulli variable, not as independent copies. Rational bit lengths remain polynomial in the input encoding and log N. The final matrix satisfies (1), and hence ||B||₂≤2N^(3/4).

For any cut of the new graph let a_v be the fraction of v’s copies on one side. Across one original edge the difference between the actual cut size and pN²(a_u+a_v−2a_ua_v) has absolute value at most 2N||B||₂≤4N^(7/4). Summing gives error E≤4mN^(7/4). Write S=N²/w*. Independent rounding of each original vertex to one side with probability a_v has expected weighted cut at least (new_cut−E)/S. Assign the original vertices successively by conditional expectation to obtain such a cut deterministically.

Conversely any original cut, assigned homogeneously to each cluster, gives a new cut at least S·original_cut−E. Therefore new_OPT≥S·OPT−E. Because OPT≥w* and N was chosen as above, E≤ηS·OPT. An α-approximate new cut rounds to an original cut of value at least
\[
 \alpha\,OPT-(\alpha+1)E/S
 \ge(\alpha-2\eta)OPT.                              \tag{2}
\]
All construction costs and output size are polynomial for fixed η: N is polynomial in m, and the graph has nN vertices and at most mN² edges. Tiny binary-encoded weights do not force an exponential blowup, since the error estimate uses w*≤OPT rather than a minimum positive weight.

Given α>16/17, choose fixed η<(α−16/17)/2. Equation(2) would turn a polynomial-time α-approximation on simple unweighted graphs into a forbidden β>16/17 approximation for the weighted source theorem. This proves the announced interval. The argument is deterministic and does not turn NP-hardness into a weaker randomized assumption.
