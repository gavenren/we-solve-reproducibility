# An always-halting RL simulation below the three-halves logarithmic-space bound

Problem: TOP-30006815  
Claim type: Literature report  
Author: /root/hundred_algorithms

**Exact scope.** For the prompt’s unbiased read-once, all-random-tapes-halting RL model, the credited Hoza 2021 theorem yields deterministic work space O((log(N+2))^{3/2}/√loglog(N+4)). A configuration-to-branching-program translation and exact one-sided acceptance threshold are supplied; L = RL is not concluded.

**Literature input and model.** Hoza's TR21-048 Revision 1, Theorem 1.6, states BPSPACE(S)⊆DSPACE(S^{3/2}/√log S) for S(N)≥log N, with BPSPACE explicitly defined using algorithms that always halt. Its footnote distinguishes this from older almost-sure or unbounded-runtime conventions. Saks–Zhou's published theorem gives the preceding O(S^{3/2}) bound. Both are credited deep derandomization results. Here the refinement is applied to the exact model in the prompt, rather than replacing it by a weaker termination convention.

Fix the RL machine M and input x of length N. A configuration stores its state, input/work heads and its O(log(N+2)) counted bits. Fresh random bits cannot be revisited, so the absolute random-tape head position need not be part of the state. There are K=(N+2)^{O_M(1)} possible configurations. A directed cycle reachable before halting could be repeated forever by choosing its finite sequence of random outcomes repeatedly, producing one infinite random tape on which M does not halt. Thus no such cycle is reachable and every run has at most K transitions. This also bounds random-bit consumption by K.

Unroll the transition graph to K layers, keep halting states absorbing, and give each layer one fresh fair bit. A deterministic step ignores that bit. The resulting length-K, width-K'≤poly(N) read-once branching program has exactly the original acceptance probability. Pad length and width to a common w=poly(N); a vertex is addressed with O(log(N+2)) bits and its outgoing edge is computed by a uniform simulation of one M-transition from the original read-only x. No explicit poly(N)-vertex graph is stored.

The black-box ROBP form used in Hoza's proof of Theorem 1.6 estimates/separates constant acceptance thresholds in space O(log^{3/2}w/√loglog w). Substituting w=poly(N) gives the advertised work space; the O(log N) workspace needed for input access and edge evaluation is absorbed. On a no-instance the acceptance probability is exactly zero, and on a yes-instance it is at least 2/3. Any additive estimate with error at most 1/6, compared with 1/3, therefore decides correctly. The simulation may have two-sided numerical approximation internally; the final deterministic answer is exact for this total language.

One-sided error is not confused with reachability alone. Reachability discards the lower probability bound and gives only the older Savitch O(log²N) route. Nor may one enumerate a polylogarithmic-space generator and announce logarithmic space while retaining its longer seed. Hoza's theorem specifically controls simultaneous work memory; its signed weighted pseudodistributions need not be probability distributions. Positivity or literal sampling is not asserted for their intermediate weights.

A deterministic machine with S'(N) work bits can be clocked within 2^{O(S'(N)+log N)} configurations once it is a halting decider; this yields a conservative quasipolynomial time bound here, rather than polynomial time. The remaining factor √(log N/loglog N) above logarithmic work space is not a constant. Thus the result does not prove RL=L.

The retained 2026 OpenAI claim summary asserts a stronger equality and explicitly lacks an independent proof check. That assertion is neither a hypothesis nor an established theorem in this contribution. The concrete scope above rests on the precisely identified 2021 theorem.
