# Every reduced class leads: the exact conditional theorem regime

Claim type: Literature report.

The literal problem asks for infinitely many strict simultaneous winning integer cutoffs for each coprime pair $(a,b)$, without assuming a Riemann hypothesis. There is a classical conditional theorem that gives the full simultaneous conclusion, stronger than separate pairwise sign changes. It is due to Rubinstein and Sarnak. This report specifies its hypotheses, translates its ordering statement to the literal winner question, and gives the exact decomposition of the winning density. It does not remove those hypotheses or claim a new prime-race theorem.

For a fixed $b\ge3$, assume GRH for the Dirichlet $L$-functions modulo $b$, and the standard grand simplicity hypothesis for the corresponding primitive $L$-functions: their nonnegative zero ordinates, counted with multiplicity, are linearly independent over $\mathbb Q$. This includes the exclusion of a zero ordinate and of repeated ordinates. Equivalently one may separately state no central zeros and linear independence of the multiset of positive ordinates. A hypothesis that all individual zeros are simple is weaker and is not substituted for this joint independence assumption.

The Rubinstein–Sarnak theorem, explicitly stated in the introduction of the primary research paper of Feuerverger and Martin, is this: for any distinct reduced classes $a_1,\ldots,a_r\bmod b$, the set
\[
 {\cal R}(a_1,\ldots,a_r)
 =\{x\ge2:\pi(x;a_1,b)>\cdots>\pi(x;a_r,b)\}
\]
has an existing strictly positive logarithmic density
\[
 \delta_b(a_1,\ldots,a_r)
 =\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac1{\log X}
   \int_{{\cal R}\cap[2,X]}\frac{dx}{x}>0. \tag{1}
\]
The associated limiting distribution gives measure zero to equality hyperplanes. All assertions in this paragraph are credited analytic inputs under GRH and grand simplicity; none is a consequence of the prime number theorem alone.

Now list **all** reduced classes, put $r=\varphi(b)$, and fix one of them, $a$. For each permutation $\tau$ of the other $r-1$ classes, consider the full ordering with $a$ first and that permutation afterward. These $(r-1)!$ race sets are disjoint. Their union is the set where $a$ leads strictly and the other counts are also pairwise unequal. The omitted set lies in the finite union of tie hyperplanes and has logarithmic density zero by the same theorem. Therefore the strict winning set has density
\[
 d_b(a)=\sum_{\tau}
 \delta_b\bigl(a,\tau(a_2),\ldots,\tau(a_r)\bigr)>0. \tag{2}
\]
For $r=2$ the sum has one term. The densities $d_b(a)$ sum to one, because there is a unique leader outside the zero-density tie set. This does not assert equality of the $d_b(a)$: ordering and leader densities can be biased. In particular replacing (2) with $1/r$ would need an additional symmetry theorem.

The positivity is already enough for the requested infinitude. If all winning real cutoffs were bounded by $M$, their integral up to $X$ would be at most $\log M$, whose quotient by $\log X$ tends to zero. This contradicts (2). Since every prime-counting function is constant between consecutive integers, arbitrarily large winning real cutoffs produce arbitrarily large winning integer cutoffs. Strict inequalities are preserved in taking the integer part.

The prompt compares $a$ with nonreduced classes as well. A prime in a nonreduced class must divide $b$, so those competing counts are bounded. Each reduced count tends to infinity by Dirichlet's theorem. After one fixed cutoff depending on $b$, every reduced class beats all nonreduced classes, so adding those competitors removes only a bounded initial set and does not change (2). For $b=1$ the comparison is empty; for $b=2$ the odd class eventually exceeds the only even prime. These boundary cases do not need GRH.

The earlier contribution proves an abstract implication from a hypothetical full-support joint distribution and performs finite races, while leaving that analytic input unestablished. The additional checked literature content here is that a published conditional framework actually supplies a positive-density conclusion for **every full ordering**, together with the explicit assumption set and the winner-density translation. Feuerverger–Martin also prove rigorous conditional numerical inequalities for certain three-way races, illustrating why ordering symmetries cannot be presumed; their decimal computations are not rerun or required here.

The remaining gap is unconditional simultaneous oscillation for arbitrary $b$. Merely proving each pair reverses infinitely often, proving GRH without the stated joint information, or observing a lead in a finite computation does not supply (1). No external formalization or announced unconditional solution is endorsed.
