# Perfect matchings with external chords: the recursive Hamilton extension

**Claim type: Literature report.** Fink's theorem is stronger than the perfect-matching subcase of the prompt: if $P$ is any perfect matching of the complete graph on $V(Q_d)$, $d\ge2$, there is a perfect matching $R\subseteq E(Q_d)$ such that $P\cup R$ is a simple Hamilton cycle. Prescribed edges may be external chords; every added edge must be a cube edge. We give a full recursive proof and identify a spanning path-cover consequence for nonperfect matchings.

These are established results, not a claimed solution of Ruskey–Savage. The earlier TOP-3181-A1 proves coordinate-direction extensions and explains why arbitrary matchings need not complete to perfect cube matchings. It mentions the ordinary two-thirds long-cycle theorem. The complete-host induction and nonperfect spanning linear-forest lemma below add different structural information.

## Recursive proof of the perfect-matching theorem

For $d=2$, the complete graph has three perfect matchings. The two cube matchings complete each other to the four-cycle. The matching of the two diagonals completes to a four-cycle using one of the cube matchings. In each case all added edges are cube edges, and they are disjoint from $P$.

Suppose $d\ge3$ and the result holds in dimension $d-1$. Choose a coordinate in which some edge of $P$ has different endpoint bits. Split the cube into halves $L,U$ along that coordinate. Let $P_L,P_U$ be the internal prescribed edges, and let $P_\times$ be the crossing prescribed edges. Crossing edges can have Hamming length greater than one.

The set $X\subseteq L$ of crossing endpoints has even size: $|L|=2^{d-1}$ is even, and $P_L$ covers an even number of vertices. It is nonempty, so $|X|\ge2$. Its partners $Y\subseteq U$ are in bijection with it through $P_\times$.

Choose any perfect matching $S$ on $X$. It need not use cube edges. Then $P_L\cup S$ is a perfect matching of the complete graph on $L$. By induction it extends to a Hamilton cycle $C_L$ whose other edges are cube edges. Delete $S$ from that cycle. Since $S$ is a nonempty matching, the result is a spanning collection of vertex-disjoint paths, with endpoints precisely $X$. These paths pair the endpoints of $X$.

Transfer this path-pairing through $P_\times$ to a perfect matching $T$ on $Y$: if a left path has endpoints $x,x'$, connect their prescribed partners $y,y'$ by an artificial edge of $T$. The union $P_U\cup T$ is a perfect matching on $U$. Induction extends it to a Hamilton cycle $C_U$ with only cube edges added.

In $C_U$, replace every artificial edge $yy'\in T$ by the path
\[
y,\ x,\ \text{the corresponding left path},\ x',\ y'.
\]
The first and last edges are the prescribed crossing edges. The replacements have disjoint internal vertex sets and together cover all of $L$. Subdividing the distinct edges of the one cycle $C_U$ in this way leaves one simple cycle, now spanning both halves.

It contains $P_U$, all of $P_\times$, and $P_L$, since no edge of $P_L$ was deleted from $C_L$. The artificial edges $S,T$ are all removed. Every edge left over outside $P$ is an inductively supplied cube edge within one half. This completes the induction and produces the required $R=C-P$; alternation with the perfect matching $P$ shows that $R$ itself is a perfect matching.

The permission to use external artificial chords is essential to the induction. Requiring $S$ or $T$ to be cube matchings would leave the argument unjustified.

## A stronger intermediate structure for arbitrary matchings

Fink's original paper also proves the following lemma: for any nonperfect matching $M$ of the complete graph on $V(Q_d)$, there is a perfect cube matching $R$ disjoint from $M$ such that $M\cup R$ is a linear forest, meaning every component is a path. This is a credited external lemma; its proof is not being inferred just from the perfect-matching theorem.

If $m=|M|$, exactly $2^d-2m$ vertices have degree one in this forest; all other vertices have degree two. Therefore the number of spanning path components is exactly
\[
2^{d-1}-m.
\]
For cube matchings this is a spanning path cover containing every prescribed edge, with an exact deficit parameter. When $m=2^{d-1}-1$, it is one Hamilton path whose endpoints are the two unmatched vertices. Closing that path requires an additional edge between those endpoints, which need not exist in the cube. Thus even this strong lemma does not close the full conjecture.

## Quantitative frontier and limitations

Fink–Mütze, arXiv2401.01769v2, Theorems5–7, prove cycle extensions for arbitrary complete-host matchings and an ordinary-cube two-thirds length guarantee. The earlier attempt already records that ordinary guarantee; it is not presented here as a new contribution. Their proof has a separately documented computer-assisted small-dimensional base, which we have not rerun.

The full prompt still asks for a cube Hamilton cycle through every possibly nonperfect cube matching. Neither a path cover nor a long cycle supplies that conclusion. All theorem priority is credited to the cited authors.
