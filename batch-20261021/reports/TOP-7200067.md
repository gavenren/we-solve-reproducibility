# Dimension nine: the exact value and what its computation establishes

Dutour Sikirić–van Woerden's arXiv:2508.20719v2 reports$\gamma_9=2$ in Corollary1. Its Theorem1 rests on a complete enumeration of$2\,237\,251\,040$ perfect forms in dimension nine, including$7\,338\,582$ extreme lattices. This is credited preprint/computer-assisted evidence as presented by the authors, not a local rerun of a billion-case classification. The literal prompt already records the value; this report explains its precise dependence and independently checks the attaining lattice.

Let
\[
D_8=\{z\in\mathbb Z^8:\sum z_i\equiv0\pmod2\},\qquad
E_8=D_8\cup((1/2,\ldots,1/2)+D_8).
\]
The first lattice has index two in$\mathbb Z^8$, hence covolume two. Adding the half-vector gives an index-two superlattice, hence$\operatorname{covol}(E_8)=1$. An integer nonzero vector in$D_8$ has squared norm at least two; a half-integral vector has at least eight coordinates of squared magnitude$1/4$, also giving two. Equality occurs in112 integer roots$\pm e_i\pm e_j$ and128 half-vectors with the required even parity. Thus$E_8$ has minimum squared norm two and240 shortest vectors.

The source's nine-dimensional lattice is
\[
\Lambda_9=\{(x+k e_1,k):x\in E_8,\ k\in\mathbb Z\}.
\]
A block-triangular basis has determinant one. For$k=0$, nonzero vectors have squared norm at least two. For$|k|\ge2$, the final coordinate already contributes at least four. For$k=\pm1$, the horizontal coset$E_8\pm e_1$ has squared norm at least one: its integer part has odd coordinate sum and cannot be zero, while its half-integral part has squared norm at least two. Therefore every nonzero vector of$\Lambda_9$ has squared norm at least two, attained at$(e_1,1)$.

There are exactly16 horizontal vectors of squared norm one in each$k=\pm1$ layer, namely the signed coordinate units; together with240 in$k=0$, this gives272 minimal vectors. Hence the Hermite invariant is independently$2/1^{2/9}=2$. This proves the lower bound$\gamma_9\ge2$ directly, but does not prove the universal upper bound.

The latter relies on the paper's exhaustive Voronoi enumeration and the theorem reducing an optimum to perfect forms. Seeing only the listed high-density lattice or many perfect forms would not establish completeness. The paper describes approximately150GB of classification data and publicly described access utilities; none was downloaded or rerun here. Also$\det(B^{\mathsf T}B)=\operatorname{covol}(B\mathbb Z^9)^2$, so the quadratic-form denominator is$\det(Q)^{1/9}$, not the lattice denominator$\operatorname{covol}(L)^{1/9}$. This distinction prevents a normalization error in using the result.
