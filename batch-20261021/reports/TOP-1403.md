# A quantitative thrackle bound and its global degeneracy consequence

**Claim type: Literature report.** A plane thrackle on \(n>3\) vertices has at most \(1.3984n\) edges, by Fulek–Pach's Theorem1 in arXiv1708.08037v1. In particular, every finite simple graph admitting such a drawing is **2-degenerate** and therefore **3-choosable**. We give the full deduction and explain why relaxing the exact-one-intersection condition changes the available density bound.

This is a credited quantitative partial result, not a proof of \(m\le n\), and the numerical bound is not presented as the latest best bound.

## Exact geometric scope and literature statements

The prompt requires each pair of distinct edges to meet exactly once, either at their common endpoint or at one proper crossing. Therefore adjacent edges have no other intersection. Fulek–Pach uses precisely that plane thrackle convention. Their Theorem1 states
\[
 m\le1.3984n\qquad(n>3). \tag{1}
\]
Its triangle-free Lemma2 gives the more precise \(1.3984(n-1)\) estimate; the report's general consequence uses only (1).

The paper also defines a quasi-thrackle: adjacent edges still meet only at their common endpoint, while independent edges may cross any **odd** number of times. Theorem2 proves
\[
 m\le{3\over2}(n-1), \tag{2}
\]
and gives infinitely many equality examples. Thus one cannot assume the smaller coefficient in (1) remains valid after replacing exact-one by arbitrary odd intersections.

The source proof of (1) uses parity embeddings and discharging. Some auxiliary arguments are explicitly omitted in that short version. The theorem is used as credited external input; no claim of a self-contained proof of those omitted structural facts is made here.

## Full deduction of degeneracy

Every subgraph \(H\subseteq G\) of a thrackle graph inherits a valid thrackle drawing by deleting the other vertices and edges. Extra isolated vertices do not affect any meeting condition.

If \(H\) has at least four vertices, (1) yields
\[
 {2|E(H)|\over |V(H)|}\le2.7968<3.
\]
Hence \(H\) has a vertex of degree at most two. If \(H\) has one, two or three vertices, simplicity gives that same conclusion directly. Therefore **every nonempty subgraph** has such a vertex, exactly the definition of 2-degeneracy.

Repeated deletion yields an ordering \(v_1,\ldots,v_n\) in which \(v_i\) has at most two neighbors among \(v_{i+1},\ldots,v_n\). For arbitrary lists \(L(v)\) of size three, color in the reverse order. At the insertion of \(v_i\), at most two already colored neighbors forbid colors, leaving at least one listed color. Every graph edge is respected when its earlier endpoint is inserted. This proves 3-choosability, a statement about all list assignments, and in particular ordinary3-colorability.

A useful necessary obstruction follows: any graph with a nonempty 3-core cannot be drawn as a thrackle. Indeed that core has minimum degree at least three, contradicting the inherited average-degree bound. This obstruction concerns subgraphs anywhere in the graph, rather than only its global average degree or the degree at a selected vertex.

## What the degeneracy certificate does not prove

A 2-degenerate simple graph can still have nearly twice as many edges as vertices. For example \(K_{2,t}\) is 2-degenerate, with \(t+2\) vertices and \(2t\) edges. For \(t\ge5\), its density already violates (1), so it cannot be a thrackle despite meeting the degeneracy condition. The reverse implication is therefore false.

Likewise three colors do not control the exact edge count: complete tripartite graphs can have quadratic edge count, although their large subgraphs violate degeneracy. The useful conclusion is a necessary structural certificate and a uniform list-coloring bound, not a replacement characterization of thrackleability.

In the quasi-thrackle model, (2) also implies average degree below three for each nonempty inherited subgraph, so the same elementary degeneracy deduction applies. Yet its sharp examples have more than \(n\) edges for all sufficiently large orders. Thus degeneracy and parity information alone cannot bridge the numerical gap to Conway's exact-one conjecture. The exact drawing condition must do additional work.

## Relation to the earlier attempt

The full Math-1403-A1 manuscript derives an exact crossing-count identity, excludes two vertex-disjoint odd cycles by planar parity, and proves \(m\le n\) for pseudoforests. It does not obtain a general coefficient below \(3/2\), nor the resulting deletion-order criterion on all subgraphs. The present report adds the credited global quantitative theorem and its complete structural/list-coloring consequences.

Its source comparison also identifies a concrete boundary of parity-only arguments: allowing more odd crossings leads to (2)'s larger sharp coefficient. No surface other than the plane is substituted into the prompt.

The actual primary v1 was freshly read at its exact statement and proof framework. Later improvements or recent computational manuscripts may exist; this report makes no current-best assertion and does not claim to have rerun any such computation. No code or finite drawing search is attached.
