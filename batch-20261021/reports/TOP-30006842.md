# Montgomery large values: exact theorem regions and the surviving critical gap

Claim type: Literature report.

The literal target is a uniform separated-sample estimate, for every fixed polynomial range $N\le T\le N^A$. The published Guth–Maynard theorem is substantially stronger than the elementary moment family in the earlier contribution. Stated in the prompt's notation with $V=N^\sigma$, its bound is
\[
 |W|\le T^{o(1)}
 \left(N^{2-2\sigma}+N^{18/5-4\sigma}
       +T N^{12/5-4\sigma}\right). \tag{1}
\]
This is their Theorem1.1, not a newly proved large-value estimate. Its hypotheses cover arbitrary complex $|b_n|\le1$ and arbitrary one-separated times, so no random-coefficient assumption is hidden here. A coefficient at the lower endpoint can be set to zero to reconcile the paper's displayed sum with $N<n\le2N$. Strict values in the prompt imply the theorem's non-strict threshold.

The paper also records the classical mean-value/Montgomery–Halász–Huxley comparison
\[
 |W|\le T^{o(1)}
 \left(N^{2-2\sigma}
       +T\min\{N^{1-2\sigma},N^{4-6\sigma}\}\right). \tag{2}
\]
All subpower factors below have the usual meaning: for each prescribed positive exponent, the implied constant may depend on it and on a fixed exponent range. These statements do not authorize a degree $A$ growing with $N$.

Write $T=N^a$, $1\le a\le A$. The directly supplied exponents in (1) and (2) are respectively
\[
 g(\sigma,a)=\max\{2-2\sigma,\ 18/5-4\sigma,\ a+12/5-4\sigma\},
\]
\[
 c(\sigma,a)=\max\{2-2\sigma,\ a+\min(1-2\sigma,4-6\sigma)\}.
\]
Thus the better available bound from these two theorems has exponent at most $\min(g,c)+o(1)$. This is an exact description of these particular inputs, not an optimality theorem over all analytic methods.

One genuine conjectured-order regime follows from (2). If
\[
 3/4<\sigma<1,\qquad N\le T\le N^{4\sigma-2},
 \tag{3}
\]
the second term is at most $N^{2-2\sigma}$: use $4-6\sigma<1-2\sigma$ and $a+4-6\sigma\le2-2\sigma$. Hence the requested estimate holds throughout (3), including its upper endpoint. This extends to a fixed-power range beyond $T\asymp N$ when $\sigma>3/4$. At $\sigma=3/4$ its upper endpoint is only $N$, making the degeneration explicit. For $\sigma\ge1$ the prompt's strict threshold is impossible since the sum has at most $N$ terms.

The critical amplitude $\sigma=3/4$ is particularly informative. Here (1) and (2) become
\[
 g(3/4,a)=\max\{3/5,a-3/5\},\qquad
 c(3/4,a)=\max\{1/2,a-1/2\}.
\]
For $a\ge1$, (1) first improves on (2) exactly when $a>11/10$. Up to $a=6/5$ the new exponent is $3/5$; above $6/5$ it is $a-3/5$. For example at $T=N^{5/4}$ it gives $N^{13/20+o(1)}$, compared with the classical $N^{3/4+o(1)}$. The conjecture asks for $N^{1/2+o(1)}$ there. The missing factor is $N^{3/20}$, which equals $T^{3/25}$ in this range, and therefore cannot be renamed $T^{o(1)}$.

For completeness, (1) itself reaches conjectured order when $\sigma\ge4/5$ and $T\le N^{2\sigma-2/5}$. Both inequalities are necessary for its last two positive terms to be bounded by its first. Formula(2) is already at least as strong in that amplitude regime, since $4\sigma-2\ge2\sigma-2/5$. Thus the new theorem's main critical improvement is not a proof of the conjecture in a new high-amplitude range; it is a quantitatively better bound in the still-unresolved critical regime. This prevents a misleading promotion of a major improvement to a full conjectural exponent.

The earlier attempt proves $T\asymp N$ and an elementary fixed-integer-moment family. The report adds the deep theorem's exact separated-sample regime and a fully checked exponent comparison, while crediting all analytic inputs. No claim is made that the elementary moment argument independently proves (1), that these are the latest possible bounds, or that the arbitrary polynomial-range conjecture has been settled.
