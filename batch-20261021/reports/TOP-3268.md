# A quadratic threshold in the bounded-order regime, and a matching scale obstruction

**Claim type: Literature report.** Fix a real \(A\ge1\). Every finite simple digraph \(D\) satisfying
\[
 n:=|V(D)|\le A\,d,\qquad d:=\delta^+(D)\ge64A^3k^2 \tag{1}
\]
contains a subdivision of the transitive tournament \(T_k\). This follows from a credited stronger theorem, and the quadratic scale in \(k\) is necessary in this bounded-order regime up to constants.

The additional ratio condition \(n/d\le A\) is essential to the reported deduction. It is not an order-independent solution of Mader's question.

## The exact external theorem

Kühn–Osthus–Young, *A note on complete subdivisions in digraphs of large outdegree*, arXivmath/0605178v1, Theorem2, proves that a simple digraph of order \(n\) and minimum outdegree at least \(d\) contains a subdivision of the **complete bidirected digraph** on
\[
 \left\lfloor{d^2\over8n^{3/2}}\right\rfloor \tag{2}
\]
vertices. Opposite arcs are allowed, loops and parallel arcs in one direction are not. All paths replacing distinct arcs have mutually disjoint interiors, with no interior branch vertices, as required by the prompt's subdivision convention.

A complete bidirected digraph contains \(T_k\) by choosing one direction from each pair according to a total ordering. Deleting the unused paths of its subdivision preserves internal disjointness. Thus (2) gives a stronger conclusion than needed whenever its floor is at least \(k\).

The actual primary theorem and its local-connectivity framework were read. Its proof is external input, not claimed as a new theorem here.

## A genuine threshold function on the specified class

Under \(n\le Ad\),
\[
 {d^2\over8n^{3/2}}
 \ge{\sqrt d\over8A^{3/2}}.
\]
The second inequality in (1) makes this at least \(k\), so the floor in (2) is at least \(k\). This proves the assertion with the explicit restricted threshold
\[
 f_A(k)=\left\lceil64A^3k^2\right\rceil .
\]
The same \(f_A\) works independently of the particular graph, but only among graphs meeting \(n/d\le A\).

More generally, the exact order-dependent sufficient condition is \(d^2\ge8k n^{3/2}\). A lower bound on \(d\) alone does not imply it when \(n\) is unrestricted. For fixed \(d\), digraphs of arbitrarily large order still lie in the original problem's quantified domain. Consequently the density theorem does not provide an unconditional \(f(k)\).

## Why the quadratic scale is necessary in this regime

Let \(B_a\) be the symmetric complete bipartite digraph with two parts of size \(a\): both possible arcs join every cross-pair and there are no within-part arcs. Then
\[
 |V(B_a)|=2a,\qquad \delta^+(B_a)=a,
\]
so it lies in the ratio class \(A=2\).

Suppose \(B_a\) contains a subdivision of \(T_t\). Let \(p\) branch vertices lie in the first part and \(q\) in the second, with \(p+q=t\). Every path replacing an arc between two first-part branch vertices must contain an internal second-part vertex, since the host is bipartite. There are \(\binom p2\) such arcs. Their interiors are disjoint and contain no branch vertices, so
\[
 \binom p2\le a-q.
\]
Similarly \(\binom q2\le a-p\). Summing gives
\[
 2a-t\ge{p^2+q^2-t\over2}\ge{t^2\over4}-{t\over2},
\]
and hence
\[
 2a\ge {t^2\over4}+{t\over2}. \tag{3}
\]
A transitive subdivision on \(t\) branches therefore needs \(a\ge t^2/8+t/4\).

In particular, a proposed threshold \(f_2(k)=o(k^2)\) cannot force \(T_k\) in all graphs with \(n=2d\). Choose large \(k\), let \(a\) be an integer at least \(f_2(k)\) but below \(k^2/8+k/4\), and use \(B_a\). It meets that threshold and ratio condition, while (3) excludes the required subdivision.

The argument counts internal vertices for **unordered** same-part branch pairs because a transitive tournament has just one arc per pair. It does not accidentally impose the stronger two-direction demand of a bidirected complete graph. Thus the obstruction applies directly to the prompt's target.
