# Exact natural-action proportions for PSL2 and monotonicity to finer coset actions

Claim type: **Partial result**.

For every prime power $q\ge4$, put $G=\operatorname{PSL}_2(q)$ and let $B$ be a stabilizer of a projective line. We prove, for the action on $G/B=\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_q)$,
\[
\delta(G,G/B)=\frac12-\frac{\gcd(2,q-1)}{2(q+1)}
=\begin{cases}q/(2(q+1)),&q\text{ even},\\(q-1)/(2(q+1)),&q\text{ odd}.
\end{cases} \tag{1}
\]
In particular it is at least $1/3>89/325$. More generally, this lower bound holds in **every** action on $G/H$ where $H$ lies in a conjugate of $B$, including imprimitive refinements and the regular action. We also prove the precise maximal-stabilizer reduction for any proposed universal lower bound.

Here is a moment computation avoiding any character-table or element-enumeration assumption. The projective action has degree $m=q+1$. It is2-transitive: the stabilizer of infinity contains all translations $x\mapsto x+b$, which act transitively on the remaining affine points. A nonidentity projective transformation has at most two fixed lines, since fixed lines are eigenspaces of a nonscalar two-by-two matrix. Let $n_i$ count nonidentity elements with exactly$i$ fixed points, for $i=0,1,2$. Let $N=|G|=q(q^2-1)/d$, where $d=\gcd(2,q-1)$.

Count pairs $(g,\alpha)$ with $g\alpha=\alpha$. Transitivity and orbit-stabilizer give total $N$, so
\[
 m+n_1+2n_2=N. \tag{2}
\]
Count instead $(g,\alpha,\beta)$ with ordered distinct fixed points.2-transitivity gives total$N$, hence
\[
 m(m-1)+2n_2=N. \tag{3}
\]
Subtracting yields $n_1=m(m-2)=q^2-1$. Now $1+n_0+n_1+n_2=N$ and (3) give
\[
 n_0=\frac N2-\frac{q(q-1)}2.
\]
Dividing by$N$ proves(1). The group order follows from $|\operatorname{SL}_2(q)|=q(q^2-1)$ and its scalar center of order$d$. Both the identity contribution and ordered-pair convention in(2)–(3) are essential; forgetting either gives a wrong constant.

If $H\le K<G$, an element fixes a coset in $G/H$ exactly when it belongs to a conjugate of$H$. Thus
\[
 \bigcup_{x\in G}H^x\subseteq\bigcup_{x\in G}K^x,
 \qquad D(G,G/K)\subseteq D(G,G/H). \tag{4}
\]
Consequently $\delta(G,G/H)\ge\delta(G,G/K)$. Applying this with$K=B$ proves the advertised extension. It does not assume that$H$ itself is maximal or that$G/H$ is primitive. Conversely every proper subgroup is contained in a maximal subgroup. Hence a universal lower bound over all transitive actions of a finite simple group is equivalent to the same bound over its maximal-subgroup coset actions. For a nonabelian simple group, every proper-subgroup coset action is faithful because its kernel is a proper normal subgroup.

For the inequality in (1), even$q\ge4$ gives at least$2/5$, while odd$q\ge5$ gives at least$1/3$. The excluded$q=3$ formula is$1/4$ and its projective group is not simple, illustrating why the family boundary matters. Cyclic simple groups, also included in the literal prompt, have faithful transitive regular actions and proportion$(p-1)/p\ge1/2$.

Burness–Fusari[1, Theorem4] establish the stronger sharp$89/325$ bound for simple **primitive** actions with soluble stabilizers. That credited result does not remove all maximal stabilizer types; the elementary calculation above supplies an exact formula and imprimitive-extension mechanism in its stated family. No general answer or new best constant for arbitrary simple actions is asserted.
