# A cycle-transversal domination bound from marked subcubic domination

**Claim type: Literature report.** Let \(G\) be a finite simple cubic graph with \(n\) vertices. If \(X\subseteq V(G)\) meets every cycle whose length belongs to \(\{3,4,5,7,8\}\), then
\[
 \gamma(G)\le |X|+\left\lfloor {n-|X|\over3}\right\rfloor
             =\left\lfloor {n+2|X|\over3}\right\rfloor. \tag{1}
\]
Consequently, if those cycles all meet a single vertex, then \(\gamma(G)\le\lceil n/3\rceil\). This proves the prompt's conclusion for that entire class of 3-connected cubic graphs, without needing connectivity in the proof. The deep input is a credited theorem of Dorbec and Henning; no novelty or unrestricted solution is claimed.

## The external marked theorem

In a marked graph, selected vertices must dominate every **unmarked** vertex; marked vertices need not be dominated but are allowed in the selected set. Write \(\dot\gamma(H)\) for the minimum size, \(\dot n\) for the number marked, and \(n_i\) for the number of unmarked vertices of degree \(i\).

Dorbec–Henning, arXiv2401.17820v2, Theorem10, proves that a marked subcubic graph \(H\) of girth at least six and containing no cycle of length seven or eight satisfies
\[
 12\dot\gamma(H)\le 4\dot n+4n_3+5n_2+8n_1+12n_0. \tag{2}
\]
Cycle exclusion here concerns subgraphs, rather than just induced cycles. This is the external theorem, whose long structural proof is not reproduced or claimed as independently certified here.

## Proof of the robust deduction

If \(X=V(G)\), selecting \(X\) proves (1). Otherwise let \(H=G-X\), and mark precisely the vertices of \(H\) with a neighbor in \(X\). Every cycle of \(H\) avoids \(X\); the assumption therefore excludes cycles of lengths three, four, five, seven and eight from \(H\). Thus \(H\) meets (2)'s hypotheses, component by component if necessary.

Every unmarked vertex has all its three original neighbors still present, so its degree in \(H\) is three. Every other vertex is marked and has weight four in (2), regardless of the degree it has after deletion. The whole right side of (2) is consequently exactly \(4(n-|X|)\), and there is a set \(S\subseteq V(H)\) of size at most \(\lfloor(n-|X|)/3\rfloor\) dominating all unmarked vertices.

The set \(D=X\cup S\) dominates \(G\): vertices in \(X\) dominate themselves; marked vertices in \(H\) already have a neighbor in \(X\); and all remaining vertices are dominated by \(S\). The sets \(X,S\) are disjoint, giving (1). The step of marking neighbors of deleted vertices is essential: applying an unmarked subcubic bound would otherwise charge their lost degrees and would not give the displayed coefficient.

Define
\[
 b(G)=\min\{|X|:X\text{ meets every cycle of lengths }3,4,5,7,8\}.
\]
We have proved the quantitative defect estimate
\[
 \gamma(G)\le\left\lfloor {n+2b(G)\over3}\right\rfloor. \tag{3}
\]
This is a bound in terms of a vertex transversal of the short-cycle obstructions, not a bound in terms of their possibly overlapping number.

For \(b(G)\le1\), (3) is at most \(\lfloor(n+2)/3\rfloor=\lceil n/3\rceil\). For \(b(G)=0\), it gives the sharper integral form \(\gamma(G)\le\lfloor n/3\rfloor\). In particular, it applies to every cubic graph of girth at least nine. More generally, it allows arbitrarily many short cycles as long as they share a common vertex. For \(b(G)\ge2\), (3) remains a valid estimate but need not reach the prompt's target; no further rounding improvement is asserted.

## Relation to the earlier contribution

The retained Math-1067-A1 manuscript proves the Hamiltonian case and estimates domination by cycle residues in a spanning 2-factor. Its general obstruction is that those residues need not sum to the desired ceiling. The present reduction instead uses the marked theorem and a short-cycle transversal; it does not assume a favorable 2-factor or a Hamilton cycle. Earlier low-connectivity counterexamples do not disprove the prompt's 3-connected statement, and none is asserted here.

## Verification and boundary

The actual v2 PDF's marked definition and Theorem10 were read, including its degree-dependent weights. The deletion, marking, integrality and extension arguments above are complete. No finite computation is used or claimed. The full conjecture for all 3-connected cubic graphs remains outside this scope.
