# Compactness with a fixed piece budget

For every integer$M\ge1$, there is a convex partition of the square into at most$M$ pieces attaining the infimum possible worst circularity among such partitions. Thus if the unrestricted infimum$c_*$ is not attained, every sequence of partitions approaching it must have unbounded piece counts. This does not decide whether global nonattainment actually occurs.

Let$c_M$ be the infimum with at most$M$ pieces. It is finite, since the one-square partition has circularity$\sqrt2$. Choose a minimizing sequence with quality bounded by a common$C$, and pad its indexing to$M$ using empty sets only as compactness placeholders. These placeholders are not partition pieces. Each nonempty piece is a compact convex subset of the fixed square. Passing to a subsequence for each of finitely many indices gives Hausdorff limits$K_1,\ldots,K_M$, allowing empty or lower-dimensional limits. Compactness follows directly from uniformly bounded Lipschitz support functions for nonempty convex sets.

The circumradius is Hausdorff-continuous: if two compact sets are within$\varepsilon$, each covering disk of one enlarged by$\varepsilon$ covers the other, giving$|R(K)-R(L)|\le\varepsilon$. For full-dimensional convex limits the inradius is continuous as well, by support inequalities or by the common-indisk homothetic squeeze. If a limit is lower-dimensional, its inradius tends to zero; otherwise a subsequence of positive-radius indisks, with centers in the square, would yield a positive-radius disk in the limit. Since$R(K_{i,j})\le C r(K_{i,j})$, every such degenerate limit also has circumradius zero and is a single point. A surviving full-dimensional limit has circularity at most$c_M$ by continuity.

The limits cover the square: for any$x\in Q$, one of the finitely many indices contains$x$ along an infinite subsequence, so$x$ belongs to its closed limit. Their interiors are disjoint. If two full-dimensional limit interiors overlapped, they would contain a common small disk; convergence of convex bodies puts a smaller such disk in both sufficiently late approximants, contradicting the partition property.

Discard the finitely many point limits. The union of the surviving full-dimensional compact pieces is closed and contains$Q$ except possibly those finitely many points. Its complement in$Q$ cannot contain an isolated omitted point, because this union already contains a dense subset of$Q$. Hence it covers all of$Q$. There is at least one surviving piece, since finitely many points cannot cover the square. We have obtained a valid partition into at most$M$ positive-area convex pieces with quality at most$c_M$, proving attainment.

If an approaching sequence for$c_*$ had bounded piece counts$M$, its qualities would give$c_M=c_*$, and the attained$c_M$ would be a finite globally optimal partition. Taking the contrapositive proves the stated consequence.

The primary Damian–O'Rourke paper and TOPP note provide credited bounds near$[1.28868,1.29950]$ and conjecture nonattainment. Their decimals and boundary bottleneck calculations do not by themselves prove that conjecture. This report adds an explicit compactness criterion: any genuine nonattainment must escape every fixed combinatorial piece budget. It neither identifies$c_*$ nor rules out a finite optimizer.
