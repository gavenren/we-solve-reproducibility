# An explicit family with unbounded marked multiplicity

The general classification is not reduced to convex regions of the standard sphere. Eremenko–Gabrielov classify spherical rectangles, meaning quadrilaterals whose angles are odd multiples of$\pi/2$. Their Theorems1.3,1.5 and Proposition1.4 provide a directly usable partial answer for the infinite family
\[
(\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3)
=(1/2,k+1/2,1/2,k+1/2),\qquad k=1,2,\ldots.
\]
There are exactly$2\lceil k/2\rceil$ one-parameter families of marked isometry classes. For each sufficiently small modulus$m>0$, and for each sufficiently large$m$, there are at least$\lceil k/2\rceil$ classes with these ordered angles. Thresholds may depend on$k$; no bound uniform in$k$ is asserted. The primary version contains conflicting endpoint-exactness passages, so only the lower bound is retained here.

Here is the precise specialization of their integer formulas. Write$\alpha_j=A_j+1/2$, and$\delta=(A_1+A_3-A_0-A_2)/2$. Their existence criterion is
\[
[\delta\ge1,\ A_1,A_3\ge1]\quad\text{or}\quad
[\delta\le-1,\ A_0,A_2\ge1].
\]
Our data give$(A_0,A_1,A_2,A_3)=(0,k,0,k)$ and$\delta=k$, so the first condition holds. If$k$ is odd the source's 'special' case applies, and its count parameter is
\[
N=\min\{(k+1)/2,\ k+(1-k)/2,\ (k+1)/2,\ k+(1-k)/2\}
=(k+1)/2.
\]
If$k$ is even the nonspecial parameter is
\[
M_1=\left\lfloor\min\{(A_1+1)/2,(A_3+1)/2,(1+\delta)/2\}\right\rfloor=k/2.
\]
The source count is twice this parameter, half of each endpoint type. Continuity and the limiting moduli of the appropriate families give the claimed lower multiplicity. Proposition1.4/Remark1.6 state stronger endpoint uniqueness, but the later Remarks and conjectures on pp22–23 explicitly call endpoint exactness conjectural. We do not resolve that inconsistency by selecting the stronger passage. The lower bound is consistent with both passages and suffices for unbounded multiplicity. This is an arithmetic corollary of the credited geometric classification, not an independently reconstructed net classification.

For example$k=1,2$ each give two families and endpoint multiplicity at least one; $k=3,4$ each give four families and endpoint multiplicity at least two. The area identity in the prompt gives$2k\pi$. In particular$k\ge3$ has area greater than$4\pi$, so it cannot be a one-to-one developed spherical region. Such examples lie squarely in the overlapping developing-map cases allowed by the prompt.

The parameter of a source family is the angle between certain supporting great circles, not automatically the conformal modulus itself. The modulus need not be globally monotone along a family, so the endpoint count is not asserted at every intermediate$m$. Nor are the two endpoint types joined by assuming every$m>0$ occurs in each family. Arbitrary positive angle quadruples, exceptional developing-circle configurations outside the rectangle classification, and exact multiplicity at intermediate modulus remain beyond this report.
