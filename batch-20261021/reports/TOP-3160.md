# Kriesell's exact threshold for graphs with even-degree nonterminals

**Claim type: Literature report.** Let \(G\) be a finite loopless multigraph, \(T\subseteq V(G)\), \(|T|\ge2\), and \(k\ge1\). Assume every cut separating two terminals has at least \(2k\) edges and every vertex outside \(T\) has even degree. Then \(G\) contains \(k\) pairwise edge-disjoint \(T\)-Steiner trees.

This is the inner-Eulerian case of the prompt, proved below using credited classical splitting and tree-packing theorems. Terminals may have odd degree, vertices of different trees may coincide, and parallel edges are distinct resources. The added parity hypothesis is a real restriction.

## External ingredients and exact hypotheses

The local splitting theorem of Mader says that at a vertex \(s\) of degree at least four with no incident bridge, some pair \(rs,st\) can be replaced by \(rt\) preserving all local edge connectivities between vertices other than \(s\). We use the explicit statement in the introduction of Bernáth–Iwata–Király–Király–Szigeti, *Recent Results on Well-Balanced Orientations*, METR2006–62, pages2–3. The original 1978 proof is credited but was not separately retrieved.

The Nash-Williams–Tutte theorem states that \(k\) edge-disjoint spanning trees exist exactly when every partition into \(q\) nonempty parts has at least \(k(q-1)\) edges between parts. Theorem1.1 of DeVos–McDonald–Pivotto, arXiv1307.7621v2, gives the checked multigraph formulation.

## Why splitting is available throughout

All terminals lie in one component. Discard other components, whose vertices have no terminal and whose removal does not change degrees in the retained component.

This component has no bridge. Indeed, if a bridge separates terminals, its cut contradicts \(2k\ge2\). Otherwise one of its two shores \(A\) contains no terminal. All vertices of \(A\) have even degree, whereas
\[
 \sum_{v\in A}d(v)=2|E(G[A])|+1
\]
would be odd. This is impossible.

The same argument will apply after every operation, provided terminal cuts and even nonterminal degrees persist. Thus the bridge condition is established repeatedly; it is not silently assumed to survive a splitting operation.

## Eliminating every nonterminal

Discard loops whenever they arise: they affect no terminal path or cut and change a degree by two. Delete isolated nonterminals. Consider a remaining nonterminal \(s\), whose degree is positive and even.

If \(d(s)=2\), suppress its two incident edges into one labelled edge between their other endpoints. A path between vertices other than \(s\) either avoids \(s\) or uses both edges. Replacing that segment, and conversely expanding it, preserves their edge connectivities. If the other endpoints coincide, the resulting loop can be discarded; the two-edge excursion was not useful to any simple path between other vertices.

If \(d(s)\ge4\), apply Mader's local theorem to find an admissible pair and split it off. In both cases the degree of \(s\) falls by two, and every other degree stays unchanged before loops are discarded. Thus all nonterminal degrees remain even. Terminal pair connectivities remain at least \(2k\), equivalently the terminal-cut bound remains true. The bridge argument above is therefore available again.

Repeat until \(s\) is isolated and delete it, then do the same with the other nonterminals. The number of edges incident to vertices being eliminated strictly decreases, so this process terminates. The result is a multigraph \(G_T\) on vertex set \(T\), with every nontrivial cut of size at least \(2k\).

For a partition of \(T\) into \(q\ge2\) parts, summing the \(q\) boundary sizes and dividing by two shows there are at least \(kq\), hence at least \(k(q-1)\), crossing edges. Tree packing gives \(k\) edge-disjoint spanning trees in \(G_T\).

## Lifting without confusing shared vertices and shared edges

Initially associate each original edge with its one-edge trail in \(G\). Each split replaces two current edges by their concatenated trail through \(s\). Maintain the invariant that the original edge sets associated with distinct current edges are disjoint. Discarding a loop or a component merely discards resources. Although a concatenated trail may repeat an earlier eliminated vertex, it never repeats an original edge.

Expand each of the \(k\) trees of \(G_T\) into its original trails. Its expanded union is connected and contains every terminal. Different expanded unions have disjoint original edge sets. An expanded union need not itself be a tree: two trails can share an eliminated vertex and thereby create a cycle. Choose a spanning tree of each union, then prune nonterminal leaves. The results are \(T\)-Steiner trees and remain edge-disjoint.

This resource argument avoids the invalid assertion that every sequence of splits lifts by mere subdivision. Vertex intersections between trees are permitted by the prompt.

## Added scope and remaining boundary

The prior Math-1071-A1 manuscript handles \(k=1\), two terminals, all vertices terminal, and degree-two suppression; it identifies branching nonterminals as the unresolved obstruction. Here all even-degree branching nonterminals, of arbitrary degree and number, are eliminated while preserving the exact \(2k\) threshold. The general \(5k+4\) sufficient threshold mentioned in that attempt is not the new contribution.

Odd-degree nonterminals can remain after every pairwise splitting, so the proof does not extend to the unrestricted conjecture. No finite experiment or implementation is claimed. The external local splitting theorem and tree-packing theorem are the only non-elementary inputs; the parity, iterative reduction and lifting arguments are supplied in full.
