# TOP-1281 — Exceptional exponents form a vector space of dimension at most two

**Claim type: Literature report.** The four-exponentials conjecture would exclude two independent parameters producing four algebraic outputs. A precise unconditional result follows from the six-exponentials theorem: for any fixed independent pair of row parameters, all parameters producing two algebraic outputs form a rational vector space of dimension at most two. This report proves that statement and an exact sparsity consequence for every three-dimensional parameter grid. The missing reduction from dimension two to one remains the original conjectural obstruction.

## 1. The theorem used and the exceptional space

Fix complex numbers $x_1,x_2$ linearly independent over $\mathbb Q$, and define
\[
V(x_1,x_2)=\{y\in\mathbb C:
 e^{x_1y},e^{x_2y}\in\overline{\mathbb Q}\}.
\]
Zero belongs to $V$. If $u,v\in V$, the exponential addition law shows that $u+v$ and $-u$ belong to $V$. If $y\in V$ and $a/b\in\mathbb Q$ with $b>0$, then
\[
\left(e^{x_iay/b}\right)^b=\left(e^{x_iy}\right)^a
\]
is a nonzero algebraic number. Hence $e^{x_iay/b}$ is algebraic, as a root of a polynomial over the algebraic numbers. This uses the actual exponential value and requires no arbitrary logarithm branch. Therefore $V$ is a $\mathbb Q$-vector space.

The six-exponentials theorem, stated precisely in Waldschmidt [1, Theorem 3], says that if $x_1,x_2$ are rationally independent and $y_1,y_2,y_3$ are rationally independent, at least one of their six exponentials is transcendental. Applying it to three hypothetical independent members of $V$ gives
\[
\dim_{\mathbb Q}V(x_1,x_2)\le2.
\tag{1}
\]
This is unconditional, with the deep theorem explicitly credited. The four-exponentials conjecture would replace the right side by one. Conversely a universal dimension bound of one would prove that conjecture, since two independent column parameters cannot both belong to $V$. Thus the missing unit of dimension is exactly the target, rather than a disguised consequence of (1).

## 2. An exact infinite-grid consequence

Let $w_1,w_2,w_3$ be any fixed rationally independent complex numbers, and take an integer $H\ge0$. Among the distinct parameters
\[
y=a_1w_1+a_2w_2+a_3w_3,\qquad
a_j\in\{-H,\ldots,H\},
\]
at most $(2H+1)^2$ can have both outputs algebraic.

To prove this, the coefficient vectors of the exceptional parameters lie in the rational subspace
\[
E=\{(q_1,q_2,q_3)\in\mathbb Q^3:
q_1w_1+q_2w_2+q_3w_3\in V\}.
\]
The coefficient map is injective, so (1) gives $\dim E\le2$. There is a nonzero rational linear functional
\[
c_1q_1+c_2q_2+c_3q_3
\]
vanishing on $E$. Choose an index with $c_j\ne0$. For each assignment of the other two integer coordinates, there is at most one possible value of the remaining coordinate on that hyperplane. This proves the bound, including $H=0$.

Consequently at least
\[
(2H+1)^3-(2H+1)^2
\]
parameters in every such grid produce at least one transcendental output. This is a theorem for every grid size and every independent triple, not a finite numerical test or an assumption that the exceptional parameters are randomly distributed. It gives density zero for simultaneous algebraicity within these growing three-dimensional grids, while permitting a two-dimensional exceptional subspace.

## 3. A concrete two-base interpretation

Let $\alpha,\beta>0$ be algebraic and multiplicatively independent, and use their real logarithms. Then $\log\alpha,\log\beta$ are rationally independent: clearing denominators in a rational relation would contradict multiplicative independence. Define
\[
T_{\alpha,\beta}=\{t\in\mathbb R:
\alpha^t,\beta^t\in\overline{\mathbb Q}\}.
\]
This is the real part of the preceding exceptional space, has rational dimension at most two, and contains $\mathbb Q$.

If there is an irrational member $\tau$, then $\{1,\tau\}$ is a rational basis, so
\[
T_{\alpha,\beta}=\mathbb Q+\mathbb Q\tau.
\tag{2}
\]
Every further exponent giving two algebraic values must lie in that exact plane. This is an either/or description, not a construction of such a $\tau$.

In particular, with bases two and three and with $1,t_1,t_2$ rationally independent, at least one of
\[
2^{t_1},3^{t_1},2^{t_2},3^{t_2}
\]
is transcendental. The already-algebraic first column $2,3$ of the six-exponentials array explains why the remaining four entries must contain one; it does not solve every prescribed four-entry array, which has different independence data.

The preceding grid bound applies to any three independent real exponents. It should not be confused with the conjecture that every irrational $t$ gives a transcendental member of $2^t,3^t$: (2) still permits an exceptional irrational direction.

## 4. A different established proper subcase

The same checked primary author manuscript [1, Section 4, after Theorem 3] records the proved four-exponentials result under the extra hypothesis
\[
\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb Q}
\mathbb Q(x_1,x_2,y_1,y_2)\le1,
\]
crediting Brownawell (1974), Corollary 7, and Waldschmidt (1973), Corollary 4. We report this as a credited theorem through that explicit source; the original 1973/1974 proofs were not independently read here.

This hypothesis constrains all four parameters, not just one row ratio or the four exponential outputs. A putative counterexample therefore cannot have parameter field of transcendence degree at most one. This is stronger scope than simply knowing algebraic-ratio special cases, but still leaves the general four complex parameters allowed by the prompt.
