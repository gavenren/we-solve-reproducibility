# Explicit IYB structures for split abelian extensions and iterated wreath constructions

Claim type: **Partial result**.

The obstacle in the prompt is global compatibility: brace structures on separate Sylow subgroups need not fit an action or an extension. We give a complete construction in a substantial split-extension class, including unrestricted actions on an abelian kernel. We then state exactly why the same formula cannot simply be applied to arbitrary Sylow data.

**Construction theorem.** Let $(B,+,\circ)$ be any finite left brace, let $A$ be a finite abelian group written additively, and let
\[
 \rho:(B,\circ)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(A,+)
\]
be any group homomorphism. On $A\times B$, set
\[
 (a,b)+(a',b')=(a+a',b+b'),
\]
\[
 (a,b)\circ(a',b')=(a+\rho(b)(a'),\ b\circ b'). \tag{1}
\]
These laws define a left brace. Its multiplicative group is the given semidirect product $A\rtimes_\rho(B,\circ)$. Thus this group is IYB by the exact definition in the prompt.

The additive law is an abelian group law. Since $\rho$ is a homomorphism, the second law is the usual semidirect-product group law; its identity is $(0,0)$, and its inverse is
\[
(a,b)^{-1}_{\circ}=(-\rho(b^{-1}_{\circ})(a),\ b^{-1}_{\circ}).
\]
It remains to check the left brace identity, rather than assume that any semidirect product inherits a brace. Let $x=(a,b)$, $y=(c,d)$, and $z=(e,f)$. The left side $x\circ(y+z)$ is
\[
(a+\rho(b)(c+e),\ b\circ(d+f)).
\]
On the other hand, $x\circ y-x+x\circ z$, where subtraction is additive, is
\[
(a+\rho(b)c+\rho(b)e,\ b\circ d-b+b\circ f).
\]
The first coordinates agree because $\rho(b)$ is additive; the second agree because $B$ is a left brace. This proves the theorem for every allowed action, with no coprimality or trivial-action assumption.

One can also exhibit the associated bijective cocycle. Put $\lambda_b(d)=-b+b\circ d$. The brace identity says that $\lambda_b$ is additive; associativity of $\circ$ gives $\lambda_{b\circ b'}=\lambda_b\lambda_{b'}$. Hence
\[
R_{(a,b)}(c,d)=(\rho(b)c,\lambda_b(d))
\]
is an action of the multiplicative semidirect product on the abelian group $A\oplus(B,+)$. The identity map of the underlying set is a bijection $\pi$, and (1) gives directly
\[
\pi(gg')=\pi(g)+R_g\pi(g').
\]
This explicitly supplies the abelian group, the action, and the cocycle, rather than merely a group embedding into an unspecified IYB group.

Start with the trivial brace on any finite abelian group, where the two operations coincide. Inductively, if a finite group $H$ has already been constructed as the multiplicative group of a brace, **every** split extension $A\rtimes H$ with finite abelian kernel $A$ has a brace by the theorem. This allows any finite number of successive such extensions and arbitrary actions at every stage. It is not restricted to direct products or to nilpotent groups.

For a concrete infinite family, let $A$ be any finite abelian group and let $H$ be any already constructed IYB group. The finite base $A^H$, with coordinatewise addition, is abelian, and left translation
\[
(h\cdot u)(t)=u(h^{-1}t)
\]
defines a homomorphism $H\to\operatorname{Aut}(A^H)$. The construction therefore proves that the regular wreath product $A\wr H=A^H\rtimes H$ is IYB, with explicit addition on its underlying set. Iterating starts from arbitrary finite abelian factors. Generalized dihedral groups $A\rtimes C_2$, with inversion action and $A$ of odd order, are a simpler nonnilpotent example; when $A\ne1$ and the action is nontrivial these are not being mistaken for abelian groups.

The semidirect-product construction for braces is established literature, credited by Cedó–Jespers–Okniński to Rump. The proof above specializes it so that **every** automorphism of the abelian kernel is automatically a brace automorphism: its brace is trivial. This explains the useful unrestricted-action feature.

For a general kernel brace $N$, a homomorphism into $\operatorname{Aut}(N,\circ)$ need not preserve its addition. Formula (1) would then fail at the first-coordinate additivity step. The prompt gives neither an abelian normal kernel with a complement nor an action preserving one chosen brace structure. Its separate Sylow-IYB assumptions do not supply either piece. Accordingly, the construction proves the indicated split classes but does not prove the original local-to-global assertion. No new or universal closure theorem for IYB groups is claimed.
