# A complete congruence-solubility theorem for three integer cubes

Claim type: **Partial result**.

We prove the full local congruence statement:
\[
k\not\equiv4,5\pmod9
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{for every }m\ge1,\ 
x^3+y^3+z^3\equiv k\pmod m
\text{ has an integer solution}. \tag{1}
\]
The chosen triple may depend on $m$. This is a classical local fact, reconstructed here with every prime-power lift, including the singular prime3, proved explicitly. It shows that replacing the elementary modulus9 test by any additional fixed modulus cannot obstruct an eligible $k$. It does **not** prove an exact integer representation or infinitude of such representations.

We first give the finite-field ingredient, including its polynomial proof. For nonempty $A,B\subset\mathbb F_p$,
\[
|A+B|\ge\min(p,|A|+|B|-1). \tag{2}
\]
If $|A|+|B|>p$, every $t$ lies in $A+B$, since $A$ and $t-B$ intersect. Otherwise put $a=|A|$, $b=|B|$ and suppose $|A+B|\le a+b-2$. Extend $A+B$ to a set $D$ of size $a+b-2$ and consider
\[
P(X,Y)=\prod_{d\in D}(X+Y-d).
\]
It vanishes on $A\times B$. Its coefficient of $X^{a-1}Y^{b-1}$ is
$\binom{a+b-2}{a-1}$, nonzero modulo$p$ because $a+b-2<p$.

That coefficient must also equal
\[
\sum_{u\in A,v\in B}
\frac{P(u,v)}
{\prod_{u'\in A\setminus\{u\}}(u-u')
 \prod_{v'\in B\setminus\{v\}}(v-v')},
\]
which is zero. To justify the identity at the stated total degree, use one-variable Lagrange interpolation: the functional
$\sum_{u\in A}u^i/\prod_{u'\ne u}(u-u')$
is zero for $i<a-1$ and one for $i=a-1$. In a monomial of total degree at most $a+b-2$, either both exponents have these exact values, or at least one is below its threshold and its functional vanishes. This proves the identity and contradiction. Thus (2) is proved, rather than imported without its prime-field restriction.

Let $C=\{x^3:x\in\mathbb F_p\}$. For $p\ne3$, cyclicity of $\mathbb F_p^\times$ shows either $C=\mathbb F_p$ or $p\equiv1\pmod3$ and $|C|=(p+2)/3$. Applying (2) twice gives
\[
|C+C+C|\ge\min(p,3|C|-2)=p.
\]
Every residue is therefore a sum of three cubes modulo$p$. We can always choose a solution with a nonzero coordinate. If $k\not\equiv0\pmod p$, any solution has this property. If $k\equiv0\pmod p$, use $(1,-1,0)$, including when$p=2$.

Choose a unit coordinate, say$x$. If a solution is known modulo$p^a$, replacing$x$ by$x+t p^a$ changes its cube modulo$p^{a+1}$ by
\[
3x^2t p^a.
\]
Since $p\ne3$ and$p\nmid x$, the coefficient $3x^2$ is invertible modulo$p$. Exactly one choice of$t\bmod p$ corrects the next digit. The remaining expansion terms are divisible by$p^{a+1}$. The coordinate stays a unit. Induction proves solubility modulo every$p^a$ for every$p\ne3$.

**The prime3.** Cubes modulo9 are exactly$0,\pm1$, whose threefold sums give precisely$0,\pm1,\pm2,\pm3$. These are all residues except4,5. In each allowed residue choose a triple with at least one coordinate prime to3; for residue0 take$(1,-1,0)$ instead of the all-zero triple.

Suppose now that$a\ge2$, the equation holds modulo$3^a$, and$x$ is such a unit coordinate. Put
\[
x'=x+t3^{a-1}.
\]
Expansion gives
\[
(x')^3-x^3\equiv t x^2 3^a\pmod{3^{a+1}}. \tag{3}
\]
The two other terms have factors$3^{2a-1}$ and$3^{3a-3}$, both divisible by$3^{a+1}$, including at$a=2$. Since$x^2$ is a unit modulo3, choose$t\bmod3$ to correct the required next digit. This preserves solubility modulo$3^a$ and the unit condition, and proves all higher powers by induction. It also implies solubility modulo3 by reduction.

For an arbitrary$m$, solve at each prime power dividing$m$ and apply the Chinese remainder theorem independently to$x,y,z$. Polynomial evaluation respects those congruences, so this proves the forward implication of(1). The converse follows simply by taking$m=9$.

The same constructions, with coherent lifts or their Cauchy limits in the prime3 construction, yield points in$\mathbb Z_p^3$ at every prime for every eligible$k$. A real point also exists, for example$(\sqrt[3]k,0,0)$. This establishes local solubility, not an integral local-to-global principle for the affine cubic surface.

The earlier contribution, read in full, proves the modulus9 obstruction, special polynomial families and a bounded square-discriminant search. It does not prove all-modulus solubility or treat the prime3 lift. The present result closes that particular local question with a complete proof. Cauchy–Davenport and its polynomial mechanism are classical, and no novelty is claimed. Neither infinitely many compatible congruence solutions nor success at every finite modulus supplies one triple of ordinary integers satisfying the equation exactly. That remaining global step is the literal conjecture.
