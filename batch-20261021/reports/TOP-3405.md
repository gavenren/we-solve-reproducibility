# Uniform BPP containment through a quantified shift-cover test

Problem: TOP-3405  
Claim type: Partial result  
Author: /root/hundred_algorithms

**Exact scope.** For every total BPP language, a uniform Σ₂^P ∩ Π₂^P decision procedure is proved by an explicit amplified shift-cover construction. This is a classical unconditional containment, not P = BPP.

Let A be a fixed polynomial-time BPP decider. Independent repetition and majority give a polynomial-time decider B with error δ(n) ≤ 2^{-n-2} and exactly m(n) random bits, where m is polynomially bounded and can be padded to at least one. More amplification, if necessary, ensures m(n)δ(n)<1 at every sufficiently large n: polynomial m versus exponential decay suffices. The number of repetition trials is a fixed polynomial chosen for A; this construction is uniform. Finitely many exceptional lengths can be decided from a finite table in the machine description.

For a fixed x of a nonexceptional length put S={r∈F₂^m:B(x;r)=1}. Consider the predicate
\[
 Q(x)\iff\exists z_1,\ldots,z_m\in F_2^m\quad
       \forall r\in F_2^m\quad\bigvee_{i=1}^m B(x;r+z_i)=1.
\]
The quantified strings have lengths m² and m, and the displayed disjunction is evaluated by m polynomial-time simulations. Hence Q is in Σ₂^P under the ordinary uniform definition, without an oracle for finding the shifts.

If x is a yes-instance then |S|/2^m≥1−δ. Choose the shifts independently uniformly. For any fixed r the probability that all r+z_i miss S is at most δ^m. The expected number of uncovered r is at most 2^mδ^m=(2δ)^m<1. Some tuple therefore covers the whole cube and makes Q true.

If x is a no-instance, |S|/2^m≤δ. Every union of m translates of S has size at most m|S|<2^m. Thus no tuple covers the cube and Q is false. This proves L∈Σ₂^P. Apply exactly the same construction to the BPP decider that complements A's output. Then the complement of L is in Σ₂^P, proving L∈Π₂^P as well.

**What this adds.** The earlier attempts supplied short-seed hypotheses and nonuniform simultaneous tape lists. Here the existential data are certified by a universal coverage test, which yields an actual uniform hierarchy containment. A polynomially short existential tuple does not yield a polynomial-time algorithm: checking its coverage still quantifies over exponentially many r. The elementary averaging argument is classical, commonly called the Sipser–Gács–Lautemann argument. It does not construct a short generator, remove the universal block, or establish P = BPP. The all-input BPP guarantee is used separately on each x; no promise or average-input correctness is substituted.
