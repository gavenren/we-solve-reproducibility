# Enhanced power graphs of PSL2 in every even field: explicit clique decomposition

Claim type: **Partial result**.

For every even prime power $q\ge4$, the simple group $G=\operatorname{PSL}_2(q)$ satisfies **both** requested avoidance properties, for every length $n\ge4$. More precisely, after deleting its identity vertex its enhanced power graph is
\[
 (q^2-1)K_1\ \sqcup\ \frac{q(q+1)}2 K_{q-2}\ \sqcup\ \frac{q(q-1)}2 K_q. \tag{1}
\]
The original graph is the cone over this graph: attach one vertex adjacent to every vertex. We prove the structure, taking care that commuting need not mean generating a cyclic group.

In characteristic2, the center of $\operatorname{SL}_2(q)$ is trivial, so it equals $G$. Let $x\ne I$. Its characteristic polynomial has either a repeated root or two distinct roots in $\mathbb F_{q^2}$. In the repeated-root case determinant1 forces that root to be1. In a suitable basis $x=I+uE_{12}$ with $u\ne0$. Directly solving $yx=xy$ in determinant-one matrices gives
\[
 C_G(x)=\{I+tE_{12}:t\in\mathbb F_q\}.
\]
This is elementary abelian of exponent2. Thus $x$ has order2, and a commuting distinct nonidentity $y$ generates a four-group with it, not a cyclic group. The only neighbor of this $x$ in the enhanced power graph is the identity.

In the distinct-root case, the commuting algebra in $M_2(\mathbb F_q)$ is $\mathbb F_q[x]$. In the split case this is the diagonal algebra in an eigenbasis and its determinant-one units form $\{\operatorname{diag}(a,a^{-1})\}$, cyclic of order $q-1$. In the nonsplit case it is the field $\mathbb F_{q^2}$, and the determinant is the norm to $\mathbb F_q$. Its norm-one subgroup is cyclic of order $q+1$. Thus $C_G(x)$ is cyclic of odd order in either case. Every pair in it generates a cyclic subgroup, so its nonidentity part forms a clique.

There are no edges between different such parts. If $x,y$ generate a cyclic group then they commute. For a distinct-root $x$, its centralizer is the torus just computed. Every nonidentity member $z$ of that torus has distinct eigenvalues too: its odd order excludes nontrivial unipotence, and a scalar determinant-one matrix in characteristic2 is the identity. It has the same commuting algebra, so $C_G(z)=C_G(x)$. Hence two torus centralizers intersect only at the identity unless equal. This proves a disjoint-clique decomposition without conflating the elementary abelian centralizers with cliques.

For the numbers in (1), the unipotent groups correspond to the $q+1$ projective lines: each nonidentity unipotent has its unique eigenline, and each line contributes $q-1$ involutions. This gives $q^2-1$ singletons. Split tori correspond to unordered pairs of distinct rational eigenlines, giving $\binom{q+1}2=q(q+1)/2$. Nonsplit tori correspond to Frobenius pairs in $\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_{q^2})\setminus\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_q)$, giving $(q^2-q)/2$. Their nonidentity sizes are $q-2$ and $q$. The vertex counts, plus identity, sum to $q(q^2-1)=|G|$.

An induced cycle of length at least4 cannot contain the cone vertex, which would create chords. Nor can it lie in the disjoint union in (1): it would stay within a single complete component, again creating chords. Therefore $E(G)$ is chordal and avoids every specified induced cycle length. This proves an unbounded simple-group family for both parts; it is not a necessary-and-sufficient classification across all finite simple groups.

The centralizer data agree with Das–Nongsiang[1, Proposition4.4], whose graph is the **commuting** graph. That paper's genus formula is not being transferred to the enhanced power graph: the involution components must be replaced by singletons, as the direct proof shows. No novelty is claimed for the classical matrix centralizers.
