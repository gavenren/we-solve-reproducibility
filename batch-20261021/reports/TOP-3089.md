# The one-third bound and the exact incidence calculation

Zeye Han's Theorem10 (Electronic Journal of Combinatorics24(1),2017,#P1.63) proves
\[
\max_{p\in P}d_P(p)\ge\lceil n/3\rceil+1
\]
for every non-collinear$n$-point set in the real plane. This is the weak Dirac result, not the stronger half-minus-an-absolute-constant statement in the prompt. No previously listed archive attempt is being repeated.

Here is the full transfer of the source's incidence inequality to the literal statistic. Put$l_r=\#\{\ell:|\ell\cap P|=r\}$ and$I=\sum_{r\ge2}r l_r=\sum_{p\in P}d_P(p)$. Each unordered pair determines exactly one line, hence
\[
\sum_{r\ge2}r(r-1)l_r=n(n-1).
\]
When no line contains more than$2n/3$ points, Han's Theorem9, credited there to Bojanowski and Pokora, states
\[
l_2+\tfrac34l_3\ge n+\tfrac14\sum_{r\ge5}r(r-4)l_r.
\]
This is the substantive imported geometric inequality; it is not an elementary pair-count estimate. Multiplying by four and collecting terms gives
\[
\sum_{r\ge2}(4r-r^2)l_r\ge4n.
\]
Substitute$\sum r^2l_r=n(n-1)+I$. Then$3I\ge n(n+3)$, and some point has degree at least
\[
\left\lceil I/n\right\rceil\ge\lceil(n+3)/3\rceil=\lceil n/3\rceil+1.
\]

For completeness the omitted rich-line regime causes no gap. If a line contains$k\ge\lceil n/3\rceil+1$ points, choose a point of$P$ off it. The$k$ joining lines are distinct, immediately giving the desired degree. Otherwise every line has at most$\lceil n/3\rceil\le\lfloor2n/3\rfloor$ points for$n\ge3$, so the imported inequality applies. These two cases cover every non-collinear set.

The lines counted here contain at least two points, with arbitrary higher multiplicities allowed. They are all determined lines incident to$p$, not just ordinary lines. The averaging uses incidences, not the number of distinct lines alone. The field is the real plane; no transfer of real order or Hirzebruch-type hypotheses to arbitrary fields is asserted.

The gap from the prompt remains linear: the proved lower bound is approximately$n/3$, whereas the requested one is$n/2$ up to a fixed additive loss. Merely increasing a constant in this theorem cannot close that gap. This report credits the established theorem and makes its exact quantifiers and incidence mechanism usable; it supplies no new value of the target constant$c$.
