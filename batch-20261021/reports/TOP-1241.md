# Hall's conjecture: an integral infinite Pell family and its precise limitation

Claim type: Literature report.

The literal target is the uniform lower estimate with exponent $1/2-\varepsilon$. This report reconstructs the classical Danilov upper-example family, credited through Dujella's paper, with the integrality congruences proved explicitly. It supplies infinitely many distinct positive integer pairs satisfying an exact gap formula. It does not prove the target lower estimate or claim a new construction.

Put $A(z)=z^2+6z+4$ and $B(z)=z^2+9z+19$. Direct expansion gives
\[
 A(z)^3-(z^2+1)B(z)^2=-27(2z+11).
\]
This is the identity used in Dujella's Pell interpretation. We now ensure that its rational rescaling is integral at infinitely many Pell points.

Work in the ring $\mathbb Z[\sqrt5]$. Let
\[
 \eta=(9+4\sqrt5)^{250},\qquad
 z_n+w_n\sqrt5=(682+305\sqrt5)\eta^n\quad(n\ge0).
\]
Every coefficient is a positive integer. Norms give $z_n^2-5w_n^2=-1$, since $682^2-5\cdot305^2=-1$ and $9^2-5\cdot4^2=1$.

Here is a proof of the needed congruence, without assuming a tabulated Pell period. In the quotient ring modulo $5$, the binomial theorem in characteristic $5$ gives $(9+4\sqrt5)^5=-1$, hence its tenth power is $1$. Write that tenth power as $1+5a$ with $a\in\mathbb Z[\sqrt5]$. Expanding $(1+5a)^{25}$ shows it is $1$ modulo $125$: its linear term is divisible by $125$, its quadratic coefficient is $25\binom{25}{2}$, and all higher terms contain $125$. Thus $\eta\equiv1\pmod{125}$ coefficientwise. Consequently
\[
 z_n\equiv57\pmod{125},\quad w_n\equiv55\pmod{125}.
\]
In particular $z_n\equiv2\pmod5$, $5\mid w_n$, and $125\mid2z_n+11$.

Define
\[
 x_n=\frac{A(z_n)}5,\qquad y_n=\frac{w_n B(z_n)}5.
\]
Both are positive integers: $A(2)\equiv0\pmod5$ and $w_n$ is divisible by $5$. Substitution into the expanded identity, using $z_n^2+1=5w_n^2$, yields the exact formula
\[
 x_n^3-y_n^2=-\frac{27(2z_n+11)}{125}<0.
\]
It is a nonzero integer, so equality of the square and cube never occurs. The sequence $z_n$ increases strictly without bound because $\eta>1$ and its positive coefficients multiply a positive starting pair; hence $x_n$ also increases strictly. This proves infinitude, rather than a finite list of unusually close pairs.

Moreover,
\[
 \frac{|x_n^3-y_n^2|}{\sqrt{x_n}}
 =\frac{27\sqrt5(2z_n+11)}
 {125\sqrt{z_n^2+6z_n+4}}
 \longrightarrow \frac{54\sqrt5}{125}<0.97.
\]
Thus all sufficiently large members give the classical bound $0<|x_n^3-y_n^2|<0.97\sqrt{x_n}$. The starting values already give $x_0=93844$ and $y_0=28748141$, with gap $-297$; the proof depends on the whole recurrence and congruence argument, not this one example.

For every fixed $\varepsilon>0$, the same family eventually has gap less than $x_n^{1/2+\varepsilon}$. It therefore rules out a universal positive lower estimate with any exponent strictly greater than $1/2$. It does **not** rule out an estimate with exponent exactly $1/2$: its normalized gap has a positive limit. Still less does it contradict the weaker displayed target $1/2-\varepsilon$, since along this family the ratio of the gap to $x_n^{1/2-\varepsilon}$ tends to infinity.

The earlier contribution treats the implication from abc, closest-square scans, polynomial degree obstructions and fixed Mordell curves. The present added content is an explicit congruence-preserving, all-parameter integral Pell realization with its exact normalized limit. The classical identity and family are credited to the literature; only their detailed verification is supplied here. No finite computation, GRH assumption, or full solution is asserted.
