# Exponential rank-tail bounds and the unboundedness quantifier

Claim type: **Literature report**.

The prompt asks whether the supremum of the actual Mordell–Weil ranks over $\mathbb Q$ is infinite. An unconditional statistical theorem gives strong restrictions on the possible frequency of large ranks, but it does not answer that existence question. We give explicit tail bounds, including their quantifiers and a counterexample to an incorrect boundedness inference.

Use the unique integral short model
\[
 E_{A,B}:y^2=x^3+Ax+B,\quad 4A^3+27B^2\ne0,
\]
with no prime $\ell$ satisfying both $\ell^4\mid A$ and $\ell^6\mid B$. Order these $\mathbb Q$-isomorphism classes by
$H(E)=\max(4|A|^3,27B^2)$, and write $\mathcal E(X)=\{E:H(E)<X\}$.

Bhargava–Shankar, arXiv:1312.7859v1, Theorem1, prove that
\[
 \frac1{\#\mathcal E(X)}
       \sum_{E\in\mathcal E(X)}\#\operatorname{Sel}_5(E)\longrightarrow6.
 \tag{1}
\]
Theorem2 supplies the same mean in families given by finitely many congruence conditions, with this height ordering. These are credited deep arithmetic-statistical inputs, not results of a new computation here. The checked arXiv record lists the2013 preprint and no journal reference; no publication status beyond that is asserted.

The Kummer exact sequence gives an injection
$E(\mathbb Q)/5E(\mathbb Q)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_5(E)$.
Since the source has size $5^{r(E)}\#E(\mathbb Q)[5]$, in particular
\[
 5^{r(E)}-1\le \#\operatorname{Sel}_5(E)-1.
 \tag{2}
\]
No finiteness assumption on the Tate–Shafarevich group, and no identification of Selmer rank with rational rank, is used.

For every **fixed** integer $R\ge1$, (1)–(2) prove
\[
 \limsup_{X\to\infty}
 \frac{\#\{E\in\mathcal E(X):r(E)\ge R\}}{\#\mathcal E(X)}
 \le \frac5{5^R-1}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Indeed each term in the numerator contributes at least $5^R-1$ to the sum in (2). Subtracting the baseline1 from the Selmer size improves the cruder bound $6/5^R$. A bound exceeding1, as at $R=1$, is simply uninformative and may be replaced by1.

There is also a bound on the *rank mass* of the tail:
\[
 \limsup_{X\to\infty}
 \frac{\sum_{\substack{E\in\mathcal E(X)\\r(E)\ge R}}r(E)}
      {\#\mathcal E(X)}
 \le \frac{5R}{5^R-1}.
 \tag{4}
\]
For integers $r\ge R$, the ratio $r/(5^r-1)$ decreases with $r$. To verify this, cross-multiplication reduces the step from $r$ to $r+1$ to
$5^r(4r-1)+1>0$. Hence
$r\le R(5^r-1)/(5^R-1)$ on this tail, and (4) follows from (1)–(2). Thus exceptionally large ranks cannot carry a substantial proportion of the height-averaged rank. Exactly the same arguments establish (3)–(4) inside every positive-density congruence family to which the source's Theorem2 applies. They are not claims about an arbitrary thin twist family.

The order of quantifiers matters. Equations(3)–(4) hold after fixing $R$ and taking $X\to\infty$. They do not assert a uniform finite-$X$ error for a moving threshold $R=R(X)$. More fundamentally, an exponentially small upper density is compatible with infinitely many examples at every rank threshold, with finitely many examples, or with none. An upper bound supplies no lower existence statement.

Even a bounded exponential moment cannot force bounded maximum rank. For an exact elementary model, define nonnegative integers
\[
 b(n)=k\quad\text{if }n=5^{2k},\ k\ge1,\qquad b(n)=0\text{ otherwise}.
\]
Their supremum is infinite. Yet
\[
 \sum_{n\le N}5^{b(n)}
 \le N+\sum_{k\le\frac12\log_5N}5^k
 =N+O(\sqrt N),
\]
so the average tends to1. This is a counterexample to the proposed logical implication, not a model asserted to describe elliptic curves.

The earlier contribution explores independent-point constructions, base-field pitfalls and an exact descent example. The added regime here is uniform rank-frequency control over all curves and congruence families, with a quantitative tail-moment estimate and an explicit explanation of why it cannot decide the selected supremum. A proof of unbounded ranks must still produce rational-rank lower bounds at arbitrarily large thresholds; no analytic rank, Selmer upper bound, or extension-field rank is substituted for them.
