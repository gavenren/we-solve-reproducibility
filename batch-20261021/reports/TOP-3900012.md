# Two proven restrictions on a perfect triangle

Makhul's isosceles obstruction and fixed-angle finiteness theorem apply to the literal prompt, since rational sides and area automatically make all altitudes rational. They do not prove nonexistence for all triangles.

The isosceles obstruction can be transferred especially transparently using Lemma3.1 of arXiv:2009.00654v2: the equation
\[
Z^2=X^4+10X^2Y^2+9Y^4
\]
has no solution in positive integers$X,Y,Z$. The primary proof is a descent argument; this arithmetic theorem is the credited external input.

Place a hypothetical perfect isosceles triangle at$(-u,0),(u,0),(0,v)$ with$u,v>0$. The half-base$u$ is rational. The altitude$v$ is rational because area equals$uv$ and the area is rational; equivalently it is the median to the base. Its equal side length$A=\sqrt{u^2+v^2}$ and twice either other median$B=\sqrt{9u^2+v^2}$ must both be rational. Multiplication gives
\[
(AB)^2=v^4+10v^2u^2+9u^4.
\]
Clear denominators of$u,v,AB$ by a common scale, noting that the left product scales quadratically, to obtain a forbidden positive integer solution. Neither$u$ nor$v$ can be zero because the prompt requires nondegeneracy. Thus no perfect triangle is isosceles. This direct product reduction avoids incomplete parametrizations of Pythagorean-type equations.

A separate theorem, Makhul1910.06888v1 Theorem1.1, states that for every fixed$0<\theta<\pi$ there are only finitely many perfect triangles up to similarity having an angle$\theta$. The proof passes to rational points on higher-genus algebraic curves and uses Faltings' finiteness theorem. It supplies no effective uniform bound as$\theta$ varies and no finite list of all possible angles.

In particular, any infinite sequence of pairwise nonsimilar perfect triangles, if one existed, would have to use infinitely many angle values: a finite list of angle values would give a finite union of the theorem's finite classes. This is an exclusion of fixed-angle parametrizations, not an algorithm deciding existence. Rational scale multiples of a single triangle would not contradict it.

The original Rathbun correspondence preserved by Eppstein includes rational-side/rational-median examples with irrational area, and examples with rational area but only two rational medians. They fail different conjuncts of the prompt and cannot be combined to claim a perfect triangle. For example the recorded$(73,51,26)$ Heron triangle has area420 and two rational medians, but its remaining median has square3796, which is strictly between$61^2$ and$62^2$ and hence is irrational. This exact check is illustrative, not a finite nonexistence search.
