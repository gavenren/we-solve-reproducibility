# Why connected sums of genus-one examples do not raise support genus

**Problem:** TOP-2843

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** Precise report of Orbegozo Rodriguez–Stenhede arXiv:2607.19892v1: support genus of a contact connected sum is at most the maximum of the two support genera. This rules out one proposed construction of support genus >1, without constructing such a structure or claiming a lower bound for all supporting open books.

Support genus minimizes over all open books supporting the same contact structure. Consequently a construction exhibiting a large-genus page cannot itself show that the support genus is large. A recent preprint removes a particularly natural approach suggested for this problem.

Orbegozo Rodriguez–Stenhede's Theorem 1.1 states that for closed connected cooriented contact 3-manifolds,
\[
 \operatorname{sg}(\xi_1\#\xi_2)
 \leq\max\{\operatorname{sg}(\xi_1),\operatorname{sg}(\xi_2)\}.        \tag{1}
\]
This is the checked July 2026 preprint theorem, not a claim of journal publication. Its argument uses a polygonal Murasugi sum of pages rather than only the usual boundary connected sum, which would add genera.

The page construction is the important point. Lemma 2.2 chooses alternating boundary/interior polygon edges so the Murasugi-sum page has genus exactly \(\max(g_1,g_2)\). The genus-one, one-boundary base construction yields Euler characteristic \(-3\) and three boundary components, hence genus one. The source extends it to higher genera and arbitrary nonempty boundary. Extending each monodromy by the identity gives an abstract open book on this page. The credited contact Murasugi-sum theorem identifies its supported contact manifold with the contact connected sum. Choosing minimal-genus pages in the two factors proves (1).

Induction now makes the obstruction to the suggested strategy exact. If every \(\xi_i\) has support genus one, then
\[
 \operatorname{sg}(\xi_1\#\cdots\#\xi_n)\leq1
\]
for every finite \(n\), regardless of how large \(n\) becomes. Thus connected summing more such examples never proves either part of the prompt. No lower bound is needed for this conclusion.

The source also explains a useful limitation of this construction. A Murasugi sum contains each original page as a subsurface, so its genus cannot fall below the larger page genus; the chosen construction is optimal *among such sums*. This does not make (1) an equality for support genera, since a completely different supporting open book can have smaller genus. In fact an overtwisted summand makes the connected sum overtwisted and planar, so a maximum formula without an inequality would be false in general.

Accordingly this is a problem-specific restriction on a proposed construction, not an example of support genus greater than one. The existence of such an example and an unbounded family remain outside what the checked theorem proves.
