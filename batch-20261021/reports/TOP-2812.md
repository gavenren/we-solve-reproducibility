# A volume obstruction and an exact-versus-floating-point audit for Menasco–Reid

**Problem:** TOP-2812

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** Every hyperbolic S³-knot complement of volume less than 2v₈ contains no closed embedded totally geodesic surface. For larger volumes, the credited exact holonomy/normal-surface algorithm is distinguished from the authors' un-rerun floating-point experiments; no universal conjecture proof is asserted.

Let \(v_8\) denote the volume of a regular ideal octahedron. A rigorous obstruction covering a whole geometric range is
\[
 \operatorname{vol}(S^3\setminus K)<2v_8
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \text{no closed embedded totally geodesic surface}.                \tag{1}
\]
The constant is exact; rounded decimal comparisons are unnecessary.

First any closed connected embedded surface in \(S^3\) is two-sided and orientable. For completeness, Alexander duality with mod-two coefficients gives two complementary components. Thus the surface separates; selecting a side gives a normal direction, and the orientation of \(S^3\) orients the surface. A surface in the knot complement remains embedded in \(S^3\), so this argument applies there as well.

If such a surface \(F\) were totally geodesic, its intrinsic curvature would be \(-1\). Hence \(g(F)\geq2\) and Gauss–Bonnet gives \(\operatorname{area}(F)=4\pi(g-1)\). Cutting the ambient manifold along \(F\) produces two boundary copies, regardless of whether the cut manifold is connected. The credited Miyamoto geodesic-boundary estimate, in the form of Basilio–Lee–Malionek's Lemma 14, is
\[
 \operatorname{vol}(M)\geq 2\mu_3(0)\operatorname{area}(F),
 \qquad \mu_3(0)={v_8\over4\pi}.
\]
Consequently \(\operatorname{vol}(M)\geq2v_8(g-1)\geq2v_8\), proving (1). More generally, a candidate of genus \(g\) must satisfy
\[
 g\leq1+{\operatorname{vol}(M)\over2v_8}.
\]
This is an upper bound on \(|\chi(F)|\), not an upper bound on the signed negative Euler characteristic.

The same 2024 paper describes an exact algorithm using a triangulation and a lifted algebraic holonomy representation into \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Q(\alpha))\). It combines a bounded-complexity normal-surface search with exact tests for Fuchsian subgroups and with a check distinguishing an actual embedded geodesic surface from the boundary of a neighborhood of a one-sided one. Its correctness is a credited algorithm theorem on that input.

That theorem must be separated from the reported census implementation. Section 4.1 explicitly replaces exact holonomy and real-trace tests by double precision and a numerical threshold. Those experiments were not rerun here and do not become certified negative theorems merely because the observed imaginary parts were large. Nor does a finite census settle all knots. The rigorous conclusion of this report is the volume obstruction (1), together with a precise account of what additional exact input the algorithm requires beyond numerical evidence.
