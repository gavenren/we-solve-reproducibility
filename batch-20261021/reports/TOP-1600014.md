# Exact autocorrelation filters and an odd-to-even Williamson construction

Claim type: **Partial result**.

We give an explicit construction transforming **any odd-order Williamson quadruple** into one of twice that order, together with exact algebraic filters on every candidate order. We distinguish this one-way closure from a purported converse: published enumeration gives existence at70 despite nonexistence at35. The contribution supplies complete constructive identities and precise source boundaries; it does not classify all orders or claim a new construction.

Represent a symmetric circulant by its first row$X=(x_0,\ldots,x_{n-1})$, with$x_i=x_{-i}$ and indices modulo$n$. Put
\[
 C_X(s)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}x_ix_{i+s}.
\]
The$(0,s)$ entry of$XX^{\mathsf T}$ is$C_X(s)$, and symmetry gives$X^{\mathsf T}=X$. Consequently the four circulants are Williamson exactly when
\[
 \sum_{X=A,B,C,D}C_X(0)=4n,\qquad
 \sum_X C_X(s)=0\quad(1\le s<n). \tag{1}
\]
The first identity is automatic for sign sequences. This is an exact equivalence, not a numerical spectral tolerance.

If$\zeta=e^{2\pi i/n}$ and$\widehat X(k)=\sum_i x_i\zeta^{ik}$, finite Fourier inversion gives
\[
 \sum_X|\widehat X(k)|^2=4n\quad(0\le k<n). \tag{2}
\]
Indeed the transform of$C_X$ is$|\widehat X|^2$ with the chosen sign convention, and the transform of$4n$ at shift0 is constant$4n$. Thus every partial sum of the nonnegative terms in(2) is at most$4n$. At$k=0$, if$r_X=\sum_i x_i$, this yields the exact integer filter
\[
 r_A^2+r_B^2+r_C^2+r_D^2=4n,\qquad r_X\equiv n\pmod2,\quad |r_X|\le n. \tag{3}
\]
Neither(3) alone nor a floating-point approximation to(2) proves existence/nonexistence; all nonzero shifts in(1) must be checked or excluded rigorously.

Now suppose$n$ is odd. Let$s=(n+1)/2$, so$2s\equiv1\pmod n$. For a symmetric row$B$ define$b'_i=b_{i+s}$. Define interleaving by
\[
 (X\diamond Y)_{2i}=x_i,\qquad (X\diamond Y)_{2i+1}=y_i.
\]
The following four length$2n$ rows are Williamson:
\[
 E=A\diamond B',\quad F=(-A)\diamond B',\quad
 K=C\diamond D',\quad L=(-C)\diamond D'. \tag{4}
\]
All entries are signs. The even positions are symmetric because$A,C$ are. At the odd positions symmetry requires$b'_i=b'_{-i-1}$. This holds since symmetry of$B$ turns the latter into$b_{i+1-s}=b_{i+s}$, using$2s\equiv1$. This direct equation fixes the shift direction and avoids an ambiguous left/right cyclic-shift convention.

For an even shift$2t$,
\[
 C_{X\diamond Y}(2t)=C_X(t)+C_Y(t).
\]
Negating$X$ changes neither autocorrelation, and a cyclic shift changes none. Summing the four rows in(4) gives$2\sum_{X=A,B,C,D}C_X(t)$, which is zero for$1\le t<n$. For an odd shift, every correlation term pairs an even position with an odd position. Negating only$X$ therefore negates the entire odd-shift correlation. The correlations of$E,F$ cancel, and so do those of$K,L$. The zero-shift sum is$4(2n)$. Hence(1) holds in length$2n$, proving the construction.

The construction mechanism is credited to Bright–Kotsireas–Ganesh[1, Theorem17 and appendix]; here its symmetry condition is expressed by the explicit congruence$2s=1$ rather than copying a shift convention. It proves a uniform implication for every odd admissible order. It cannot be iterated blindly because its input order must be odd.

The same source's v2 introduction reports Williamson nonexistence at35 and its own complete even-order enumeration through70, including existence at70. These are credited computational literature results, not computations independently rerun here. They show that existence at$2n$ need not imply existence at$n$. Also nonexistence of a Williamson quadruple is not nonexistence of an arbitrary Hadamard matrix of order$4n$: Williamson is a restricted construction. Neither a general even-order theorem nor a complete admissible-order list follows.
