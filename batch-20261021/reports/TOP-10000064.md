# A conformally stable subclass with exactly one accumulation point

We prove the requested assertion for any packing whose radii are uniformly bounded below, and for its sphere-inversion images with pole outside every closed ball. The statement holds in every dimension and does not need the lattice's adjacency beyond infinitude. The inversion images give explicit bounded$\mathbb Z^3$ packings with radii tending to zero and a single finite accumulation point. Arbitrary lattice packings are not reduced to this subclass.

First let$r_v\ge a>0$. Only finitely many balls can meet any compact set. It suffices to use a compact containing ball$B(0,R)$. Whenever a packing ball$B(c,r)$ meets it, choose$x$ in their intersection. Inside$B(c,r)$ there is a radius-$a$ subball whose center is within distance$a$ of$x$: if$x$ is already at distance at most$r-a$ from$c$, center the subball at$x$; otherwise move toward$c$ until the distance is$r-a$. The move has length at most$a$. Therefore the chosen subball is contained in$B(0,R+2a)$. These subballs have disjoint interiors. Volume comparison bounds their number by$((R+2a)/a)^d$. This argument allows arbitrarily large original radii and does not assume that their centers lie near the compact set.

Consequently there is no finite accumulation point. An infinite packing has$\infty$ as an accumulation point in the one-point compactification: if some compact set's exterior met only finitely many balls, then the infinitely many remaining balls would all meet that compact set, contrary to local finiteness just proved. Thus its accumulation set is exactly$\{\infty\}$.

Let$I$ be inversion of unit radius about$p$, with$p$ outside every closed ball of such a packing. On the one-point compactification it is a homeomorphism exchanging$p$ and$\infty$. It maps every original closed ball to a closed ball, rather than an exterior region, and preserves disjoint interiors and tangency. The accumulation set of a family transforms under any homeomorphism: a neighborhood meets infinitely many transformed sets exactly when its preimage meets infinitely many original sets. Hence the new packing has exactly the single accumulation point$p$. Its tangency graph is unchanged.

For an explicit example start with
\[
B_v=B(2v,1),\qquad v\in\mathbb Z^3.
\]
Distances between distinct centers are at least two, with equality exactly for nearest-neighbor indices. Choose$p=(1,1,1)$; its distance from every center is at least$\sqrt3>1$, so inversion is allowed. With$d_v=\|2v-p\|$, the transformed center and radius are
\[
c'_v=p+\frac{2v-p}{d_v^2-1},\qquad
r'_v=\frac1{d_v^2-1}.
\]
The image ball is within distance$1/(d_v-1)\le1/(\sqrt3-1)$ of$p$, so the entire packing is bounded and$\infty$ is not an accumulation point. As$\|v\|\to\infty$, its centers tend to$p$ and its radii tend to zero, while the homeomorphism argument excludes all other accumulation points. This verifies a genuinely shrinking-radius case rather than assuming a positive radius bound after inversion.

The contribution supplies a sufficient geometric hypothesis, its conformal transport, and a concrete family satisfying the exact lattice graph condition. It leaves open whether every realization of$\mathbb Z^3$ admits any conformal normalization with a uniform positive radius bound; no such unproved normalization is used.
