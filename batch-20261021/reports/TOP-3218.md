# Integer five-flows from modulo-three flows, with an unbounded sharpness family

**Claim type: Literature report.** A finite bidirected graph admitting a nowhere-zero \(\mathbb Z_3\)-flow has a nowhere-zero integer **5-flow**, and hence satisfies the prompt's integer6-flow conclusion. The credited theorem also confines magnitude four to underlying bridges. We give the precise translation to the prompt's fixed half-edge convention and a complete unbounded sharpness example showing that integer4-flows need not exist under this modular premise.

This is a genuine restricted class, not a substitution of modular flows for all integer-flow-admissible inputs.

## Exact external lifting theorem

DeVos, Li, Lu, Luo, Cun-Quan Zhang and Zhang Zhang, *Flows on flow-admissible signed graphs*, arXiv1908.10853v2, Theorem3.2, proves: if a signed graph admits a nowhere-zero \(\mathbb Z_3\)-flow, it has an integer5-flow \(g\) with
\[
 g(e)\ne0,\qquad |g(e)|\ne3,\qquad
 \{e:|g(e)|=4\}\subseteq B(G), \tag{1}
\]
where \(B(G)\) denotes the set of underlying bridges. In particular, a bridgeless graph under this additional modular hypothesis has an integer3-flow.

The source's general Theorem1.1 gives an integer11-flow for every flow-admissible signed graph. That general bound does not establish Bouchet's6-flow target; it is context rather than a replacement conclusion. The long lifting proof is credited external input.

## Why this applies to the exact fixed incidences

Let \(\sigma(h)\in\{+1,-1\}\) be the prompt's incidence sign, with \(+1\) pointing in. Give each edge signed-graph signature
\[
 \epsilon(e)=-\sigma(h_1)\sigma(h_2),
\]
including both incidences of a loop. In the source, the bidirection variable has \(+1\) pointing out. Taking its variable to be \(\tau(h)=-\sigma(h)\) gives \(\tau(h_1)\tau(h_2)=-\epsilon(e)\), as required by its definition. Its flow equations are just the negatives of the prompt's equations. Their integer and modular kernels therefore coincide.

If a theorem constructs a flow using another bidirection for the same signature, its two signs on each edge differ from the fixed signs either on neither incidence or on both. Multiplying that edge's value by minus one in the latter case returns to the prescribed incidences, without changing its magnitude or support. This works for loops too; a negative loop contributes twice the signed edge value, rather than disappearing from the equation.

Thus (1) is a statement about flows respecting the fixed input incidences, not permission to change the problem's signs.

## An unbounded family requiring five rather than four

Let \(T\) be any finite tree whose degrees are one or three and that has at least one degree-three vertex. At every leaf add one **negative** loop. Keep all tree edges positive. The resulting signed multigraph \(G_T\) is cubic when loops count twice, and has arbitrarily large order as the trivalent tree grows.

First it has a nowhere-zero \(\mathbb Z_3\)-flow. Bipartition the tree into \(A,B\), orient its tree edges from \(A\) to \(B\), and choose both incidences of each added loop to have the same direction as that leaf's tree-edge incidence. Every vertex then has three incidences all pointing in or all pointing out. Assign value one to every edge in \(\mathbb Z_3\): each vertex boundary is \(3\) or \(-3\), hence zero. Negative loops permit exactly the chosen equal incidence directions. By the credited lifting theorem, \(G_T\) has an integer5-flow.

We now prove that it has **no** nowhere-zero integer4-flow, in any equivalent bidirection. Suppose such a flow existed. At a leaf \(v\) of \(T\), its conservation equation is
\[
 \pm f(e_v)\pm2f(\ell_v)=0.
\]
With all magnitudes between one and three, this forces \(|f(\ell_v)|=1\) and \(|f(e_v)|=2\).

Reduce all vertex equations modulo two. Loop contributions are even, and signs disappear in characteristic two. Therefore the subset of tree edges on which the flow is odd has even degree at every vertex. A nonempty edge subset of a tree is a nonempty forest and has a degree-one vertex, so no such even-degree subset exists. Every tree-edge flow is consequently even. Since it is nonzero and has magnitude below four, every tree edge has magnitude exactly two.

At a degree-three vertex of \(T\), conservation is now a sum of three terms, each equal to \(+2\) or \(-2\). Such a sum is never zero. This contradiction proves the nonexistence of a4-flow.

Consequently \(G_T\) has exact minimum nowhere-zero integer-flow bound five. The family includes branching trees of arbitrary size; it is not merely the path joining two negative loops from the earlier attempt. It also explains why the source's allowance of bridge value four is substantive. No claim that every bridge necessarily has value four is made.

## What the modular hypothesis does and does not buy

Equation(1) supplies values only in \(\{\pm1,\pm2,\pm4\}\), all allowed by the prompt's6-flow target. The modulus-three certificate therefore gives a substantial sufficient condition and a stronger resulting bound.

However, the premise of Bouchet's conjecture is existence of some integer nowhere-zero flow, not existence of a nowhere-zero \(\mathbb Z_3\)-flow. Reducing an arbitrary integer flow modulo three can turn an edge value divisible by three into zero. There is no assertion that this reduction preserves nowhere-zero support. The source's11-flow theorem does not repair that gap by an elementary reduction.

Math-1136-A1 handles balanced Eulerian inputs, one unbalanced-cycle obstruction and the path/negative-loop handcuff example. Here the full credited modular-lifting class and its branching sharpness family go beyond those elementary scopes. All proofs of the family and sign translation are given; no finite computation or source-algorithm rerun is claimed.
