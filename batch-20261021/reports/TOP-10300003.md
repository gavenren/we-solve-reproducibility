# The all-plane leaf class, with a uniform intrinsic coarse model

**Problem:** TOP-10300003

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** For C² foliations on closed connected orientable 3-manifolds, the singleton topological leaf class {R²} forces M≅T³; T³ realizes it by a smooth taut linear foliation. Every leaf of that example is uniformly bilipschitz to Euclidean R² for every ambient smooth metric. No classification from coarse type alone is claimed.

Consider the particular collection \(\mathscr C=\{\mathbb R^2\}\), with \(M\) closed connected orientable and the foliation \(C^2\). Rosenberg's all-plane theorem forces \(M\) to be diffeomorphic to \(T^3\). The full checked 1974 Chatelet–Rosenberg–Weil paper explicitly records this theorem in its introduction; its stronger orbit-foliation classification concerns nonsingular \(\mathbb R^2\)-actions. We use the stated all-plane conclusion and do not identify an arbitrary foliation with a smooth action without a theorem.

Here is an explicit converse meeting the prompt's stronger single-closed-transversal formulation. Choose a nonzero covector \(a=(a_1,a_2,a_3)\) whose coordinates are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\), and let
\[
 T^3=\mathbb R^3/\mathbb Z^3,\qquad
 \mathcal F_a=\ker(a_1dx_1+a_2dx_2+a_3dx_3).
\]
The leaves are images of affine planes \(P_c=\{x:a\cdot x=c\}\). Their stabilizer under deck translations is
\(\{z\in\mathbb Z^3:a\cdot z=0\}=\{0\}\).
Therefore each leaf, with its leaf topology, is a plane. An immersed dense leaf need not be an embedded subset with the subspace topology; the claim uses the correct intrinsic leaf topology.

Select a primitive integer vector \(v\) with \(a\cdot v\ne0\). The circle \(\gamma(t)=tv\bmod\mathbb Z^3\) is everywhere transverse. Given any point represented by \(x\), choose a real \(t=(a\cdot x)/(a\cdot v)\). Then \(tv-x\in\ker a\), so the leaf through \(x\) meets this same circle. Thus one closed transversal meets every leaf, exactly as required by the prompt.

There is also an intrinsic geometric conclusion, independent of density in the ambient torus. Let \(g\) be any smooth Riemannian metric on \(T^3\), and lift it periodically to \(\mathbb R^3\). Compactness gives positive constants \(m,M\) with
\[
 m|w|^2\leq\widetilde g_x(w,w)\leq M|w|^2
 \quad(x\in\mathbb R^3,\ w\in\mathbb R^3).
\]
Restrict to any \(P_c\) and compare lengths of all leafwise paths. The Euclidean straight segment gives the upper distance bound, while every path has Euclidean length at least the endpoint distance. Hence
\[
 \sqrt m\,|x-y|\leq d_{P_c,g}(x,y)\leq\sqrt M\,|x-y|.
\]
The constants are independent of the leaf. Every leaf in this example is therefore uniformly bilipschitz, and in particular quasi-isometric, to Euclidean \(\mathbb R^2\).

This resolves one complete topological leaf collection on its stated regularity/compactness scope and realizes its Euclidean coarse version. It does not assert that every surface quasi-isometric to \(\mathbb R^2\) is topologically a plane or classify all manifolds satisfying only that coarse requirement. A homeomorphism of foliations alone would not justify the metric estimate; periodic metric comparison is what proves it here.
