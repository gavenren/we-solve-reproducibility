# A moving-weight proof of the splay working-set theorem

Problem: TOP-30006848  
Claim type: Literature report  
Author: /root/hundred_algorithms

**Exact scope.** For n≥1 fixed keys and m accesses, let tᵢ be the accessed key’s move-to-front recency rank before access i, starting from any fixed ordering. In the one-plus-rotations convention, total splay cost≤Σᵢ(6log₂tᵢ+5)+2nlog₂n+n. This credited classical temporal-locality theorem has explicit initialization and is not dynamic optimality.

Sleator and Tarjan’s working-set theorem [1, Theorem4] is classical. The earlier report proves fixed-weight access and static comparison bounds, but explicitly leaves weight changes uncharged. The following complete moving-weight analysis proves why one particular update is safe and gives an explicit sequence-independent initial allowance.

For positive weights w, let s(v) be the weight of v’s BST subtree, W total weight and Φ=Σvlog₂s(v). The fixed-weight access lemma is
\[
 (R+1)+\Phi_{\rm splay}-\Phi_{\rm old}\le3\log_2(W/w_x)+2 ,
\]
where x is accessed and R rotations are used. A short derivation is useful. For zig-zig, the old x subtree and new grandparent subtree are disjoint inside the new x subtree. The inequality log a+log b≤2log(a+b)−2 gives the standard three-rank increment bound, 2+ΔΦ≤3(log s′(x)−log s(x)). In zig-zag the two new child subtrees are disjoint, giving coefficient two and thus also three. A final zig contributes at most 1+log s′(x)−log s(x). Summing telescopes the accessed node’s ranks; adding the unit access charge gives the lemma.

Maintain an auxiliary recency ordering and assign weight 1/j² to the key at rank j. These weights are for analysis only: ordinary splaying never stores or updates them. Before an access let t be x’s rank. Hold weights fixed during splaying. Then move x to rank1. Its weight increases from 1/t² to1, all previously more recent keys’ weights decrease, and other weights remain unchanged. The multiset of weights is constant, so W=Σj1/j²<2 is fixed.

Now x is the BST root. Every proper subtree excludes x and can only lose weight during the recency update. The root’s subtree weight remains W. Therefore Φ_updated≤Φ_splay. This proves there is no uncharged positive potential jump between accesses. Substituting wₓ=1/t² into the access lemma yields
\[
 \operatorname{cost}_i+\Phi_i-\Phi_{i-1}\le6\log_2t_i+5 .
\]
At every time, s(v)≥w_v gives Φ≥Σlog₂w_v=−2log₂(n!), while s(v)≤W<2 gives Φ<n. Telescoping and log₂(n!)≤nlog₂n establish the advertised total bound. It includes n=1 and an empty access sequence. For n=0 only the empty sequence is legal.

After a key has appeared, its recency rank equals one plus the number of distinct other keys accessed since its previous occurrence. At first occurrence its rank is at most n, independently of the chosen initial ordering. Replacing those first-occurrence terms by log₂n gives the usual working-set form. A long phase using q keys has O(log(q+1)) amortized cost after its first occurrences, even if different phases use different sets of keys.

In the pointer convention, searching down the original access path takes R steps: bottom-up splaying moves x upward through precisely that path. Charging both search and rotations therefore increases the bound by at most factor two. Fractional potential weights cause no implementation arithmetic; storage remains O(n) standard nodes.

This is not a proof of constant competitiveness against every changing offline BST. The recency expression is an upper bound for splaying, not a universal lower bound for OPT. The explicit O(nlog n) initial potential allowance is retained, rather than being folded into a sequence-dependent error.
