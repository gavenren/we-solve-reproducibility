# The optimal simplex-block growth rate is attained uniquely in dimension13

Write$c_d=(d+1)d^d/d!$ for the normalized projection-body volume of a$d$-simplex. Among all positive block dimensions, the unique maximum of$c_d^{1/d}$ is at$d=13$. In particular, for products of simplices the optimal exponential growth rate per ambient dimension is
\[
\alpha=c_{13}^{1/13},\qquad c_{13}=23298085122481/34214400.
\]
For dimension$13q$, a product of$q$ thirteen-dimensional simplices maximizes the ratio among all products of simplices, and every maximizing block partition consists solely of13s. This is a theorem about a specified product class, not about all convex bodies.

The complete earlier archive entry gives the$T_{10}\times T_{10}$ counterexample. We additionally fetched its full pinned primary TeX. That source already proves the product identity and exponential excess using ten-dimensional factors; therefore neither fact is represented here as new added scope. The new calculation optimizes the factor dimension over every positive integer, with an analytic proof that finite checking closes the infinite search.

The credited geometric identity is
\[
R_{a+b}(A\times B)=R_a(A)R_b(B),\qquad
R_d(K)=|\Pi K|/|K|^{d-1}.
\]
For polytopes it follows by separating product facets:
$\Pi(A\times B)=(|B|\Pi A)\times(|A|\Pi B)$.
The simplex value follows from$\Pi T_d$ being a translate of$([0,1]^d+[0,\mathbf1])/(d-1)!$, whose numerator has volume$d+1$. Thus every product with block dimensions$d_1,\ldots,d_s$ has value$\prod_i c_{d_i}$. Intervals may be included with the natural$R_1=2=c_1$ convention; excluding them does not change the optimum below.

The attached exact checker verifies
\[
c_m^{13}<c_{13}^{\,m}
\quad\text{for every }1\le m<1000,\ m\ne13.
\tag{1}
\]
It uses rational integer-power comparisons, with no logarithmic rounding. Here is the independent all-large-$m$ proof that makes(1) sufficient. The exponential series gives$e<11/4$ and$m^m/m!<e^m$, so
\[
c_m<(m+1)(11/4)^m.
\]
At$m=1000$, the single binomial term$\binom{1000}{6}/100^6>1001$ proves$(101/100)^{1000}>1001$. Induction then gives$(101/100)^m>m+1$ for every$m\ge1000$, since$(101/100)(m+1)\ge m+2$. Consequently
\[
c_m^{1/m}<\frac{1111}{400}
<c_{10}^{1/10}<c_{13}^{1/13}.
\tag{2}
\]
Both final strict comparisons and the binomial inequality are exact rational certificates checked by the program. The infinite induction and exponential-series estimates are proved here, not inferred from testing1000 cases. Together(1)–(2) prove unique maximality over all$m$.

Every product of ambient dimension$N$ therefore has value at most$\alpha^N$. If$N=13q$, the all-13 partition attains it, and uniqueness follows because each non-13 factor makes its own rate strictly smaller. More generally all sufficiently large$N$ have products within a fixed positive factor of$\alpha^N$: use13-blocks and a residual simplex of dimension$2,\ldots,12$; for residual1 replace one13-block and that residual by a14-block. There are only13 residual cases, so the minimum resulting ratio is positive. This proves the exact asymptotic rate of the product-class optimum.

The checker also independently optimizes all block partitions through dimension80 by dynamic programming, confirming the stated divisible-by13 cases there. Those finite checks are not the proof of the all-$N$ rate or a search over arbitrary convex bodies. Normal and optimization-refusal runs are recorded; no source Lean development was run.
