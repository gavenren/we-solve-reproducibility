# Effective confinement of very small Margulis numbers

**Problem:** TOP-2821

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** The precise Futer–Purcell–Schleimer theorem confines every orientable finite-volume hyperbolic three-manifold with μ(M)≤0.29 to a closed bounded-volume class with explicit positive systole bound. The report explains finiteness and the distinction between this effective exceptional class and the prompt's proposed Weeks minimizer. It does not certify the candidate value or uniqueness.

A useful rigorous part of the optimization is the following effective confinement theorem. In the stated orientable finite-volume class, Futer–Purcell–Schleimer, Theorem 9.29(2), proves
\[
 \mu(M)\le0.29\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 M\text{ closed},\quad
 \operatorname{vol}(M)\le52.78,\quad
 \operatorname{sys}(M)\ge2.73\cdot10^{-8}.
\]
These are full displacement/short-loop thresholds, not injectivity-radius numbers divided by two. The volume estimate comes from Shalen; the effective systole estimate uses controlled drilling and cone deformation.

Why is the systole condition substantive? Bounded volume alone does not make the collection of closed hyperbolic three-manifolds finite: Dehn fillings can have bounded volumes and core geodesics tending to zero length. The additional uniform systole bound excludes precisely that degeneration. Standard hyperbolic compactness then gives a finite set of isometry types in the displayed class; the cited paper explains how its effective bounds make the exceptional class computable. This is stronger than a bare assertion of finiteness from an algebraic-limit argument.

One may also read the theorem contrapositively: every sufficiently short systole forces \(\mu(M)>0.29\), and so does volume above the displayed bound. Long Dehn-filling families therefore cannot provide an uncontrolled sequence of examples with Margulis numbers at most \(0.29\), despite their bounded volumes.

The geometric convention can be compared directly with the prompt. A short based essential loop lifts to a displacement of a deck transformation at a chosen lift of its base point. In a horocusp or a geodesic tube, all such loops belong to the same abelian peripheral/cyclic subgroup. Conversely, overlap of sufficiently short-loop neighborhoods would exhibit short noncommuting elements if two distinct elementary subgroups contributed. This is the standard thin-part form of the Margulis condition used in the cited work; no orbifold torsion is introduced.

This theorem localizes the **very small** range only. The candidate Weeks value in the prompt is much larger than \(0.29\), so testing the finite exceptional class would not by itself compare every manifold with that candidate. The original global bound and uniqueness claim require further arguments over the whole remaining interval. No finite census, floating-point Weeks computation, or unperformed computer verification is supplied here as a substitute.
