# The binary model: factor orbits, the exact linear obstruction, and the conditional mechanism

**Claim type: Literature report.** The prompt asks whether
\[
F_n(T)=T^n+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\xi_jT^j+1,\qquad
\Pr(\xi_j=0)=\Pr(\xi_j=1)=\tfrac12,
\]
is irreducible with probability tending to one. This report reconstructs the mechanism connecting the exact model to prime root counts, explains a multiplicity issue in that reduction, and derives its unavoidable linear-factor probability. It then combines two substantial, explicitly credited theorems to obtain the conditional limit. This is an exposition and synthesis of known results, not a claim of a new unconditional solution.

## 1. What the unconditional theorems actually give

Bary-Soroker–Koukoulopoulos–Kozma [BSKK, Theorems 1(b), 2] imply that there are constants $\delta,\theta,c>0$ such that, for all sufficiently large $n$,
\[
\Pr(F_n\text{ irreducible})\ge\delta,\qquad
\Pr\bigl(F_n\text{ has a nonconstant divisor of degree }\le\theta n\bigr)
 \le n^{-c}.                                                   \tag{1}
\]
Their model samples all nonleading coefficients from the uniform law on $\{0,1\}$ conditional on a nonzero constant term. Conditioning forces that term to be 1 and leaves the other coefficients independent, exactly as here. For Theorem 2 one may take fixed $H=3$ and $\varepsilon=1/2$ in its bounded-support and maximum-atom hypotheses.

The second conclusion is not irreducibility: two factors can both have degrees larger than $\theta n$. In particular, the linear-scale exclusion is kept distinct from the probability-$\delta$ conclusion and from the requested probability-one limit.

## 2. The exact factor-orbit reduction, including powers

Write $R(f)$ for the number of **distinct** monic irreducible factors of a monic integral polynomial $f$ of positive degree and constant term 1. A general polynomial with $R(f)=1$ need not be irreducible: it can be a proper power. For this binary model the loophole disappears.

Indeed, if $F_n=g^m$ for a monic $g\in\mathbb Z[T]$ and $m\ge2$, then $g(0)=\pm1$. If $a_jT^j$ is its lowest nonconstant nonzero term, the coefficient of $T^j$ in $g^m$ is $m g(0)^{m-1}a_j$, of absolute value at least 2. This contradicts the binary coefficients. Gauss's lemma shows that if $R(F_n)=1$, its factorization is of precisely this form with integral monic $g$. Consequently
\[
R(F_n)=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad F_n\text{ is irreducible}.   \tag{2}
\]

Let $\Omega$ be the set of distinct complex roots of the squarefree part of $f$, and let $G$ be its splitting-field Galois group. The $G$-orbits are exactly its distinct irreducible factors. Burnside's identity gives
\[
R(f)=\frac1{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}|\operatorname{Fix}_{\Omega}(\sigma)|.
                                                                    \tag{3}
\]
For all but finitely many primes, the number $\nu_f(p)$ of distinct roots modulo $p$ equals the number of fixed points of a Frobenius permutation. Chebotarev's theorem, applied to each conjugacy class, therefore yields the fixed-polynomial identity
\[
\lim_{Y\to\infty}\frac1{\pi(Y)}\sum_{p\le Y}\nu_f(p)=R(f).        \tag{4}
\]
Bad primes, including those introduced by repeated factors, do not affect this limit. At a fixed $n$ the sample space is finite, so averaging (4) over its $2^{n-1}$ members is legitimate. From (2),
\[
\Pr(F_n\text{ reducible})\le\mathbb E R(F_n)-1.                 \tag{5}
\]
Thus control of an averaged prime-root count down to $1+o(1)$ would suffice.

This reduction does **not** permit interchanging the limits in $n$ and $Y$ without uniform estimates. That uniformity is a central analytic difficulty, not a consequence of the elementary orbit identity. In particular, evaluation at a fixed $a\in\mathbb F_p$ is an affine random walk: with $X_0=1$ and
\[
X_{j+1}=aX_j+\xi_{n-1-j}\quad(0\le j\le n-2),
\qquad F_n(a)=aX_{n-1}+1,
\]
one has $\mathbb E\nu_{F_n}(p)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb F_p}\Pr(aX_{n-1}+1=0)$. Treating these probabilities as exactly $1/p$ would assume the mixing that has to be proved. At $a=0$ the evaluation is always 1. This root-count/random-walk connection, with effective prime estimates under hypotheses on Dedekind zeta functions, is part of Breuillard–Varjú's method [BV, §1.3]; (2)–(5) isolate the bookkeeping in this endpoint-fixed model.

## 3. Why a polynomial rather than exponential error is unavoidable

There is an exact unconditional obstruction. Put $t_n=\Pr(F_n(-1)=0)$. For $n=2m$, the random even-position sum $E$ has law $\operatorname{Bin}(m-1,1/2)$ and the odd-position sum $O$ has law $\operatorname{Bin}(m,1/2)$, independently. Since $F_{2m}(-1)=2+E-O$,
\[
t_{2m}=2^{-(2m-1)}\binom{2m-1}{m+1}.                          \tag{6}
\]
To see this without an asymptotic approximation, $O+(m-1-E)$ has the binomial law with $2m-1$ trials, and the zero condition makes it $m+1$. Out-of-range binomial coefficients are zero, so the formula also covers $n=2$.

For $n=2m+1$, the endpoints cancel and $E,O$ are independent $\operatorname{Bin}(m,1/2)$ variables. Vandermonde's identity gives
\[
t_{2m+1}=2^{-2m}\sum_{r=0}^{m}\binom mr^2
       =2^{-2m}\binom{2m}{m}.                                  \tag{7}
\]
Stirling's formula in each parity gives
\[
t_n\sim\sqrt{\frac2{\pi n}}.
\]
Since $T+1$ is a proper factor whenever it divides $F_n$,
\[
\Pr(F_n\text{ reducible})\ge t_n.                              \tag{8}
\]
Thus an exponentially small total reducibility probability would contradict an explicit feature of the literal model.

## 4. How the conditional theorem removes the remaining factors

Theorem 2(1) of [BV] applies with the interior law $\mu=(\delta_0+\delta_1)/2$: its squared $\ell^2$ norm is $1/2<1-\tau$, for instance with $\tau=1/4$. The deterministic leading and constant coefficients are expressly allowed. Assume RH for the Dedekind zeta functions of $\mathbb Q$ and all fields $\mathbb Q(a)$ generated by roots of the binary polynomials under consideration. Then, outside an event of probability
\[
\exp(-c_1\sqrt n/\log n),
\]
their theorem gives
\[
F_n=\Phi P,\qquad P\text{ irreducible},\qquad
\deg\Phi\le K\sqrt n,
\]
where $\Phi$ is a product of cyclotomic polynomials and a power of $T$. The power of $T$ is absent because $F_n(0)=1$.

Intersect this event with the unconditional small-divisor event in (1). Once $K\sqrt n\le\theta n$, a nonconstant $\Phi$ would itself be a forbidden divisor. Hence $\Phi=1$ and $F_n$ is irreducible. The union bound proves, under the stated Dedekind-RH hypothesis,
\[
\Pr(F_n\text{ reducible})
 \le n^{-c}+\exp(-c_1\sqrt n/\log n)\longrightarrow0.           \tag{9}
\]
This combination displays exactly how a linear exclusion supplies the missing step in the conditional “one large factor” theorem. It does not remove that theorem's RH assumption. The larger compositum-field hypotheses of [BV, Theorem 2(2)], used for a Galois-group conclusion, are unnecessary here.

The more refined cyclotomic analysis in [BV, Corollary 4] gives the sharper credited result
\[
\Pr(F_n\text{ reducible})=\sqrt{\frac2{\pi n}}+O(n^{-1})
\]
under Dedekind RH. In conjunction with (6)–(8), it identifies $T+1$ as the leading obstruction. We have derived the exact parity formulas, not independently reproved the paper's uniform bounds for all higher cyclotomic factors.

## Scope, prior comparison and audit

The fresh official prompt and canonical metadata list no earlier contributions. The work here is a problem-specific reconstruction of the orbit-count reduction, its proper-power boundary, the exact linear obstruction and the intersection of two deep factorization regimes. Every quoted probabilistic estimate is credited to its authors. No finite simulation, new irreducibility record, or unconditional probability-one conclusion is claimed. The remaining gap is unconditional uniform control of the large-factor obstruction. The proof-only author check covered the endpoint conditioning, distinct versus repeated factors, both parities, fixed-$n$ limit exchange, and the precise root-field RH assumptions; no program was executed for this report.
