# Specified-point smooth closing for Hamiltonian surface diffeomorphisms

**Problem:** TOP-3432

**Claim type:** Partial result

**Exact scope:** For every smooth Hamiltonian diffeomorphism of a closed oriented symplectic surface and every specified point p, arbitrarily C-infinity-close Hamiltonian perturbations make p periodic; hence every finite-C^r neighborhood of that smooth input also admits such a perturbation.

The literal problem asks that the specified nonwandering point \(p\), rather than an unspecified nearby point, become periodic. Here is a substantial established regime with that quantifier retained: let \((S,\omega)\) be a closed oriented smooth surface, let \(f\in\operatorname{Ham}(S,\omega)\) be smooth, and fix any \(p\in S\). There are smooth Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms \(g_j\to f\) in \(C^\infty\) with \(g_j^{m_j}(p)=p\) for some positive integers \(m_j\). In particular this answers the prompt for every prescribed finite \(C^r\) neighborhood of such a smooth input.

The deep input is Asaoka–Irie, Theorem 1.1: for each nonempty open \(U\subset S\), smooth Hamiltonian perturbations tending to \(f\) have a periodic point in \(U\). This is a published theorem, not a new general closing lemma. The conversion to the prompt's specified-point conclusion needs an additional argument.

Choose a Darboux chart about \(p\), with \(p\) at the origin and area form \(dx\wedge dy\). Take neighborhoods \(U_j\) of \(p\) whose chart diameters tend to zero. Metrizability of the smooth topology on a compact manifold and the cited theorem let us choose \(f_j\in\operatorname{Ham}(S,\omega)\) with \(f_j\to f\) smoothly and periodic points \(q_j\in U_j\). Explicitly, take the perturbation from the theorem for \(U_j\) inside the \(j\)-th member of a decreasing neighborhood basis of \(f\). Write \(v_j\) for the coordinates of \(q_j\), so \(v_j\to0\).

Choose one fixed smooth cutoff \(\chi\), supported in the chart and equal to one on a fixed smaller ball about zero. For all sufficiently large \(j\), that smaller ball contains the segment from \(0\) to \(v_j\). With the convention \(\iota_{X_H}\omega=-dH\), define, in the chart,
\[
 H_j(x,y)=\chi(x,y)\big((v_j)_y x-(v_j)_x y\big),
\]
and extend by zero. On that segment the Hamiltonian vector field is exactly the constant vector \(v_j\). Its time-one map \(\psi_j\) therefore sends \(p\) to \(q_j\). Because the cutoff is fixed, every derivative of \(H_j\) is bounded by a constant (depending on the derivative order) times \(|v_j|\). Consequently \(H_j\to0\) in every smooth seminorm and \(\psi_j\to\mathrm{id}\) in \(C^\infty\), by smooth dependence of flows on a compact manifold.

Set \(g_j=\psi_j^{-1}f_j\psi_j\). Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms form a group, so \(g_j\) is Hamiltonian. Composition and inversion are continuous in the smooth diffeomorphism topology, giving \(g_j\to f\). If \(f_j^{m_j}(q_j)=q_j\), then
\[
 g_j^{m_j}(p)=\psi_j^{-1}f_j^{m_j}\psi_j(p)
            =\psi_j^{-1}(q_j)=p .
\]
Discarding finitely many initial indices causes no difficulty.

This statement even permits every \(p\in S\). That agrees with the nonwandering hypothesis: an area-preserving map of a compact area surface has no wandering open set. Indeed, if \(f^n(U)\cap U\) were empty for all \(n>0\), invertibility would make all forward iterates of a nonempty open \(U\) pairwise disjoint, contradicting their equal positive areas and the finite total area.

The proof keeps the perturbation Hamiltonian and controls all derivatives. The period may grow and is not prescribed. The imported theorem requires a smooth Hamiltonian input on a surface. Merely observing \(C^\infty\) convergence does not establish the full conjecture for nonsmooth \(C^r\) inputs, for arbitrary diffeomorphisms, in higher dimensions, or for the flow question. Those portions are left unresolved here. The contribution is the precisely sourced substantial surface regime and its explicit quantifier-preserving deduction, with no novelty claim for the underlying theorem.
