# A complete representation-theoretic certificate for the n+1-form case

Claim type: **Partial result**.

For **every**$n\ge1$ and degree sequence$a_1,\ldots,a_n,d\ge1$, the formula in the prompt holds for$r=n+1$. This classical case is recorded by Fröberg–Lundqvist[1, Section2] as a consequence of Stanley's work. We give a concrete maximal-rank certificate valid for every degree sequence, rather than a finite generic-coefficient test. The universal lower-bound input is credited explicitly.

Let
\[
 T=\mathbb C[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/(x_1^{a_1},\ldots,x_n^{a_n}),\quad
 L=x_1+\cdots+x_n,\quad S=\sum_i(a_i-1).
\]
We claim multiplication by$L^d:T_j\to T_{j+d}$ has rank$\min(\dim T_j,\dim T_{j+d})$ for every$j,d$. Here is a representation-theoretic proof of that claim. On the factor$V_a=\mathbb C[x]/(x^a)$ define
\[
 E x^j=x^{j+1},\quad F x^j=j(a-j)x^{j-1},\quad
 Hx^j=(2j-a+1)x^j.
\]
Terms outside$0\le j<a$ are zero. Direct computation, including endpoints, gives$[H,E]=2E$, $[H,F]=-2F$, $[E,F]=H$. Thus these operators define the standard irreducible$\mathfrak{sl}_2$ representation of dimension$a$; for$a=1$ it is the trivial representation.

The tensor product$T=\bigotimes_iV_{a_i}$ has raising operator$E=\sum_i x_i=L$ and weight$2j-S$ in degree$j$. Finite-dimensional complex$\mathfrak{sl}_2$ representations split into irreducibles. In each irreducible the nonzero weight spaces form a single raising string, one-dimensional at successive weights; in terms of degree its support is an interval centered at$S/2$. All these intervals are nested because they have the same center. Therefore the number of strings meeting both$j$ and$j+d$ is the smaller of the numbers meeting each endpoint. On each string the$d$th raising map between these endpoints is nonzero. This proves the maximal-rank assertion. It also proves that$h_j=\dim T_j$ is symmetric about$S/2$ and unimodal.

Consequently the explicit quotient$T/(L^d)$ has Hilbert function
\[
 b_j=\max(h_j-h_{j-d},0),\qquad
 \sum_jh_jt^j=\prod_{i=1}^n(1+t+\cdots+t^{a_i-1}), \tag{1}
\]
where$h_j=0$ outside$0\le j\le S$. The coefficients$h_j-h_{j-d}$ are nonnegative for$j\le(S+d)/2$ and nonpositive thereafter, by symmetry and unimodality. In particular once a negative coefficient occurs, a later coefficient cannot become positive. Thus the coefficientwise positive part in(1) is exactly the prompt's truncation from the first **negative** coefficient, including any intervening zeros. We obtain
\[
 H_{T/(L^d)}(t)=\left[\frac{\prod_{i=1}^n(1-t^{a_i})(1-t^d)}{(1-t)^n}\right]_+. \tag{2}
\]

To pass from this special tuple to generic forms one needs two directions, not merely an example. The ranks of the degreewise multiplication matrices are maximal on Zariski open sets, so the generic quotient Hilbert function is no larger than this specialized one. Only finitely many degrees need be considered: the specialization is zero after degree$S$, and surjectivity in one degree forces zero in all higher degrees for a homogeneous quotient generated in nonnegative degrees. The finite intersection of the resulting open conditions is nonempty. In the reverse direction use the established general lower bound recorded in[1, Section2]: for any ideal generated by forms of the indicated degrees, its Hilbert series is coefficientwise at least the conjectured truncated series. This is a credited theorem, not an unproved inference from semicontinuity. With(2) the two inequalities coincide, proving the generic formula.

As an explicit all-dimensional slice, if all$n+1$forms are quadrics then
\[
 H(t)=[(1-t^2)(1+t)^n]_+,
 \qquad b_j=\max\{\binom nj-\binom n{j-2},0\}.
\]
Binomial coefficients outside$0,\ldots,n$are zero. For example$n=4$gives$1+4t+5t^2$; the zero coefficient in degree3 is retained before truncation. This example follows from the uniform proof, not a numerical rank search.

The argument depends on characteristic zero through complete reducibility and the nonvanishing of the raising-string constants. It must not be copied verbatim to positive characteristic. It supplies every degree sequence when$r=n+1$, and does not establish the unrestricted conjecture for$r\ge n+2$.
