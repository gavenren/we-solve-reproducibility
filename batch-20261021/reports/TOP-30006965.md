# A concrete entanglement obstruction and its exact prime-order boundary

Claim type: **Literature report**.

The literal conjecture assumes the corrected joint constant $C_E>0$. The following classical example, credited in Zywina's paper to N. Jones, explains why that hypothesis cannot be replaced by the absence of rational torsion or by separate positive local avoidance probabilities. It is not a counterexample to the stated positive-constant conjecture.

Consider
\[
 E:\quad y^2=x^3+9x+18.
\]
Zywina §1.1 records that this non-CM curve is not $\mathbb Q$-isogenous to a curve with nontrivial rational torsion, but its corrected constant for prime orders is zero. We give the exact obstruction and check the small-prime boundary:
\[
 \{p:\ E\text{ has good reduction at }p,\ \#E(\mathbb F_p)
       \text{ is prime}\}=\{5\}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The no-isogenous-torsion assertion is a credited source fact; the proof of (1) below does not require it.

The polynomial $f(X)=X^3+9X+18$ has discriminant
\[
 -4\cdot9^3-27\cdot18^2=-11664=-2^4 3^6.
 \tag{2}
\]
The Weierstrass discriminant is16times(2), so every prime $p>3$ is good. At2 and3 the displayed integral curve is bad; Zywina records conductor $2^4 3^4$, confirming these are exactly the bad primes.

If $p\equiv3\pmod4$, the discriminant of $f$ is nonsquare modulo $p$. A separable cubic over a finite field has nonsquare discriminant precisely when Frobenius permutes its three roots oddly. The odd permutations in $S_3$ are transpositions; therefore exactly one root is rational over $\mathbb F_p$. That root supplies a nonzero2-torsion point of $E(\mathbb F_p)$, so its order is even. This uses the discriminant square class, not a guess about individual roots.

If $p\equiv1\pmod4$, choose $i\in\mathbb F_p$ with $i^2=-1$. The point
\[
 P=(-3,6i)
\]
lies on $E$, since $f(-3)=-36=(6i)^2$. It has order3. Indeed its tangent slope is
$(3(-3)^2+9)/(12i)=-3i$, so the doubling formula gives $x(2P)=-3$ and $y(2P)=-6i$. Thus $2P=-P$, and $P\ne O$; the denominators and $6i$ are nonzero for $p>3$. Consequently3 divides the group order.

These two cases exhaust all good odd primes:
\[
 2\mid\#E(\mathbb F_p)\quad(p\equiv3\bmod4),\qquad
 3\mid\#E(\mathbb F_p)\quad(p\equiv1\bmod4).
 \tag{3}
\]
For $p\ge11$, Hasse's lower bound $(\sqrt p-1)^2>3$ makes the divisible order composite. At $p=7$ the even order is at least4. At $p=5$, direct substitution gives affine points only at $x=2$, with two values of $y$; adding $O$ gives order3. This proves (1) without a finite search being extrapolated.

The joint obstruction is stronger than a torsion obstruction at one fixed prime. Zywina computes the individual divisibility densities
$\vartheta_2=2/3$, $\vartheta_3=3/4$ and the joint density $\vartheta_6=5/12$. Thus each single-level avoidance density is positive, but
\[
 1-\vartheta_2-\vartheta_3+\vartheta_6=0.
\]
The source attributes this to the common subfield $\mathbb Q(i)$ of the2- and3-division fields. Equations(2)–(3) make the mechanism explicit: the two Frobenius possibilities on this common quadratic field force different small prime divisors. In particular every element of the joint level6 image has $\det(I-g)$ divisible by2or3. This proves $\delta_E(6)=0$, hence $C_E=0$ directly from the prompt's joint definition. A product of positive marginal factors would miss it.

The same geometric obstruction survives every number-field base change. If $K$ is any number field and $\mathfrak p$ has residue characteristic greater than3, its residue field $k$ has odd cardinality. If $-1$ is square in $k$, the explicit point $P$ gives3-torsion. Otherwise (2) and the finite-field Frobenius argument give2-torsion. Hence
\[
 \gcd(\#E(k),6)>1
\]
at every such prime ideal. Hasse shows that a prime-order reduction then requires $\#k\le(\sqrt3+1)^2$, leaving only finitely many prime ideals of each fixed $K$. This extension uses no linear-disjointness hypothesis on $K$ and $\mathbb Q(i)$.

The prior contribution reconstructs general matrix counts, the corrected constant, a toy entanglement example and the moving-sieve gap. Here the added content is an actual elliptic curve, a complete torsion/discriminant proof with the exact prime-order exception, and its field-extension persistence. None supplies the fixed-curve asymptotic when $C_E>0$; that remains the selected problem.
