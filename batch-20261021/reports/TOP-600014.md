# A universal obstruction to quadratic disc parametrizations

For a smooth map$f:U\to\mathbb R^7$, with$U\subset\mathbb R^3$ open, the two affine tangent3-planes at distinct parameters$u,v$ have affine span of dimension seven exactly when
\[
\Delta(u,v)=\det[Df_u\mid Df_v\mid f(v)-f(u)]\ne0.
\]
The columns are three derivatives at each point and one displacement. This criterion is equivalent to the literal maximal affine-span condition, including disjointness and absence of common parallel tangent directions. It is useful before separately verifying embedding and regularity.

No polynomial map of total degree at most two can satisfy it on an open3-disc. The exact quadratic midpoint identity is
\[
f(v)-f(u)=\tfrac12(Df_u+Df_v)(v-u).
\]
It holds coordinatewise by expansion of constant, linear and quadratic terms. The last determinant column is therefore in the span of its first six, so$\Delta(u,v)=0$ for every pair. The argument does not assume a graph representation, rank of the derivative or nonsingularity of a bilinear term; it rules out every degree-two polynomial parametrization on an open parameter set. More generally, if the restriction to any parameter line segment is a quadratic vector polynomial, that segment contains failed pairs, because its directional endpoint derivatives already give the same identity. A successful polynomial ansatz must have genuinely higher order on every parameter line segment.

A third-order expansion sharpens this diagnostic. Fix$u$ and a unit vector$\omega$, and write$v=u+t\omega$. Suppose$f$ is$C^3$ in a neighborhood. Subtract the first derivative block from the second, and subtract$\tfrac12(Df_u+Df_v)t\omega$ from the displacement column. Taylor's formula gives
\[
Df_v-Df_u=tD^2f_u(\omega,\cdot)+O(t^2),
\quad
f(v)-f(u)-\tfrac12(Df_u+Df_v)t\omega
=-\tfrac{t^3}{12}D^3f_u(\omega,\omega,\omega)+o(t^3).
\]
Multilinearity of the determinant then yields
\[
\Delta(u,u+t\omega)=-\frac{t^6}{12}
\det[Df_u\mid D^2f_u(\omega,\cdot)\mid D^3f_u(\omega,\omega,\omega)]+o(t^6).
\]
The three columns in the middle block are the second derivative applied to$\omega$ and each coordinate basis vector. The sign and factor$1/12$ follow from$1/6-1/4=-1/12$ in the last column. Thus cubic terms enter first at degree six in this pair determinant, rather than as an arbitrary small quadratic perturbation.

For a compact parameter set$K$ whose neighborhood lies in$U$, suppose the absolute value of the displayed jet determinant is bounded below by$c>0$ for every$u\in K$ and every unit$\omega$. Uniform Taylor remainders and compactness give$\delta>0$ such that$\Delta(u,v)\ne0$ whenever$u,v\in K$ and$0<\|u-v\|<\delta$. This is a sufficient near-diagonal condition only. Pairs farther apart, injectivity, and rank of the map must still be checked for a full disc construction. Conversely a zero leading coefficient does not imply a failed pair: higher-order terms can decide it. These quantifiers prevent turning a local jet diagnostic into the desired global existence claim.

There is no conflict with Ghomi–Tabachnikov's quadratic construction for spheres. Their domain is a sphere in a higher-dimensional ambient parameter space, and its tangent vectors are constrained to the sphere. The difference$v-u$ need not be a tangent vector at either endpoint. Our midpoint identity uses all coordinate directions of an open affine3-dimensional parameter domain; it cannot be applied by replacing those directions with sphere tangent spaces. Nor does their closed-manifold lower bound for embeddings exclude a disc with boundary.
