# A balanced product criterion with polynomial loss

**Claim type: Literature report; conditional reduction.** If there are constants \(C\ge0\) and \(n_0\) such that
\[
 R(m+n,m+n)\ge
 \frac{R(m,m)R(n,n)}{(m+n)^C}
 \quad\text{whenever }n_0\le m\le n\le2m,
 \tag{1}
\]
then the limit asked in the prompt exists. **We do not prove(1).** We prove the implication, with an explicit error estimate, using the balanced-merge idea behind near-subadditive convergence theorems of de Bruijn–Erdős and Füredi–Ruzsa [1]. Requiring(1) only for comparable indices and allowing polynomial loss is a genuine weakening of the earlier attempt's bounded-defect all-pairs condition.

Write \(a_n=\log R(n,n)\). The elementary Ramsey upper bound \(R(n,n)\le\binom{2n-2}{n-1}\le4^{n-1}\), and positivity, bound \(a_n/n\) between0 and \(\log4\). Equation(1) says
\[
 a_{m+n}\ge a_m+a_n-C\log(m+n)
 \quad(n_0\le m\le n\le2m).
 \tag{2}
\]
The following proof works for any real sequence with bounded slopes satisfying(2).

Fix an integer \(k\ge\max(3,n_0)\). For every sufficiently large \(N\), write
\[
 N=(q-1)k+\beta,\qquad q=\lfloor N/k\rfloor,\quad k\le\beta<2k.
\]
Start with \(q-1\) blocks of weight \(k\) and one of weight \(\beta\). Repeatedly merge the two smallest weights, adding their weights. At every stage the largest weight is at most twice the smallest: initially this is true, and if the two smallest are \(u\le v\), every unmerged weight is between \(v\) and \(2u\le2v\), while \(u+v\) is between \(v\) and \(2v\). The same ratio bound is therefore preserved. All merges satisfy the size hypotheses of(2).

If \(\mathcal T\) is the resulting binary merge tree and \(w_z\) the total leaf weight below an internal node \(z\), repeated application of(2) gives
\[
 a_N\ge(q-1)a_k+a_\beta-C\sum_{z\text{ internal}}\log w_z.
 \tag{3}
\]
We bound this sum uniformly in \(N\). Charge the cost \(\log w_z\) to its descendant leaves in proportion to their weights. Per unit weight of a fixed leaf, its charge is \(\sum_z\log w_z/w_z\) along its ancestral chain. Whenever a weight \(w\) is merged, its sibling is at least \(w/2\), so its parent's weight is at least \(3w/2\). A leaf has weight at least \(k\). Its \(j\)-th ancestor therefore has weight at least \(k(3/2)^j\). The function \(\log x/x\) decreases for \(x\ge3\), so the charge per unit weight is at most
\[
 \sum_{j\ge1}\frac{\log(k(3/2)^j)}{k(3/2)^j}
 =\frac{2\log k+6\log(3/2)}{k}.
\]
The equality uses \(\sum_{j\ge1}(2/3)^j=2\) and
\(\sum_{j\ge1}j(2/3)^j=6\). Summing leaf weights, which total \(N\), turns(3) into
\[
 \frac{a_N}{N}\ge
 \frac{(q-1)a_k+a_\beta}{N}
 -\frac{C(2\log k+6\log(3/2))}{k}.
\]
For fixed \(k\), there are finitely many possible \(\beta\); hence the first term tends to \(a_k/k\). It follows that
\[
 \liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{a_N}{N}\ge
 \frac{a_k}{k}-\frac{C(2\log k+6\log(3/2))}{k}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Choose \(k\to\infty\) along a subsequence attaining the bounded limsup. The error tends to0, so liminf is at least limsup. Thus \(a_N/N\) converges and exponentiating proves the claimed implication.

This argument includes thresholds \(n_0>1\), avoids merging a large index with1, and controls accumulated polynomial losses without incorrectly using a linear chain of merges. A linear chain would charge \(O(N\log N)\) error rather than the \(O(N\log k/k)\) error needed in(4).

The broader theorem in [1] allows a nondecreasing additive error \(f(n)\) with \(\sum f(n)/n^2<\infty\), even for a fixed comparable-index ratio greater than1. Its summability hypothesis is essential to that statement and must not be dropped. Our proof above needs only the explicitly stated logarithmic-error case and proves it directly. It neither establishes a new Ramsey product construction nor identifies the numerical limit.

[1] Zoltán Füredi and Imre Z. Ruzsa, *Nearly subadditive sequences*, arXiv:1810.11723v1, Sections2 and4–6, especially the near-subadditivity result with its summability condition: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.11723
