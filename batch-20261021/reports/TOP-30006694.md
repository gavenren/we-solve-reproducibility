# A fully charged random-walk 3-SAT upper bound

Problem: TOP-30006694  
Claim type: Partial result  
Author: /root/hundred_algorithms

**Exact scope.** A Schöning-style one-sided randomized3SAT algorithm with O((4/3)^n poly(|F|,n)) worst-case bit time and error below1/3, proved via an explicit6n-step change-of-measure calculation and bounded fair-bit sampling. This algorithmic upper bound is credited and does not refute the prompt's deterministic positive-rate ETH.

Normalize a3-CNF by removing tautologies and duplicate literals. An empty clause proves unsatisfiability; an empty conjunction is satisfiable. For n>=1, a trial starts from a uniformly random assignment. If it does not satisfy F, choose the first falsified clause under a fixed input order, pad it to three literal occurrences by repetitions if needed, choose an occurrence uniformly from those three, and flip its variable. Stop a trial after6n steps, checking every visited assignment. Repeated literal padding does not change the clause's truth value and preserves a decrease probability of at least1/3. Every declared satisfying assignment is verified directly, so there are no false yes answers.

Fix any satisfying assignment a*. At a current nonsatisfying assignment, each falsified clause has at least one literal made true by a*, so flipping an appropriate occurrence decreases Hamming distance to a*. Conditional on the entire history, the probability of decreasing that distance is at least1/3. Couple the distance to a pessimistic walk Y starting at the same k, with downward probability1/3 and upward probability2/3, absorbing at0. The actual distance is no greater until the algorithm succeeds. One rigorous coupling uses one uniform random choice at each step with the walk's down event contained in the actual down event when the two distances agree. If their gap is at least two, their next moves cannot reverse the ordering. Both distances have the same parity until absorption. Thus success of Y by a given time implies that the trial has succeeded by then.

**Finite-time hitting probability.** Compare Y with a tilted walk Z whose down probability is2/3 and up probability1/3. A path first hitting zero from k has D-U=k. Its probability under Y divided by its probability under Z is
\[
 (\tfrac{1/3}{2/3})^D(\tfrac{2/3}{1/3})^U=2^{-k}.
\]
This holds for every first-hit path and every finite hitting time, so summing such paths up to time6n gives
\[
 \Pr[Y\text{ hits0 by }6n]=2^{-k}\Pr[Z\text{ hits0 by }6n].
\]
For Z stopped at its hitting time tau, conditional drift before hitting is-1/3. Hence E[Z_{t wedge tau}]=k-E[t wedge tau]/3>=0, so by monotone convergence E[tau]<=3k. Markov then gives Pr[tau>6n]<=k/(2n)<=1/2. A trial starting at distance k therefore succeeds with probability at least2^{-k-1}. The k=0 case succeeds immediately and also satisfies this bound.

The initial k has the Binomial(n,1/2) distribution. Averaging gives a trial-success lower bound
\[
 \frac12\sum_k2^{-n}\binom nk2^{-k}=\frac12(3/4)^n.       \tag{16}
\]
Use R=ceil(4^{n+1}/3^n) independent trials. With ideal uniform choices, the probability of no success is at most exp(-2)<1/6. All repetition counts are computed by integer arithmetic on O(n)-bit numbers; no real-valued running-time oracle is used.

**Bounded random bits and worst-case time.** To choose uniformly among three slots, read two fair bits, accept00,01,10 as the three choices and reject11. Cap the number of such draws per slot choice at B=ceil(log_4(72Rn))+1 and declare this entire execution unsuccessful on exhaustion. There are at most6Rn choices, so the total probability of any exhaustion is at most6Rn4^{-B}<1/12. On the nonexhaustion event, this procedure is exactly coupled to ideal uniform slots. Thus total failure is below1/6+1/12<1/3. It uses O(n) bits/time per slot choice in the worst case, since log R=O(n). Formula evaluation and variable updates are polynomial in the full literal/index encoding length. Overall worst-case bit time is O((4/3)^n poly(|F|,n)), with polynomial working space apart from the read-only input and counters. Unsatisfiable formulas are always correctly rejected after the bounded search; a false no answer is possible only on satisfiable inputs.

This is a conservative-constant reconstruction of the established Schöning random-walk algorithm, not a new fastest3SAT algorithm. The factor4/3 has positive base-two exponent log_2(4/3); it is not a subexponential bound. Moreover ETH here excludes deterministic algorithms of sufficiently small positive rate. This randomized upper bound proves neither a deterministic ETH refutation nor a randomized ETH lower bound. Its value is a fully charged explicit positive algorithm and a finite-time probability proof, distinct from separator and representation obstructions in the earlier attempt.
