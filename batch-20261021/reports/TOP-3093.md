# Exact two-piece perimeter extrema for arbitrary triangles

Let $C$ be any nondegenerate Euclidean triangle. This report determines both extrema in the prompt for$n=2$, including nonsymmetric triangles. Write $p$ for its perimeter, and $m_1,m_2,m_3$ for its medians. At vertex$i$, let $u_i,v_i>0$ be the lengths of its adjacent sides and let $\theta_i\in(0,\pi)$ be the included angle. Define
\[
x_i=\min\left(1,\max\left(\tfrac14,\frac{v_i}{2u_i}\right)\right),
\qquad
d_i=u_i^2x_i+\frac{v_i^2}{4x_i}-u_iv_i\cos\theta_i.
\]
Then the exact values are
\[
P_{\min}(C,2)=p+2\sqrt{\min_i d_i},\qquad
P_{\max}(C,2)=p+2\max_i m_i.
\]
The proof also gives attaining cut segments. No symmetry is assumed; optimization over all$n$ and arbitrary convex bodies remains open in this report.

First, two convex pieces with nonempty disjoint interiors must be separated by a line. Apply finite-dimensional convex separation to their open interiors; taking closures puts the two pieces in opposite closed half-planes. Since their union is$C$, each piece is exactly$C$ intersected with its assigned half-plane. Thus every admissible partition is a straight chord bisection. If its chord length is$\ell$, the exterior boundary contributes$p$ once and the chord contributes$2\ell$, giving $P=p+2\ell$.

Every chord in a triangle joins two of its sides. Those sides share some vertex$i$. Choose the corner triangle bounded by them and the chord. Equal areas force this corner triangle to have half the original area, regardless of which piece it is called. Let the chord endpoints be at fractions$t$ and$s$ along the two adjacent sides from vertex$i$. Then $0<t,s\le1$ and area scaling gives$ts=1/2$. Consequently $1/2\le t\le1$ and$s=1/(2t)$. Conversely each such parameter gives an admissible equal-area chord; boundary cases include a cut through another triangle vertex.

By the cosine law, its squared length is
\[
\ell_i(t)^2=u_i^2t^2+\frac{v_i^2}{4t^2}-u_iv_i\cos\theta_i.
\]
Set$x=t^2\in[1/4,1]$. The variable part $u_i^2x+v_i^2/(4x)$ has strictly positive second derivative there and derivative $u_i^2-v_i^2/(4x^2)$. Its constrained minimum occurs at the clipped stationary value$x_i$ displayed above. Taking the minimum over the three choices of vertex proves the lower formula and attainment. For example, when$u_i/2\le v_i\le2u_i$, the stationary value is feasible and$d_i=u_iv_i(1-\cos\theta_i)$.

A convex function on a closed interval attains its maximum at an endpoint. At$t=1$ the chord runs from one original vertex to the midpoint of its opposite side, hence is a median; the same holds at$t=1/2$. Across the three choices of corner, the endpoint candidates are exactly the three medians, with repetitions. Therefore the longest equal-area chord is a longest median, proving the upper formula and its attaining partition.

As a boundary and normalization check, an equilateral triangle of side$a$ has shortest half-area chord$a/\sqrt2$ and longest chord$\sqrt3a/2$. The resulting perimeter sums are $3a+\sqrt2a$ and$3a+\sqrt3a$, respectively. They differ although each piece always has the same area. For a non-equilateral triangle the clipped formula retains the endpoints when a formal stationary point lies beyond a side; omitting this clipping would produce a nonexistent cut.

The complete earlier attempt solves two-piece partitions of centrally symmetric bodies and rectangles. A nondegenerate triangle is not centrally symmetric. Its area-product constraint and three constrained optimizations are additional scope, and no greedy assertion for$n>2$ is made. All calculations are exact analytic arguments; no numerical optimization or computer search is used.
