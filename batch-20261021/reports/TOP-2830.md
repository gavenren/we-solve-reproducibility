# A terminating genus-two surface-isotopy algorithm, with the point at infinity retained

**Problem:** TOP-2830

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** Baroni's oriented genus-two classification algorithm decides ambient isotopy for connected tame genus-two surfaces in R³ after orienting by the unbounded complementary side. The passage from S³ to R³ and compatible gluing requirement are explained. No arbitrary-genus or disconnected-input decision is claimed.

For the substantial genus-two case the literal decision question has an affirmative answer. Baroni's Theorem 4.1 gives a terminating algorithm for isotopy of two **oriented** genus-two surfaces embedded in \(S^3\). Remark 4.2 also states the unoriented variant. The algorithm includes arbitrary knotted genus-two embeddings, not only Heegaard surfaces or boundaries of handlebodies.

For the Euclidean-space input one must use the oriented version carefully. Compactify \(\mathbb R^3\) by a point \(\infty\). Give each surface the orientation whose positive complementary side contains \(\infty\), using the fixed ambient orientation. Rational polyhedral input can be placed in a finite triangulation of a large ball, capped by another ball to obtain the triangulated pair in \(S^3\). The unbounded side can be identified from that outer ball. Apply the oriented-surface algorithm with these induced orientations.

An ambient isotopy in \(\mathbb R^3\) preserves the unbounded component and hence gives such an oriented isotopy in \(S^3\). Conversely suppose the oriented algorithm returns yes. Its isotopy gives an orientation-preserving endpoint homeomorphism \(f\) of \(S^3\) taking the first surface to the second and its positive side to the positive side. Therefore \(f(\infty)\) and \(\infty\) lie in the same component of the complement of the second surface. Move the former point to the latter along a path in that component, by an ambient isotopy supported away from the surface. Composing gives a homeomorphism \(g\) fixing \(\infty\) and carrying the surfaces to one another.

One can choose an isotopy from the identity to \(g\) fixing \(\infty\) throughout. Indeed point evaluation on the orientation-preserving homeomorphism group is the isotopy-extension fibration over \(S^3\). Its base is simply connected, and the total group is connected in the tame three-sphere category. The exact sequence on components therefore says its point stabilizer is connected. Equivalently the track of \(\infty\) in any isotopy can be contracted and removed by isotopy extension. Restricting this point-fixed isotopy to \(S^3\setminus\{\infty\}\) proves the required Euclidean ambient isotopy. Thus keeping the unbounded-side orientation makes the two decision problems agree.

The theorem also addresses the compatibility issue emphasized in the prompt. In §4.1, if the complementary closures are \(M_i,N_i\), the test is for homeomorphisms \(f:M_1\to M_2\) and \(g:N_1\to N_2\) whose boundary restrictions agree up to isotopy. The mapping-class and compression algorithms enforce this matching; independently recognizing the two complementary manifolds would omit it. Genus two already admits embeddings with neither side a handlebody, so restricting to knot exteriors would also lose valid inputs.

This is a credited terminating procedure, not a polynomial-time estimate. Its proof is imported rather than implemented or rerun here. The restriction to connected genus-two surfaces is essential to the report: the arbitrary-genus and disconnected-placement questions are not answered by this theorem.
