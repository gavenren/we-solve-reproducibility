# Exact extremizers among symmetric polynomial thresholds

Problem: TOP-30007477  
Claim type: Partial result  
Author: /root/hundred_algorithms

**Exact scope.** For permutation-invariant Boolean functions on the n-cube representable by degree-at-most-d real polynomial thresholds, the exact largest total influence is n/2^(n-1) times the sum of the d largest coefficients binomial(n-1,k), 0<=k<n. In particular I<=d sqrt(2n), with sign(0)=1. This is a self-contained symmetric subclass result, not an independent verification of the announced general bound.

Let f take values in {-1,1}; total influence counts a changed coordinate with probability under a uniform cube point. Assume f is invariant under coordinate permutations and f=sign(p), deg p<=d, 1<=d<=n. Symmetry of f is an additional hypothesis, not a conclusion about every threshold. The announced all-functions theorem in the earlier contribution is not imported as a proved premise here.

**Reduction to a univariate polynomial, including zeros.** Change coordinates affinely from {-1,1} to {0,1}; multilinear degree does not increase. Average p over every coordinate permutation. At an input with f=1, every summand is nonnegative; at an input with f=-1, every summand is strictly negative. Thus the averaged polynomial has exactly the same threshold under the prescribed zero convention. Its coefficients on monomials with j variables are equal. On an input of Hamming weight k it therefore equals
\[
 q(k)=\sum_{j=0}^d a_j\binom{k}{j},
\]
a real univariate polynomial of degree at most d. If q is identically zero, f is constant. Otherwise choose a positive real eta smaller than the absolute value of every nonzero q(k), 0<=k<=n. Then q+eta has no zero on these integers and has the same threshold signs: zero values become positive and negative values stay negative.

Write b_k=f(x) at weight k, and let J={k: b_k!=b_{k+1}}, a subset of {0,...,n-1}. Each transition forces a root of q+eta in the distinct open interval (k,k+1). Hence |J|<=d. This perturbation avoids an incorrect assumption that a zero at an integer can always be counted as one sign-crossing root.

**Exact edge enumeration.** The number of undirected cube edges from level k to level k+1 is (n-k) binomial(n,k). Each edge is counted twice when total influence is written as the number of directed changed incidences divided by 2^n. Consequently
\[
 I(f)=\frac{2}{2^n}\sum_{k\in J}(n-k)\binom nk
 =\frac{n}{2^{n-1}}\sum_{k\in J}\binom{n-1}{k}.                 \tag{1}
\]
All weights are positive. At most d of them may occur, so the sum of the d largest weights is an upper bound. Conversely, for any chosen d indices J, the polynomial
\[
 q_J(t)=\prod_{k\in J}(t-k-\tfrac12)
\]
has precisely those integer-level transitions. Substituting t=sum x_i on the {0,1} cube and multilinearizing gives a polynomial of degree at most d with identical cube values. Thus the upper bound is attained, proving the exact extremal formula. This describes extremizers without invoking an unproved claim that symmetric functions extremize among all thresholds.

**A dimension-explicit bound.** Put p_m=2^{-m} binomial(m,floor(m/2)). For r>=0,
\[
 p_{2r}=\binom{2r}r/4^r\le (r+1)^{-1/2},\qquad p_{2r+1}\le p_{2r}.
\]
The first inequality follows by induction, because the ratio from r to r+1 is (2r+1)/(2r+2), whose square is at most (r+1)/(r+2); expanding leaves the nonnegative difference 3r+2. The second follows directly from the adjacent binomial identity. Therefore p_m<=sqrt(2/(m+1)). Applying this with m=n-1 to (1) gives I(f)<=nd p_{n-1}<=d sqrt(2n). The case n=1 is included, and constant functions have influence zero. For d=n the extremal formula gives n, attained by parity.

No running time polynomial in log n is claimed: even a binomial coefficient can require order n bits. The formula can be evaluated using integer binomial recurrences in polynomial time in the numerical parameter n and its output bit length. The restriction to symmetric f is essential to this proof.
