# The exact split higher-rank congruence kernel, including its nontrivial finite case

Claim type: **Literature report**.

Bass–Milnor–Serre [1, Theorem14.1] determine the kernel in the prompt for two entire split families, not merely its finiteness. In the standard arithmetic convention, let $K$ be a global field and $S$ a finite nonempty set of places containing all archimedean places. Put $R=\mathcal O_S$. For
\[
 G=\mathrm{SL}_n\quad(n\ge3),\qquad G=\mathrm{Sp}_{2m}\quad(m\ge2),
\]
their theorem gives a central congruence kernel
\[
 C^S(G)\cong
 \begin{cases}
 \mu(K),&K\text{ totally imaginary and }S\text{ consists exactly of its archimedean places},\\
 1,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases} \tag{1}
\]
Here $\mu(K)$ is the finite group of roots of unity in $K$. This is a credited published theorem, not a new proof of the general conjecture. The source's phrase “$S$ totally imaginary” means exactly the first case, as defined at the start of its §14. In a convention listing only the finite inverted places, the archimedean places must first be restored to $S$ when computing total rank.

Each split local rank is $n-1$ or $m$, so every place contributes positive rank and the total is at least2. Thus (1) directly proves the higher-rank conclusion in all these cases. It also shows why the prompt correctly asks for **finiteness rather than triviality**: for $K=\mathbb Q(i)$, $S$ archimedean, and $G=\mathrm{SL}_n$, the kernel has order4. For a real embedding the kernel vanishes. Neither assertion says anything about anisotropic groups or the rank-one conclusion.

We spell out a useful consequence in the vanishing case. For $K$ a number field with a real embedding, every finite group homomorphism
\[
 f:\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathcal O_K)\longrightarrow F
\]
factors through $\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathcal O_K/I)$ for some nonzero ideal $I$. Indeed the finite-index kernel is open in the profinite topology. By (1) the profinite-to-congruence completion map is an isomorphism of compact Hausdorff groups. The two induced topologies on the dense arithmetic subgroup therefore coincide, so the kernel contains a principal congruence subgroup. This proves factorization; it is not inferred merely from centrality of the kernel. In the totally imaginary case a nontrivial finite kernel prevents this conclusion for all finite quotients.

For completeness the target completion here has a concrete description:
\[
 \widehat{\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathcal O_K)}\cong
 \prod_{\mathfrak p}\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathcal O_{K,\mathfrak p})
 \quad\text{if }K\text{ has a real embedding}. \tag{2}
\]
To identify the congruence completion with the product, the reduction maps to $\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathcal O_K/I)$ are surjective. The finite ring $\mathcal O_K/I$ is a product of local rings by the Chinese remainder theorem. Over a local ring a unimodular column contains a unit; elementary row operations put it into the first coordinate, clear the other entries, and induction reduces a determinant-one matrix to a diagonal matrix. The determinant-one diagonal blocks are elementary, using the usual $2\times2$ identity for $\operatorname{diag}(u,u^{-1})$. Thus the special linear group is generated by elementary matrices, each of which lifts to $\mathcal O_K$. Taking inverse limits and using the Chinese remainder theorem gives the product. Finally (1) identifies that congruence completion with the full profinite completion. The local factors in (2) are completions of rings of integers, not the unrestricted groups over $K_{\mathfrak p}$.

The arithmetic input underlying (1) is substantial. BMS define $E_n(R,I)$ as the normal closure of elementary matrices of level $I$ and prove cofinality among finite-index subgroups. Their Corollary4.3 calculates the quotients $\mathrm{SL}_n(R,I)/E_n(R,I)$ using universal Mennicke symbols. Their §14 passes to the inverse limit; this is the step producing $\mu(K)$ in the imaginary case. We have checked those statements and the §14 deduction, not reproduced all of the intervening reciprocity arguments.

Prior A1 proves abstract finite-defect and centrality criteria and correctly warns that centrality alone does not imply finiteness. The new scope supplies an actual exact arithmetic determination in unbounded ranks and all global fields for two families, together with the explicit quotient/completion consequences. It does not turn those two families into a solution for every absolutely almost simple simply connected group.
