# The degree-two packing theorem with its integer slack exposed

**Claim type: Literature report.** If \(G_1,G_2\) are simple graphs on the same \(n\) vertices, \(\Delta(G_1)\le2\), and
\[
 n\ge3\Delta(G_2)+2,
 \tag{1}
\]
then they pack. This follows from the substantial universality theorem of Aigner and Brandt [1]: an \(n\)-vertex graph of minimum degree at least \((2n-1)/3\) contains every graph on at most \(n\) vertices with maximum degree at most2. The theorem is credited, not reproved. We give the exact translation and a sharp obstruction to lowering its host-degree condition.

Packing means that some bijection of the vertex sets makes the two edge sets disjoint. Put \(b=\Delta(G_2)\), and take the simple complement \(H=\overline{G_2}\). For every vertex,
\[
 d_H(v)=n-1-d_{G_2}(v),\qquad
 \delta(H)=n-1-b.
\]
Under(1),
\[
 n-1-b\ge(2n-1)/3,
\]
since this inequality is equivalent to \(n\ge3b+2\). Aigner–Brandt yields an embedding \(f:G_1\hookrightarrow H\). Both graphs have \(n\) vertices, so this injection is a bijection. Every edge of \(G_1\) maps to a nonedge of \(G_2\), exactly the required packing. The statement also covers paths, isolated vertices and mixtures of cycles; there is no step augmenting an arbitrary degree-two graph to a2-factor, which could introduce unjustified small-component assumptions.

The literal conjectured condition in this class, when \(\Delta(G_1)=2\), is
\[
 3(b+1)\le n.
\]
It implies \(n\ge3b+3\), which is one integer stronger than(1). Hence the whole degree-two class is covered, not only pairs for which both maximum degrees are at most2. If \(\Delta(G_1)<2\),(1) remains a valid sufficient condition, though it is not advertised as the sharp conjectural threshold in those lower-degree cases.

The host theorem's constant has a genuine obstruction. For \(n=3t\), \(t\ge1\), take the complete tripartite graph \(H'=K_{t-1,t,t+1}\). Its minimum degree is \(2t-1=\lceil(2n-1)/3\rceil-1\). A triangle in \(H'\) uses one vertex from every part. Therefore \(t\) vertex-disjoint triangles cannot span \(H'\), because its smallest part has only \(t-1\) vertices. With \(G_1=tK_3\), which has maximum degree2, and \(G_2=\overline{H'}\), no packing exists. Here \(b=t\), and \(n=3b\), so this does **not** contradict the conjecture or(1); it shows why a degree threshold below the universality theorem cannot be assumed.

For \(t=1\), the first tripartite part is empty; the same triangle-factor obstruction holds and the minimum-degree calculation still gives1. For all \(t\ge2\), all three parts are nonempty, yielding an unbounded family. We do not claim that the intervening packing parameter \(n=3b+1\) is settled by this obstruction.

The previous attempt proves the Sauer–Spencer product condition and some low-degree pair cases, but not the full class with one arbitrary degree-two factor and unbounded other degree. Aigner–Brandt supplies this missing structural scope. No algorithm execution or independent reproduction of their full embedding proof is claimed.
