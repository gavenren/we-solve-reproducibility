# The compatible-subdivision argument in Ge’s September2026 geometric-triangulation preprint

**Problem:** TOP-2801

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** Reports the full positive geometric ideal-triangulation theorem claimed in arXiv:2609.27635v1, with a detailed checked reconstruction of its affine compatible-subdivision argument, its termination bound and its application to Epstein–Penner cells. This is a credited preprint report, not a new independently formalized full solution.

The exact target is a compatible decomposition of the given complete finite-volume cusped hyperbolic metric into nondegenerate ideal tetrahedra. Huabin Ge's preprint arXiv:2609.27635v1, dated23September2026, states this in Theorem1.1, including nonorientable manifolds and positive orientation when the manifold is orientable. This is a report of that primary preprint and its actual mathematical argument. No publication, acceptance or computer-formal verification is asserted.

The decisive part is a compatible subdivision theorem, not a claim that one can arbitrarily cone every polyhedron independently. The following reconstruction records why the proposed compatibility mechanism differs from the failed naive construction.

Consider finitely many convex three-dimensional affine polyhedra \(P_i\), with all polygonal faces paired by affine maps, and each reverse pairing the inverse. Put \(v_i,e_i,f_i\) for vertex, edge and face counts. A vertex-height assignment on \(P_i\) determines a regular subdivision by taking lower faces of its lifted vertex hull. Adding an affine function to the heights changes no subdivision. Thus the relevant height-class space is
\[
 H_i=\mathbb R^{V(P_i)}/\operatorname{Aff}(P_i),
 \qquad \dim H_i=v_i-4.
\]
For a paired polygonal face with \(q_\alpha\) vertices, compatibility modulo affine functions imposes at most \(q_\alpha-3\) linear conditions. The restrictions are well-defined on \(H_i\), because composing an affine function with an affine face pairing is affine. The kernel of the resulting linear map is the space of compatible height classes.

Every edge of a polyhedron belongs to two faces, while every face is counted once in a pair. Consequently
\[
 \sum_i e_i=\sum_\alpha q_\alpha,\qquad
 \sum_i f_i=2(\text{number of pairs}).
\]
Using \(v_i-e_i+f_i=2\), the difference between source and target dimensions is
\[
 \sum_i(v_i-4)-\sum_\alpha(q_\alpha-3)
      =\frac12\sum_i(f_i-4).
\]
Each summand is nonnegative, and some is positive if a polyhedron is not a tetrahedron. Therefore, whenever a non-tetrahedral cell remains, a nonzero compatible height class exists [Lemma3.2].

Such a class makes at least one regular subdivision nontrivial: a trivial subdivision would put all lifted vertices of each polyhedron on one affine lower hyperplane, making every local class zero. The restriction property of regular subdivisions ensures that subdivisions agree across the paired faces. The new interior faces are paired by the identity, and the subdivided boundary faces inherit the old affine pairings. Thus the refined cells form another system of the same kind [Proposition3.3].

Termination is an actual finite argument. Fix an original polyhedron. Every three-dimensional descendant cell contains four affinely independent original vertices. Distinct cells of one subdivision cannot contain the same such quadruple, since their intersection would contain the interior of its tetrahedron. The number of descendants is therefore at most the number of independent four-element vertex subsets. A nontrivial refinement strictly increases the total cell count, which has this fixed finite upper bound. Iterating must stop, and at that point all cells are tetrahedra [Theorem3.4]. No new vertex is introduced.

For the hyperbolic application, the source uses affine cells in Minkowski space from the Epstein–Penner light-cone construction. Deck maps act linearly there, so their restrictions to paired faces are affine. This affine statement is not the generally false assertion that arbitrary hyperbolic face maps are affine in one global Klein chart. After compatible subdivision, project radially to the Klein model. If four vertices in one supporting affine hyperplane are affinely independent, their light-cone vectors are linearly independent: applying the functional defining that hyperplane to a linear relation makes its coefficients sum to zero. Hence their radial projections are affinely independent and span a genuine, positive-volume ideal tetrahedron. The tetrahedra subdivide the original geometric cells, so the inherited metric is the original complete metric. Quotienting gives finitely many tetrahedra.

This directly addresses the flatness and compatibility caveats in the official question. It does not replace the original manifold by a finite cover, and it allows identified quotient vertices while keeping the local vertices of each tetrahedron distinct in its geometric lift. For an orientable manifold the local orderings can be chosen to agree with its orientation.

The source contains a proof, rather than an announcement alone; the detailed affine argument above was inspected. Nevertheless its status here remains a credited recent-preprint result. This report claims neither original discovery nor a separate complete verification of every geometric and external input. Any unconditional resolution asserted by the source is the author's theorem, with the stated version and status preserved.
