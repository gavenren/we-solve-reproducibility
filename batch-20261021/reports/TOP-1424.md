# Universally spanning typical trees and polylogarithmic-degree embeddings

**Claim type: Literature report.** Two theorems of Mycroft and Naia give strong forms of tournament universality on substantial tree classes. Almost every labeled oriented tree on $n$ vertices embeds in every tournament on $n$ vertices. Separately, every oriented tree of polylogarithmic maximum degree embeds in every tournament on $(1+\alpha)n$ vertices, for fixed positive $\alpha$ and sufficiently large $n$. These are credited results, with their quantifiers made explicit; they do not resolve Sumner's exact bound for all trees and all orders.

The earlier TOP-1424-A1 proves stars, consistently directed paths, very small orders and the sharp lower obstruction. It records the general sufficiently-large-order Sumner theorem. The typical-tree universal host statement and the growing-degree near-spanning regime below are different, stronger conclusions on restricted classes.

## The exact two inputs

Call an oriented $n$-vertex tree unavoidable if every $n$-vertex tournament contains an orientation-preserving copy of it. Embeddings need not be induced.

Mycroft–Naia, arXiv1609.03393v1, Theorem1.2, states that a uniformly random labeled oriented tree on $n$ vertices is unavoidable with probability tending to one.

Their Theorem1.7 states
\[
\forall\alpha,C>0\ \exists n_0(\alpha,C)\ \forall n\ge n_0:
\quad \Delta(T)\le(\log n)^C,\quad |V(G)|\ge(1+\alpha)n
\ \Longrightarrow\ T\subseteq G
\]
for every oriented $n$-vertex tree $T$ and every tournament $G$. The maximum degree is that of the underlying undirected tree. Both constants are fixed before $n$ grows. This does not allow choosing a parameter $C$ that grows with $n$.

## The random object and the universal quantifier

There are $n^{n-2}$ labeled undirected trees on $[n]$, each with $2^{n-1}$ orientations. Uniform sampling means choosing from these $2^{n-1}n^{n-2}$ labeled oriented trees. The conclusion is
\[
\Pr_T\big[\ \forall\ n\text{-vertex tournaments }G,\ T\subseteq G\ \big]\longrightarrow1.
\]
The universal host quantifier is inside the probability event. This is stronger than saying that an independent random pair $(T,G)$ usually has an embedding. A small failure probability for one random host would not justify a union bound over all $2^{\binom n2}$ hosts.

For every unavoidable $T$, an embedding exists in every larger tournament too: select any $n$ vertices of the larger host. In particular the theorem proves the exact Sumner requirement for a proportion $1-o(1)$ of labeled oriented trees, while even reducing the required host size from $2n-2$ to $n$ on that proportion. It is not a proof for every isomorphism class or for every orientation of one fixed underlying tree.

## What structural input drives the typical-tree theorem

The paper's deterministic Theorem1.4 requires two hypotheses: maximum degree at most $(\log n)^C$, and a fixed positive linear supply of two specified types of disjoint pendant oriented stars. In one type the attachment edge points outward from the pendant star and it contains an out-leaf; in the other the attachment points inward and both an in-leaf and an out-leaf occur.

The paper's Theorem1.6 shows that a random labeled oriented tree has the required structure with a fixed constant $1/250$. Its stated degree estimate also gives the polylogarithmic bound with probability tending to one. Intersecting those two high-probability events and applying the deterministic theorem yields Theorem1.2. Merely assuming a polylogarithmic maximum degree does not yield the exact spanning conclusion without the additional structural hypothesis; it yields the near-spanning conclusion of Theorem1.7.

## Why the all-tree factor-two obstruction remains

An out-star on $n$ vertices needs a host vertex of outdegree at least $n-1$. A regular tournament on $2n-3$ vertices has every outdegree $n-2$, so that star is absent. Thus uniform near-$n$ universality cannot hold for every oriented tree.

This does not conflict with the typical-tree result. For $n\ge3$ there are only $2n$ labeled directed in-stars and out-stars, among $2^{n-1}n^{n-2}$ labeled oriented trees. Their relative frequency tends to zero. Rare obstructions can prevent a uniform statement while leaving a probability-one asymptotic theorem intact.

The source provides no explicit finite verification of every remaining order, and none is claimed here. Its asymptotic theorems and deterministic structural criterion are the exact scope being reported.

Reference: [Richard Mycroft and Tássio Naia, Unavoidable trees in tournaments, Theorems1.2,1.4,1.6,1.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.03393v1).
