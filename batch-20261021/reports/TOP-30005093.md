# The denominator-saving mechanism in a published six-value beta theorem

Claim type: **Literature report**.

Catalan's constant is the individual value $\beta(2)$. Zudilin's published Theorem1 proves the disjunction
\[
\beta(2),\beta(4),\beta(6),\beta(8),\beta(10),\beta(12)
\quad\text{are not all rational}. \tag{1}
\]
We explain the exact arithmetic normalization in that proof and why it cannot be projected to $\beta(2)$ alone. This supplies a rigorous published benchmark for evaluating integral constructions, rather than another bounded decimal or denominator calculation.

The primary construction uses positive forms
\[
r_n=a_{0,n}+\sum_{j=1}^{6}a_{2j,n}\beta(2j),
\qquad a_{i,n}\in\mathbb Q,
\]
along even positive $n$. Its parameters are
\[
s=13,\quad(\eta_0,\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_{13})
=(31,10,10,10,10,10,11,11,11,11,12,12,12,12).
\]
Zudilin's Lemmas2–5 supply positivity, partial fractions, symmetry eliminating odd beta values, and the inclusions
\[
\Phi_n^{-1}d_{11n}^{13-i}a_{i,n}\in\mathbb Z
\quad(i=0,2,\ldots,12),
\qquad d_m=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,m). \tag{2}
\]
The proof compares exponential decay and denominator growth with a strict margin, and obtains
\[
0<\Lambda_n:=\Phi_n^{-1}d_{11n}^{13}r_n\longrightarrow0. \tag{3}
\]
These assertions are credited primary results; we do not claim to have independently certified the paper's numerical optimization.

Here is the complete arithmetic implication of that package. For each even $i$ in (2),
\[
\Phi_n^{-1}d_{11n}^{13}a_{i,n}
=d_{11n}^{i}\bigl(\Phi_n^{-1}d_{11n}^{13-i}a_{i,n}\bigr)
\]
is an integer, including $i=0$. Thus
\[
\Lambda_n\in
\mathbb Z+\mathbb Z\beta(2)+\cdots+\mathbb Z\beta(12).
\tag{4}
\]
If all six constants were rational, choose one positive common denominator $Q$ for them. Then $Q\Lambda_n$ is a positive integer for every allowed $n$, while (3) makes it tend to zero, a contradiction. Positivity supplies nonvanishing; the denominator inclusion supplies integrality. Each is essential.

The arithmetic saving is substantial, rather than a cosmetic rescaling. The normalizing factor $\Phi_n$ arises from prime valuations of partial-fraction coefficients. Replacing the carefully bounded constant-term denominator by a larger lcm can destroy decay after clearing denominators. The paper's simpler Section2 construction yields eight beta values; the optimized Section3 yields the six in (1). The exact change in support is therefore part of the theorem, not an interchangeable choice.

We now isolate the additional work needed to turn any such multi-value family into a proof of the literal prompt. This is an exact elimination statement, independent of the analytic source. Suppose, at one index, five rational forms are available,
\[
L_k=b_{k,0}+b_{k,2}G+
 \sum_{j=2}^{6}b_{k,2j}\beta(2j),\qquad k=1,\ldots,5.
\]
Write $M=(b_{k,2j})_{k=1,\ldots,5;\,j=2,\ldots,6}$. If $M$ is invertible, **no** nonzero rational combination of the five rows eliminates all five unwanted values: the required row vector $u$ must satisfy $uM=0$, forcing $u=0$. With six rows, a nonzero left-kernel vector for the five unwanted columns does exist, but that fact alone still leaves three tasks. The coefficient of $G$ must survive; the resulting form must be nonzero at the actual constants; and the new coefficients, including minors or denominator-clearing factors, must preserve smallness. A determinant argument must check all three. Algebraic elimination by itself controls none of the required size or nonvanishing estimates.

In particular, assuming just $G\in\mathbb Q$ in (4) does not make $\Lambda_n$ rational: the other five beta values remain. This is the exact point where (1) falls short of irrationality of Catalan's constant.
