# Topological rigidity for virtually solvable fundamental groups, with an explicit nilmanifold family

**Problem:** TOP-30006734

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** Every homotopy equivalence between closed aspherical topological manifolds of dimension at least five with virtually solvable fundamental group is homotopic to a homeomorphism, by Wegner’s coefficient Farrell–Jones theorem and Bartels–Lück Proposition0.3; includes an explicit nonabelian nilmanifold family.

The literal target concerns the given homotopy class. There is a published broad regime beyond the hyperbolic and CAT(0) cases discussed in the earlier exploration: if \(M,N\) are closed aspherical topological manifolds of dimension \(n\ge5\), and their fundamental group is virtually solvable, then **every** homotopy equivalence \(f:M\to N\) is homotopic to a homeomorphism. This is a credited consequence of Wegner's theorem, not a new proof of the unrestricted Borel conjecture.

Here “virtually solvable” means that the group has a solvable subgroup of finite index; it does not mean virtually abelian. Wegner's Theorem1.1 proves both the algebraic \(K\)- and \(L\)-theoretic Farrell–Jones conjectures, with coefficients in additive categories, for every virtually solvable group, relative to virtually cyclic subgroups. The coefficient statement is relevant to nonorientable manifolds: their orientation character \(w:G\to\{\pm1\}\) twists the involution used in surgery \(L\)-theory.

To make the application precise rather than suppressing the surgery hypotheses, use Bartels–Lück Proposition0.3. For a torsion-free \(G\), that proposition requires the \(K\)-assembly over \(\mathbb Z\) to be bijective in degrees at most zero and surjective in degree one, together with all-degree \(L^{\langle-\infty\rangle}\)-assembly, permitting the orientation twist. It then gives the Borel conclusion in dimensions at least five, including the statement about each particular homotopy equivalence. Wegner's stronger coefficient theorem supplies every one of those requirements.

The fundamental group of a closed aspherical manifold is torsion-free, as needed. One way to see the relevant finiteness obstruction is to use its finite cohomological dimension over \(\mathbb Z\), coming from the finite-dimensional contractible universal cover. A subgroup of prime order would inherit a finite-length projective resolution of the trivial integral module by restriction. This contradicts the nonzero periodic integral cohomology in arbitrarily high degrees of a cyclic group of prime order. Every nontrivial finite-order element would generate a subgroup containing one of prime order.

In the proof of the cited surgery criterion, the \(K\)-theory assumptions kill the Whitehead and lower \(K\)-theory obstructions and identify the required \(L\)-theory decorations. Assembly then makes the relevant structure set trivial. Its elements are manifolds equipped with homotopy equivalences to the reference manifold. Triviality of that marked structure set is precisely why the conclusion is \(f\simeq h\), not merely that \(M\) and \(N\) accidentally have the same homeomorphism type. These deep assembly and surgery results are imported from their authors.

Here is a concrete family illustrating the nonabelian scope. Let \(H\) be the real Heisenberg group, written \((x,y,z)\in\mathbb R^3\), with multiplication
\[
 (x,y,z)(x',y',z')=(x+x',y+y',z+z'+xy').
\]
The integer-coordinate subgroup \(\Delta\) is discrete. Its left action has a compact fundamental domain obtained by successively reducing \(x,y,z\) modulo integers, so \(\Delta\backslash H\) is a closed smooth three-manifold. The universal cover \(H\) is diffeomorphic to \(\mathbb R^3\). Direct computation gives commutators
\[
 [(x,y,z),(x',y',z')]=(0,0,xy'-x'y),
\]
which are central; thus \(\Delta\) is two-step nilpotent and hence solvable. It is nonabelian. For each \(k\ge2\),
\[
 P_k=(\Delta\backslash H)\times T^k
\]
is a closed aspherical manifold of dimension \(3+k\ge5\), with solvable group \(\Delta\times\mathbb Z^k\). The conclusion applies to every closed aspherical topological manifold homotopy equivalent to \(P_k\) and to every specified equivalence between them. No prescribed nonpositive-curvature metric is involved.

This report supplies no unrestricted four-dimensional statement: the separate goodness qualification in Proposition0.3 is not discarded. It supplies no diffeomorphism conclusion, no assertion about noncompact manifolds, and no general proof for groups outside the virtually solvable class. The published virtually solvable theorem is the substantive added regime; the explicit construction and the marked surgery discussion explain exactly how it meets the problem's quantifiers.
