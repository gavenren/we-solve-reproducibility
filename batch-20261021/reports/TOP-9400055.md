# Vandiver: unconditional even-character vanishing in a growing window

Claim type: Literature report.

The requested assertion is $p\nmid h_p^+$ for every prime, not class number one. There are unconditional theorems excluding entire character components for infinitely many primes, which are materially different from the earlier small-prime Minkowski computations. This report states those theorems with their exact index conventions and derives an explicit growing-window consequence. The K-theoretic inputs are credited, not reproved.

For an odd prime $p$, let $C$ be the $p$-primary subgroup of $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathbb Q(\zeta_p))$. Regard it as a finite $\mathbb Z_p$-module, and let $\omega:\Delta\to\mathbb Z_p^\times$ be the Teichmüller character, with $\sigma(\zeta_p)=\zeta_p^{\omega(\sigma)}$. Set
\[
 C^{(j)}=\{a:\sigma a=\omega(\sigma)^j a\text{ for every }\sigma\in\Delta\}.
\]
Indices are modulo $p-1$. The idempotents
\[
 e_j=\frac1{p-1}\sum_{\sigma\in\Delta}\omega(\sigma)^{-j}\sigma
\]
are integral over $\mathbb Z_p$, pairwise orthogonal and sum to one, by finite geometric sums. Thus $C=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{p-2}C^{(j)}$. Conjugation has $\omega=-1$, so its fixed part is precisely the sum of the even components. The standard extension/norm identities for the degree-two extension identify this fixed $p$-part with $\operatorname{Cl}(K_p^+)[p^\infty]$, since multiplication by two is invertible on a finite odd-primary group. Consequently Vandiver demands vanishing of **all** even components simultaneously.

Kurihara's unconditional theorem gives
\[
 C^{(p-3)}=0\qquad\text{for every odd prime }p.
\]
This includes the full $p$-power component, not just elements of order $p$. Its arithmetic input compares the relevant étale cohomology to $K_4(\mathbb Z)$; the original argument's absence of odd torsion except possibly at three suffices, with $p=3$ handled separately. The theorem is Corollary3.8 of the original paper, also stated unambiguously in Ghate's Theorem3 and Soulé's introduction. No assertion that all higher $K_{4m}(\mathbb Z)$ vanish is being assumed.

Soulé proves a larger effective regime. In the convention above, for every odd integer $n\ge5$,
\[
 \log p>n^{224n^4}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad C^{(p-n)}=0. \tag{1}
\]
The enormous hypothesis implies $p>n$, so this is an actual even index between zero and $p-3$, without an unnoticed index wrap. In Soulé's v1 this follows from §3.2's first Theorem4 and the bound immediately following it; the displayed numerical bound there is explicitly proved for $n\ge5$. The case $n=3$ is supplied by Kurihara, not by extending that numerical argument.

One mechanism behind (1) is a surjection from an appropriate finite $K$-group to $C^{(p-n)}/pC^{(p-n)}$, followed by an explicit torsion bound obtained from Voronoi complexes. If this quotient vanishes, the whole component vanishes: for a finite group $D$ killed by $p^a$, equality $D=pD$ iterates to $D=p^aD=0$. It would be incorrect to stop at the quotient without this finite-primary argument.

Here is a quantitative consequence with a parameter allowed to grow with $p$. Fix
\[
 0<c<56^{-1/4},\quad L=\log\log p,\quad
 N(p)=\left\lfloor c\left(\frac{L}{\log L}\right)^{1/4}\right\rfloor.
\]
For all sufficiently large primes, every odd $n$ with $5\le n\le N(p)$ satisfies (1). Indeed
\[
 224n^4\log n
 \le 224c^4\frac L{\log L}
 \left(\tfrac14\log L+O(1)\right)
 =(56c^4+o(1))L<L.
\]
Exponentiating this inequality gives $n^{224n^4}<\log p$, exactly the required strict condition. Together with $n=3$, this excludes every even component $p-3,p-5,\ldots,p-n$ in a window containing an unbounded number of indices as $p\to\infty$. This is an unconditional, all-primes-eventually result, not a finite verification range.

The window still covers only a vanishing fraction of the roughly $p/2$ even indices. For each fixed odd $n$, knowing $C^{(p-n)}=0$ for all sufficiently large $p$ does not imply simultaneous vanishing for every odd $n\le p$ at one prime. The threshold depends drastically on $n$; inserting $n$ of size $p$ into (1) defeats its hypothesis. A hypothetical surviving component can move outside every fixed or slowly growing window without contradicting either theorem.

The earlier contribution proves small-prime class number-one cases and explains plus/minus decomposition and Bernoulli irregularity. The additional content here is the primary K-theoretic vanishing regime and its explicitly quantified moving-window consequence. No complete Vandiver proof, current computational record, or unverified claimed solution is endorsed.
