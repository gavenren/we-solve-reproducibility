# All Hodge classes on K3 self-squares with scalar or CM transcendental endomorphisms

Claim type: **Literature report**.

Let $S$ be a projective complex K3 surface and let $T=\operatorname{NS}(S)_{\mathbb Q}^{\perp}\subset H^2(S,\mathbb Q)$. If
\[
 E=\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T)=\mathbb Q
 \quad\text{or}\quad E\text{ is a CM field}, \tag{1}
\]
then **every rational Hodge class on $S\times S$, in every codimension, is algebraic**. The CM case is explicitly Buskin's introductory corollary in arXiv:1510.02852v3; the argument below makes the linear-algebra reduction and its exact boundary explicit.

The deep input is Buskin Theorem1.1: a rational Hodge isometry between the full second cohomologies of two projective complex K3 surfaces is induced by a rational algebraic correspondence. The theorem concerns isometries; it cannot simply be applied to an arbitrary Hodge endomorphism. The following spanning argument supplies the missing passage in(1).

For a CM field $E$, write $\bar{\phantom u}$ for complex conjugation, $E^+=\{x:\bar x=x\}$ and $E^-=\{x:\bar x=-x\}$. The $\mathbb Q$-span $V$ of elements $z\in E$ with $z\bar z=1$ is all of $E$. Indeed $V$ contains1 and is closed under multiplication. It is a finite-dimensional subalgebra of the field $E$, and is itself a field: multiplication by a nonzero element is injective and hence surjective on $V$. For $u\in E^-$ and rational $t$ for which the denominator is nonzero,
\[
 z(t)=\frac{1+tu}{1-tu},\qquad z(t)\overline{z(t)}=1.
\]
All these elements belong to $V$. In the vector-space quotient $E/V$, the rational function $z(t)$ vanishes at infinitely many rational values of $t$. Clear its scalar polynomial denominator (for example the norm of $1-tu$); every coefficient must vanish in that quotient. Its Taylor coefficient at zero is $2u$, so $u\in V$. Choose a nonzero $u\in E^-$. For any $a\in E^+$, also $ua\in E^-$; hence $a=u^{-1}(ua)\in V$. The decomposition $E=E^+\oplus E^-$ proves the assertion.

For the polarized transcendental Hodge structure of a K3 surface, the adjoint involution on its CM endomorphism field is complex conjugation, as used in Buskin's corollary. Therefore every $z$ with $z\bar z=1$ acts as a rational Hodge isometry on $T$. Extend it by the identity on $\operatorname{NS}(S)_{\mathbb Q}$; the orthogonal direct sum is the full rational $H^2$, so Buskin applies. The projector onto the Néron–Severi summand is algebraic: choose divisor classes $D_i$ forming a rational basis and use the inverse of their nonsingular intersection matrix to form the correspondence $\sum_{i,j}(Q^{-1})_{ij}D_i\times D_j$. Subtracting it, and the point-degree Künneth pieces if needed, isolates the action on $T$. Rational linear combinations of these correspondences realize every element of $E$ by the spanning lemma. If $E=\mathbb Q$, only the identity is needed, and it is supplied by the diagonal with the same algebraic projections.

To see that no Hodge classes have been omitted, Künneth and Poincaré duality identify the transcendental part of the rational $(2,2)$ classes in $H^4(S\times S)$ with $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(T)$. The other pieces are point classes times the fundamental class and $\operatorname{NS}(S)_{\mathbb Q}\otimes\operatorname{NS}(S)_{\mathbb Q}$, all algebraic. Cross terms between $T$ and Néron–Severi would require a rational $(1,1)$ vector in $T$, which is zero by Lefschetz(1,1) and orthogonality. In codimension1, Hodge classes are divisor classes. In codimension3 the only nonzero Künneth terms are $H^2\otimes H^4$ and $H^4\otimes H^2$, again divisors times points; odd cohomology vanishes for a K3. Codimensions0 and4 are immediate. This proves the entire assertion on the self-square.

The field restriction is not cosmetic. If a larger totally real endomorphism field occurs, its adjoint is identity and the norm-one equation becomes $z^2=1$, whose solutions cannot span that larger field. The CM spanning proof gives no result there. Nor does the two-factor endomorphism calculation establish algebraicity of arbitrary higher tensors on all powers or on products of unrelated K3 surfaces. Those are not claimed. The geometric isometry theorem and the polarized-Hodge-structure fact are credited inputs; the spanning and all-codimension reductions are supplied explicitly.
