# A published regular two-dimensional regime over the rationals

Claim type: **Literature report**.

The literal Fontaine–Mazur target ranges over all number fields and dimensions. A substantial established regime is the following. Let$p\ge5$, let$E/\mathbb Q_p$ be finite, and let
\[
 \rho:G_{\mathbb Q}\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_2(E)
\]
be continuous and absolutely irreducible. Assume finite ramification, oddness
$\det\rho(c)=-1$, and potential semistability at$p$ with two distinct Hodge–Tate weights. Then the geometric-realization conclusion in the prompt holds. We keep potential semistability explicitly in the hypothesis of this reported sector, as in the primary theorem; no local-monodromy equivalence is needed as an unmentioned input.

Pan's published Theorem1.0.4, checked in arXiv:1901.07166v2, proves that such a representation comes from a cuspidal eigenform up to twist. For$p\ge5$ it imposes **no residual irreducibility or residual-character-ratio hypothesis**. The introduction combines the residually reducible theorem with other cases to obtain this statement. At$p=3$ the theorem has an additional local residual exclusion; it is not included in the sector stated here. Prime2 is also outside it. The title's “residually reducible” must therefore not be read as a restriction on the final$p\ge5$ theorem.

The theorem's conventions matter. Pan gives the cyclotomic character$\varepsilon$ Hodge–Tate weight$-1$ and uses geometric Frobenius in local reciprocity. We only require the weights to be distinct, a condition invariant under changing the sign convention or applying a Tate twist. If their difference has absolute value$w-1$, the corresponding classical eigenform has weight$w\ge2$. Thus a weight-one Artin case is not being silently inserted into the regular cohomological argument.

Here is the bridge from this modularity theorem to the exact category in the prompt. Scholl's primary construction realizes the Galois representation of a normalized newform of weight$w\ge2$ in the cohomology of a smooth projective variety over$\mathbb Q$, after the appropriate coefficient extension. For$w>2$ the construction uses a desingularized Kuga–Sato variety, with cohomological degree$w-1$; the weight2 case uses the Jacobian of a modular curve. His §1.2 gives the projector and Hecke decomposition. Depending on dual/Frobenius conventions, take the dual representation; Poincaré duality converts a dual into another cohomology group with an allowed Tate twist.

An “up to twist” qualification cannot be ignored. If the modularity identification is written
$\rho\simeq\rho_f\otimes\chi$, both$\rho$ and$\rho_f$ are de Rham. The scalar line generated by that identification inside
$\operatorname{Hom}(\rho_f,\rho)$ transforms by$\chi$. Closure of de Rham representations under tensor products, duals and subrepresentations shows that$\chi$ is de Rham. It is also finitely ramified. Every such one-dimensional character of$G_{\mathbb Q}$ is a finite-image character times an integral power of$\varepsilon$.

For specificity, the last assertion follows from the rank-one argument in Bellaïche's proof of Proposition1.2. Twist to Hodge–Tate weight0. Sen's theorem makes the inertia action at$p$ finite. Global class field theory, together with finite ramification and continuity away from$p$, then makes the resulting idele-class character factor through a finite quotient. In idelic terms its kernel contains an open subgroup of$\mathbb Z_p^\times$, an open subgroup of$\prod_{\ell\ne p}\mathbb Z_\ell^\times$, and the positive real factor; the resulting quotient modulo$\mathbb Q^\times$ is finite. This classification is special to$\mathbb Q$ in the form used here, not a claim that all geometric characters over arbitrary number fields are finite Tate twists.

A finite-image character$\tau$ occurs in the regular representation
$H^0((\operatorname{Spec}L)_{\overline{\mathbb Q}},E)$ for a finite Galois extension$L/\mathbb Q$ cutting it out. Tensoring its summand with the cohomological realization of$\rho_f$, and using Künneth and exactness over coefficient fields, realizes$\rho_f\otimes\tau$ as a subquotient of a smooth projective scheme's cohomology. The remaining power of$\varepsilon$ is precisely an allowed Tate twist.

If “variety” is required to be geometrically connected, this intermediate disconnected scheme$Z$ causes no loophole. Embed$Z$ smoothly in a sufficiently large$\mathbb P^N$ in codimension at least2. The smooth geometrically integral blow-up$Y=\operatorname{Bl}_Z\mathbb P^N$ has
$H^i(Z)(m)$ as a direct summand of$H^{i+2}(Y)(m+1)$ by the blow-up/Gysin formula. The earlier contribution supplies this explicit splitting; we use that structural repair rather than claim it as new.

Likewise a temporary coefficient extension does not alter the conclusion over$E$. Restriction of scalars makes$\rho$ occur among the composition factors of copies of the original$E$-cohomology: over a finite extension$E'/E$, the underlying$E$-representation of$\rho\otimes_EE'$ is$\rho^{\oplus[E':E]}$. Jordan–Hölder then puts$\rho$ among the factors of the original cohomology. No conjectural semisimplicity of all Galois cohomology is assumed.

The prior work proves potential-realization descent and finite-image Tate cases and describes more recent narrowly scoped claims. The added input here is a checked published theorem eliminating residual restrictions at$p\ge5$, plus its precise geometric bridge. General$K$, higher dimension, even representations and repeated Hodge–Tate weights remain outside this reported sector. No universal Fontaine–Mazur solution is claimed.
