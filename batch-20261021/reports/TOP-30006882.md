# A closed, nontrivial class where a sharper bound is proved

For every full-dimensional zonotope$Z\subset\mathbb R^n$ generated by at most$n+1$ segments,
\[
L_Z\le1/\sqrt{12},
\]
with equality exactly for affine images of the cube. This is Christian Kipp's Proposition6.1, arXiv:2407.01353v2,27May2025. It is a global upper bound throughout a closed subclass, not an assertion that an unknown global maximizer has a simplicial vertex.

A zonotope is a translate of a Minkowski sum of segments. The earlier exploratory archive attempt computes product bodies, balls and cross-polytopes, derives a smooth obstruction, and explains why approximating arbitrary bodies by locally maximizing polytopes is invalid. It mentions Kipp's simplicial-vertex theorem, but does not extract this$n+1$-generator theorem and its within-class deformation. The other earlier record only reports an unverified claimed full simplex theorem. Neither broad claim is used as a proof here.

The checked proof supplies a finite-dimensional route unavailable for arbitrary nonsmooth bodies. After an affine transformation and changes of generator signs, write
\[
Z=C_n+[-z,z],\qquad z_i\ge0.
\]
The cube$C_n=[-1,1]^n$ accounts for$n$ linearly independent generators; all remaining variation is the one extra segment. For two positive coordinates$z_i,z_j$, Kipp's Lemma6.3 moves their sum between those coordinates, along the line$z+t(e_i-e_j)$ while nonnegativity is preserved. It is an RS-movement within this zonotope class. The source's strict convexity theorem for a nonaffine speed shows that$L^{2n}$ at the original interior parameter is strictly less than at an endpoint. The two endpoints are affinely equivalent by a coordinate permutation. At an endpoint one positive coordinate has vanished.

Iterating eliminates all but one positive coordinate; then the extra segment merely lengthens a cube direction, giving a parallelepiped. Thus the process strictly increases$L$ unless the original zonotope already was a parallelepiped. The moment calculation for a product of intervals gives$L=1/\sqrt{12}$ there. This explains both the global bound and its equality case without inferring them from numerical examples. The RS strict-convexity result is imported from the checked source, not newly proved by the outline.

The class also admits the compact parametrization$C_n+[-y,y]$ with$y\in[0,1]^n$ after choosing a suitable independent generator basis (Lemma6.4). Hence actual extrema exist in this class; one is not merely deforming a hypothetical maximizer in a nonclosed family.

For$n\ge2$, the cube value is below the conjectured simplex value. Explicitly their$2n$-power ratio is
\[
\frac{L_{\Delta_n}^{2n}}{(1/12)^n}
=\frac{12^n(n!)^2}{(n+1)^{n+1}(n+2)^n},
\]
which is only a comparison of the displayed constants, not a proof for bodies outside the generator restriction. No bound for all zonotopes, all centrally symmetric bodies, or arbitrary convex bodies follows from this report.
