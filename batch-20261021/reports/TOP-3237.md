# Degree-local correspondence coloring and the half-degree target

**Claim type: Literature report.** A checked theorem of Davies, de Joannis de Verclos, Kang and Pirot allows list budgets to follow individual degrees in triangle-free graphs. We state a precise corollary of their Theorem8 and explain both its stronger coloring model and its relation to the literal half-degree question. The result is established literature, not a new proof of the entire prompt.

## Exact source theorem on the regime used here

Fix $0<\varepsilon<1$. For sufficiently large maximum degree $\Delta$, put
\[
b_\Delta=(192\log\Delta)^{2/\varepsilon}.
\]
Let $G$ be triangle-free and suppose $\delta(G)\ge b_\Delta$. In a correspondence cover, every vertex $v$ has a cluster $L(v)$ of permissible choices. Clusters are disjoint cliques; between adjacent vertices the forbidden pairs form a matching; nonadjacent vertices have no cross-conflicts. A valid coloring chooses one entry from each cluster and no forbidden pair.

Theorem8 of the checked arXiv1812.01534v2 implies that such a choice exists whenever
\[
|L(v)|\ge\left\lceil(1+\varepsilon)\frac{d(v)}{\log d(v)}\right\rceil
\quad\text{for every }v.
\]
Indeed, its full theorem requires the maximum of $d(v)/\log d(v)$ and $b_\Delta/\log b_\Delta$. In our regime all degrees exceed $b_\Delta>e$, and $x/\log x$ is increasing there, so that maximum is the first term. The report deliberately uses this regime to avoid undefined expressions at degrees zero and one.

Ordinary list coloring is a special correspondence cover: forbid equal color names across an edge. Thus the theorem supplies both ordinary list and DP/correspondence colorings, with leading asymptotic constant one and nonuniform budgets.

## Why local budgets matter

For vertices of degree $d(v)=\Delta^{a+o(1)}$, with fixed $0<a<1$, the required budget has scale
\[
(1+\varepsilon)\frac{\Delta^{a+o(1)}}{a\log\Delta},
\]
rather than the global maximum-degree scale $\Delta/\log\Delta$. Since every fixed positive power of $\Delta$ eventually exceeds the polylogarithmic floor $b_\Delta$, this is a genuine regime of the theorem. Exact integral budgets remain the displayed ceilings; the asymptotic comparison is not a substitute for them.

This is additional scope beyond TOP-3237-A1's critical-graph/Mycielski examples and its cited finite-degree ordinary-coloring theorem. The separate OpenAI claim-summary revision concerns an unspecified forbidden-clique constant and lacks full independent verification in that record. We do not use that claim as an input.

## Recovering the global asymptotic DP bound

The minimum-degree restriction in the corollary need not be imposed to obtain a uniform global palette bound. Let
\[
q=\left\lceil(1+\varepsilon)\frac{\Delta}{\log\Delta}\right\rceil.
\]
For sufficiently large $\Delta$, $q>b_\Delta$. Repeatedly remove a vertex whose current degree is less than $b_\Delta$. If the process empties the graph, restore vertices in reverse order, choosing an available entry: each already restored neighbor forbids at most one choice, and fewer than $q$ such neighbors are present.

Otherwise the surviving core has maximum degree $\Delta'\le\Delta$ and minimum degree at least $b_\Delta$. Since $b_\Delta\to\infty$, its maximum degree eventually exceeds the source theorem's threshold. Also $b_{\Delta'}\le b_\Delta$. The local theorem applies to the core with parameter $\Delta'$. Monotonicity of $x/\log x$ shows that its required list budgets are at most $q$. After coloring the core, reverse restoration works as before. Thus every triangle-free graph satisfies the global DP bound with the stated $q$ once $\Delta$ is sufficiently large.

This argument uses the matching condition of a correspondence cover when counting forbidden choices. With arbitrary conflict graphs between clusters, one neighbor could forbid many entries and the restoration step would fail.

## Relation to the exact official inequality

Because $(1+\varepsilon)/\log\Delta\to0$, the global bound is eventually at most $\lceil\Delta/2\rceil+2$. It supplies no explicit numerical cutoff unless the theorem's sufficiently-large threshold is also made effective. In particular it does not settle the bounded degree interval left by the earlier checked finite-degree result, and an interval of maximum degrees still contains infinitely many graphs.

The contribution is the source-checked degree-local correspondence theorem and its exact implications. It neither changes the official ceiling convention to the stronger floor convention nor claims a uniform solution at every degree.

Reference: [Davies–de Joannis de Verclos–Kang–Pirot, arXiv1812.01534v2, Theorems1 and8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.01534v2).
