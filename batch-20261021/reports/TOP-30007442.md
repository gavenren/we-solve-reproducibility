# Finite-extension transfer and exceptional-prime specialization

Problem: TOP-30007442  
Claim type: Partial result

**Exact scope.** For every fixed finite extension E/F, formal-bilinear matrix-multiplication exponents satisfy ωE=ωF. A fixed exact rational bilinear block identity also specializes unchanged outside its finite denominator-prime set. These proved transfer lemmas delimit valid characteristic arguments; they do not independently establish the prompt’s numerical exponent threshold.

Its primary introduction was freshly retrieved: Theorem thm:main states that claim and the following paragraph expressly distinguishes arithmetic cost from bit complexity. This report supplies separate transfer proofs and does not claim independent verification of the manuscript’s tensor extraction or Lean files.

Here ω is explicitly defined using exact **formal bilinear polynomial identities** for matrix multiplication, with fixed scalar coefficients, rather than arbitrary circuits that happen to be correct only on the input field’s finite set of points. Pointwise correctness over a finite field does not ensure a formal identity: Frobenius relations can vanish on that field but fail over an extension. All identities transferred below are formal coefficient identities.

Let E/F have fixed finite degree d, and choose a basis e₁=1,e₂,…,e_d with fixed structure constants eᵢeⱼ=Σk cᵢⱼₖe_k. Represent an E element by its d F coordinates. Addition costs d F operations, and multiplication costs O(d³) F operations by that formula. Each E circuit constant has fixed F coordinates.

Apply an E matrix-multiplication algorithm to matrices with entries in F, simulate all operations in coordinates, and read their output e₁ coordinates. The cost is O(d³T_E(n)+dn²). Because d is fixed, ωF≤ωE. In the reverse direction, the formal bilinear polynomial identities expressing an F multiplication algorithm remain valid when its input variables range over E. The same circuit with its F constants embedded in E multiplies E matrices. Thus ωE≤ωF and equality follows.

This is explicitly the formal-bilinear exponent convention; no equivalence with unrestricted pointwise-correct division-free circuits over finite fields is needed or asserted here. An unrelated algorithm with division by an input-dependent expression requires its domain to be checked before transfer; a rational-function equality alone is not being substituted for a total multiplication algorithm. In effective circuit families, coordinate simulation preserves uniformity when the fixed field constants and structure constants have the same effective representation convention.

In particular every fixed F_(p^d) has the same exponent as F_p, and C has the same exponent as R. The proof does not cross characteristic, since characteristic-zero and positive-characteristic fields do not embed in one another. If d grows with n, the O(d³) overhead cannot be absorbed into a field-dependent constant. Abstract arithmetic over an arbitrary fixed field also supplies no bit complexity without an effective encoding and costs for its operations.

Now take one fixed exact bilinear m×m multiplication algorithm over Q, with m≥2, with R products of linear forms and rational reconstruction coefficients. Let D be the product of the finitely many nonzero coefficient denominators. Expanding the algorithm gives coefficient identities in Z[1/D]. For each prime p not dividing D, reduction modulo p is a ring homomorphism, hence preserves all of those identities and the R-product count. The same identity then works over any field of characteristic p. Recursive blocking and padding give exponent≤max(2,log_mR), accounting for additions and output costs.

For p dividing D, an expression such as 1/p has no specialization. Rounding it or treating it as zero changes the identity. Moreover a sequence of successively better rational constructions need not have one common finite exceptional set: denominator primes may change with the construction. An all-field theorem must separately handle every excluded characteristic or prove identities integrally valid there. Finite-extension invariance and a characteristic-zero estimate alone do not do that.

Even a valid arithmetic exponent claim gives no explicit practical crossover or rational bit-growth bound, and its constants may depend on a fixed construction and exponent slack. These are precisely the limitations retained by the source. The transfer lemmas establish what can safely be deduced without independently certifying that claimed numerical improvement.
