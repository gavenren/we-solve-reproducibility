# Tate classes and numerical equivalence on every power of an ordinary elliptic curve

Claim type: **Partial result**.

Let $E/\mathbb F_q$ be an elliptic curve, $q=p^f$, with Frobenius trace $a$ satisfying $p\nmid a$ (the ordinary elliptic case). For **every** $m\ge1$, every $\ell\ne p$ and every codimension $r$, both assertions of the prompt hold for $A=E^m$. The Tate surjectivity holds over every finite extension of $\mathbb F_q$ as well. We give a direct proof using divisors; no general Tate theorem in higher codimension is assumed.

Use the standard étale-cohomology facts for abelian varieties: cohomology is the exterior algebra on $H^1$, compatibly with the Galois action, and Künneth/Poincaré duality hold. These are stated and proved in Milne, *Abelian Varieties*, v2.00, TheoremI.12.1 and RemarkI.12.5. Write the eigenvalues of geometric Frobenius on $H^1(E)$ as $\alpha,\beta$, with
\[
 \alpha+\beta=a,\qquad\alpha\beta=q.
\]
They are distinct and $\alpha/\beta$ is not a root of unity. To check the latter, embed their algebraic number field in $\overline{\mathbb Q}_p$. Their valuations sum to $f$ and are nonnegative because they are algebraic integers. Their sum $a$ is a unit, so one valuation is0 and the other is$f$. Their ratio has nonzero valuation, whereas every root of unity has valuation0. This also proves distinctness. The argument uses no numerical approximation of their complex arguments.

Extend coefficients to a finite field extension $L/\mathbb Q_\ell$ splitting the polynomial. On $H^1(E^m,L)$ choose eigenvectors $x_1,\ldots,x_m$ of eigenvalue $\alpha$ and $y_1,\ldots,y_m$ of eigenvalue $\beta$. A basis wedge in $H^{2r}(E^m,L(r))$ having $u$ of the $x$'s and $2r-u$ of the $y$'s has eigenvalue
\[
 \alpha^u\beta^{2r-u}/q^r=(\alpha/\beta)^{u-r}.
\]
It is fixed precisely when $u=r$. Replacing Frobenius by any positive power changes none of these conditions. Thus the invariant space has basis
\[
 x_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{i_r}\wedge
 y_{j_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge y_{j_r}, \tag{1}
\]
with each index set of size$r$; indices may overlap between the two sets. Its dimension is $\binom mr^2$ when $0\le r\le m$, and it is zero outside this range.

Every invariant in degree2 is a divisor class, **explicitly**. The classes $x_i\wedge y_i$ are factor point divisors. For $i\ne j$, the cross Künneth summand on $E_i\times E_j$ identifies via the elliptic polarization with $\operatorname{End}(H^1(E))$. Its Frobenius-invariant part is the two-dimensional space of endomorphisms diagonal in the $\alpha,\beta$ eigenbasis. The diagonal and the graph of the $q$-Frobenius endomorphism give respectively identity and Frobenius under the correspondence identification (using the transpose graph if the convention requires it). Since $\alpha\ne\beta$, these two matrices span that space. Their factor Künneth components can be subtracted using point divisors. Pulling back to $E^m$ therefore makes both $x_i\wedge y_j$ and $y_i\wedge x_j$ $L$-linear combinations of classes of divisors defined over $\mathbb F_q$.

Each wedge(1) is, up to sign, a product of$r$ classes $x_{i_k}\wedge y_{j_k}$. It follows that all invariant classes in every degree are $L$-linear combinations of products of these explicit divisors. The cycle-class map over $\mathbb Q_\ell$ is therefore surjective: its cokernel tensored with the field $L$ is zero, so its cokernel is zero by faithful scalar extension. All generating cycles are already defined over $\mathbb F_q$. The same eigenvalue proof works over $\mathbb F_{q^s}$ for every$s>0$.

Finally let $z$ be a rational codimension-$r$ cycle on $E^m_{\overline{\mathbb F}_q}$. It is defined over some finite extension. The invariant subspace in(1) pairs nondegenerately with the balanced subspace of complementary degree: the wedge dual of a basis element is obtained by the complementary $x$ and $y$ index sets. By what was proved, that whole complementary invariant space is algebraic. If $z$ is numerically trivial, its intersections with all these algebraic complementary classes are zero, so nondegeneracy forces its cohomology class to be zero. Conversely homological triviality makes every intersection number zero by compatibility of cycle class and degree. This proves numerical equals $\ell$-adic homological equivalence, with the correct algebraic-closure quantifier.

This is an all-dimensions and all-codimensions family with an explicit cycle-generation mechanism, not a finite computation. It is a classical special case, not a claim of new priority. The proof does not cover an arbitrary abelian variety, nor infer multiplicative independence of multiple unrelated Frobenius pairs.
