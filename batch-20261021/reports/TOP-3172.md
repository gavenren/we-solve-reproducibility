# Arbitrary generating sets on mixed-prime nilpotent groups

**Claim type: Literature report.** Ghaderpour and Witte Morris prove that every connected Cayley graph on a finite nilpotent group with cyclic commutator subgroup has a Hamiltonian cycle [1, Theorem1.2]. We apply precisely that theorem, for arbitrary actual generating sets, and verify an explicit unbounded nonabelian family with mixed-prime commutator order. The theorem is a credited deep input, not proved anew.

The prompt assumes inverse-closed \(S\subseteq\Gamma-\{1\}\) generating a group of order at least3. The source defines undirected edges \(g\)--\(gs\) and may start with a non-inverse-closed generating set; taking \(S\cup S^{-1}\) gives the same undirected graph. Thus its theorem applies to the literal Cayley-graph convention. We impose the prompt's order bound, avoiding any disagreement about Hamiltonian cycles on one or two vertices. The theorem does not require a particular generator to have large order, a quotient cycle to have generating voltage, or a bound on the cardinality of \(S\).

Here is a complete family check. For every integer \(m\ge2\), put
\[
 H_m=\{(a,b,c):a,b,c\in\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\},\qquad
 (a,b,c)(a',b',c')=(a+a',b+b',c+c'+ab').
\]
Associativity follows because the third coordinate of a triple product in either association is
\(c+c'+c''+ab'+ab''+a'b''\). The identity is \((0,0,0)\), and the inverse of \((a,b,c)\) is \((-a,-b,-c+ab)\). Hence this is a group of order \(m^3\).

A direct calculation gives
\[
 [ (a,b,c),(a',b',c')]=(0,0,ab'-a'b)
\]
for the convention \([x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}\). The set
\(Z_0=\{(0,0,c):c\in\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z\}\) is central and cyclic of order \(m\). Every commutator belongs to \(Z_0\), while the choices \((a,b)=(1,0),(a',b')=(0,z)\) produce every \((0,0,z)\). Thus \(H_m'=Z_0\). In particular the group is nonabelian and its lower central series is \(H_m,H_m',1\): it is nilpotent of class2.

The theorem therefore proves:
\[
 \text{for every }m\ge2\text{ and every inverse-closed generating }S
 \subset H_m-\{1\},\quad \operatorname{Cay}(H_m,S)\text{ is Hamiltonian}.
\]
This quantifies over **all** such generating sets, not just a convenient grid set. As a concrete nonempty example, \(x=(1,0,0)\) and \(y=(0,1,0)\) generate \(H_m\): their commutator generates \(Z_0\), and \(x^a y^b=(a,b,ab)\), after which a central correction realizes every \(c\). Hence \(S=\{x,x^{-1},y,y^{-1}\}\), with repeated elements removed when \(m=2\), is among the permitted sets.

When \(m\) has two distinct prime divisors, the cyclic commutator group has non-prime-power order. For instance \(m=6\) gives order216 and commutator order6. The older cyclic-*prime-power*-commutator theorem alone does not cover this parameter. Nor can the all-\(p\)-group theorem simply be applied to a product without checking the generating set: arbitrary generators may mix factors. The nilpotent cyclic-commutator theorem is what supplies that uniformity.

The preceding attempt proves a voltage lifting lemma and particular cyclic/dihedral generating-set cases; its additional order-\(kp\) contextual theorem is different. We do not claim that cyclic commutator alone suffices without nilpotence, or that all nilpotent groups have been treated by [1]. No computed Hamiltonian cycle or program execution is asserted.
