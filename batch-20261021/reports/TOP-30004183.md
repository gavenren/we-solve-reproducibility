# Degenerate cup-length bounds on symplectically aspherical manifolds via spectral rigidity

**Problem:** TOP-30004183

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** For every smooth Hamiltonian diffeomorphism of a closed connected symplectic manifold with omega and c1 vanishing on pi2, proves the fixed-point bound ell_F(M)+1 for any fixed coefficient field, including degenerate maps, from Buhovsky–Humilière–Seyfaddini Theorem1.4 and spectrality; distinguishes both cup-length conventions and the nonsmooth boundary.

A substantial regime of the literal cup-length assertion holds even for degenerate fixed points. Let \((M^{2n},\omega)\) be closed and connected, and assume
\[
 \omega|_{\pi_2(M)}=0,\qquad c_1(TM)|_{\pi_2(M)}=0.
\]
For any fixed coefficient field \(\mathbb F\), let \(\ell_{\mathbb F}(M)\) be the largest number of positive-degree cohomology classes with nonzero cup product. Then every smooth Hamiltonian diffeomorphism \(\phi\) has at least \(\ell_{\mathbb F}(M)+1\) distinct fixed points. This is a credited established result in the symplectically aspherical regime; it is not asserted here for every closed symplectic manifold.

The prompt does not specify whether “cup length” means \(\ell\) or \(\ell+1\). We state the numerical bound explicitly. It implies the prompt under either of those conventions, for the same chosen coefficients. No nondegeneracy or transversality hypothesis is added.

The deep input is Buhovsky–Humilière–Seyfaddini, Theorem1.4. In their notation, if a Hamiltonian homeomorphism of such an \(M\) has fewer than \(\ell_{\mathbb F}(M)+1\) distinct spectral invariant values, its fixed set is homologically nontrivial: every open neighborhood of that set has inclusion into \(M\) nonzero on some positive-degree homology group. Their Proposition3.1(1) says that for a smooth Hamiltonian \(H\), each spectral invariant \(c(a,H)\), for nonzero homology \(a\), belongs to the Hamiltonian action spectrum. Both facts include degenerate smooth Hamiltonians; the source constructs their spectral invariants by continuity from nondegenerate ones.

Here is the precise deduction of a fixed-point count. Choose a smooth time-periodic Hamiltonian \(H\) whose time-one map is \(\phi\). For a contractible one-periodic trajectory \(z\), with capping disk \(u\), the source uses
\[
 \mathcal A_H(z)=\int_0^1 H(t,z(t))\,dt-\int_{D^2}u^*\omega .
\]
If two cappings are chosen, their difference is a sphere; the hypothesis on \(\omega\) makes the two action values equal. Moreover a fixed point \(p\) determines just one trajectory \(z_p(t)=\phi_H^t(p)\), by uniqueness for the Hamiltonian ODE. Thus a fixed point with contractible trajectory contributes at most one action value for this fixed generating Hamiltonian.

If the fixed set is infinite, the desired finite bound is immediate. Otherwise let its cardinality be \(N\). The contractible action spectrum then has at most \(N\) elements, and spectrality gives at most \(N\) distinct spectral invariant values. If \(N<\ell_{\mathbb F}(M)+1\), Theorem1.4 would make this finite fixed set homologically nontrivial. But finitely many points have a neighborhood which is a disjoint union of small coordinate balls. This neighborhood has zero homology in all positive degrees, a contradiction. Therefore \(N\ge\ell_{\mathbb F}(M)+1\). Coincident action values only strengthen the contradiction; no assumption that all fixed-point actions are distinct was used.

For an explicit broad example, take a product of \(k\) closed oriented surfaces of positive genus, with product area form. Its \(\pi_2\) is zero, so both asphericity conditions hold. On each surface choose degree-one classes \(a_i,b_i\) whose product is its orientation class. The product of the \(2k\) pulled-back classes is the product orientation class, hence nonzero. No product of more than \(2k\) positive-degree classes can survive by dimension. Consequently \(\ell_{\mathbb F}=2k\), giving at least \(2k+1\) fixed points for every smooth Hamiltonian diffeomorphism, including degenerate ones.

Finally, the smoothness boundary is essential to this particular deduction. The source's extended spectral invariants exist for uniform limits of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms, but in that larger homeomorphism category they need not be actions of individual fixed points. Therefore counting spectral values does not justify the same fixed-point lower bound there. Nor does the absence of nondegeneracy let one freely drop the symplectic-asphericity assumptions, where sphere cappings and quantum products require different arguments. The report resolves the literal assertion only in the stated substantial class and does not adjudicate the earlier announcement.
