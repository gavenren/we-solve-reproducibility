# A common Sylow-intersection witness for abelian Frobenius kernels

Claim type: **Partial result**.

The prompt requires a single conjugator for **arbitrary preselected** Sylow subgroups, and minimality is by inclusion. We prove an exact common-witness theorem for finite semidirect products with an abelian Frobenius kernel. In this class the witness can be chosen from a whole prescribed subset independently of all the Sylow choices.

**Theorem.** Let $G=A\rtimes C$, where $1\ne A$ is finite abelian, $\gcd(|A|,|C|)=1$, and the action of $C$ on $A$ satisfies
\[
 C_C(a)=1\quad\text{for every }1\ne a\in A. \tag{1}
\]
For each prime $p\mid |G|$, choose an arbitrary Sylow subgroup $H_p$. Then **every** $1\ne a\in A$ satisfies
\[
H_p\cap H_p^a=
\begin{cases}
A_p,&p\mid |A|,\\
1,&p\mid |C|,
\end{cases} \tag{2}
\]
Here $A_p$ is the unique Sylow $p$-subgroup of $A$, and the convention is $H^a=a^{-1}Ha$. Every intersection in (2) is inclusion-minimal among all $H_p\cap H_p^g$, so the same $a$ answers all prime coordinates at once.

If $p\mid |A|$, coprimality ensures that $A_p$ has the full Sylow order in $G$. It is characteristic in $A$, and therefore normal in $G$. It is the unique Sylow $p$-subgroup of $G$, so $H_p=A_p$ and $H_p\cap H_p^g=A_p$ for every $g$. Its family of intersections has just this one member, giving the first line and minimality.

Now let $p\mid |C|$. A Sylow $p$-subgroup of $C$ is also Sylow in $G$. By Sylow conjugacy and the decomposition $G=AC$, the chosen $H_p$ can be written
\[
H_p=P_p^{b_p}\quad\text{with }P_p\le C\text{ Sylow and }b_p\in A. \tag{3}
\]
For example, start with a conjugator in $AC$; its $C$-part merely changes the chosen Sylow subgroup inside $C$, while its $A$-part gives (3). No single complement containing all the preselected $H_p$ is assumed.

Fix any $1\ne a\in A$. We claim that $P\cap P^a=1$ for every subgroup $P\le C$. Suppose $u$ lies in that intersection. Then $u=a^{-1}va$ for some $v\in P$. Apply the projection $G\to C$: its restriction to $P$ is injective and both $a$ and $a^{-1}$ project to the identity, so $u=v$. Thus $a$ commutes with $u$. Hypothesis (1) implies $u=1$, proving the claim.

Since $A$ is abelian, $a$ commutes with every $b_p$. Using (3) and the claim,
\[
H_p\cap H_p^a=P_p^{b_p}\cap P_p^{b_pa}
=\bigl(P_p\cap P_p^a\bigr)^{b_p}=1.
\]
The identity subgroup is inclusion-minimal, so this proves the second line of (2) and the simultaneous assertion. The argument does not merely minimize cardinality separately and hope that the conjugators coincide: it exhibits the same witness set $A\setminus\{1\}$ for all the prime families.

One infinite collection of examples is obtained from a finite field $\mathbb F_q$: take $A=(\mathbb F_q,+)$ and any subgroup $C\le\mathbb F_q^\times$, acting by multiplication. Its order is coprime to $q$. If $c\ne1$, the equation $ca=a$ has only the solution $a=0$, so (1) holds. This includes $\operatorname{AGL}_1(q)$ when $C=\mathbb F_q^\times$, and every nonzero field translation is a valid common conjugator for any initially chosen Sylow subgroups.

The theorem is not restricted to cyclic complements. Over $\mathbb F_5$ use
\[
I=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&3\end{pmatrix},\qquad
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\4&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
They satisfy $I^2=J^2=-1$ and $IJ=-JI$. The eight matrices $\{\pm1,\pm I,\pm J,\pm IJ\}$ are distinct and form a quaternion group. Every noncentral nonidentity matrix squares to $-1$, and $-1$ itself has no nonzero fixed vector in characteristic five. Thus no nonidentity matrix has eigenvalue one: if $Mv=v$ and $M^2=-1$, then $v=-v$, forcing $v=0$. Apply these same matrices diagonally on $A=(\mathbb F_5^2)^r$, for any $r\ge1$. This gives the unbounded family
\[
G_r=(C_5)^{2r}\rtimes Q_8,
\]
all satisfying the theorem. The matrix identities supply the fixed-point-free hypothesis directly, without a table lookup of Frobenius groups.

The proof uses standard Sylow conjugacy and elementary semidirect-product algebra; it makes no novelty claim for Frobenius-group structure. It gives a precise common witness for the literal simultaneous-minimality condition. For groups with nonabelian kernels, primes shared between kernel and complement, or nontrivial complement fixed points, the argument no longer supplies (2). The unrestricted finite-group question remains outside this contribution.
