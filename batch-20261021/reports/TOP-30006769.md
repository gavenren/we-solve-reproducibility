# Uniform constant-depth completeness gives actual polynomial-time isomorphisms

Problem: TOP-30006769  
Claim type: Literature report  
Author: /root/hundred_algorithms

**Exact scope.** Agrawal’s uniform constant-depth isomorphism theorem implies the Berman–Hartmanis conclusion for NP languages complete under DLOGTIME-uniform AC⁰ many-one reductions. Both the bijection and its inverse are polynomial-time computable. Completeness merely under arbitrary polynomial-time reductions is not covered.

**Credited theorem.** The checked 6 March 2010 author manuscript proves that for a class C closed under TC⁰ reductions and any uniformity measure u containing DLOGTIME, sets complete for C under u-uniform AC⁰ reductions are isomorphic via u-uniform AC⁰-computable isomorphisms. The special case C=NP and u=DLOGTIME is sufficient here. This is a known deep theorem, not a new construction for every polynomial-time complete language. The paper's manuscript date and the later journal publication are distinguished in the references.

NP satisfies the closure premise: a uniform constant-depth threshold-circuit reduction has polynomial output length and is computable in deterministic polynomial time. Composing such a reduction with an NP verifier produces an NP verifier with polynomial certificate length and running time. For two NP languages A,B complete under DLOGTIME-uniform AC⁰ many-one reductions, the theorem therefore supplies a bijection h of all binary strings carrying A onto B, whose forward and inverse functions have uniform polynomial-size constant-depth circuits (with the source's polynomial length/output encodings).

This really yields the **uniform polynomial-time** isomorphism demanded by the literal question on this subclass. Given x, generate the relevant circuit using its uniformity procedure and evaluate its polynomially many gates, retaining polynomial space if necessary. Compute the required output-length encoding and its output bits by the same polynomial-time simulation. Repeat for the inverse family to compute h^{-1}. DLOGTIME-uniformity is stronger than needed for this sequential simulation, so no length-dependent circuit advice is supplied as an oracle. A bijection only on yes-instances would not suffice; the source theorem is an isomorphism on the whole string domain.

The theorem is substantially stronger than applying arbitrary mutual Karp reductions and hoping they are reversible. Its proof outline has three stages: a gap theorem to weak local reductions, a superprojection construction which preserves decodable input coordinates, and an isomorphism construction. Uniformity is a central issue: the earlier gap theorem had a nonuniform stage, and the author explicitly explains why adding the stronger uniformity conclusion is nontrivial. This report uses the final uniform theorem rather than silently turning a nonuniform AC⁰ isomorphism into a polynomial-time algorithm.

The restriction falls on **how a language is complete**, not on whether the language itself belongs to AC⁰. An NP-complete target can have a very difficult membership problem while all NP languages reduce to it by simple uniform local maps. Conversely a polynomial-time many-one complete language is not shown here to be complete under these stronger reductions. Supplying that missing upgrade for all Karp-complete sets would go beyond the cited theorem and is not inferred from its conclusion.

The older effective Cantor–Bernstein lemma remains useful when one already has length-increasing invertible embeddings. Here a substantial known theorem actually provides isomorphisms for an explicitly delimited family of complete sets. No oracle result, random-oracle conclusion or unreviewed full conjecture claim is used to enlarge the scope. Historical novelty and resolution of the unrestricted conjecture are not claimed.
