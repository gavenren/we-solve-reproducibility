# Fourier rank for XOR matrices and a tight addressing benchmark

Problem: TOP-30006402  
Claim type: Partial result  
Author: /root/hundred_algorithms

**Exact scope.** For every XOR sign matrix M(x,y)=F(x+y) over F₂^m, prove rank_R(M)=|supp(F̂)| and D(M)≤dim_F₂ span(supp(F̂))+1, yielding the log-rank bound whenever that Fourier dimension is polylogarithmic in the support size. An explicit addressing family has rank k² and one-output communication between 2log₂k and 2log₂k+1, while its Fourier dimension is k+log₂k, showing the dimension criterion is sufficient but not necessary.

Let F:F₂^m→{−1,1}. For s∈F₂^m write χ_s(x)=(−1)^{s·x} and F̂(s)=2^{-m}Σ_xF(x)χ_s(x). The characters form an orthogonal real basis: Σ_xχ_s(x)χ_t(x) is 2^m when s=t and zero otherwise, by pairing x with x+e_j at any differing coordinate. For M(x,y)=F(x+y),
\[
 (M\chi_s)(x)=\sum_yF(x+y)\chi_s(y)
 =2^m\widehat F(s)\chi_s(x).
\]
Hence the real rank is exactly s₀=|supp(F̂)|. No finite-field rank or approximate rank is substituted.

Let b₁,…,b_d be a binary linear basis of the support span. Alice sends the d bits b_i·x. Bob knows b_i·y and can recover every s·(x+y) for s in the Fourier support. The exact Fourier expansion then determines F(x+y); local computation is free in deterministic communication complexity. Bob sends one output bit if both players must know the answer. Thus D≤d+1. If d≤(log₂(s₀+2))^C, this gives a valid polylog-rank bound for the whole specified XOR subclass, including every subproblem with that structural promise. The basis and Fourier coefficients depend only on the known function, not on the players' private inputs. This is not a polynomial-time procedure for learning F from an exponentially large truth table.

To test the limitation of this sufficient condition, let k=2^t≥2 and index k data bits z_j by j∈F₂^t. Define the addressing sign function F(a,z)=(−1)^{z_a}. With m=t+k its expansion is
\[
 F(a,z)=\sum_{j\in F_2^t}1_{a=j}(-1)^{z_j}
 =\frac1k\sum_{j\in F_2^t}\sum_{u\in F_2^t}
           (-1)^{u\cdot j}\chi_{(u,e_j)}(a,z).
\]
Exactly k² distinct coefficients occur, all nonzero of magnitude 1/k. Therefore its XOR matrix has rank k². Its Fourier-support span has dimension k+t: vectors (0,e_j) span all k data directions; subtracting (0,e_j) from (u,e_j) yields every address direction (u,0). So the preceding dimension protocol would cost k+t+1, exponentially larger than log rank as t grows.

There is instead a direct one-output protocol of 2t+1 bits. Alice sends her address a (t bits), Bob sends his address b (t bits), and both know j=a+b. Alice sends z_j (one bit). Bob combines it with his own data bit w_j and outputs (−1)^{z_j+w_j}. If both parties must learn the output, Bob sends one additional answer bit, for a bound of 2t+2. The exact scope uses the standard one-output convention for the upper endpoint 2t+1.

For completeness the rank lower bound follows from any deterministic c-bit protocol: its at most 2^c transcript rectangles partition the input matrix. Under a both-output convention they are monochromatic and rank at most one. Under the one-output convention Bob’s final sign may depend on his private y as well as the transcript, but it is constant across all Alice inputs sharing that transcript; the signed rectangle is still an outer product of an Alice indicator and a Bob sign vector, hence rank at most one. Thus rank M≤2^c and c≥log₂rank M=2t. This proves the stated one-output communication interval and a both-output interval [2t,2t+2]. The family therefore satisfies log-rank almost tightly although Fourier dimension alone misses it. This is an exact structural benchmark, not a counterexample to the conjecture, a new general upper bound, or a claim that arbitrary low-rank matrices are XOR matrices.
