# Exact gluing of six-matching covers across two- and three-edge cuts

**Claim type: Literature report.** Write \(\mathrm{BF}(G)\) for the existence of six indexed perfect matchings, repeats permitted, covering each edge of the loopless cubic multigraph \(G\) exactly twice. We give exact restriction and gluing proofs across cuts of size two and three. They reduce the prompt to cyclically 4-edge-connected pieces and provide a way to assemble actual six-matching certificates from such pieces.

These are structural reductions, not a new best connectivity reduction. Máčajová and Mazzuoccolo prove the stronger published reduction to cyclically **5**-edge-connected snarks, *Proceedings AMS*148(2020),4643–4652, DOI10.1090/proc/15057. Their author's 2019 slides and primary repository abstract were checked; their complete published proof is not asserted to have been retrieved. Our complete proof below needs no black-box use of that stronger theorem.

## Conventions and cut parity

Work first in a connected bridgeless cubic multigraph \(G\). The disconnected case is componentwise: six covers on different components can be indexed arbitrarily and united.

For any perfect matching \(M\) and \(X\subseteq V(G)\),
\[
 |X|=2|M\cap E(G[X])|+|M\cap\delta(X)|. \tag{1}
\]
Also \(3|X|=2|E(G[X])|+|\delta(X)|\), so \(|X|\equiv|\delta(X)|\pmod2\).

A cut of size two or three in a connected bridgeless graph has connected shores. Otherwise each of two components in one shore has a nonempty boundary, each boundary has size at least two because there are no bridges, and the total is at least four.

All edge replacements below preserve labels: a new edge remains distinct even when parallel to an existing one.

## Two-edge cuts: exact equivalence

Let \(\delta(X)=\{x_1y_1,x_2y_2\}\), with \(x_i\in X\). The endpoints \(x_1,x_2\) are distinct, as are \(y_1,y_2\). If, for example, both cut edges meet the same vertex \(x\), the sole remaining edge at \(x\) would be a bridge to \(X-\{x\}\). That set is nonempty because a loopless cubic singleton cannot have a boundary of size two.

Form \(G_X\) from \(G[X]\) by adding a new edge \(f_X=x_1x_2\); define \(G_Y\) similarly on the other shore. Both are loopless cubic multigraphs and bridgeless. To see the latter, an alternate path in \(G\) avoiding any given edge can have its excursion through the opposite shore replaced by the new edge. The new edge itself has an alternate path through the connected shore.

We claim
\[
 \mathrm{BF}(G)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathrm{BF}(G_X)\ \text{and}\ \mathrm{BF}(G_Y). \tag{2}
\]
The shore \(X\) has even order by (1)'s parity observation. Every perfect matching meets the two-edge cut in either zero or both edges. In a BF cover exactly two of the six matchings use both edges, since each cut edge has multiplicity two. Restrict each matching to \(G[X]\), adding \(f_X\) precisely for those two matchings; this gives a BF cover of \(G_X\), and likewise of \(G_Y\).

Conversely, in a BF cover of each closed shore, its new edge belongs to exactly two indices. Permute the six indices on one shore so these two indices agree with those on the other. At those indices delete both new edges and insert the two original crossing edges. At each of the other four indices simply unite the two matchings. Every vertex is matched once, every internal edge retains multiplicity two, and both crossing edges occur exactly at the aligned two indices. This proves (2), including the actual gluing prescription.

## Three-edge cuts: exact equivalence

Let \(\delta(X)=\{x_jy_j:1\le j\le3\}\) be nontrivial, so neither shore is a single vertex. Form \(G_X\) by replacing the other shore by a new vertex \(z_Y\), retaining the three labelled edges \(x_jz_Y\). Define \(G_Y\) symmetrically. Both are loopless cubic multigraphs. They are bridgeless because contraction of a connected shore preserves an alternate path for every retained nonloop edge.

Again,
\[
 \mathrm{BF}(G)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \mathrm{BF}(G_X)\ \text{and}\ \mathrm{BF}(G_Y). \tag{3}
\]
Here \(|X|\) is odd. By (1), each perfect matching meets the cut in one or three edges. In a BF cover the sum of its six cut-intersection sizes is \(2\cdot3=6\). All six are positive odd integers, so all equal one. Restriction to either shore, with the unique used cut edge represented by the edge at the new vertex, yields its six-cover.

Conversely, at the degree-three new vertex of a closed shore each matching chooses exactly one of its three incident edges. Since each edge has multiplicity two, the six indices are partitioned into three labelled pairs \(I_1,I_2,I_3\). On the other shore the same three labels partition its indices into three pairs. Choose a permutation of its six indices matching these pairs label by label. For an aligned index in \(I_j\), remove the two new-vertex edges, unite the remaining matching edges and insert \(x_jy_j\). This matches every original vertex exactly once. Each internal edge remains twice covered, and each cut edge is used at its pair of indices. Thus (3) follows.

The necessary one-edge intersection condition, observed in the earlier contribution, is therefore enough for full gluing once the two closed-shore covers exist. Mere arbitrary indexing of two cover lists would not guarantee compatibility; the label-respecting permutation is essential.

## Termination and precise reduced problem

For a nonempty shore \(X\) inducing a forest with \(c\ge1\) components,
\[
 |\delta(X)|=3|X|-2(|X|-c)=|X|+2c. \tag{4}
\]
Consequently a two-edge cut has no acyclic shore; a three-edge cut with an acyclic shore has exactly one vertex on that shore. Thus every two-edge cut and every nontrivial three-edge cut in a bridgeless cubic graph is cyclic, meaning both shores contain a cycle. This includes multigraph cycles of length two.

Apply (2) or (3) whenever possible. Every resulting piece has strictly smaller order: for a two-cut its order is the size of a proper shore; for a nontrivial three-cut it is that shore's size plus one, while the opposite shore has at least three vertices by parity and nontriviality. Repetition terminates.

Every final piece has no cyclic cut of size at most three, so is cyclically 4-edge-connected. Covers on these pieces can be glued backwards with the explicitly described index permutations. Conversely any original cover restricts to covers on all pieces. This proves the equivalence of the unrestricted conjecture with its restriction to these pieces, without resolving the existence question on them.

## Scope, prior work and verification

Math-1082-A1 supplies the three-edge-colourable/bipartite case, a Petersen certificate and the necessary three-cut intersection observation. It does not give either exact gluing equivalence or the terminating decomposition. The present report proves those global certificate operations; it does not count repeated finite examples as universal progress.

The stronger known cyclically5-connected reduction is credited so that this exposition is not mistaken for a new structural theorem or the sharpest reduction. No executable or empirical evidence is claimed. The unresolved core is constructing six covers on arbitrary cyclically highly connected non-three-edge-colourable cubic graphs.
