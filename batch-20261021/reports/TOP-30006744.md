# The randomized partial-MCSP hardness theorem and its exact totalization gap

**Exact scope.** Hirahara’s 2022 Theorem 1.2 is reported with its explicit partial truth table, randomized one-query reduction, circuit-size gap and input-length normalization. Its implication for partial-MCSP approximation is derived, and a precise sufficient deterministic totalization condition is stated. No deterministic NP-hardness of ordinary total MCSP is claimed.

**Checked primary theorem.** Hirahara's TR22-119, dated 24 August 2022, Theorem 1.2, gives a universal constant γ>0 and randomized polynomial-time one-query NP-hardness for an explicitly listed partial table f:{0,1}^n→{0,1,*}, a size parameter s and a distribution D supported on specified rows. In the yes case an s-time program of size s and a circuit of size s/log s and depth O(log s) agree with f with D-probability one. In the no case every circuit of size at most (s/log s)n^γ agrees with probability at most 1/2+n^{-γ}; the source also states the corresponding program bound. Its definitions of program access and time are not ordinary circuit definitions and are not interchanged here.

These parameters are a deep literature theorem, credited in full. The proof uses algorithmic information extraction and a compact secret-sharing construction, rather than a deterministic explicit circuit lower bound. This report does not independently certify that entire construction. The source itself emphasizes that n=O(log N₀) when the source NP input has length N₀: the reduction prints 2^n partial-table entries in polynomial time. Treating n as the printed input length would obscure the substantial reduction-size constraint.

Here is the circuit consequence with its quantifiers exposed. Put a=s/log s and b=a n^γ. For sufficiently large n, 1/2+n^{-γ}<1. If every row having positive D-mass is specified, a circuit exactly consistent with the entire partial table would have agreement one. Therefore the no-case agreement statement excludes all exactly consistent circuits of size at most b, whereas the source's yes circuit satisfies every positive-mass specified row. For the literal MCSP* corollary one uses the support/table construction supplied in §8, which the theorem explicitly states yields MCSP* hardness; mere containment supp(D)⊆specified rows alone would not ensure a yes circuit satisfies other specified rows. This distinction prevents silently inferring the stronger corollary from an incomplete support hypothesis.

The source's explicit conclusion is that MCSP* is NP-hard, under randomized polynomial-time reductions, to approximate within (log N)^γ' for a universal γ'>0, where N is the explicit partial-table input length (polynomially related to 2^n and the parameter encodings). The inverse-polynomial soundness correction and circuit-description logarithm are already accounted for in that stated corollary. Constants can be weakened and finite exceptional lengths absorbed. This is not a polynomial-factor-in-N hardness claim.

**What would transfer to the literal target?** Suppose a deterministic polynomial-time map T takes every explicit partial-table gap instance (p,a,b) to one explicit total table F_p and integer threshold t, with
C*(p)≤a ⇒ C(F_p)≤t, and C*(p)>b ⇒ C(F_p)>t.
Here C* is minimum unrestricted circuit size over completions, and all output lengths, including the 2^{number of inputs of F_p} table, must be polynomial in the partial input length. Composing T with the theorem would give randomized hardness for total MCSP. It would still not produce the **deterministic** NP-hardness asked in the prompt: the upstream reduction remains randomized. A deterministic partial gap reduction of the corresponding strength, together with T, would be sufficient for deterministic hardness. Neither missing ingredient is supplied by the literature theorem stated above.
