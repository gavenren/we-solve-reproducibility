# Two all-dimensional benchmarks for average cell diameter

This report concerns the equally weighted mean over bounded cells, with graph diameter and the full general-position assumptions in the prompt. The results below are credited consequences of Deza–Xie, arXiv:0710.0328v1, Section4; they do not settle arbitrary arrangements with arbitrarily many hyperplanes.

For every dimension $d\ge1$ and every simple arrangement of exactly $d+2$ hyperplanes, the mean is
\[
\overline\delta=\frac{2d}{d+1}.
\]
Deza–Xie's Proposition7 gives the required arrangement classification: there are two simplex cells and $d-1$ remaining cells, each a product of two positive-dimensional simplices. In odd dimension these products occur in complementary pairs; in even dimension the middle product occurs once. This classification, rather than a bound on a selected cell, is the imported geometric theorem.

Here is the graph calculation translating that classification into the prompt's statistic. A simplex has complete graph, hence diameter one. The graph of a Cartesian product of two polytopes is the Cartesian product of their graphs: an edge changes exactly one factor along an edge. Shortest-path distances therefore add. Both nontrivial simplex factors have diameter one, so every other cell has diameter two. The cell count is $\binom{d+1}{d}=d+1$, and the sum of diameters is $2+2(d-1)=2d$. For $d=1$ the remaining family is empty and the same formula gives one. For $d=2,3$ it gives $4/3,3/2$ respectively. Thus this infinite family of dimensions satisfies the conjecture strictly when $d>1$.

There is also a useful quantitative statement in the opposite regime. Proposition8 constructs, for $n\ge2d$, a simple cyclic-type arrangement containing $\binom{n-d}{d}$ cubical bounded cells out of $I=\binom{n-1}{d}$ bounded cells. Each cube has graph diameter $d$, and every remaining full-dimensional cell has diameter at least one. Consequently this particular arrangement satisfies
\[
\overline\delta\ge1+(d-1)\frac{\binom{n-d}{d}}{\binom{n-1}{d}}.
\]
This improves the estimate obtained by discarding the noncubical cells entirely. The ratio is
\[
\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}\left(1-\frac{d-1}{n-1-j}\right).
\]
All factors belong to $[0,1]$. Induction on the number of factors gives $\prod_j(1-a_j)\ge1-\sum_j a_j$ there. Since $n-1-j\ge n-d$, we obtain the completely explicit estimate
\[
\overline\delta\ge d-\frac{d(d-1)^2}{n-d}.
\]
For example, any integer $n\ge\max(2d,d+d(d-1)^2/\varepsilon)$ supplies an arrangement with mean at least $d-\varepsilon$. This is an existence statement about specified arrangements, not an upper bound for all arrangements. The geometric construction and its cubical-cell count are imported; the graph calculation and quantitative error estimate are supplied here.

The present report instead identifies the precise primary propositions, transfers their combinatorics to the literal averaging convention, and quantifies the asymptotic approach.
