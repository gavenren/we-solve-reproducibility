# What simultaneous approximation proves about Euler’s constant

Claim type: **Literature report**.

The literal question concerns $\gamma$ alone. A published theorem of Rivoal controls it jointly with the Euler–Gompertz constant
\[
\delta=\int_0^\infty\frac{e^{-t}}{1+t}\,dt.
\]
We state the precise input, derive two useful consequences with their conditional status intact, and distinguish this route from finite-denominator exclusion.

**Credited primary theorem.** Rivoal's Theorem1(ii), specialized to $z=1$, gives, for every $\varepsilon>0$, a constant $c_\varepsilon>0$ such that
\[
\left|\gamma-\frac pq\right|+
\left|\delta-\frac rq\right|
 \ge c_\varepsilon H^{-3-\varepsilon},
\quad H=\max(|p|,|q|,|r|),\qquad
p,q,r\in\mathbb Z,\ q\ne0. \tag{1}
\]
Theorem2(ii) at the same parameter gives
\[
\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb Q(e,\gamma,\delta)\ge2. \tag{2}
\]
These are deep published results, respectively from Hermite–Padé approximation and algebraic independence of values of $E$-functions. We use them as theorems and do not claim their proofs here. The qualitative theorem also allows algebraic $z\notin(-\infty,0]$; the quantitative theorem has the stricter hypothesis $z>0$ rational. At $z=1$ both apply.

**Conditional one-number irrationality measure.** If $\gamma$ were rational, then $\delta$ would be transcendental and its irrationality exponent would be at most three. Conversely, if $\delta$ were rational, the same two conclusions would hold for $\gamma$.

Here is the denominator argument, which is necessary because (1) is a simultaneous statement. Suppose $\gamma=a/b$, with integers $a$, $b>0$. Given any $r\in\mathbb Z$, $q\ge1$, substitute
\[
(p',q',r')=(aq,bq,br)
\]
in (1). The first error is exactly zero, while the second is $|\delta-r/q|$. When $|\delta-r/q|<1$, we have $|r|\le(|\delta|+1)q$, so
\[
H'\le C_{a,b,\delta}q.
\]
It follows that
\[
|\delta-r/q|\ge c'_\varepsilon q^{-3-\varepsilon}. \tag{3}
\]
When the error is at least one the same bound follows after reducing the positive constant. Thus (3) holds for all rational approximants, not just denominators divisible by $b$. The standard definition of irrationality exponent then gives $\mu(\delta)\le3$. Equation(2), together with algebraicity of $\gamma$, gives
\[
\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb Q(e,\delta)=2,
\]
so $\delta$ is transcendental, indeed algebraically independent of $e$. Interchanging the two coordinates in this argument proves the converse conditional assertion.

Consequently the following are precise sufficient conditions for the requested irrationality of $\gamma$: rationality of $\delta$, or a proof that the irrational number $\delta$ has irrationality exponent greater than three. Neither premise is established here. Equation(2) also proves that $\gamma,\delta$ cannot both be algebraic, which is stronger than merely ruling out their simultaneous rationality, but it still does not select which number is transcendental.

**Why the auxiliary constant cannot be silently eliminated.** The analytic identity underlying the argument is
\[
\gamma=-\mathcal E(-1)-e^{-1}\delta,\qquad
\mathcal E(z)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{z^n}{n\,n!}. \tag{4}
\]
The series is entire by the ratio test. For completeness, (4) can be checked directly: termwise integration of the uniformly convergent exponential expansion gives
\[
\int_0^1e^{-t}\log t\,dt
 =\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n!(n+1)^2}
 =\mathcal E(-1).
\]
The interchange is justified by absolute integrability of the summed absolute series. Also
\[
\int_1^\infty e^{-t}\log t\,dt
 =\int_1^\infty\frac{e^{-t}}t\,dt=e^{-1}\delta
\]
by integration by parts and substitution. Finally $\Gamma'(1)=-\gamma$ yields (4). This classical identity is also Proposition1(ii) in the primary paper.

Even knowing that $\mathcal E(-1)$ is transcendental does not make both terms on the right independently algebraic or force their sum to be irrational. The simultaneous theorem retains exactly this dependence instead of discarding it.

The earlier contribution supplies signed harmonic remainders, rational intervals and a finite exclusion up to denominator100000. We have read that full attempt. The present account instead uses published simultaneous approximation and proves its one-coordinate conditional consequences, including the common-denominator rescaling. No new individual irrationality theorem, numerical record, or full solution is asserted. The unconditional status of $\gamma$ alone is left unresolved by these results.
