# Generic unmarked simple-length rigidity and the full-spectrum comparison

**Problem:** TOP-2764

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** For each fixed closed connected orientable surface S of genus at least two, the identified Baik–Choi–Kim theorem gives a meagre exceptional set V⊂T(S) such that X∉V and equality of unmarked simple-length multisets with arbitrary Y∈T(S) imply isometry, possibly orientation reversing. This leaves the exceptional locus and comparisons across different topological types unresolved.

The following quantifier is stronger than saying that two generic surfaces can be distinguished. In the identified version of Baik–Choi–Kim, Theorem 1.2 gives, for a closed orientable \(S=S_g\), \(g\ge2\), a meagre set \(V\subset\mathcal T(S)\) such that
\[
 X\notin V,\quad Y\in\mathcal T(S),\quad
 \mathcal L_{\rm simp}(X)=\mathcal L_{\rm simp}(Y)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad X\cong Y .
\]
Only the first metric must be generic. The resulting isometry may reverse orientation, exactly as allowed by the problem.

This is an imported substantial rigidity theorem. Its method goes beyond the generic absence of repeated simple lengths: identities among lengths recover certain intersecting configurations of curves, which permit reconstruction of the hyperbolic surface. Distinct lengths by themselves supply a bijection between two lists, not a proof that this bijection preserves disjointness or intersection. Thus marked length rigidity cannot simply be applied to an arbitrary matching of the two multisets.

There is also a precise obstruction to a common proposed shortcut. Aougab–Lahn–Loving–Miller's Theorem 1 constructs finite covers of every closed negative-Euler-characteristic surface which are full-length-isospectral over the base but generically have different simple length spectra. Their Corollary 2 gives equal full spectra while the bounded-self-intersection spectra differ for every finite bound. Accordingly an ordinary isospectral nonisometric pair is not automatically a counterexample to this problem.

The elementary reason the data cannot be filtered from a bare full spectrum is that it lists lengths and multiplicities, without identifying which closed geodesic has a self-intersection or is an iterate. The property of being simple belongs to the missing curve class. The cited examples turn that warning into actual geometric pairs.

The theorem above settles the fixed-type question off a precisely described exceptional set. It does not show that every point of that exceptional set has a partner, that no point does, or that the exceptional set is empty. It also does not supply an isometry from agreement below any finite cutoff. The remaining all-surfaces assertion in the literal prompt is therefore not claimed.
