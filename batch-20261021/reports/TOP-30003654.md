# A squarefree obstruction and sharp multiplier conditioning

The literal source omits the exact ambient nonnegative cone and certificate identity. We therefore state both explicitly: take$S=\mathbb R^{k+m}$ and ask for
\[
p=q+r h,\qquad q\ge0\text{ on all of }S,
\]
with unrestricted real polynomials$q,r$. The results below concern this natural specified certificate, not every possible copositive formulation.

Let$k\ge2$, let$A$ be a symmetric positive-definite real$k\times k$ matrix, set$h(x,y)=x^TAx$, and choose a nonzero vector$b\in\mathbb R^k$. The feasible variety is$\{x=0\}\times\mathbb R^m$, on which$p(x,y)=b\cdot x$ is identically zero. Nevertheless no such certificate exists.

Indeed, on the variety we would have$q(0,y)=0$. A globally nonnegative differentiable function attaining zero at an interior point has zero gradient, so$\nabla_xq(0,y)=0$. Also$h(0,y)=0$ and$\nabla_xh(0,y)=0$, hence$\nabla_x(rh)(0,y)=0$. Differentiating the asserted identity would give$b=0$, a contradiction. This works for every multiplier degree and even for smooth multipliers, not just a fixed-degree relaxation.

Unlike the earlier$x^2$ example, this$h$ is squarefree and irreducible over$\mathbb R$. After an invertible linear change it is$\sum_{i=1}^k x_i^2$. A nontrivial factorization of this degree-two polynomial would have two real linear factors, whose zero hyperplanes would be contained in$\{x=0\}$, impossible for$k\ge2$. Thus ordinary radicality of the principal ideal is insufficient; the real zero set has vanishing ideal containing each$x_i$, while$(h)$ does not. The first-order obstruction survives removal of repeated factors.

There is a sharp quantitative counterpart. For$\delta>0$, use a constant correction and put
\[
q_\delta=\delta+b\cdot x+c\,x^TAx,\qquad r_\delta=-c.
\]
Completing the square shows that$q_\delta$ is globally nonnegative exactly when
\[
c\ge \frac{b^TA^{-1}b}{4\delta}.
\]
For$c>0$, its minimum is$\delta-(b^TA^{-1}b)/(4c)$, attained at$x=-A^{-1}b/(2c)$; for$c\le0$, the nonzero linear term or a negative quadratic direction rules out global nonnegativity. At equality this gives an exact degree-two certificate for$p_\delta=\delta+b\cdot x$. Thus strictly positive feasible values admit certificates of fixed degree while the least constant correction diverges exactly as$1/\delta$. The bound optimizes constant corrections only, not all polynomial multipliers.

The earlier complete attempt gives the repeated-factor obstruction$h=x^2,p=x$, approximate certificates and fixed-degree coefficient blow-up. The added result here is the irreducible squarefree positive-definite family and its exact inverse-matrix conditioning constant. This explicitly repairs the tempting but invalid inference that merely making a defining polynomial squarefree removes the obstruction. It does not identify the missing original cone or solve the source's broad unbounded-set question.
