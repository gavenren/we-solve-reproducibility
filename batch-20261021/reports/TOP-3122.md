# The exact threshold-nine claw-free theorem with its list-coloring boundary

Problem: TOP-3122  
Claim type: Literature report  
Author: /root/hundred_graphs

**Exact scope.** Cranston–Rabern's published theorem [1] states that every finite simple claw-free graph G with
\[
 \Delta(G)\ge9,\qquad \omega(G)\le\Delta(G)-1
\]
has
\[
 \chi(G)\le\Delta(G)-1. \tag{1}
\]
This is the hard clique-free case of the literal Borodin–Kostochka bound, at the exact degree threshold nine, on the entire claw-free class. It is a credited theorem checked from its primary publisher statement and the full primary manuscript [1a], not a new proof. The full primary manuscript was retrieved; its theorem statement and proof framework were inspected, while its deep structural proof is not independently reproduced or certified.

A claw is an induced \(K_{1,3}\): a vertex with three pairwise nonadjacent neighbors. “Claw-free” forbids this induced pattern, not every ordinary three-edge star. In particular dense neighborhoods are allowed, maximum degree is unbounded, and the theorem is not a maximum-degree-three result.

The retained earlier A1 develops high-degree critical cores, Kempe paths, low-degeneracy coloring and perfect elimination orderings. Its cited correspondence theorem has a very large degree threshold. The claw-free theorem supplies a different substantial regime: the literal threshold9, no elimination-order hypothesis and no fixed upper bound on clique number. The other prior claim summary about fixed forbidden cliques is neither assumed nor substituted for this result.

The theorem's two numerical boundaries admit complete sharpness checks.

First, let \(H=C_5[K_3]\): replace each vertex i of a five-cycle by a three-vertex clique \(\{(i,0),(i,1),(i,2)\}\), and join all vertices of consecutive cliques. Its independence number is2: an independent set uses at most one vertex in a clique, and its class indices must form an independent set in \(C_5\). Thus H is claw-free. Every vertex has degree \(2+3+3=8\). A clique uses at most two adjacent classes, so \(\omega(H)=6\).

Every color class has size at most2, so \(\chi(H)\ge\lceil15/2\rceil=8\). To attain8, use the following seven independent pairs as color classes, and leave \((4,2)\) as a singleton:
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \{(0,0),(2,0)\},\quad \{(1,0),(3,0)\},\\
 \{(0,1),(2,1)\},\quad \{(1,1),(3,1)\},\\
 \{(4,0),(1,2)\},\quad \{(4,1),(2,2)\},\quad
 \{(0,2),(3,2)\}.
\end{gathered}
\]
Each pair has class indices at cyclic distance2, hence is nonadjacent; the seven pairs and singleton partition all vertices. Therefore \(\chi(H)=8\), while
\(\max\{\Delta(H)-1,\omega(H)\}=7\). The degree threshold in (1) cannot be lowered from9 to8, even within claw-free graphs.

Second, for t≥5 let \(J_t=K_t\vee C_5\), the join that adds every edge between a t-clique and a five-cycle. Again its independence number is2, so it is claw-free. Clique vertices have degree t+4, cycle vertices have degree t+2, and
\[
 \Delta(J_t)=t+4,\qquad \omega(J_t)=t+2,\qquad
 \chi(J_t)=t+3=\Delta(J_t)-1.
\]
The chromatic equation follows because the two joined parts need disjoint palettes, with t colors on the clique and3 on the odd cycle. A maximum clique uses the t-clique and a cycle edge. This family proves that, at arbitrarily large degrees, the conclusion cannot be strengthened to
\(\chi\le\max\{\omega,\Delta-2\}\), even on the stated class. It also has an induced five-cycle and hence admits no perfect elimination ordering: the earliest cycle vertex would have two nonadjacent later neighbors. The new class is therefore not contained in the earlier ordering proof.

A second problem-specific deduction explains the proof mechanism without pretending to reproduce the whole structural theorem. Suppose G is vertex-critical with \(\chi(G)=\Delta\), and let F be an induced subgraph of minimum degree at least2. Color G−F with \(\Delta-1\) colors, as criticality permits. For each v∈F remove from the common palette the colors occurring on neighbors outside F. Its residual list has size at least
\[
 (\Delta-1)-(d_G(v)-d_F(v))\ge d_F(v)-1. \tag{2}
\]
If F can be colored from every list assignment of these sizes, the residual coloring extends to all of G, a contradiction. Thus no such universally colorable induced F can occur in a critical obstruction. This is the \(d_1\)-choosable-subgraph reduction explained in [1a, §2.1], with all degree terms visible. It does not assume lists remain as large as their internal degrees: the lost one in (2) is essential. Finding the necessary reducible induced configurations and applying the claw-free structure theorem are deep parts of the credited paper, not completed here by the elementary list count.

Ordinary, list and correspondence coloring also require separate statements. The primary publisher abstract of the distinct paper [2] records a list-coloring analogue for claw-free graphs at \(\Delta\ge69\). That larger threshold must not be silently replaced by9. Ordinary coloring allows one common freely chosen palette; list coloring asks for a coloring from arbitrary prescribed lists; correspondence coloring permits edge-specific matchings of forbidden color pairs. The ordinary theorem (1) alone establishes neither stronger conclusion.

The report therefore certifies a broad, precisely delimited published resolution of the hard case at the literal threshold, clarifies the induced-forbidden pattern and distinguishes it from the earlier chordal and large-degree scopes. It gives no claim for all graphs of maximum degree at least9, and no independent certification of the deep claw-free structural proof.
