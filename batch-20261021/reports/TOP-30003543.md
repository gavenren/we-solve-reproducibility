# The published total-curvature reduction with the literal dimension constant preserved

**Problem:** TOP-30003543

**Claim type:** Conditional result

**Exact scope:** Under the precise all-convex-hypersurfaces total Gauss–Kronecker curvature hypothesis of Ghomi–Spruck Theorem7.1 in a Cartan–Hadamard m-manifold, derives the sharp Euclidean isoperimetric inequality and hence the weaker coefficient printed in the prompt; explains the finite-perimeter and dimension normalizations without claiming an unconditional all-dimensional theorem.

Write \(m=n+1\ge2\), and interpret \(\omega_m\) as the Euclidean unit \(m\)-ball volume. The prompt's displayed coefficient is \((m-1)^{-m/(m-1)}\omega_m^{-1/(m-1)}\). The usual sharp Euclidean coefficient is \(m^{-m/(m-1)}\omega_m^{-1/(m-1)}\). The literal inequality is therefore weaker, not stronger, than the standard sharp one. The report below keeps the literal statement and gives a precisely checked substantial sufficient hypothesis for it.

The imported theorem is Ghomi–Spruck's published Theorem7.1. Let \(M^m\) be complete, simply connected, with nonpositive sectional curvature. Assume that for every closed convex \(C^{1,1}\) hypersurface \(\Gamma\subset M\),
\[
 \mathcal G(\Gamma):=\int_\Gamma |\det A|\,d\sigma
       \ \ge\ |S^{m-1}|=m\omega_m.                    \tag{TC}
\]
Here \(A\) is the almost-everywhere-defined shape operator. The theorem concludes the sharp Euclidean isoperimetric inequality for bounded finite-perimeter regions, with equality only for regions isometric to Euclidean balls. The condition (TC) is an additional hypothesis, not a result asserted here in every dimension.

The argument is substantially more than applying arithmetic–geometric means to the boundary of an arbitrary domain. General boundaries need not be convex, and high-dimensional isoperimetric minimizers can have singularities. The source first works with isoperimetric regions inside a large geodesic ball \(B\), and relates their convex hulls to the original boundary. In its Lemma7.2, the part of the minimizing boundary meeting the hull is regular enough: singular points are separated from the hull boundary, while the obstacle boundary near \(\partial B\) has \(C^{1,1}\) regularity. Its convex-hull curvature result identifies the total curvature of the hull with the contribution from the original boundary where the two meet. These are deep geometric-measure-theoretic inputs from the source; they are not omitted assumptions of an elementary calculation.

To see the decisive numerical mechanism, let \(I_B(v)\) be the minimum perimeter at enclosed volume \(v\), and \(H_0(v)\) the normalized mean curvature of a minimizing region, using the convention that an Euclidean sphere of radius \(r\) has normalized mean curvature \(1/r\). On its convex-hull contact part, all principal curvatures are nonnegative and the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality gives
\[
 \det A\le H^{m-1}.
\]
The source's hull and regularity arguments, followed by (TC), yield
\[
 m\omega_m\le H_0(v)^{m-1}I_B(v).
\]
The first-variation identity is \(I'_B(v)=(m-1)H_0(v)\) at the relevant differentiability points. Consequently
\[
 \frac{d}{dv}\,I_B(v)^{m/(m-1)}
 \ge m(m\omega_m)^{1/(m-1)} .
\]
The integration/comparison justification for the profile, including its regularity, is part of the cited theorem. Starting at zero volume gives
\[
 I_B(v)^{m/(m-1)}
 \ge m(m\omega_m)^{1/(m-1)}v.
\]
Taking geodesic balls large enough to contain any chosen bounded region gives
\[
 |\Omega|\le
 m^{-m/(m-1)}\omega_m^{-1/(m-1)}
 |\partial\Omega|^{m/(m-1)}.                         \tag{1}
\]
For sufficiently regular regions, perimeter equals boundary hypersurface measure. Thus the finite-perimeter theorem includes the prompt's regularity regime.

Since \(m>m-1>0\), the coefficient in (1) is strictly smaller than the printed coefficient. Replacing it by the larger printed coefficient proves the literal requested inequality under (TC). For a nonempty bounded open region with positive volume, equality in the literal inequality cannot occur when (1) holds: its positive boundary measure makes the increase in coefficient strict. Equality for the sharp inequality, by contrast, is the Euclidean-ball rigidity stated by the source. These are different equality questions.

The assumption ranges over all convex hypersurfaces in the fixed ambient manifold. Verifying it just for small geodesic spheres, or just for the boundary of the region being tested, does not supply Theorem7.1. In particular, the comparison formula for smooth parallel hypersurfaces before the cut locus does not by itself prove that a global inward deformation remains smooth through all singularities. This is the missing general step that cannot be filled by a local curvature computation.

The result is conditional and fully credited. It neither asserts an unconditional all-dimensional resolution nor infers one from an announcement or from a normalization correction already noticed in the earlier contribution. It records the exact published route from a total-curvature statement to the literal isoperimetric target, including the dimensional constants and the analytic hypotheses that route requires.
