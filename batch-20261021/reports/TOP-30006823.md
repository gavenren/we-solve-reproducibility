# Majority lower bounds for every fixed power-of-two modulus

Problem: TOP-30006823  
Claim type: Partial result  
Author: /root/hundred_algorithms

**Exact scope.** Majority is outside nonuniform polynomial-size constant-depth circuits with AND, OR, NOT and MOD_{2^a} gates for every fixed a≥1. A full reduction of MOD_{2^a} to polynomial-size AC⁰[2] is proved, and the parity-modulus lower bound follows from the credited Smolensky–Szegedy approximate-degree lemma plus an explicit probabilistic-polynomial construction. Moduli with distinct prime factors remain outside this result.

We use one precise deep input: every polynomial over F₂ that agrees with majority on a fraction at least 9/10 of the Boolean cube has degree Ω(√n). This is the Smolensky–Szegedy lemma, stated as Lemma 3.2 in the checked primary Rossman–Srinivasan article. Its constant depends only on the fixed agreement advantage. Its full lower-bound proof is not independently certified here. The prompt uses the threshold ≥⌈n/2⌉, while that source uses strict majority; on odd n they coincide, and restricting any hypothesized all-length circuit family to odd lengths suffices for nonmembership.

Here is the transfer to arbitrary constant-depth parity circuits with unrestricted sharing. For OR of m Boolean inputs y, choose t independent uniform linear forms L_j(y) over F₂ and set
\[
 P(y)=1-\prod_{j=1}^t(1-L_j(y)).
\]
At zero this always equals zero. At any nonzero y the L_j(y) are independent fair bits, so it incorrectly equals zero with probability 2^{-t}. Thus P is a pointwise-error 2^{-t} probabilistic polynomial of degree t. AND follows by complementing all inputs and output. NOT is 1−y and a parity/MOD₂ gate is represented exactly by an affine polynomial (possibly complemented according to its zero-residue convention).

For a depth-d circuit with S≥1 gates, choose t=⌈log₂(10S)⌉ and independently replace every AND/OR gate by that construction, then substitute recursively. On each fixed input, the probability that any replaced gate errs, assuming its children are correct, is at most S·2^{-t}≤1/10. Gate sharing causes no extra events: there are S original gate computations, not an exponentially unfolded formula. The resulting output polynomial has degree at most t^d. Averaging its error over uniform inputs and then over its random coefficients shows that some fixed polynomial agrees with the exact circuit function on at least 9/10 of all inputs. If the circuit computes majority, the cited lemma forces t^d≥c√n. For each fixed d this excludes polynomial S, and indeed gives S≥exp(Ω_d(n^{1/(2d)})). This conservative exponent does not claim the sharp known bound.

Now let a≥1 be fixed and let a MOD_{2^a} gate see m Boolean input bits, with Hamming weight w. In F₂[z],
\[
 \prod_{i=1}^m(1+y_i z)=(1+z)^w
 =\prod_{j\ge0}(1+z^{2^j})^{w_j},
\]
where w_j are the binary digits of w. The coefficient of z^{2^j} is therefore w_j. On the left it is the parity of all conjunctions ∏_{i∈T}y_i with |T|=2^j. Consequently all the low a bits of w can be computed by parity gates over these conjunctions. The original MOD_{2^a} gate outputs one iff every such bit is zero. This gives a depth-three AND/parity/NOT construction of size m^{O(2^{a-1})}, with the a=1 case immediate.

Substitute this construction at every modular gate of a fixed-depth polynomial-size MOD_{2^a} circuit. Fan-in is polynomial in n and the original size under the standard wire encoding; repeated copies of the same wire can first have their multiplicity reduced modulo 2^a, so they cannot hide superpolynomial uncharged input multiplicities. Since a and original depth are fixed, the new parity circuit still has polynomial size and constant depth. The preceding majority lower bound contradicts its existence. This argument covers composite moduli 4,8,16,…, not just modulus two.

The elementary DNF/CNF and exact-real-degree arguments in the earlier attempt do not survive modular composition. The approximate F₂ polynomial mechanism used here does survive all permitted parity-circuit gates, which is the substantive extra ingredient. Combining gates of modulus 2^a with a modulus having a different prime divisor would leave this simulated class; no extension to MOD₆ or to all ACC⁰ is established. The literal majority-versus-all-fixed-moduli question is therefore only partially addressed.
