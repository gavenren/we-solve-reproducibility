# A rational upper bound and the finite-density conversion

**Claim type: Literature report.** Jeong, Park, Im, Lee and Yang prove in arXiv:2609.27495v2, Theorem1.1, that
\[
 \pi(K_4^{(3)})\le
 B:=\frac{14993367693127837}{26880000000000000}
 <0.557789.
 \tag{1}
\]
This is a2October2026 preprint theorem, with an exact flag-algebra certificate and an author-reported Lean formalization. We have read its statement and certificate criterion, but have not rerun that certificate or formalization. We derive the correct finite implication and separately optimize the prompt's entire two-part construction. Neither finite conjecture is claimed solved.

Let \(H\) be a \(K_4^{(3)}\)-free3-graph with \(N\ge1\) vertices and \(M\) edges. Replace each vertex by \(t\) independent clones and replace every original edge by all \(t^3\) triples with one clone at each of its three vertices. Call this balanced blow-up \(H_t\). It has \(Nt\) vertices and \(Mt^3\) edges. It remains \(K_4^{(3)}\)-free: a complete4-set cannot contain two clones of one original vertex, since any triple containing those two is absent; four distinct original vertices would instead give a \(K_4^{(3)}\) in \(H\).

By definition of the Turán density, and letting \(t\to\infty\),
\[
 \frac{6M}{N^3}
 =\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{Mt^3}{\binom{Nt}{3}}
 \le\pi(K_4^{(3)})\le B.
\]
Here existence of the usual density limit follows from the standard averaging fact that the maximum induced-subset edge density is nonincreasing with the order: a uniform smaller vertex set has the same expected edge density as its host. Consequently the unconditional finite consequence of(1) is
\[
 |E(H)|\le\left\lfloor\frac{B N^3}{6}\right\rfloor.
 \tag{2}
\]
It is **not** justified to replace \(N^3/6\) by \(\binom N3\) in(2) merely because the theorem is asymptotic.

At the prompt's order \(N=3n\),(2) becomes
\[
 |E(H)|\le\left\lfloor\frac92 Bn^3\right\rfloor.
\]
The target is \(5n^3/2-3n^2/2\). The difference before taking the floor is
\[
 \frac92(B-5/9)n^3+\frac32n^2>0,
\]
because \(9\cdot14993367693127837-5\cdot26880000000000000
=540309238150533>0\). Thus even the improved rational density bound has a genuine gap from the exact target, rather than a rounding issue.

For the second finite problem, take a partition into sizes \(a,b\), \(a+b=2n\), and include all triples meeting both parts. It is \(K_5^{(3)}\)-free because every5-set has at least3 vertices in one part, whose triple is absent. Its exact number of edges is
\[
 \binom a2b+a\binom b2
 =\frac{ab(a+b-2)}2=(n-1)ab.
\]
Since \(ab=n^2-(a-n)^2\), this is at most \(n^2(n-1)\), with equality exactly when \(a=b=n\) if \(n\ge2\). For \(n=1\), every such construction has0 edges. Hence balancing is optimal over the **entire two-part cross-triple template**, not just over a few examples. This is not an upper bound on all \(K_5^{(3)}\)-free hypergraphs, which may have no such partition.

For completeness the first witness's forbidden-clique check is also structural. With triple types \(XYZ,XXY,YYZ,ZZX\), a4-set with3 vertices in one part misses their triple. A distribution2+2 would require both opposite repeated-part types, only one of which is allowed. A distribution2+1+1 would require both repeated-part types from its doubled part, again impossible. These exhaust all distributions, so the balanced construction is indeed \(K_4^{(3)}\)-free with the claimed \(n^3+3n\binom n2\) edges.
