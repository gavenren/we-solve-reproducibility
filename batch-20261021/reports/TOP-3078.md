# A product bound for the unrestricted cube parameter

Keep exactly the prompt's unrestricted parameter $T(n)$: additional vertices and non-face-to-face intersections are permitted. We prove
\[
T(a+b)\le\binom{a+b}{a}T(a)T(b)\qquad(a,b\ge1).
\]
In particular, writing $n=4q+r$, $0\le r\le3$, gives
\[
T(n)\le n!\left(\frac23\right)^q
\begin{cases}1,&r=0,1,2,\\5/6,&r=3.\end{cases}
\]
This is a transparent constructive bound, not a new record. Haiman's related product-lifting method and stronger small-dimensional inputs are explicitly credited in the original problem discussion. The contribution here is a proof that also works with the prompt's unrestricted dissections, without assuming that they form a simplicial complex.

First consider a product of an $a$-simplex and a $b$-simplex. Each factor is affinely equivalent to an order simplex $0\le u_1\le\cdots\le u_a\le1$ or $0\le v_1\le\cdots\le v_b\le1$. Interleave the two ordered lists, preserving the order inside each list. Each of the $\binom{a+b}{a}$ interleavings gives a region
\[
0\le z_1\le\cdots\le z_{a+b}\le1,
\]
which is an $(a+b)$-simplex. Every pair of ordered lists has such an interleaving; when ties occur it can belong to several regions, but those intersections have empty full-dimensional interior. Affine transport supplies a decomposition of the original simplex product with exactly the stated number of pieces.

Now choose minimizing decompositions of $Q_a$ and $Q_b$. Their pairwise products cover $Q_a\times Q_b=Q_{a+b}$. Products of distinct source pieces have disjoint full-dimensional interiors: if a product interior met another, its two coordinate projections would lie in the interiors of both corresponding source simplices. Subdivide each product by the interleaving construction. This preserves coverage and disjoint interiors, so counting pieces proves the inequality. No agreement of triangulations along product boundaries is required under $T$'s definition.

Normalize $t(n)=T(n)/n!$. The product inequality becomes $t(a+b)\le t(a)t(b)$. The known decompositions $T(1)=1$, $T(2)=2$, $T(3)=5$, $T(4)=16$ therefore give the displayed formula. For completeness, the four-dimensional construction can be described without trusting a numerical search. Cut off the eight odd-parity cube vertices: each removed corner is a standard4-simplex of volume$1/24$. The convex hull of the eight even-parity vertices is a4-dimensional cross-polytope. Coning its facets not incident to a fixed vertex decomposes it into eight4-simplices. Thus sixteen pieces suffice. The parity-cut regions have disjoint interiors because two odd vertices differ in at least two coordinates; their strict corner inequalities cannot both hold. The remainder is exactly the even-parity hull, equivalently the demicube, which in dimension4 is the cross-polytope. This standard small-dimensional construction is recorded in the original source discussion and is used here as a credited input.

For multiples of four, the ratio to the elementary $n!$ decomposition is at most $(2/3)^{n/4}$ and hence decreases exponentially. The product inequality can instead use any independently established better low-dimensional decomposition; it does not certify such input values. In particular no corner-only lower bound from Glazyrin is silently transferred to unrestricted $T$: that paper expressly distinguishes additional-vertex dissections.

The earlier attempt gives the determinant lower bound and $T(3)=5$, but not this unrestricted product argument or its four-dimensional amplification. Neither the product bound nor its finite examples determine $T(n)$ exactly, and no equality between $T$ and $T^*$ is assumed.
