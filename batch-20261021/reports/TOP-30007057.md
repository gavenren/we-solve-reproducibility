# Numerical semiampleness of adjoints on every complex klt surface

Claim type: **Literature report**.

For a projective klt **surface** pair $(X,B)$ over $\mathbb C$, the conclusion in the prompt is a known theorem in full, without an extra bigness assumption. Lazić–Peternell, *On Generalised Abundance,I*, arXiv:1808.00438v2, CorollaryC, states that if $K_X+B$ is pseudo-effective and $L$ is nef rational Cartier, then the class of $K_X+B+tL$ is effective for every $t\ge0$; if $K_X+B+L$ is nef, that class contains a semiample rational divisor. Put $L=M$ and $t=1$. This gives exactly
\[
 D=K_X+B+M\equiv D',\qquad D'\text{ semiample and rational Cartier}.
\]
All surface klt pairs of the literal question over $\mathbb C$ are covered. No higher-dimensional assertion or arbitrary-field descent is claimed here.

A numerical conclusion is essential, even in this surface range. Let $E$ be a complex elliptic curve and choose a nontorsion line bundle $P\in\operatorname{Pic}^0(E)$. On $X=E\times E$ set $B=0$ and let $M$ be a Cartier divisor with line bundle $\operatorname{pr}_1^*P$. Then $K_X\sim0$ and $M\equiv0$, so both $M$ and $D=M$ are nef and $K_X+B$ is pseudo-effective. However for every $m>0$,
\[
 H^0(X,\mathcal O_X(mD))
 =H^0(E,P^{\otimes m})\otimes H^0(E,\mathcal O_E)=0. \tag{1}
\]
Indeed a nonzero section of a degree-zero line bundle on a smooth projective curve has an effective zero divisor of degree zero, hence no zeros, so trivializes the line bundle. Nontorsion excludes this for $P^{\otimes m}$. Thus $D$ itself is never semiample, whereas $D\equiv0$ and the zero divisor is semiample. This is an example **satisfying**, not refuting, the literal numerical assertion; it refutes the stronger unasked claim that $D$ itself must be semiample.

One part of the surface theorem explains why numerical representatives are the correct tool. Suppose $K_X+B\equiv0$, and let $L$ be nef Cartier. Theorem8.2 of the same source gives numerical semiampleness of $L$. Its proof obtains an effective rational $G\equiv L$. Choose a small rational $\delta>0$ so that $(X,B+\delta G)$ remains klt. Then
\[
 K_X+B+\delta G\equiv\delta L
\]
is nef. Surface log abundance makes this actual adjoint rational divisor semiample; rescaling it by $1/\delta$ yields a semiample rational representative of $L$. The effective representative is the nontrivial input; a numerically trivial twist cannot be discarded when claiming linear equivalence.

For example, in the smooth $q(X)=0$, $\chi(\mathcal O_X)\ge1$ subcase of that proof, an elementary Riemann–Roch calculation detects effectivity. For $L$ nef and numerically nonzero, $H^2(X,L)=H^0(X,K_X-L)=0$: an effective $K_X-L$ would have negative intersection with an ample class because $K_X\equiv-B$ and $L$ is nonzero nef. Also
\[
 \chi(X,L)=\tfrac12L\cdot(L-K_X)+\chi(\mathcal O_X)
 =\tfrac12L\cdot(L+B)+\chi(\mathcal O_X)\ge1.
\]
Thus $h^0(X,L)=\chi(X,L)+h^1(X,L)>0$. The irregular and remaining surface cases require the source's nef-reduction and surface arguments; the displayed calculation is not represented as their replacement.

The proof of the full surface-adjoint statement is credited to CorollaryC and Section8. Its hypotheses include pseudo-effectivity of $K_X+B$, which is not deleted merely because $D$ is nef. Rational coefficients also remain in force. The example(1) shows why replacing “numerically equivalent to some semiample divisor” by “semiample” would introduce a false conclusion in the simplest Calabi–Yau surface setting.
