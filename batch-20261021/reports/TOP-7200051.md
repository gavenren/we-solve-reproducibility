# The bound for arbitrary coordinatewise three-level sets

Let$S\subset\ell_1^d$ be equilateral, and suppose each coordinate assumes at most three different values on$S$. Then$|S|\le2d$. The allowed levels and their two gaps may be different in every coordinate. This includes the sharp cross-polytope configuration; it is not a bound for unrestricted coordinates.

We use a squared-Euclidean realization of each coordinate distance. For three levels$a<b<c$, map them respectively to
\[
(\sqrt{b-a},0),\quad(0,0),\quad(0,\sqrt{c-b}).
\]
The squared Euclidean distance between any pair is its original absolute difference, including$a$ versus$c$. For two levels use the one-dimensional map$0,\sqrt{b-a}$; for one level use no coordinate. Concatenating these maps gives an injective map$\Phi:S\to\mathbb R^D$, $D=\sum_i(k_i-1)\le2d$, such that
\[
\|\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)\|_2^2=\|x-y\|_1.
\]
If$m=|S|$, these are vertices of a Euclidean equilateral simplex, so$m\le D+1$. To see the affine independence directly, for coefficients$\lambda_j$ with sum zero the equal squared distance$a_0>0$ gives
\[
\left\|\sum_j\lambda_j\Phi(x_j)\right\|^2
=-\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}\lambda_i\lambda_j
\|\Phi(x_i)-\Phi(x_j)\|^2
=\tfrac{a_0}{2}\sum_j\lambda_j^2.
\]
Thus no nonzero affine dependence exists.

Suppose toward contradiction$m>2d$. Necessarily$m=2d+1$, $D=2d$, and every coordinate has all three levels. The images form a full-dimensional regular simplex in$\mathbb R^D$. Write$z_j=\Phi(x_j)$ and$\bar z=m^{-1}\sum_jz_j$. For a regular simplex its centered covariance is a scalar multiple of the identity:
\[
\frac1m\sum_j(z_j-\bar z)(z_j-\bar z)^{\mathsf T}
=\frac{a_0}{2m}I_D.
\]
This follows from the preceding affine Gram identity on the sum-zero subspace, whose dimension is$D$.

Choose the two feature coordinates from any original three-level coordinate. In every image at least one feature is zero, so their uncentered mixed second moment is zero. But both features occur positively somewhere, so both of their centroid coordinates are strictly positive. Their centered covariance entry is therefore the negative of a strictly positive product, contradicting the zero off-diagonal entry in the scalar covariance matrix. This excludes$m=2d+1$ and proves the claimed bound.

The argument includes$d=1$ and coordinates constant on$S$. It gives the stronger$m\le d+1$ if every coordinate has at most two levels. The sharp$2d$ set$\{\pm e_i\}$ uses exactly the three levels$-1,0,1$ in every active coordinate, so no improvement over$2d$ is possible for this subclass.

The primary Swanepoel paper supplies the conjecture and its unrestricted formulation. The proof here relies on the special orthogonal two-feature representation and a full-dimensional-simplex covariance obstruction. With four or more levels per coordinate the feature dimension can exceed$2d$, so the contradiction no longer follows. That limitation is not removed by a finite coordinate search.
