# Persistent higher-divisor errors: reconstruction of a conditional interval theorem

**Claim type: Literature report.** With the prompt's exact residue main term, let
\[
\Delta_k(x)=\sum_{n\le x}d_k(n)
-\operatorname{Res}_{s=1}\left(\zeta(s)^k\frac{x^s}{s}\right),
\qquad a=\frac{k-1}{2k}.
\]
The question asks for the optimal **pointwise** exponent $\alpha_k$. The following is a proof-level reconstruction of a different but directly relevant regime: large values at the conjectural scale persist throughout many intervals. The underlying theorem and moment estimates are due to Baluyot–Castillo [BC]. No novelty or improved record is claimed.

**Reconstructed conclusion.** Assume RH. For each fixed integer $k\ge3$ there are constants $c_k,c'_k>0$ such that, for every fixed
\[
0<\varepsilon<\eta_k:=\frac1{k(k-1)}
\]
and all sufficiently large $X$, at least $\gg_{k,\varepsilon}X^{\eta_k-\varepsilon}$ pairwise disjoint subintervals of $[X,2X]$ have common length
\[
H\asymp_k X^{1-1/k}(\log X)^{-k^2-2},                         \tag{1}
\]
and on each one $\Delta_k$ has a fixed sign and
\[
|\Delta_k(x)|>c'_k X^a.                                      \tag{2}
\]
Signs may differ between intervals. We do not claim both signs occur in this collection, or that its intervals are maximal.

## 1. The exact analytic inputs

Write $A=X^a$. The checked author version of [BC] supplies the following inputs; all constants below allow fixed $k$.

First, its equation (1.3) records Tong's mean-square asymptotic
\[
\int_X^{2X}\Delta_k(x)^2\,dx
 \sim C_k^2\int_X^{2X}x^{2a}\,dx,\quad
C_k=\frac1\pi\left(\frac1{2k}
 \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{d_k(n)^2}{n^{1+1/k}}\right)^{1/2}>0. \tag{3}
\]
It is unconditional for $k=3$, conditional on Lindelöf for $k\ge4$. We assume RH, which implies Lindelöf, throughout this reconstruction. In particular the left side is at least $b_k XA^2$ for a fixed $b_k>0$ and all large $X$.

Second, [BC, Theorem 1.6], under RH, gives uniformly for $1\le h\le X/8$,
\[
\int_X^{2X}|\Delta_k(x+h)-\Delta_k(x)|^2dx
 \ll_k Xh\bigl(\log(X/h)\bigr)^{k^2}.                          \tag{4}
\]
Third, [BC, Theorem 1.7], under Lindelöf, gives for every $\varepsilon>0$,
\[
\int_X^{2X}|\Delta_k(x)|^4dx
 \ll_{k,\varepsilon}X^{3-1/(k-1)+\varepsilon}.                 \tag{5}
\]
These substantial estimates are inputs, not independently established here. Their different hypotheses and the factor $X$ in the unnormalized integrals matter.

## 2. Turning increment means into a maximal estimate

Let $H=2^L$ be an integer power of two with $1\le H\le X/8$, and set
\[
M_H(x)=\sup_{0\le h\le H}|\Delta_k(x+h)-\Delta_k(x)|.
\]
Every integer $0\le r\le H$ has a binary decomposition. Telescoping along its aligned dyadic blocks uses at most one block of each length $2^j$. Cauchy–Schwarz followed by adding all possible blocks therefore proves the pointwise inequality
\[
\max_{0\le r\le H}|\Delta_k(x+r)-\Delta_k(x)|^2
\le(L+1)\sum_{j=0}^{L}\sum_{\nu=0}^{H/2^j-1}
 |\Delta_k(x+(\nu+1)2^j)-\Delta_k(x+\nu2^j)|^2.               \tag{6}
\]
The inner ranges contain integers. The case $r=H$ is its single top-length block.

After changing variables, each integral on the right is over a shifted interval contained in $[X,3X]$. Cover that interval by $[X,2X]$ and $[2X,4X]$ and apply (4) at the corresponding dyadic scales. Its contribution is
$\ll_k X\,2^j(\log X)^{k^2}$. There are $H/2^j$ blocks at level $j$, so both the sum over levels and the outer Cauchy factor cost $O((\log X)^2)$. This yields
\[
\int_X^{2X}\max_{0\le r\le H}
 |\Delta_k(x+r)-\Delta_k(x)|^2dx
 \ll_k XH(\log X)^{k^2+2}.                                   \tag{7}
\]

Passing from integer $r$ to real $h$ requires attention to jumps. For any fixed $\delta>0$, the elementary fixed-$k$ divisor bound is $d_k(n)\ll_{k,\delta}n^\delta$. A half-open unit interval contains at most one integer; the derivative of the residue main term is $O_k((\log X)^{k-1})$ on $[X,4X]$. Thus, for $r=\lfloor h\rfloor$,
\[
|\Delta_k(x+h)-\Delta_k(x+r)|
 \ll_{k,\delta}X^\delta+(\log X)^{k-1}.
\]
Consequently, fixing $0<\delta<a$,
\[
\int_X^{2X}M_H(x)^2dx
 \ll_k XH(\log X)^{k^2+2}+O_{k,\delta}(X^{1+2\delta}).          \tag{8}
\]
This establishes the needed maximal estimate with the actual discontinuous error function. It does not assume continuity at integers.

## 3. Positive mass that is stable over the whole interval

Choose $H$ to be the largest power of two at most
\[
c_k A^2(\log X)^{-k^2-2}.
\]
For sufficiently large $X$, it satisfies $1\le H\le X/8$ and (1). Choose $c_k$ small enough that (8) and $\delta<a$ give
\[
\|M_H\|_{L^2[X,2X]}\le\tfrac14\sqrt{b_k}\,A\sqrt X.
\]
Fix $0<\lambda\le\sqrt{b_k}/4$ and define
\[
G(x)=\bigl(|\Delta_k(x)|-M_H(x)-\lambda A\bigr)_+,\qquad
S=\{x\in[X,2X]:G(x)>0\}.
\]
Here $u_+=\max(u,0)$. Pointwise,
$|\Delta_k|\le G+M_H+\lambda A$, so the triangle inequality and (3) imply
\[
\|G\|_2\ge\tfrac12\sqrt{b_k}\,A\sqrt X.                       \tag{9}
\]
Also $0\le G\le|\Delta_k|$. Applying Cauchy–Schwarz on $S$ and then (5),
\[
|S|\ge\frac{(\int G^2)^2}{\int G^4}
 \gg_{k,\varepsilon}
 \frac{X^{4-2/k}}{X^{3-1/(k-1)+\varepsilon}}
 =X^{2a+\eta_k-\varepsilon}.                                \tag{10}
\]
This computation explains the interval-count exponent: it is the excess of the available fourth-moment exponent over the conjectural fourth-moment size, combined with the spatial persistence scale.

For every $x\in S$ and $0\le h\le H$,
\[
|\Delta_k(x+h)-\Delta_k(x)|<|\Delta_k(x)|-\lambda A.
\]
The whole value $\Delta_k(x+h)$ therefore stays on the same side of zero as $\Delta_k(x)$ and has magnitude greater than $\lambda A$. This **distance inequality**, rather than nonvanishing alone, handles the possibility of jumping across zero.

## 4. Packing genuinely disjoint intervals

Discard possible base points in $(2X-H,2X]$. Their measure is at most $H$, which is $o(|S|)$ by (10), (1) and $\varepsilon<\eta_k$. All remaining intervals $[x,x+H]$ lie in $[X,2X]$.

Partition the possible base points into cells of length $H$. At least a constant times $|S|/H$ cells meet the remaining set. Color their integer indices modulo three. One color contains at least one third of these cells; choosing one base point in each of them gives pairwise disjoint intervals, with room between them. Hence their number is
\[
\gg_{k,\varepsilon}\frac{|S|}{H}
 \gg_{k,\varepsilon}X^{\eta_k-\varepsilon}
       (\log X)^{k^2+2}
 \gg_{k,\varepsilon}X^{\eta_k-\varepsilon},
\]
as claimed. The proof reaches an unspecified positive amplitude constant; it does not optimize [BC]'s sharper threshold $(C_k/2-\varepsilon)x^a$.

## Relation to the literal open problem

This result gives persistence at the predicted scale; it supplies no pointwise upper bound of the form $x^{a+\varepsilon}$. Rare larger peaks are not excluded. Thus it does not determine $\alpha_k$ or prove its conjectured value under RH. In particular it is distinct from arithmetic bookkeeping for a $k=2$ exponent.

The interval theorem is credited to [BC, Theorem 1.1]; the argument above reconstructs its maximal, moment and packing mechanisms with a simpler amplitude threshold. For $k=3$, [BC, Theorem 1.8] uses additional third-moment information to improve the count to $X^{1/3-\varepsilon}$ at the same logarithmic length scale. Our general fourth-moment argument gives $X^{1/6-\varepsilon}$ in that case and is explicitly not the best available count.
