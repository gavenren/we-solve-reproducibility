# Donovan finiteness for normal defect via an explicit finite cocycle reduction

Claim type: **Literature report**.

Fix a finite$p$-group$D$and an algebraically closed field$k$of characteristic$p$. Donovan's conclusion holds for **all blocks whose defect group is normal in the ambient finite group**, with no bound on the ambient group order. The proof separates a credited structure theorem from the finiteness of its scalar twist parameters, which cannot be justified merely by saying the group is finite while$k$is infinite.

Külshammer's normal-defect theorem, in the form stated by Sambale[1, Theorem1.20], gives a Morita representative
\[
 k_\alpha[D\rtimes E], \tag{1}
\]
where$E$is a$p'$inertial quotient acting on$D$and$\alpha$is inflated from a2-cocycle on$E$with values in$k^\times$. The theorem is imported with credit; the report does not reprove the block-theoretic reduction or assume the block is itself an ordinary group algebra. In particular a nontrivial twist must be retained. The inertial quotient embeds in$\operatorname{Out}(D)$, and its action can be represented in$\operatorname{Aut}(D)$as in the semidirect-product form of the theorem. Its order$m$is bounded by$A=|\operatorname{Aut}(D)|$.

For each fixed finite group$E$of order$m$we prove directly that only finitely many cocycle classes can occur. Let$\alpha:E\times E\to k^\times$be a normalized cocycle, with trivial action on scalars. Thus
\[
 \alpha(g,h)\alpha(gh,x)=\alpha(h,x)\alpha(g,hx).
\]
Multiply this identity over$x\in E$and put$\beta(g)=\prod_x\alpha(g,x)$. Right translation permutes$E$, so
\[
 \alpha(g,h)^m=\frac{\beta(g)\beta(h)}{\beta(gh)}. \tag{2}
\]
Choose$\gamma(g)\in k^\times$with$\gamma(g)^m=\beta(g)$, using algebraic closedness, and choose$\gamma(1)=1$. Replace$\alpha$by the cohomologous cocycle
\[
 \alpha'(g,h)=\alpha(g,h)\frac{\gamma(gh)}{\gamma(g)\gamma(h)}.
\]
Equation(2) gives$\alpha'(g,h)^m=1$. Since$p\nmid m$, the group$\mu_m(k)$has exactly$m$elements. Hence there are at most$m^{m^2}$functions$E^2\to\mu_m(k)$, and in particular at most that many normalized cocycles or their cohomology classes. Cohomologous twists give isomorphic twisted algebras by rescaling the group basis, so this finite bound is sufficient. This proof makes no assumption that all roots of unity in$k$are confined to one finite set; it confines the needed roots to order$m$.

There are finitely many multiplication tables on sets of cardinality$m\le A$, and finitely many homomorphisms from each such group to the finite group$\operatorname{Aut}(D)$. Combining these with the cocycle bound proves finiteness of all representatives(1), hence of their Morita classes. For an intentionally coarse explicit overcount one may use
\[
 \sum_{m=1}^{A}m^{2m^2}A^m. \tag{3}
\]
Here one factor$m^{m^2}$overcounts all multiplication tables, $A^m$overcounts all actions, and the other$m^{m^2}$overcounts twist cocycles. Many of these data fail the required axioms or give the same algebra; overcounting cannot invalidate the upper bound. When$D=1$, $A=1$and the argument is compatible with the single defect-zero Morita class.

The theorem controls the **Morita class**, not the dimension of the original block algebra. Matrix enlargements can have the same Morita class and arbitrarily large dimensions. The representatives(1) do have dimension$m|D|\le A|D|$, but that fact alone would not imply finiteness over an infinite field without the twist argument.
