# Two defining-characteristic Sylow subgroups generate every projective special linear group

Claim type: **Partial result**.

We establish the repeated-prime assertion for **all ranks and fields** in the projective special linear family. Let $q=p^f$, $n\ge2$, and $G=\operatorname{PSL}_n(q)$. The images of the upper and lower unitriangular groups are Sylow $p$-subgroups and generate $G$. Thus whenever this $G$ is simple, it satisfies the prompt for the prescribed pair $(p,p)$. The proof also applies to the exceptional nonsimple small matrix groups, without using them as simple examples.

Write $e_{ij}(a)=I+aE_{ij}$ for $i\ne j$. Upper unitriangular matrices contain $e_{ij}(a)$ for $i<j$, and lower unitriangular matrices contain those for $i>j$. We show that these elementary matrices generate $\operatorname{SL}_n(q)$.

Left multiplication by $e_{ij}(a)$ adds $a$ times row$j$ to row$i$. Given an invertible matrix, a nonzero pivot can be placed into each successive diagonal position by determinant-one signed row interchange. On two coordinates the needed interchange matrix is
\[
 w(1)=e_{12}(1)e_{21}(-1)e_{12}(1)
 =\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Signs are harmless in characteristic2 as well. Row additions then eliminate entries below the pivot, and finally above it. This reduces a determinant-one matrix to a diagonal matrix $D=\operatorname{diag}(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$ with $\prod a_i=1$, using only elementary matrices.

Such a diagonal matrix is also generated by them. For $t\ne0$,
\[
 w(t)=e_{12}(t)e_{21}(-t^{-1})e_{12}(t)
 =\begin{pmatrix}0&t\\-t^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 w(t)w(-1)=\operatorname{diag}(t,t^{-1}).
\]
Embed this formula on any pair of coordinates. Products of the diagonal matrices with $a_i$ at position$i$ and $a_i^{-1}$ at position$n$, $1\le i<n$, give precisely $D$. Undoing the elimination proves generation. This is an explicit construction and does not assume that a pair of cyclic root subgroups generates over an arbitrary extension field: all parameters $a\in\mathbb F_q$ occur in the Sylow subgroups.

To verify the Sylow assertion, count
\[
|\operatorname{GL}_n(q)|=q^{n(n-1)/2}\prod_{i=1}^n(q^i-1),\qquad
|\operatorname{SL}_n(q)|=\frac{|\operatorname{GL}_n(q)|}{q-1}.
\]
Every factor $q^i-1$ is prime to$p$, so the $p$-part is $q^{n(n-1)/2}$, exactly the number of upper unitriangular matrices. The lower group has the same order. The scalar center of $\operatorname{SL}_n(q)$ has order dividing $q-1$, and hence is prime to$p$. Consequently the two images keep their orders in $\operatorname{PSL}_n(q)$ and are Sylow there. Surjective projection of the generation identity gives generation of the projective group.

The theorem keeps the prompt's existential choice and equal-prime option: the upper and lower subgroups are suitable choices, and generally are different. It proves generation by their union, **not** the set equality $G=UV$. Indeed $|UV|\le|U||V|$ can be far smaller than $|G|$. The ordinary Gaussian elimination proof is classical; no novelty is claimed. Its contribution here is a complete uniform witness in the equal-prime case that cannot be dismissed by taking the two requested Sylows to be identical. Prime pairs different from the defining characteristic and other simple families remain outside the result.
