# Weighted treewidth-two embeddings and exact K2,n distortion

Problem: TOP-1404  
Claim type: Literature report  
Author: /root/hundred_graphs

**Exact scope.** A credited theorem establishes the literal weighted GNRS conclusion for the entire \(K_4\)-minor-free family, with distortion below14. In addition, a complete cut-metric argument gives the exact least \(L_1\) distortion of every unit-edge \(K_{2,n}\), n≥2:
\[
 c_1(K_{2,n})=2-\frac{2\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor}{n(n-1)}. \tag{1}
\]
The constant tends to \(3/2\). This does not prove GNRS for arbitrary excluded minors or isometry for series-parallel metrics.

Gupta–Newman–Rabinovich–Sinclair [1, Theorem4.1] prove that a weighted graph of treewidth at most two, with shortest-path metric d, has an \(L_1\)-embeddable metric \(\widetilde d\) satisfying
\[
 C^{-1}d\le\widetilde d\le d,\qquad C<14.
\]
Their source treats nonnegative edge weights; the prompt's positive lengths are covered. Scaling by C gives the prompt's noncontracting normalization. Treewidth at most two is equivalently \(K_4\)-minor-free. The source's Corollary4.5 gives the matching uniform flow-cut statement. This deep embedding construction is credited, not re-proved here.

The constant below14 comes from Lemma4.2's contraction factor
\[
 a(\xi)=\frac{(1-\xi)(2\xi-1)}{1+\xi},\qquad \tfrac12<\xi<1,
\]
optimized at \(\xi=\sqrt3-1\). This theorem does not assert distortion2 for all series-parallel metrics. A special no-cut-removal subcase in Lemma4.4 has factor2, and the distinct terminal-relative-minor theorem4.6 has constant28; neither should replace Theorem4.1's stated hypotheses.

Here is a sharp calibration inside this family. Let u,v be the two vertices on one side of \(K_{2,n}\), and \(w_1,\ldots,w_n\) the other side. With unit edges, distances across the two parts equal1; distinct same-part distances equal2. Put
\[
 \beta=\frac{2\lfloor n^2/4\rfloor}{n(n-1)}.
\]
For n≥2, \(1/2<\beta\le1\).

Every finite \(L_1\) metric is a nonnegative sum of cut semimetrics. One direct justification suffices: for a real coordinate r, \(|r(x)-r(y)|\) equals the integral over thresholds t of
\(|1_{r(x)>t}-1_{r(y)>t}|\). For finitely many points only finitely many coordinate order intervals occur; coordinate integration and summation combine them into finitely many nonnegative cut weights. Thus it suffices to prove linear inequalities for individual cuts.

Average an arbitrary \(L_1\) metric over swapping u,v and permuting all w's. This remains an \(L_1\) metric and preserves any bounds \(d\le\rho\le Dd\), because each automorphism preserves d. Denote the resulting three distance values by
\[
 A=\rho(u,v),\quad B=\rho(w_i,w_j)\ (i\ne j),\quad
 C=\rho(u,w_i)=\rho(v,w_i).
\]
For a single cut, A,B,C mean the corresponding averages of its zero-one separation indicators. We claim
\[
 B\le2C-(1-\beta)A. \tag{2}
\]

If a cut separates u and v, then A=1, C=1/2. If exactly s of the n right vertices lie on one side, then
\[
 B=\frac{2s(n-s)}{n(n-1)}\le\beta,
\]
which is (2). If it does not separate u and v, complement the cut so both lie outside. If it contains s right vertices, then A=0, C=s/n, and
\[
 B=\frac{2s(n-s)}{n(n-1)}\le\frac{2s}{n}=2C.
\]
The s=0 case is immediate; for s≥1 the inequality uses \(n-s\le n-1\). This proves (2) for all cuts and hence for every \(L_1\) metric.

Suppose the embedding has been scaled to be noncontracting with distortion D. Then A≥2, B≥2 and C≤D. Substitution in (2) gives
\[
 2\le2D-2(1-\beta),\qquad D\ge2-\beta. \tag{3}
\]

For an embedding meeting this lower bound, put equal total cut weight2 on the cuts containing exactly one of u,v and exactly \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) of the w's, averaging over all choices and over u,v. Their induced values are
\[
 (A,B,C)=(2,2\beta,1).
\]
Also assign weight \(1-\beta\) to each singleton cut \(\{w_i\}\). These add
\[
 (0,2(1-\beta),1-\beta)
\]
to the three values. The complete metric therefore has
\[
 (A,B,C)=(2,2,2-\beta).
\]
Realize each cut of weight a by one coordinate taking values0 and a on its two sides. This is an explicit finite-dimensional \(L_1\) embedding. Its same-part distances are exact and its cross distances stretch by \(2-\beta\), proving equality in (3) and hence (1).

For example, n=2 gives an isometric four-cycle; n=3 and n=4 give4/3; n=5 and n=6 give7/5. More generally the limit is3/2. Since each \(K_{2,n}\) is \(K_4\)-minor-free, this proves that a uniform constant for that family cannot be less than3/2. It supplies no lower bound matching14 and does not claim this family determines the worst distortion among all treewidth-two graphs.
