# The integral Bloch statement for every surface below general type

Claim type: **Literature report**.

The exact **integral** statement holds for every smooth projective complex surface$S$ with$p_g(S)=0$ and Kodaira dimension$\kappa(S)<2$:
\[
 \operatorname{alb}_S:\mathrm{CH}_0(S)^0\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Alb}(S)(\mathbb C).
\]
In particular, if also$q(S)=0$, then$\deg:\mathrm{CH}_0(S)\cong\mathbb Z$. This is the theorem of Bloch–Kas–Lieberman [1], including all Enriques surfaces and the non-general-type elliptic cases. It is not restricted to rational surfaces. We explain the exact integral step, since replacing Chow groups by rational coefficients would discard part of the prompt.

Write$A_0(S)=\mathrm{CH}_0(S)^0$ and$T(S)=\ker\operatorname{alb}_S$. The primary paper proves that for each surface in this class there exists an integer$N>0$ with$N\,T(S)=0$. It then uses that$T(S)$ is divisible to conclude$T(S)=0$. This is valid because for any$z\in T(S)$, divisibility gives$y\in T(S)$ with$Ny=z$, and the uniform annihilator gives$Ny=0$. Torsion of each element with an exponent depending on that element would not by itself justify the same conclusion. Nor would mere divisibility rule out torsion, as$\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ shows.

Here is the source's divisibility mechanism. Every degree-zero zero-cycle can be placed on a smooth projective curve in$S$ after choosing a sufficiently ample smooth curve through its finite support. Its divisor class is in$\operatorname{Pic}^0(C)$, a divisible complex abelian group, so$A_0(S)$ is divisible. To pass divisibility to the kernel one additionally needs surjectivity on$N$-torsion:
\[
 A_0(S)[N]\longrightarrow\operatorname{Alb}(S)[N]. \tag{1}
\]
A smooth sufficiently ample hyperplane curve$C\subset S$ has$H_1(C,\mathbb Z)\to H_1(S,\mathbb Z)$ surjective by Lefschetz. After quotienting torsion and reducing modulo$N$, this gives surjectivity on the$N$-torsion of the complex Albanese tori. The image of$\operatorname{Pic}^0(C)[N]$ in$A_0(S)$ consists of$N$-torsion cycles, proving(1). Now if$z\in T(S)$, choose$y\in A_0(S)$ with$Ny=z$. Its Albanese image is$N$-torsion. Choose$c\in A_0(S)[N]$ with the same image by(1). Then$y-c\in T(S)$ and$N(y-c)=z$. This proves divisibility of the kernel without the false general inference that a kernel between divisible groups is automatically divisible.

The uniform annihilator is the substantial geometric part credited to [1]. For$\kappa<0$, classification reduces to rational or ruled surfaces and the cycle group reduces to that of a curve. For$0\le\kappa<2$, the paper passes through elliptic fibrations and the associated Jacobian fibration. Its correspondences multiply a zero-cycle by the square of a multisection degree; fiberwise Abel's theorem supplies the equality. The cases$q=0$ and$q=1$ are then analyzed separately to show the Jacobian-side kernel vanishes. This produces the **one fixed** annihilating integer for$T(S)$ required above. We have checked that argument's source statement and transfer, but do not present its classification-dependent geometry as a new proof.

Surjectivity of the Albanese map follows from the curve/Jacobian construction as well. When$q=0$, the Albanese variety is zero, so every degree-zero class vanishes; a complex point splits the degree map and gives$\mathrm{CH}_0(S)=\mathbb Z$. For an Enriques surface, this does not assert torsion-free integral cohomology or simple connectedness: the zero-cycle theorem does not erase the familiar topological torsion. The source explicitly notes this distinction.

Earlier A1 states a claimed all-$p_g=0$ theorem, including general type, without verified proof. Our report gives the entire classical non-general-type range with the exact integral deduction and its uniform-versus-elementwise torsion issue. It neither verifies nor dismisses the newer general-type claim. General-type surfaces with$p_g=0$ remain outside the theorem used here.
