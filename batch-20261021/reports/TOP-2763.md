# A withdrawn Powell proof and the surviving genus-four connectivity criterion

**Problem:** TOP-2763

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** Documents the explicit counterexamples invalidating Lemma 2.5 of the former all-genus Powell proof, and reports the 2026 genus-four reduction to connectivity for spheres avoiding a common nonseparating disk. Neither the failed lemma nor genus-three results are used to assert finite generation for g≥4.

An older title or abstract for arXiv:2406.13309 cannot be used to answer this question affirmatively. The current checked v3 is the authors' error note, *On the invalidity of Lemma 2.5 in our previous work on the Powell Conjecture*. It explicitly withdraws a key all-genus argument and constructs counterexamples to the lemma.

The false lemma said that, for disjoint essential disks \(D_1,\ldots,D_k\) in one handlebody and a disjoint nonseparating disk \(E\) in the other, there is a reducing sphere disjoint from all of them which separates the \(D_i\)'s from \(E\). The note gives configurations whose simultaneous compressions leave knotted tori. A putative separating reducing sphere would decompose the original splitting into unknotted Heegaard pieces, incompatible with the resulting knotted-exterior component. Section 2 extends the construction to every \(g\geq3\). Thus an induction assuming such a sphere always exists has a genuine geometric obstruction; merely adding more generators does not repair it.

There is a later, narrower positive theorem. Cho–Koda–Lee's April 2026 preprint proves the following genus-four criterion. Let \(\mathcal R(\Sigma_4)\) be the reducing-sphere complex. Then it is connected if and only if:
for every nonseparating compressing disk \(E\), any two reducing spheres disjoint from \(E\) determine vertices in the same component of \(\mathcal R(\Sigma_4)\) (Theorem 1.2).

The conclusion asks for a path in the full complex. It does **not** require all intermediate spheres to remain disjoint from \(E\), which would be a stronger statement about a subcomplex. The common-disk condition itself is still a hypothesis to prove, not an already established connectivity theorem.

The new geometric replacement also has an actual hypothesis. Lemma 1.1 says that a nonseparating weak reducing pair in genus four is separated by a reducing sphere if the surface compressed along that pair remains compressible to both sides. The source's proof then builds weak reducing sequences of nonseparating disks and uses these controlled sphere constructions to reduce general connectivity to the common-disk condition. One must not delete the two-sided compressibility assumption and recover the invalid old lemma.

This is meaningful progress directly at \(g=4\), together with a concrete correction of an apparent universal solution. It supplies neither an explicit finite generating set nor its completeness proof for every \(g\geq4\). Genus-three Powell results are outside the prompt's range, and connectivity criteria alone must not be presented as having already verified their remaining condition. The report therefore records the surviving precise theorem and the failure that any further all-genus proof must overcome.
