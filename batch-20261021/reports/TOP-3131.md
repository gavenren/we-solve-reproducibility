# Uniform exclusion of one-cell Godsil–McKay switching in a random graph

**Claim type: Partial result.** Let \(G\sim G(n,1/2)\), the exact labelled random-graph model in the prompt. With probability \(1-o(1)\), there is **no** even-sized set \(X\), \(4\le |X|\le n\), such that \(G[X]\) is regular and every outside vertex has \(0,|X|/2\), or \(|X|\) neighbors in \(X\).

Thus all sizes of the usual one-cell Godsil–McKay switching mechanism disappear simultaneously. This does not show that almost every graph is determined by its spectrum: other cospectral constructions and more general switching partitions are not excluded. No novelty priority is claimed.

## The exact mechanism being excluded

For an even set \(X\) of size \(s\), the hypotheses above permit replacing each outside half-neighborhood in \(X\) by its complement. The switch preserves the adjacency spectrum. Indeed let \(Q=2J_s/s-I_s\). Then \(Q^2=I_s\), and regularity of the symmetric adjacency block \(B=A(G[X])\) gives \(QBQ=B\). Multiplying a binary column of weight \(s/2\) by \(Q\) complements it, while columns of weight zero or \(s\) are fixed. Conjugating the whole adjacency matrix by \(\operatorname{diag}(Q,I)\) performs exactly this switch.

This is the one-cell form of the classical framework, checked in Abiad–Brouwer–Haemers, *Godsil–McKay switching and isomorphism*, arXiv1406.4170v1, Section2/Proposition1. The case \(s=2\) merely exchanges two vertex labels and cannot produce a nonisomorphic mate. We exclude it throughout.

## An elementary binomial point bound

Let
\[
 b_t=\max_j 2^{-t}\binom tj .
\]
For \(t=2r\ge2\),
\[
 b_{2r}={\binom{2r}r\over4^r}
       =\prod_{i=1}^r\left(1-{1\over2i}\right)
       \le \exp(-H_r/2)\le(r+1)^{-1/2}.
\]
Here \(\log(1-u)\le-u\), and \(H_r\ge\log(r+1)\). For odd \(t=2r+1\), its maximum is \((2r+1)/(2r+2)\) times \(b_{2r}\). Including \(t=1\) directly gives
\[
 b_t\le\sqrt{2/(t+1)}\qquad(t\ge1). \tag{1}
\]

For an even \(s\ge4\), a fixed outside vertex has an allowed neighborhood size with probability
\[
 \theta_s={2+\binom s{s/2}\over2^s}.
\]
The central point mass decreases along even \(s\), as does \(2^{1-s}\). Since \(\theta_4=1/2\), we have
\[
 \theta_s\le1/2,\qquad \theta_s\le3/\sqrt{s}. \tag{2}
\]
The second estimate follows from (1) and \(2^{1-s}\le1/\sqrt{s}\) for \(s\ge4\). For a fixed \(X\), the outside-vertex events use disjoint random edges, so their joint probability is exactly \(\theta_s^{n-s}\).

## Large induced regular graphs are also rare

For even \(s\), partition a fixed \(X\) into equal sets \(A,B\) of size \(s/2\), and expose all edges inside \(A\). If \(G[X]\) is regular of degree \(r\), then every \(a\in A\) must have exactly \(r-d_{G[A]}(a)\) neighbors in \(B\). The cross-edge rows for different vertices of \(A\) are independent. Conditional on the exposure, this necessary event has probability at most \(b_{s/2}^{s/2}\) for each \(r\).

There are at most \(s\) possible regular degrees. Summing them and applying (1) proves
\[
 \rho_s:=\mathbb P(G[X]\text{ regular})
 \le s\,b_{s/2}^{s/2}
 \le s\left({2\over\sqrt s}\right)^{s/2}. \tag{3}
\]
We need not expose or constrain edges inside \(B\); ignoring their degree equations only increases the bound. This conditional exposure avoids any false independence assumption about all \(s\) total vertex degrees.

## A uniform union bound in three ranges

Put \(L=\lceil(\log n)^2\rceil\), and assume \(n\) is sufficiently large that \(4\le L\le n/2\) and all bases used below are smaller than one.

For \(4\le s\le L\), use only the outside-neighborhood condition and (2):
\[
 \sum_{\substack{4\le s\le L\\s\ {\rm even}}}
 \binom ns\theta_s^{n-s}
 \le L n^L2^{-(n-L)}=o(1). \tag{4}
\]
The logarithm of the prefactor is \(O((\log n)^3)=o(n)\).

For \(L<s\le n/2\), (2) gives \(\theta_s\le3/\sqrt L\) and \(n-s\ge n/2\), so
\[
 \sum_{\substack{L<s\le n/2\\s\ {\rm even}}}
 \binom ns\theta_s^{n-s}
 \le2^n\left({3\over\sqrt L}\right)^{n/2}=o(1). \tag{5}
\]
Its logarithm is at most \(n\log2+(n/2)(\log3-\tfrac12\log L)\), tending to minus infinity faster than a fixed negative multiple of \(n\).

For \(n/2<s\le n\), outside constraints are weak or absent, so use regularity (3). Uniformly,
\[
 \rho_s\le n\left({2\sqrt2\over\sqrt n}\right)^{n/4},
\]
because \(2/\sqrt s\le2\sqrt2/\sqrt n<1\) and \(s/2\ge n/4\). Therefore
\[
 \sum_{\substack{n/2<s\le n\\s\ {\rm even}}}\binom ns\rho_s
 \le n2^n\left({2\sqrt2\over\sqrt n}\right)^{n/4}=o(1). \tag{6}
\]
The negative term \(-(n/8)\log n\) dominates its logarithm.

Every candidate size lies in one of (4)–(6). Their sum proves the stated simultaneous exclusion. No interchange of a limit with an uncontrolled sum over sizes occurs.

## What this adds and what remains

The full Math-1055-A1 manuscript establishes the switching conjugation, a genuine nonisomorphic cospectral pair, and \(\binom ns\theta_s^{n-s}=o(1)\) for each **fixed** even \(s\). It explicitly leaves unbounded \(s\) uncontrolled. Estimates (3)–(6) close that particular uniformity gap, including sets occupying almost all vertices.

The probability conclusion concerns the absence of a specified sufficient mechanism for cospectrality. It does not assert that every cospectral mate arises through it, and it says nothing about all equitable multi-cell variants of switching. Accordingly the almost-all spectral determination question remains open within this argument. The result is proved without finite tests; no software execution is claimed.

Reference: A. Abiad, A. E. Brouwer and W. H. Haemers, *Godsil–McKay switching and isomorphism*, arXiv1406.4170v1, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.4170v1 .
