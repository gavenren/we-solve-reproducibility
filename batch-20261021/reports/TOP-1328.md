# A quantified Erdős–Hajnal deduction for bounded semialgebraic descriptions

Problem: TOP-1328  
Claim type: Literature report  
Author: /root/hundred_graphs

**Exact scope.** Fix a real dimension d and a bound on the number and degrees of polynomials in an adjacency formula. For the resulting class of graphs with a symmetric semialgebraic adjacency relation, a primary theorem of Alon–Pach–Pinchasi–Radoičić–Sharir [1] gives a constant \(c\in(0,1/4]\), independent of the graph order, such that
\[
 \alpha(G)\omega(G)\ge n^\delta,\qquad
 \max\{\alpha(G),\omega(G)\}\ge n^{\delta/2},
 \qquad \delta=\frac{\log2}{\log(1/c)}>0. \tag{1}
\]
The complete quantitative deduction is below. These statements cover, in particular, induced-H-free graphs that additionally admit such a bounded geometric description. They do not establish (1) for every induced-H-free graph.

The earlier A1 shows that arbitrary triangle-free graphs need not contain a complete or anticomplete pair of linearly large vertex sets. That obstruction is correct and prevents using a pure-pair induction without extra hypotheses. Here the extra hypothesis is a fixed bound on geometric description complexity. We identify the credited theorem that supplies the previously invalid step on this substantial class, and account for integer sizes and the coefficient-one normalization.

A concrete model is a finite set \(V\subset\mathbb R^d\), with distinct points adjacent exactly when a fixed Boolean formula in signs of s polynomials \(P_i(x,y)\) holds. Their degrees are bounded by a fixed D. The relation is symmetric on V. The constants in what follows may depend on d,s,D and the formula, but not on |V|, point coordinates or polynomial coefficient values. Taking induced subgraphs retains all these bounds. Let \(\mathcal C\) denote this hereditary class.

The external geometric input [1, Theorem1.1 and its following bipartite remark] gives \(\varepsilon>0\) such that, for any two finite disjoint point subfamilies U,W, there exist \(A\subseteq U\), \(B\subseteq W\), with
\[
 |A|\ge\varepsilon|U|,\qquad |B|\ge\varepsilon|W|,
\]
for which every cross pair is adjacent, or every cross pair is nonadjacent. Points can be treated as singleton semialgebraic objects; the relation has bounded complexity in the pair of parameter vectors. This statement uses the geometry, not an induced forbidden graph. Its proof in [1] uses geometric partitioning and linearization and is credited rather than reproduced.

To obtain disjoint sets uniformly in every induced subgraph, first split its N≥2 vertices into U,W of sizes \(\lfloor N/2\rfloor,\lceil N/2\rceil\). Each size is at least N/3. Apply the bipartite theorem. After reducing the constant if needed, set
\[
 c=\min\{\varepsilon/3,1/4\}.
\]
Then the resulting disjoint nonempty A,B satisfy \(|A|,|B|\ge cN\). This preliminary split avoids any issue about overlapping same-family subsets or the relation on the diagonal.

We now prove the first inequality in (1) by induction on N≥1. For a one-vertex graph it is equality. Suppose the claim holds for all smaller induced subgraphs. Choose A,B as above. They are proper vertex subsets because they are disjoint and nonempty.

If the cross pair is anticomplete, then independent sets in A and B combine, whereas a largest clique from either remains a clique. Therefore
\[
 \alpha(G)\ge\alpha(A)+\alpha(B),\qquad
 \omega(G)\ge\max\{\omega(A),\omega(B)\}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \alpha(G)\omega(G)
 \ge\alpha(A)\omega(A)+\alpha(B)\omega(B).
\]
If the cross pair is complete, interchange the roles: cliques combine and an independent set from either side remains available. The very same product inequality follows.

By induction, in either case,
\[
 \alpha(G)\omega(G)
 \ge |A|^\delta+|B|^\delta
 \ge2(cN)^\delta=N^\delta,
\]
because the chosen \(\delta\) satisfies \(2c^\delta=1\). No rounding has been dropped: A,B are actual integer vertex sets and the source gives their sizes at least cN. The base case and the uniform split handle small N. The second inequality in (1) follows from \(\max(a,b)\ge\sqrt{ab}\).

A consequence is that no class with these fixed geometric bounds can contain n-vertex graphs whose clique and independence numbers are both \(n^{o(1)}\) along an unbounded sequence. Indeed, the fixed exponent \(\delta/2\) in (1) eventually exceeds any exponent tending to zero. Allowing the dimension, polynomial degrees or formula size to grow with n removes the uniform constant and invalidates this conclusion.

As an explicit geometric example, intersection graphs of closed Euclidean disks in the plane fit this framework. Encode a disk by \((x,y,r)\), with r≥0. Two disks intersect exactly when
\[
 (x-x')^2+(y-y')^2\le(r+r')^2.
\]
This is one polynomial inequality in a fixed-dimensional parameter space. Arbitrary disk radii and locations are allowed. The homogeneous set in (1) corresponds to a family of pairwise intersecting disks or a family of pairwise disjoint disks. Pairwise intersection is not being confused with existence of one point common to every disk.

Finally, bounded-complexity geometry is not proved here for every induced-H-free graph. The previous triangle-free construction forces its geometric complexity to escape any one fixed choice of the above bounds; it does not contradict the geometric theorem. The deep pure-pair input and the full product induction together provide a rigorously quantified class where the recursion works. The unrestricted Erdős–Hajnal conjecture remains outside the scope.
