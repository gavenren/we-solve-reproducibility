# The finite-smoothed-polygon reduction and the remaining octagon problem

The2011 source in the prompt gives a conditional structural reduction. A stronger checked primary source is Hales–Vajjha's2024 monograph, Theorem16.3.1: a global minimizer of the Reinhardt problem is a finite-sided smoothed polygon, with straight boundary segments alternating with rounded hyperbolic arcs. Its optimal-control representative has finitely many switches. This is Mahler's First conjecture, credited to those authors.

In the2011 paper the corresponding conclusion requires piecewise analyticity. The2024 result addresses possible infinite switching, or chattering, rather than assuming it absent. The monograph's proof uses a blowup near the singular control locus, a Poincaré recurrence map and a cluster-point theorem. Some components are computer-assisted. These are substantive imported results; opening the theorem does not mean independently rerunning those computations or auditing all283pages.

The exact logical reduction is useful. Let$\mathcal K$ be the centrally symmetric convex bodies and$\mathcal H$ the subclass of finite smoothed polygons of the specified hyperbolic type. Existence of a minimizer and the structural theorem give
\[
\inf_{K\in\mathcal K}\delta(K)
=\min_{K\in\mathcal K}\delta(K)
=\min_{H\in\mathcal H}\delta(H).
\]
The first minimum is attained by the cited existence theorem; the structural theorem supplies at least one minimizer in$\mathcal H$. The opposite inequality between the two infima follows from$\mathcal H\subset\mathcal K$. Thus the equality is a consequence with all quantifiers explicit, not a numerical extrapolation from a few rounded polygons.

This does not say the right side is minimized by eight sides. To finish the literal conjecture one would still need to show every relevant finite smoothed polygon has density at least
\[
c_R=\frac{8-\sqrt{32}-\log2}{\sqrt8-1},
\]
and analyze equality up to a common invertible affine transformation. The structural theorem gives no uniform finite bound on the number of sides that by itself turns this into one finite search. Also a candidate satisfying the optimal-control equations is only a necessary candidate, not automatically a global minimum.

Affine changes preserve packing density: they multiply the body area and every lattice covolume by the same absolute determinant, and map interior-disjoint translates bijectively. Therefore identifying affine images is justified; separate normalizations of individual packing copies would not be. This explains exactly the quotient in the uniqueness assertion.
