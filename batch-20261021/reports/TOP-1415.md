# A nontraceable family approaching toughness9/4

**Claim type: Literature report.** Bauer, Broersma and Veldman construct nontraceable graphs with toughness arbitrarily close to \(9/4\) from below [1–2]. Consequently any universal Hamiltonicity threshold in the literal prompt must be at least \(9/4\). This refutes the specific threshold2, but **does not** refute the existence of some larger finite threshold. We explain the general terminal-gadget obstruction and calculate its exact approach to the barrier.

Let \(H\) be a finite simple graph on at least3 vertices with distinct terminals \(x,y\), having no Hamilton path from \(x\) to \(y\). Take \(m\) disjoint copies \(H_i\) with terminals \(x_i,y_i\). Add all edges among the \(2m\) terminals. Finally add a clique \(T=K_\ell\) and join every vertex of \(T\) to every vertex of the copies. Call the resulting graph \(G(H,x,y,\ell,m)\). The terminal edges are the only edges between distinct copies outside \(T\).

**Terminal obstruction.** If \(m\ge2\ell+3\), this graph has no Hamilton path. Suppose a spanning path \(P\) exists. At most two copies contain an endpoint of \(P\). Since every vertex of \(T\) has path degree at most2, at most \(2\ell\) copies have a path edge to \(T\). There is consequently a copy \(H_i\) of neither kind.

Pick a nonterminal vertex of this copy, which exists because H has at least3 vertices. Its component in the restriction of P to the copy cannot end at a nonterminal: such an end would be a global endpoint or have a path edge to T, both excluded. Thus that component has the two distinct terminals as its ends. No second component can exist, since it would also need terminal ends and the two terminals are already used. We obtain a spanning path with ends x_i,y_i. The terminal-clique operation may have added the edge x_i y_i inside the copy, but a path spanning at least3 vertices and having these endpoints cannot use that edge. Hence the path lies in the original H_i, contradicting its terminal hypothesis.

The source takes a specific8-vertex graph \(L\), with terminals \(u,v\), shown in its Figure1. Its substantive exact toughness theorem is
\[
 \tau(G(L,u,v,\ell,m))
 =\frac{\ell+4m}{2m+1}
 \quad(\ell\ge2,\ m\ge1).
 \tag{1}
\]
This equality, including its lower bound over **all** separating vertex sets, is credited to the original construction; it is not inferred merely from a displayed convenient cut. The coauthors' primary Y2K report [1, Theorems4–5] gives the construction, nontraceability criterion and exact formula. We do not independently reconstruct the8-vertex gadget's entire cut analysis.

Choose \(m=2\ell+3\), \(\ell\ge2\). The terminal obstruction gives nontraceability, stronger than non-Hamiltonicity, while(1) becomes
\[
 \tau=\frac{9\ell+12}{4\ell+7}
 =\frac94-\frac{15}{4(4\ell+7)}.
 \tag{2}
\]
The graph has \(\ell+8m=17\ell+24\) vertices, so this is an unbounded family. Given any \(\varepsilon>0\), choosing an integer
\(\ell\ge\max(2,\lceil15/(16\varepsilon)\rceil)\)
makes the positive gap in(2) smaller than \(\varepsilon\). The graph is therefore \((9/4-\varepsilon)\)-tough and nontraceable.

If a proposed universal threshold \(t_0<9/4\) existed, choose \(\varepsilon=(9/4-t_0)/2\). The resulting graph is tougher than \(t_0\) but has no Hamilton cycle, a contradiction. This proves the necessary inequality \(t_0\ge9/4\). Equation(2) never reaches \(9/4\), so it neither excludes that endpoint nor gives arbitrarily large toughness.

The preceding contribution's Petersen example has toughness4/3 and establishes basic necessary conditions. This substantially stronger obstruction, its reusable terminal-gadget argument and its exact epsilon quantifiers are distinct. The source's historical “2-tough conjecture” must not be confused with the current prompt, which asks for **some** absolute constant. No source enumeration, gadget checker or new computation was run.
