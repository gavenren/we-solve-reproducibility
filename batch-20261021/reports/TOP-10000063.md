# A universal equal-ball construction and the exact one-dimensional class

For every simple finite graph$G$ on$n\ge2$ vertices, there is a packing of equal-radius balls in$\mathbb R^{n-1}$ whose tangency graph is exactly$G$. The construction gives an explicit positive-definite rational Gram matrix before centering. It establishes a dimension bound depending on the number of vertices, not a characterization in a prescribed fixed dimension. Separately, in dimension one the finite realizable graphs are exactly linear forests.

Let$A$ be the adjacency matrix,$D$ the maximum degree, and choose$\varepsilon=1/(2\max(1,D))$. The matrix$B=I+\varepsilon A$ is positive definite. For every real vector$x$,
\[
|x^{\mathsf T}Ax|
=\left|2\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}x_ix_j\right|
\le\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}(x_i^2+x_j^2)
\le D\sum_i x_i^2.
\]
Hence$x^{\mathsf T}Bx\ge(1-\varepsilon D)\|x\|^2\ge\|x\|^2/2$. Choose vectors$v_1,\ldots,v_n\in\mathbb R^n$ with Gram matrix$B$, for example by a real Cholesky factor. Their squared pair distances are
\[
\|v_i-v_j\|^2=
\begin{cases}2(1-\varepsilon),&\{i,j\}\in E,\\2,&\{i,j\}\notin E.\end{cases}
\]
Subtract their common centroid. The translated vectors sum to zero and therefore lie in a linear space of dimension at most$n-1$, without changing any pair distance. Set every ball radius to$r=\sqrt{(1-\varepsilon)/2}>0$. Adjacent centers are at distance$2r$; nonadjacent centers are strictly farther apart. Thus every two interiors are disjoint, and the contacts encode exactly the edges, with no unwanted tangencies. Isometrically identifying the centered span with a subspace of$\mathbb R^{n-1}$ proves the construction. If$n=1$, one ball in$\mathbb R$ suffices.

For rational input adjacency data the Gram entries are rational and finitely encoded. The centers and radius may be algebraic, rather than rational: a Euclidean packing has no rational-coordinate requirement in the prompt. The construction is an existence theorem, not a claim that all radicals have constant-cost binary encodings or that a prescribed lower-dimensional realization can be efficiently recognized. A nonsingular Gram factor can be specified algebraically by its defining matrix equations.

For the exact finite$d=1$ classification, balls are closed intervals of positive length with disjoint interiors. Order the intervals from left to right. A tangency can occur only between consecutive intervals in this order, so the graph is a subgraph of a path and hence a disjoint union of paths and isolated vertices. Conversely place unit-radius intervals along each path with centers spaced two units apart, and leave a gap greater than two between components. This realizes exactly the required linear forest. Positive length excludes branching at a common endpoint: at most one interval can extend in either direction without interior overlap.

The upper-dimensional construction is useful as a certificate that no finite abstract graph is forbidden in every dimension. It neither bounds the minimum dimension sharply nor characterizes the$d=2,3$ classes, arbitrary infinite graphs, or graphs constrained to a fixed radius ratio.
