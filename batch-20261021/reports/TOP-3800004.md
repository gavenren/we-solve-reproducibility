# Exact balanced face counts for polygon joins

The source credits the join-of-polygons construction to David Eppstein. Here we give its full face polynomial, optimize the five-dimensional family for each integer vertex count, and keep the nonempty-proper-face convention of the literal prompt. The result concerns a known construction and does not settle four-dimensional fatness.

Give a face its weight$t^{\dim F+1}$, with the empty face weighted one. A convex$m$-gon therefore has polynomial
\[
\mathcal F_m(t)=1+mt+mt^2+t^3.
\]
For polytopes in independent affine subspaces defining their join, every nonempty join face has a unique expression$F*G$, allowing one factor to be empty; its dimension plus one is the sum of the two weights' exponents. One proof is to expose by a functional: its restrictions expose faces in the factors, and equality of their maxima allows both factors to appear; unequal maxima select just one. Conversely a supporting functional for each specified pair can be adjusted by constants on the two affine spans to equalize maxima. Thus the complete face polynomial multiplies.

The join of$r$ polygons of sizes$m_1,\ldots,m_r\ge3$ has dimension$3r-1$ and polynomial
\[
\prod_{j=1}^r(1+m_jt+m_jt^2+t^3).
\]
The coefficient of$t$ is$\sum m_j$, and by reciprocal symmetry the facet coefficient is the same. Evaluating at one and deleting the empty and whole faces gives
\[
f_0=f_{3r-2}=\sum_jm_j,\qquad
F=\prod_j(2m_j+2)-2.
\]
These equations give every intermediate face count as well, rather than just a growth assertion. For equal sizes$m$ and$n=rm$, they give$F=(2n/r+2)^r-2$ in dimension$3r-1$.

In dimension five,$r=2$. Fix$n=a+b\ge6$. The balanced choice$a=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$, $b=\lceil n/2\rceil$ maximizes$(a+1)(b+1)$ among the polygon-join constructions, by the identity$(a+1)(b+1)=((n+2)^2-(a-b)^2)/4$. Hence
\[
M_5(n)\ge
\begin{cases}
n^2+4n+2,&n\text{ even},\\
n^2+4n+1,&n\text{ odd}.
\end{cases}
\]
For two triangles the vector is$(6,15,20,15,6)$, so the proper-face total is62, agreeing with the formula. Adding the empty face and whole polytope gives64; confusing these conventions would introduce a constant-two error. The optimization above is only within the join family, and does not assert exact equality for$M_5(n)$.

The original problem-author note records both this known quadratic example in dimension five and the upper-bound-theorem order$O(n^2)$ in fixed dimension five. Thus this construction has the correct order there; the general upper bound is credited, not reproved here. It also supplies an explicit$\Omega(n^r)$ sequence in each dimension$3r-1$. A join raises dimension by a factor's dimension plus one, so these calculations cannot be read as a superlinear construction in dimension four. The constraint$f_0=f_{d-1}$ is verified exactly, rather than replaced by arbitrary four-polytope fatness.
