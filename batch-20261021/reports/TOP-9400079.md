# Selberg's conjecture: unconditional density control without individual exclusion

Claim type: Literature report.

The target excludes every nonconstant eigenvalue below $1/4$ for every congruence subgroup, with the prompt's hyperbolic Laplacian normalization. A different unconditional theorem controls how many cuspidal violations of a fixed size could occur as the level grows. We specify this regime using Humphries's primary Theorem1.5. It supplies substantial quantitative information without treating a sublinear count as zero.

Write a weight-zero cuspidal Laplace eigenvalue as
\[
 \lambda_f=\tfrac14+t_f^2=\tfrac14-\nu_f^2
 \quad\text{when }t_f=-i\nu_f,\ 0<\nu_f<1/2.
\]
Let $V_\Gamma=\operatorname{vol}(\Gamma\backslash\mathbb H)$ in the source's normalization. For every **fixed** $\alpha\in(0,1/2)$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, the published density theorem gives
\[
 \#\{f:\nu_f>\alpha\}\ll_{\alpha,\varepsilon}
 \begin{cases}
 V_{\Gamma_0(q)}^{\,1-4\alpha+\varepsilon},\\
 V_{\Gamma_1(q)}^{\,1-3\alpha+\varepsilon},\\
 V_{\Gamma(q)}^{\,1-(8/3)\alpha+\varepsilon}.
 \end{cases} \tag{1}
\]
The first line uses trivial nebentypus; all counts are in the source's Hecke eigenbasis of the cuspidal space, so multiplicities are counted. These are exact specializations of equations1.8,1.6,1.7 respectively: take the possibly empty auxiliary-prime set to be empty and $\mu_0=1$. The ramified-character correction factors in the first line are then one. No assertion about arbitrary characters is obtained by silently dropping them.

Here are consequences with their quantifiers visible. Choosing $\varepsilon<2\alpha$ in the first line gives
\[
 \frac{\#\{\lambda_f<1/4-\alpha^2\}}{V_{\Gamma_0(q)}}
 \ll V_{\Gamma_0(q)}^{-2\alpha}\longrightarrow0.
\]
For the other two families choose $\varepsilon$ less than half the displayed positive saving, obtaining the same sublinear conclusion with their respective exponents. Thus a fixed-sized violation carries vanishing normalized cuspidal spectral mass. If $h$ is any bounded function supported in $(0,1/4-\alpha^2)$, its contribution to the normalized spectral trace is at most
\[
 \frac1{V_\Gamma}\left|\sum_f h(\lambda_f)\right|
 \le\|h\|_\infty\frac{\#\{\nu_f>\alpha\}}{V_\Gamma}=o(1).
\]
This conclusion follows directly from counting and makes no unjustified pointwise estimate on individual eigenfunctions.

It is still not Selberg's assertion. A bound $C V^{1-c\alpha+\varepsilon}$ with positive exponent can allow one exceptional eigenvalue at every level. For instance the abstract count sequence constantly equal to one satisfies such an estimate; it does not tend to zero as an integer sequence. Also $\alpha$ is fixed before the level tends to infinity. Sending $\alpha$ to zero with $q$ without controlling the constants would not count all violations uniformly. An arbitrarily small $\nu_f$ can evade every one fixed threshold.

The restriction to cusp forms is another real boundary. The constant eigenfunction is not counted, and a theorem stated for the cuspidal basis is not automatically a statement about residual spectrum or every discrete $L^2$ eigenfunction. The report therefore neither adds the constant to (1) nor claims to settle those additional parts of the literal spectrum by a density estimate.

The added regime is an unconditional level-density theorem and its precisely normalized trace consequence. Deep Kuznetsov and amplification inputs remain credited to Humphries. This is not a new spectral-gap theorem or a rerun of a numerical exclusion. The all-subgroup zero-exception conjecture remains unresolved by these deductions.
