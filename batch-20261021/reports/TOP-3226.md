# Reed's bound on claw-free graphs, with a critical-graph proof for stability at most two

**Claim type: Literature report.** Reed's exact bound
\[
 \chi(G)\le\left\lceil{\Delta(G)+1+\omega(G)\over2}\right\rceil \tag{1}
\]
holds for all claw-free graphs. This is Theorem1.6 of King and Reed, a credited established result. We also give a complete shorter deduction for every graph with independence number at most two, using Gallai's critical-complement order theorem. The latter includes all join decompositions and the rounding step.

The entire claw-free class is a substantial scope of the prompt, not a proof for arbitrary graphs or arbitrary lists.

## Exact external results and access boundary

King–Reed, *Claw-free graphs, skeletal graphs, and a stronger conjecture on \(\omega,\Delta,\chi\)*, arXiv1212.3036v1, Theorem1.6, proves (1) whenever no vertex has three pairwise nonadjacent neighbors. Their Theorem1.7 gives a stronger local bound for three-cliqued claw-free graphs. We use only the global theorem here. The actual PDF's theorem statements and structural framework were read; its entire32-page proof is not claimed independently certified.

Gallai's theorem says that a vertex-\(h\)-critical graph whose complement is connected has at least \(2h-1\) vertices. The exact statement was checked in the primary research abstract of Stehlík, *Critical graphs with connected complements*, JCTB89(2003),189–194. Its full author PDF retrieval failed; this classical order theorem is an explicit external input rather than a proof attributed to this report.

## A full deduction for \(\alpha(G)\le2\)

Choose an induced vertex-critical subgraph \(H\subseteq G\) with \(\chi(H)=\chi(G)\), by repeatedly deleting a vertex whenever the chromatic number stays the same. Then \(\alpha(H)\le2\), and both \(\Delta(H)\le\Delta(G)\) and \(\omega(H)\le\omega(G)\). It suffices to prove (1) for \(H\).

Let \(F=\overline H\). Since \(\alpha(H)\le2\), \(F\) is triangle-free. Write its connected components as \(F_1,\ldots,F_s\), and put \(H_i=\overline{F_i}\) on the corresponding vertex sets. Distinct \(H_i\)'s are joined completely in \(H\). Therefore
\[
 n=\sum_i n_i,\qquad
 h:=\chi(H)=\sum_i h_i,\qquad
 \omega(H)=\sum_i w_i,
\]
where \(n_i=|V(F_i)|\), \(h_i=\chi(H_i)\), and \(w_i=\omega(H_i)=\alpha(F_i)\).

Each \(H_i\) is vertex-critical. Indeed, if deleting a vertex from \(H_i\) preserved \(h_i\), complete joins to the other components would preserve the full chromatic number of \(H\), contrary to criticality. Its complement \(F_i\) is connected, so Gallai gives
\[
 n_i\ge2h_i-1. \tag{2}
\]
This includes a singleton component, for which both sides equal one.

Write \(d_i=\delta(F_i)\), and choose \(j\) with \(d_j=\min_i d_i\). Every neighborhood in a triangle-free graph is independent, so
\[
 w_i=\alpha(F_i)\ge\Delta(F_i)\ge d_i.
\]
Also \(w_i\ge1\), since each component is nonempty. Consequently
\[
 \sum_iw_i-\min_i d_i
 =w_j-d_j+\sum_{i\ne j}w_i\ge s-1. \tag{3}
\]
The maximum degree in \(H\) is \(n-1-\delta(F)=n-1-\min_i d_i\). Combining (2) and (3),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \Delta(H)+1+\omega(H)
 &=n-\min_i d_i+\sum_iw_i\\
 &\ge\sum_i(2h_i-1)+(s-1)
 =2h-1.
\end{aligned}
\]
Thus the ceiling of half the left side is at least \(h\), proving (1). Monotonicity of \(\Delta,\omega\) under induced subgraphs now transfers it to \(G\).

The join components cannot simply be ignored: \(\chi\) adds across them, so a separate rounding bound on each component does not automatically sum to the desired global ceiling. Inequality(3) supplies exactly the lost \(s-1\) units. It is the structural point that makes the argument valid for arbitrary, possibly disconnected complements.

## Scope comparison and remaining problem

All graphs with \(\alpha\le2\) are claw-free, since three independent neighbors would be an independent triple. They include much more than the equal-clique \(C_5[K_t]\) equality family computed in Math-1141-A1. The complete earlier manuscript proves that family and basic criticality constraints; it does not prove the full stability-two class or the all-claw-free theorem.

The source theorem also handles claw-free graphs having large independence number, beyond the short proof above. That extension relies on its deep decomposition and skeletal reductions. Neither the elementary proof nor the cited theorem supplies (1) for all graphs. Ordinary vertex coloring and the exact outer ceiling are retained; there is no claimed list-coloring counterpart.

Verification is mathematical/source-based only. The critical reduction, triangle-free complement, component degree identity, Gallai quantifiers and final integral ceiling have all been stated explicitly. No software or finite testing is asserted.
