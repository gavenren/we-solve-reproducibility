# An exponential permanent lower bound with precisely necessary positivity

Problem: TOP-30001375  
Claim type: Partial result  
Author: /root/hundred_algorithms

**Exact scope.** For n>=3, every monotone fan-in-two arithmetic circuit over nonnegative real constants computing the formal permanent has at least binomial(n,floor(n/3)) operation gates, even with arbitrary fan-out. Gives a complete balanced-gate support proof; credits the stronger classical Jerrum–Snir result. This restricted positivity theorem does not establish characteristic-zero VP!=VNP.

Work with fan-in-two + and multiplication gates, arbitrary fan-out, variables x_{ij}, and nonnegative real constants. The computed polynomial must equal per_n formally. Negative constants, subtraction and division are excluded. This is a genuine restriction of the prompt's arithmetic model, in which arbitrary characteristic-zero field constants are permitted. The positivity assumption, not a symmetry assumption on circuit gates, is the resource used below.

Delete zero or irrelevant gates so every retained gate has a positive-coefficient monomial which can contribute to the nonzero output. For any retained gate g with polynomial p_g, fix one upward path from that gate to the output, choosing at each multiplication a positive monomial from the other factor and at additions the branch containing g. If shared occurrences arise, hold their chosen monomials fixed as well. This gives a fixed context monomial q such that every monomial of p_g times q occurs positively in the final output. Nonnegative coefficients prevent cancellation.

Every permanent monomial uses each row and each column exactly once. Hence all monomials of p_g use the same row set R_g and column set C_g, namely the complements of q's row and column sets. Each is squarefree with degree d_g=|R_g|=|C_g|. In particular p_g is homogeneous. A multiplication gate's children use disjoint row/column sets and their degrees sum to its degree; a nonzero addition can only combine equal such multidegrees. This multidegree conclusion is derived from the output and positivity, not silently assumed as a syntactic restriction.

**Balanced gate cover.** Take any one permanent monomial and any parse tree contributing it: at each addition keep a branch supplying that monomial and at multiplication keep both factors. The root degree is n. Descend while the current degree is greater than2n/3. At an addition follow the selected equal-degree child. At a multiplication, if a child has degree greater than2n/3 follow it. Otherwise take a child of largest degree; because its parent degree was greater than2n/3, that child has degree greater than n/3 and at most2n/3. Thus the parse includes an operation gate g with
\[
                         n/3<d_g\le2n/3.
\]
For n>=3 such a chosen gate can be taken noninput: at n=3 its degree is2, and for larger n it is at least2. Constants cannot serve as this gate.

If a permutation monomial uses gate g in its parse, its permutation must map R_g exactly onto C_g. The number of permutations with that property is
\[
 d_g!(n-d_g)!=n!/\binom n{d_g}
 \le n!/\binom n{\lfloor n/3\rfloor}.
\]
Every one of the n! permanent monomials has at least one such balanced gate. A circuit with s operation gates therefore covers all these monomials by at most s fixed row/column-pair classes. Even allowing overlaps, the union bound gives
\[
                 s\ge\binom n{\lfloor n/3\rfloor}.       \tag{14}
\]
This is exponentially large: with r=floor(n/3), binomial(n,r)>=binomial(3r,r)>=3^r, since product_{i=1}^r(2r+i)/i>=3^r. Thus the lower bound is at least3^{floor(n/3)}, sufficient to exclude every polynomial size in this restricted model. Arbitrary real magnitudes do not affect the argument, since it concerns positive monomial supports rather than a finite encoding count.

Jerrum and Snir's1982 primary paper Section4.3 proves a stronger exact monotone multiplication bound, n(2^{n-1}-1), in its semiring straight-line framework. We credit that established result rather than present(14) as a new exponential lower bound. The shorter support proof above deliberately proves only its displayed weaker quantity and does not rely on the stronger theorem's full content-bound machinery.

**Why this cannot be promoted to the literal conjecture.** With negative coefficients an internal monomial outside the permanent's support can cancel later. There need not be a fixed R_g,C_g for every gate; the first support/context lemma then fails. The inclusion–exclusion permanent circuit in the earlier attempt uses exactly such cancellation. Replacing negative constants by nonnegative ones is not a semantics-preserving circuit transformation. Nor does this result address arbitrary complex constants by interpreting an order on C. Thus(14) is a complete exponential lower bound for unrestricted sharing under positivity, and a precise demonstration of the hypothesis that still separates it from the general VP/VNP task.
