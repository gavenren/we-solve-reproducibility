# The original Dubrovin package for every complex Grassmannian

Claim type: **Literature report**.

For $X=\operatorname{Gr}(r,N)$, the entire package in the literal prompt is a known theorem, rather than only the existence of an exceptional collection. The precise input is Galkin–Golyshev–Iritani (GGI), *Gamma classes and quantum cohomology of Fano manifolds: Gamma conjectures*, arXiv:1404.6407v4, Theorem6.1.1 and Corollary6.1.2. Here is how their result supplies the **original** normalization, without substituting a different conjecture.

Write $d=r(N-r)$ and $b=\binom Nr$. Schubert cells show that $H^{\mathrm{odd}}(X)=0$ and $\dim H^*(X)=b$. Kapranov's full exceptional collection has objects $\mathbb S^\nu V^*$ indexed by partitions in the $r\times(N-r)$ rectangle. Thus the number of objects agrees with the rank of the quantum connection. The collection and its fullness are credited inputs used by GGI; no elementary proof of fullness is claimed here.

The small quantum algebra at a nonzero quantum parameter is semisimple: its spectral points are indexed by $r$-element subsets of the $N$ distinct roots appearing in the projective-space quantum algebra. This can be expressed by the exterior-power quantum Satake description in GGI Section6.2. A product of copies of $\mathbb C$ has no nilpotents; semisimplicity is an open condition on a finite-dimensional algebra family, so big quantum cohomology is generically semisimple. Distinct subsets can have the same **sum** of roots. Consequently this argument does not assert that quantum multiplication by $c_1(X)$ has distinct eigenvalues at the small point. GGI Section6.3 treats the big deformation needed for the marked asymptotic data.

Under the analytic and admissible-phase conventions of Sections2,4.5–4.6, Theorem6.1.1 identifies an asymptotic basis with
\[
 A_i=\widehat\Gamma_X\operatorname{Ch}(E_i),\qquad 1\le i\le b,
\]
where $(E_i)$ is obtained by the required mutations of Kapranov's collection. The modified Chern character is $\operatorname{Ch}(E)=\sum_p(2\pi i)^p\operatorname{ch}_p(E)$. Neither an arbitrary phase nor the unmutated order of the collection is prescribed by this statement.

The theorem now gives the original two matrices as follows. By the asymptotic pairing and the Gamma-class form of Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch, the Stokes matrix satisfies
\[
 S_{ij}=[A_i,A_j)=\chi(E_i,E_j).
\]
For the central connection matrix use exactly GGI Section4.6:
\[
 C=(2\pi)^{-d/2}[A_1\ \cdots\ A_b]
   =C'C'',\qquad
 C'=(2\pi)^{-d/2}(\widehat\Gamma_X\cup-),\quad
 C''=[\operatorname{Ch}(E_1)\ \cdots\ \operatorname{Ch}(E_b)].
\]
This factor has the demanded commutation property. Indeed $\widehat\Gamma_X$ is an even cohomology class, so associativity and graded commutativity give
\[
 (\widehat\Gamma_X\cup-)(c_1(X)\cup-)
 =(c_1(X)\cup-)(\widehat\Gamma_X\cup-).
\]
Its constant term is1; its positive-degree part is nilpotent in the finite cohomology ring. Thus it is invertible, with inverse the finite geometric series in that positive-degree part. The scalar $(2\pi)^{-d/2}$ introduces no obstruction. This explicitly checks the part of the original package that can be lost when one merely says “GammaII holds.” GGI Proposition4.6.6 records this implication to the original parts(2) and(3).

We therefore have a full exceptional collection, generic semisimplicity, and a collection whose Stokes and central matrices have precisely the required Euler and commuting-factor descriptions for **every** Grassmannian, in arbitrary dimension. The deep analytic identification of the asymptotic basis is the cited theorem, not a new proof here. The endpoint cases $r=1,N-1$ recover projective spaces; $r=0,N$ are points outside the stated range. Nothing above extends the equivalence to a general Fano manifold, changes the admissible-phase hypotheses, or identifies all possible Stokes choices with one fixed collection.
