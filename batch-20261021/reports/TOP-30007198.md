# All nonsingular systems over residually unitary groups

Claim type: **Literature report**.

The coefficient-injectivity assertion holds for every group $G$ whose finite-dimensional unitary representations separate its elements. This includes every residually finite group, with no finite-generation or cardinality restriction. The deep topological input is the Gerstenhaber–Rothaus degree theorem [1]; we give its exact role and the complete rectangular-system/separation reduction.

Let $w_1,\ldots,w_m\in G*F_n$, $m\le n$, have exponent-sum matrix $E$ of row rank$m$ over$\mathbb Q$. Choose $m$ columns forming a nonsingular minor. Rename those variables $x_1,\ldots,x_m$ and set the remaining variables to1. This produces a square system $v_i$ with exponent matrix$D$ satisfying $\det D\ne0$. A simultaneous solution of the reduced system is a solution of the original one; coefficients have not changed. Thus it is enough to treat square systems.

The primary theorem states the following. If $H$ is a compact connected Lie group of rank$r$, then the word map with fixed coefficients
\[
 T:H^m\longrightarrow H^m,\qquad T(x)=(v_1(x),\ldots,v_m(x))
\]
has topological degree $(\det D)^r$. In particular it is onto. For $H=\mathrm U(d)$, $r=d$, so this degree is nonzero. The connectedness assumption is satisfied by the **ambient** unitary group; the coefficient image itself need not be connected. One must not incorrectly apply the theorem to a disconnected finite coefficient group as its own ambient Lie group.

Here is the mechanism of the degree formula, also described in [1]. Rational cohomology of a compact connected Lie group is an exterior algebra on$r$ odd-degree primitive generators. In the pullback of each primitive generator under the$i$th word component, the coefficients of the copies from the $m$ source factors are exactly the exponent sums $d_{ij}$. Multiplication contributes addition on primitives, inversion contributes a minus sign, and translations by fixed coefficients are homotopic to the identity. On the top exterior product for each generator the coefficient is $\det D$; multiplying over$r$ generators gives the stated degree. This identifies why the determinant of the exponent matrix, rather than a condition on each word separately, governs the result. The cohomology structure/degree theorem is credited; it is not derived from a finite calculation.

For each unitary representation $\rho:G\to\mathrm U(d)$, evaluate the coefficient images in$\mathrm U(d)$ and choose a solution $s_{\rho,1},\ldots,s_{\rho,m}$ using the nonzero degree. By the universal property, the map from$G*F_m$ sending coefficients through$\rho$ and variables to the chosen solution kills all relators. Consequently, for
\[
 Q=(G*F_m)/\langle\!\langle v_1,\ldots,v_m\rangle\!\rangle,
\]
there is a homomorphism $Q\to\mathrm U(d)$ whose composite with the coefficient map is$\rho$. If $g\ne1$ in$G$, choose a representation with$\rho(g)\ne1$; then$g$ cannot die in$Q$. This proves coefficient injectivity. Equivalently, take the product over a set of separating representations and their chosen solution tuples; the diagonal map embeds$G$ in an overgroup containing one simultaneous solution. No compactness or countable diagonal subsequence is needed. A set of at most one witness representation for each nonidentity element suffices.

For a residually finite$G$, choose a finite quotient detecting$g$. Its left regular permutation representation embeds it in a unitary group, so the required separation holds. The same proof applies to any other residually unitary group, even when finite quotients do not separate all elements. The separating property is an additional hypothesis; this argument does not establish it for every abstract group.
