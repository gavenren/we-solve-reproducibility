# Finite-dimensional motives for every Hilbert scheme of a curve-dominated surface

Claim type: **Literature report**.

Let$S$ be a smooth projective complex surface whose rational Chow motive is generated by motives of curves; in particular this includes every abelian surface and every surface admitting a generically finite surjective morphism from a product of two smooth projective curves. Then for **every**$n\ge0$, the Hilbert scheme$S^{[n]}$ has a Kimura finite-dimensional rational Chow motive. Any finite product of such Hilbert schemes is also covered. This is an unconditional unbounded-dimensional class, obtained from established motive theorems, not from an assumed nilpotence property of an unknown motive.

We use two exact source inputs. Motives of curves and abelian varieties are finite-dimensional, and this property is preserved under finite direct sums, tensor products, Tate twists and direct summands; these Kimura–O’Sullivan facts are stated and used in Guletskiĭ–Pedrini [1, §3/Theorem11], with the full categorical closure discussion in André [2, Theorem2.8 and Lemma3.7]. The de Cataldo–Migliorini decomposition [3, Theorem6.2.1] gives
\[
 h(S^{[n]})\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\vdash n}
 h(S^{(\lambda)})\otimes\mathbb L^{\otimes(n-\ell(\lambda))}, \tag{1}
\]
where, if$\lambda$ has$a_i$ parts of size$i$, then $S^{(\lambda)}=\prod_i S^{(a_i)}$ and $\ell(\lambda)=\sum_i a_i$. The rational motive of the possibly singular symmetric product means the invariant summand in$h(S^{\ell(\lambda)})$ under$\prod_i\mathfrak S_{a_i}$, in the quotient-motive formalism used by [3]. It must not be treated as the ordinary motive of a smooth variety without explaining this convention.

Explicitly, the invariant summand is cut out by the idempotent
\[
 e_\lambda=\frac1{\prod_i a_i!}
       \sum_{\sigma\in\prod_i\mathfrak S_{a_i}}\Gamma_\sigma.
\]
Group multiplication shows$e_\lambda^2=e_\lambda$; rational coefficients supply the denominator. As$h(S)$ is finite-dimensional, its tensor power$h(S^{\ell(\lambda)})$ is finite-dimensional. Closure under **arbitrary direct summands**, not only parity-preserving visibly diagonal projectors, applies to the image of$e_\lambda$. This closure is a genuine Kimura theorem; it is not inferred just from finite Betti numbers. Tate twisting preserves the property because$\mathbb L$ is an even one-dimensional invertible motive. Every summand on the right of(1) is therefore finite-dimensional, and there are finitely many partitions of$n$, proving the assertion.

For clarity, a generically finite morphism$f:C_1\times C_2\to S$ gives the required finite-dimensional surface motive without any conjecture. The graph and transpose graph define pullback and pushforward correspondences, with$f_*f^*=(\deg f)\operatorname{id}_{h(S)}$. Thus $(\deg f)^{-1}f_*$ is a left inverse to$f^*$, so$h(S)$ is a direct summand of$h(C_1)\otimes h(C_2)$. Apply the credited curve and summand theorems. For an abelian surface, use the abelian-variety theorem directly; no domination by a product of two curves is assumed. This separation avoids imposing an unnecessary geometric restriction on abelian surfaces.

When$n=2$, (1) reads $h(S^{[2]})\cong h(S^{(2)})\oplus h(S)\otimes\mathbb L$, which already illustrates both the finite-group invariant summand and the positive Lefschetz twist. For$n=0$, the Hilbert scheme is a point, so its motive is even and finite-dimensional. Tensoring the established decompositions proves the finite-product extension. We claim existence of the even/odd decomposition and vanishing powers required by the prompt; no algorithm computing minimal exponents is asserted.

Prior A1 gives a conditional lifting/nilpotence construction for a general motive; A2 reports unverified$p_g=0$ surface and pure-Tate claims. Our surfaces include abelian surfaces with$p_g=1$, and the Hilbert schemes have arbitrarily high dimension. The result follows from checked established correspondences, not by assuming the prior's tensor-power nilpotence hypothesis. It does not imply that all varieties have finite-dimensional motive or that these motives are pure Tate.
