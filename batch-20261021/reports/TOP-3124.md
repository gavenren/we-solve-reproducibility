# Semicomplete strong arc decomposition and the sharp four-vertex obstruction

Problem: TOP-3124  
Claim type: Literature report  
Author: /root/hundred_graphs

**Exact scope.** The credited Bang-Jensen–Yeo theorem characterizes the semicomplete case: a2-arc-strong semicomplete digraph has a strong arc decomposition exactly when it is not \(S_4\) [1, Theorem1.1]. Consequently threshold3 suffices throughout the semicomplete class, while threshold2 suffices for tournaments. We prove the exceptional graph's exact obstruction below. No absolute threshold for arbitrary digraphs is established.

A semicomplete digraph is loopless and has at least one arc between every pair of distinct vertices; both opposite arcs are allowed. A tournament has exactly one of the two possible arcs per pair. A strong arc decomposition partitions the arcs into two sets, each giving a strongly connected spanning digraph. Both pieces contain all vertices; disjointness concerns arcs.

The earlier A1 proves the symmetric case by reversing a strong orientation of an undirected graph. Semicomplete digraphs have no such reverse-arc hypothesis, and tournaments have no reverse pairs at all. This is a distinct substantial class, using a deep credited characterization rather than the symmetric proof.

The exact primary restatement [1, Theorem1.1] says: if D is2-arc-strong and semicomplete, it has a strong arc decomposition if and only if D is not isomorphic to the graph obtained from the complete bidirected graph on four vertices by deleting a directed four-cycle. When the decomposition exists, it can be constructed in polynomial time. The source explicitly credits Bang-Jensen–Yeo. Its proof is external; the larger composition characterization in the2019paper is not needed as an extra premise.

For a concrete representation of the exception, take vertices0,1,2,3 modulo4 and arcs
\[
 i\to i+1\quad(i=0,1,2,3),\qquad
 0\leftrightarrow2,\quad1\leftrightarrow3. \tag{2}
\]
These eight arcs are isomorphic to \(S_4\): reverse the deleted directed-cycle convention if necessary. Every vertex has indegree and outdegree2.

Every nonempty proper cut has at least two outgoing arcs. Singleton cuts have exactly2. Three-vertex cuts have exactly2 as well, since this is the indegree of the omitted vertex. For a two-vertex set X, its two vertices have total outdegree4 and at most two internal arcs, so at least2 leave X. Hence \(S_4\) is2-arc-strong, with exact arc-connectivity2.

Any strongly connected spanning digraph on four vertices has at least four arcs, since every vertex has an outgoing arc. If two such digraphs were arc-disjoint in (2), they would each have exactly four arcs. Every vertex would then have exactly one outgoing and one incoming arc. Strong connectivity forces one directed Hamilton cycle, rather than several disjoint cycles. Thus a decomposition of \(S_4\) would require two arc-disjoint Hamilton cycles.

In fact (2) has only one Hamilton cycle, the displayed directed four-cycle. To see this without relying on a search, call the arcs joining0 to2 or1 to3 diagonals. Cycle arcs change parity; diagonals preserve parity. A four-cycle must use an even number of diagonals.

With no diagonal, the only choices are the four displayed cycle arcs. With four diagonals, the selected arcs lie in the two disjoint digons and cannot give a Hamilton cycle. With exactly two diagonals, they must consist of one even-to-even diagonal and one odd-to-odd diagonal: two consecutive diagonals within one parity would immediately revisit a vertex. Rotating labels, suppose the even diagonal is0→2. The only unused outgoing option from2 is2→3, so the odd diagonal must be3→1; then1's cycle arc leads back to2 rather than closing at0. The reversed even diagonal is the same argument after rotation. Thus two diagonals are impossible. There is only one Hamilton cycle, and the desired pair cannot exist.

This proves the exceptional obstruction completely, including its strong-connectivity threshold. It also shows why “every2-arc-strong digraph decomposes” is false, even under semicompleteness.

The external characterization now gives the positive subclass conclusions. A3-arc-strong semicomplete graph is2-arc-strong and cannot be \(S_4\), whose arc-connectivity is exactly2. It therefore decomposes. This gives one uniform absolute constant3 independent of order throughout the class. For tournaments, \(S_4\) is not a tournament because it contains opposite arcs, so every2-arc-strong tournament decomposes. Threshold1 is insufficient even there: a directed triangle is strongly connected, but each outgoing arc is indispensable in any strong spanning subdigraph.

The cut condition is also necessary for a strong arc decomposition in any digraph. Each of the two strong pieces must send an arc across every nonempty proper cut, and the two required arcs are distinct. Thus at least2 outgoing arcs are needed in the original cut. Applying this to complementary cuts also supplies entering arcs. This necessary argument alone is not sufficient: \(S_4\) meets it and still fails.

The semicomplete theorem, its exact exception and the different tournament threshold are therefore relevant progress on the literal packing problem. They do not justify assigning reverse arcs that need not exist, replacing strong connectivity by branchings, or extending the constant3 conclusion to unrestricted digraphs.
