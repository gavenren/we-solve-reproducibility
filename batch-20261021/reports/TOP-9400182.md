# Integral Brumer–Stark: the published odd part, the later preprint, and the factor of two

Claim type: **Literature report**.

The literal prompt includes the prime two and the abelian root-extension condition. A status account based only on the published Dasgupta–Kakde theorem would miss a later, directly relevant primary preprint. We distinguish the two sources and check the algebra which makes the integral distinction essential. This is a literature and normalization report, not a new proof of Brumer–Stark.

Write $a=w_K\theta_{K/k,S}\in\mathbb Z[G]$, with precisely the character and ideal-action conventions in the prompt. Nomura's Conjecture1.2 is the same assertion: for every fractional ideal $\mathfrak A$, an anti-unit generator of $\mathfrak A^a$ with an abelian $w_K$-root extension.

**The checked status boundary.** Dasgupta–Kakde's published Annals197(2023) theorem proves the smoothed class-group assertion after inverting2. The later Dasgupta–Kakde–Silliman–Wang preprint, arXiv:2310.16399v1, states an integral theorem, including2. Its Theorem1.1 gives the smoothed Brumer–Stark unit for a completely split finite prime, with the specified valuations, absolute values and congruences. The author-hosted research list still labels this four-author work a preprint. The present report has checked the stated hypotheses and the deduction in its Section2.2; it has not independently audited all its modular-form and Galois-cohomology arguments. “Published away from2” and “an integral preprint theorem exists” are distinct factual statements.

We spell out the exact formulation used there. Put $F=k$, $H=K$ and $n=[k:\mathbb Q]$. Let $T$ be disjoint from $S$ and satisfy the stated sufficient Deligne–Ribet condition: two primes of different residue characteristic, or one of residue characteristic greater than $n+1$. For $\mathfrak p\notin S\cup T$ splitting completely in $K$, fix $\mathfrak P\mid\mathfrak p$. The preprint supplies $u$ with
\[
|u|_w=1\ (w\nmid\mathfrak p),\qquad
u\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak q\mathcal O_K}\ (\mathfrak q\in T),
\]
and, for every character $\chi$,
\[
\sum_{\sigma\in G}\chi(\sigma)
 \operatorname{ord}_{\sigma^{-1}\mathfrak P}(u)
 =L_{S,T}(0,\chi). \tag{1}
\]
The absolute-value condition includes the complex places. It implies $u\,j(u)=1$: every embedding of the element $u\,j(u)$ has value1.

There is no hidden inversion of characters in converting (1) into an ideal statement. Define $\Theta_{S,T}=\sum_g b_g g$ by
\[
\chi(\Theta_{S,T})=L_{S,T}(0,\chi^{-1}).
\]
Then the ideal $\mathfrak P^{\Theta_{S,T}}$ has valuation $b_{\sigma^{-1}}$ at $\sigma^{-1}\mathfrak P$, whose character sum is
\[
\sum_\sigma\chi(\sigma)b_{\sigma^{-1}}
 =\chi^{-1}(\Theta_{S,T})=L_{S,T}(0,\chi).
\]
Finite-character Fourier inversion shows that these valuations are exactly those in (1). All other finite valuations vanish, so
\[
(u)=\mathfrak P^{\Theta_{S,T}}.
\tag{2}
\]
In the prompt's notation $\theta=\sum_\chi L_S(0,\overline\chi)e_\chi$, the identity $\overline\chi=\chi^{-1}$ for finite-group characters gives the same convention. Smoothing multiplies it by the Euler factors
\[
\Theta_{S,T}=\theta_{K/k,S}
 \prod_{\mathfrak q\in T}(1-N\mathfrak q\,\operatorname{Frob}_{\mathfrak q}^{-1}).
\tag{3}
\]
Thus (2) is a smoothed prime-ideal unit theorem. One must retain the smoothing and class-field-theoretic compatibility when comparing it to the prompt's arbitrary-ideal, unsmoothed root-extension statement; it is not legitimate simply to set $T=\varnothing$ in a theorem requiring $T$.

**Two elementary reasons an odd-part result cannot be called integral.** If an element $x$ of a finite abelian group maps to zero after tensoring with $\mathbb Z[1/2]$, the actual conclusion is that $2^r x=0$ for some $r$. Indeed this is exactly the kernel criterion for localization; conversely any element killed by a power of2 vanishes there. In particular, the group $\mathbb Z/2^m\mathbb Z$ has zero localization for every $m$, while its generator is nonzero. No amount of odd-primary checking alone rules that out.

A second loss occurs even after principalization. Let $b\in\mathbb Z[G]$ satisfy $jb=-b$, and suppose $\mathfrak A^b=(\beta)$. Then
\[
\left(\frac{\beta}{j(\beta)}\right)
 =(\mathfrak A^b)^{1-j}
 =\mathfrak A^{2b},\qquad
\frac{\beta}{j(\beta)}
 j\left(\frac{\beta}{j(\beta)}\right)=1. \tag{4}
\]
This constructs an anti-unit for **twice** the exponent. It does not construct one for $b$ itself. Section2.2 of the integral preprint explicitly handles the compensating powers of2 in its stronger annihilator statement before using this operation. The archimedean requirement therefore cannot be dismissed as a free choice of generator.

Finally, principalization with an anti-unit is still weaker than all of the literal prompt: the extension $K(\alpha^{1/w_K})/k$ must be abelian. The smoothed congruences are part of the arithmetic framework encoding this requirement; an arbitrary generator of a principal ideal is not automatically suitable. We do not present (2) or (4), without those further comparisons, as our own full solution.

The useful conclusions are the precise current source distinction, an exact match of the character/ideal conventions, and proofs of the two factor-of-two obstructions. The primary preprint reports completion of the integral conjecture; this report credits that work and does not assert novelty, publication of that preprint, or an independent verification of its complete proof.
