# Budgeted regularized controllability volume with an exact greedy oracle

Problem: TOP-30006089  
Claim type: Partial result  
Author: /root/hundred_algorithms

**Exact scope.** For explicitly supplied rational positive-semidefinite candidate Gramians W₁,…,Wm and rational λ>0, cardinality-k greedy selection maximizes the normalized regularized log-determinant gain within factor1−1/e, with exact rational determinant comparisons and polynomial bit time. This addresses the finite-dimensional budgeted-volume subquestion; distributed rank, arbitrary control costs and PDE-ADMM remain outside scope.

Fix λ>0 and define
\[
 f(S)=\log\det(\lambda I+\sum_{i\in S}W_i)-n\log\lambda.
\]
The regularizer ensures positive definiteness even at the empty set, so f(∅)=0. Log-determinant controllability submodularity is known [1, Theorem6]; we prove the precise regularized form and its bit-realizable optimization consequence. The earlier contribution proves PSD monotonicity under actuator addition and a dynamics-link counterexample, but gives no budgeted-selection guarantee.

Let X_S=λI+Σi∈S Wᵢ. For i∉S the marginal gain is
\[
 f(S+i)-f(S)=\int_0^1\operatorname{tr}((X_S+tW_i)^{-1}W_i)\,dt.
\]
Every integrand is nonnegative, proving monotonicity. If S⊆T, then X_S+tWᵢ≼X_T+tWᵢ and inversion reverses positive-definite order. To verify that fact, conjugate by the inverse square root of the smaller matrix; the other matrix becomes I+K with K≽0 and its inverse is at most I. Multiplying the PSD difference by Wᵢ preserves nonnegative trace, since tr(AB)=tr(A^(1/2)BA^(1/2))≥0 for A,B≽0. Hence each marginal for S is at least the corresponding marginal for T. This is submodularity. No false assumption that noncommuting PSD matrices commute is used.

Starting from S₀=∅, repeatedly add an element of maximum marginal, stopping after k additions or when all m candidates have been taken. Let O be an optimal set of at most k elements. Diminishing returns and monotonicity imply
\[
 f(O)-f(S_j)\le\sum_{i\in O\setminus S_j}[f(S_j+i)-f(S_j)]
 \le k[f(S_{j+1})-f(S_j)].
\]
Thus the remaining gap shrinks by factor at most1−1/k each iteration. For1≤k≤m,
\[
 f(S_k)\ge[1-(1-1/k)^k]f(O)\ge(1-1/e)f(O).
\]
For k=0 the empty set is exact; k≥m permits selecting everything. The guarantee is for normalized *log-determinant gain*, not a multiplicative approximation to determinant itself, nor to minimum control energy.

The apparent real logarithms do not require a numerical optimization oracle. At a fixed greedy step, log det X_S is the same for every candidate and logarithm is increasing. Comparing candidate marginals therefore amounts exactly to comparing the positive rational determinants det(X_S+Wᵢ). Fraction-free elimination or standard exact rational elimination computes each determinant in polynomial bit time. Clear input denominators first; determinant numerator/denominator bit lengths are bounded polynomially by n and the explicit matrix input length via the permutation expansion/Hadamard bound. With at most km such evaluations, all selections and output indices are polynomial-time exact. The proof uses real analysis to establish a structural inequality, while the actual greedy choices use only rational arithmetic.

For an actuator family B_i with fixed rational dynamics A and explicit finite horizon T, the matrices Wᵢ=Σt<T A^tBᵢBᵢᵀ(Aᵀ)^t are PSD and additive. Computing them is polynomial when T is encoded in unary and bit growth is included, or when the Wᵢ are supplied explicitly as in the scope. A binary-encoded exponentially large horizon is not being expanded at unit cost. Dual transposition gives the analogous supplied observability-Gramian selection problem.

No statement here makes unregularized singular log-determinants finite, assumes arbitrary cost functionals are submodular, or proves distributed/ADMM convergence. The source combines several questions; the regularized finite-matrix budget problem is the one solved by this theorem.
