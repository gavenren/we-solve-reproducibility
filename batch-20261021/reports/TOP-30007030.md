# The sharp generalized Mukai bound for every smooth toric Fano variety

Claim type: **Literature report**.

The full inequality **and its equality characterization** are established for every smooth toric Fano variety, in every dimension. This is the smooth subcase of Casagrande, *The number of vertices of a Fano polytope*, Annales de l'Institut Fourier56(2006),121–130, Theorem1(ii), which applies more generally to Q-factorial Gorenstein toric Fano varieties:
\[
 \rho_X(\iota_X-1)\le\dim X,
 \qquad
 \rho_X(\iota_X-1)=\dim X
 \Longleftrightarrow X\simeq(\mathbb P^{\iota_X-1})^{\rho_X}. \tag{1}
\]
All hypotheses in the literal prompt are retained. A smooth projective toric Fano is Gorenstein because its canonical divisor is Cartier and Q-factorial because smooth local rings are factorial. Its anticanonical divisor is ample by the Fano assumption. Therefore (1) applies directly, including the equality classification. This is a published result, not a new proof of the general conjecture.

It is useful to check the sharp examples with the actual **pseudoindex** rather than replace it by the divisibility index of $-K$. Let
\[
 X=\prod_{j=1}^s\mathbb P^{a_j},\qquad a_j\ge1.
\]
The pullbacks $H_j$ of the hyperplane classes form a basis of its Picard group, so $\rho_X=s$, $\dim X=\sum_j a_j$ and
\[
 -K_X=\sum_{j=1}^s(a_j+1)H_j. \tag{2}
\]
For any integral rational curve $C\subset X$, let $d_j=H_j\cdot C$. The line bundles $H_j$ are nef, so every $d_j$ is a nonnegative integer. At least one is positive: if all projections of the normalization of $C$ were constant then $C$ would be a point. Hence
\[
 -K_X\cdot C=\sum_j(a_j+1)d_j\ge\min_j(a_j+1).
\]
A line in a factor where $a_j$ is minimal, with the other coordinates fixed, attains this bound. Therefore
\[
 \iota_X=1+\min_j a_j,
 \qquad
 \rho_X(\iota_X-1)=s\min_j a_j\le\sum_j a_j=\dim X. \tag{3}
\]
Equality in (3) holds exactly when every $a_j$ equals the same integer $a$, giving $(\mathbb P^a)^s$ with $a=\iota_X-1$. This verifies both the numerical sharpness and the exact geometric form in (1) for all products.

For comparison, the Fano **index** of this product is $\gcd_j(a_j+1)$, obtained from the integral basis in(2). These two invariants differ: $\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^2$ has pseudoindex2 but index1. An argument using only the index would therefore miss the asserted inequality with the larger pseudoindex. The product calculation cannot by itself prove the general equality characterization; that direction is the substantive content of Casagrande's theorem.

The combinatorial reformulation also identifies the precise theorem being imported. If the fan has ray set $\Sigma(1)$, smooth completeness gives $\rho_X=|\Sigma(1)|-n$. The anticanonical Fano polytope is simplicial and reflexive in the Q-factorial Gorenstein setting of the source. Casagrande controls this ray/vertex count using the pseudoindex and proves the extremal splitting, rather than merely applying the weaker bound $\rho_X\le2n$. The latter is Theorem1(i) and does not imply (1).

No claim for a nontoric Fano variety follows from this reduction. In particular, the general manuscript claim listed in the official prompt has not been used as a theorem. The positive-dimensional requirement matters: for $\iota=1$ the left side is zero and equality cannot occur when $n>0$, in agreement with the displayed product classification. All remaining dimensions and all smooth toric Fano varieties, rather than a finite classification list, are included.
