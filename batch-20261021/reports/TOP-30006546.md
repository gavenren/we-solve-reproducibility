# The corrected half-leading-constant upper bound

For unrestricted translative coverings of$\mathbb R^n$ by congruent Euclidean balls, Dumer's Corollary2 in arXiv:math/0606002v2 gives
\[
\theta_T(B_2^n)\le(1/2+o(1))\,n\log n.
\]
The exact checked version is dated20May2018. This is an established upper bound with a leading constant, not the optimal asymptotic density and not a lattice covering theorem.

Its main finite-parameter theorem concerns the$n$-dimensional sphere$S_r^n\subset\mathbb R^{n+1}$ for every$n\ge3$ and$r>1$: unit balls cover it with normalized cap density at most
\[
\left(\tfrac12+\frac{3\log\log n}{\log n}+\frac3{\log n}\right)n\log n
=\tfrac12 n\log n+3n\log\log n+3n.
\]
The paper then passes from large-radius spherical coverings to complete Euclidean space in Corollary2. The residual is$o(n\log n)$ as$n\to\infty$ because$\log\log n/\log n\to0$. The radius quantifier is uniform in the stated sphere theorem; it is not a claim for the solid ball of every fixed radius. The paper expressly leaves an analogous arbitrary-radius solid-ball bound as a further question.

The proof uses a random family of caps, estimates the small number of unsaturated or porous residual caps, and adds centers for those holes. The explicit error terms record this alteration step; merely showing that a typical point is covered with high probability would not ensure coverage of every point. The theorem and its proof framework are imported and credited, rather than certified by an unexecuted random simulation.

Version choice matters here. The addendum in v2 identifies an incorrect cap-volume comparison in the2007 published version: a power of a chord-length ratio had been used where the appropriate comparison involved angular radii. The author states that it was not used in the main Lemmas6–8, but affected preliminary residual estimates; the corrected version removes it and tightens other estimates. We therefore cite v2's exact theorem and residual, rather than silently copying an old display from the journal PDF. No independent numerical certification of every auxiliary estimate is claimed.

The two earlier archive records report a universal single-lattice$n\log n$ claim and a lattice multi-covering extension. Even if those claims hold, they supply no half-leading-constant theorem for unrestricted ball coverings; moreover their worst-case body lower bound would not identify the ball's own optimum. A lattice is an allowed translative set, so an upper lattice bound transfers to arbitrary coverings, but a lattice lower bound does not transfer in that direction. This report avoids that reversal.

The classical linear lower bound credited in Dumer's introduction leaves a logarithmic gap from this upper bound. Its original1959 proof was not separately audited here, so no new sharp lower constant or current-best-status claim is attached. The ball problem is not settled by the general-body worst-case statements. The report supplies a precise checked upper theorem, correct geometry/model and version-sensitive source correction.
