# Published four- and five-dimensional obstruction with nonnegative scalar-curvature rigidity

**Problem:** TOP-30004759

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** For every smooth closed aspherical manifold of dimension 4 or 5, reports the published Chodosh–Li obstruction to positive scalar curvature and the stronger conclusion that any nonnegative-scalar-curvature metric is flat; explains smooth-structure and finite-cover implications.

For the literal universal assertion in dimensions \(n\ge4\), a published theorem resolves the entire dimension-four and dimension-five strata, with a rigidity conclusion at scalar curvature zero. Chodosh–Li, Theorem 2, proves that if \(M\) is a smooth closed aspherical manifold and \(\dim M\in\{4,5\}\), then it has no smooth Riemannian metric of positive scalar curvature. Furthermore, if a smooth metric \(g\) on such an \(M\) satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{Scal}_g(x)\ge0\quad\text{for all }x\in M,
\]
then \(g\) is flat, meaning that its full Riemann curvature tensor vanishes.

These hypotheses match the prompt in these dimensions. Aspherical means that the universal cover is contractible. It does not mean that a chosen metric has nonpositive sectional curvature. In particular the theorem ranges over every metric on an arbitrary smooth closed aspherical four- or five-manifold; it is not restricted to the metrics witnessing a special geometric structure. No spin or first-Betti-number condition is part of its statement.

The flatness clause is appreciably stronger than exclusion of metrics whose scalar curvature is strictly positive at every point. On an arbitrary manifold, a nonnegative scalar curvature function can vanish on a large subset while the curvature tensor is nonzero elsewhere. The quoted theorem excludes this possibility for these aspherical manifolds: if any full curvature component is nonzero anywhere, then the scalar curvature must be negative somewhere. This last assertion is a direct contrapositive. If it were nonnegative everywhere, Theorem 2 would make the entire tensor zero.

There are two useful exact consequences that keep the quantifiers straight. First, suppose a closed topological aspherical manifold \(X\) of dimension four or five is given any smooth structure. Its underlying universal covering space is still contractible; changing the smooth structure does not change that topological fact. Applying Theorem 2 to that smooth manifold rules out positive scalar curvature for that smooth structure. Thus within the smoothable case the obstruction does not depend on selecting a preferred smoothing. This assertion does not assert that every topological manifold is smoothable.

Second, finite covers cause no additional orientation restriction. For a finite covering \(p:\widehat M\to M\), compactness and the absence of boundary are preserved, and both spaces have the same contractible universal cover. A metric pulls back to a metric for which \(p\) is a local isometry, so
\[
 \operatorname{Scal}_{p^*g}=\operatorname{Scal}_g\circ p.
\]
If one passes to the orientation double cover to use an oriented formulation of an intermediate argument, positive scalar curvature would pull back, and flatness of the pullback would descend because curvature is local and \(p\) is surjective. The published Theorem 2 itself already states the conclusion without an orientability assumption; the covering discussion explains why no such extra assumption should inadvertently enter this application.

The deep proof uses stable solutions of prescribed-mean-curvature problems, the authors' generalized soap bubbles or \(\mu\)-bubbles, together with global topological arguments. Those analytical and dimension-sensitive results are credited to the source, not derived from the elementary observations above. In particular, the dimension bounds cannot be removed merely by applying the word “aspherical” or taking a finite cover.

The report therefore verifies the full literal obstruction for \(n=4,5\), and a stronger metric rigidity statement in the same dimensions. It makes no assertion here about all \(n\ge6\), about noncompact manifolds, incomplete metrics, metrics with boundary, or an unverified all-dimensional announcement. Those extensions require additional results and remain outside the checked scope.

References actually checked:

- O. Chodosh and C. Li, Generalized soap bubbles and the topology of manifolds with positive scalar curvature, arXiv:2008.11888v4 (20 September 2023), Theorem 2; published Ann. of Math. (2) 199 (2024), 707–740, DOI 10.4007/annals.2024.199.2.3.
