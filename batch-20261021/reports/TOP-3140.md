# Subdivided cliques, even-cycle products, and exact host normalization

**Claim type: Literature report.** Conlon, Kim, Lee and Lee establish that the1-subdivision of every clique has Sidorenko's property, that subdivision preserves that property, and that Cartesian product with an even cycle preserves it [1, Theorems1.3–1.4]. We use these substantive theorems as credited inputs to identify an explicit unbounded family with exact exponents in the prompt's normalization. The earlier hosted claimed counterexample is not used as a theorem and is not independently verified here.

For integers \(r\ge3,a\ge1,t\ge0\), and \(s_1,\ldots,s_t\ge2\), let
\[
 H=S^a(K_r)\square C_{2s_1}\square\cdots\square C_{2s_t},
\]
where \(S\) subdivides each edge once, with different new vertices for different edges. Then for every simple host \(G\) with \(N\ge1\) vertices and \(p=2|E(G)|/N^2\),
\[
 \operatorname{hom}(H,G)\ge N^{v(H)}p^{e(H)}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The source hypotheses hold by induction. The initial \(S(K_r)\) has the property by Theorem1.3(i); repeating Theorem1.3(ii) gives \(S^a(K_r)\); then each even-cycle factor is permitted by Theorem1.4. This does not claim that arbitrary edge subdivisions with unrelated lengths are covered, or that arbitrary Cartesian factors preserve the property.

Put \(D=\binom r2\), \(P=\prod_{j=1}^t2s_j\), with empty product1. We now compute the full exponent data. After \(a\) subdivisions, each original edge is a path of length \(2^a\), and all new internal vertices on distinct original edges are distinct. Thus
\[
 v_a=r+(2^a-1)D,\qquad e_a=2^aD.
\]
For a graph \(F\) with \(v\) vertices and \(e\) edges,
\[
 v(F\square C_L)=Lv,\qquad
 e(F\square C_L)=Le+Lv,
\]
because each of the \(L\) layers contributes \(e\) edges and each of the \(v\) cyclic fibers contributes \(L\). Iteration gives
\[
 v(H)=Pv_a,\qquad e(H)=P(e_a+t v_a).
 \tag{2}
\]
Equations(1)–(2) supply an exact finite host bound, not an asymptotic embedding statement. Each subdivision is bipartite, and a Cartesian product of bipartite graphs is bipartite by summing the factor2-colors modulo2, so the patterns remain inside the prompt's hypotheses. They are connected and have unbounded order and dimension.

For example \(r=4,a=1,t=1,s_1=2\) gives \(v=40,e=88\); the conclusion is
\(\operatorname{hom}(S(K_4)\square C_4,G)\ge N^{40}p^{88}\).
Images are allowed to coincide. Replacing homomorphisms by injective copies or using \(2|E|/(N(N-1))\) would change the assertion. If \(p=0\), the right side vanishes because \(e(H)>0\), so there is no division-by-zero exception.

A useful separate exact fact explains amplification claims in the preceding contribution. For the categorical product \(G^{\times m}\), two \(m\)-tuples are adjacent precisely when all corresponding coordinates are adjacent in \(G\). For every fixed graph \(F\),
\[
 t(F,G^{\times m})=t(F,G)^m,\qquad
 p(G^{\times m})=p(G)^m.
\]
A homomorphism into the product is exactly a tuple of coordinate homomorphisms, proving both formulas. If a genuine finite counterexample has \(p(G)>0\) and ratio \(0\le\rho=t(F,G)/p(G)^{e(F)}<1\), then its product powers have ratio \(\rho^m\to0\). This validates the amplification implication **conditional on a verified initial deficit**; it does not certify the claimed35-vertex pattern or its purported host. Categorical host products and Cartesian pattern products are different operations and are not interchangeable.
