# An explicit individual height formula for every type of an abelian CM field

Claim type: **Literature report**.

The prompt's individual identity is known for every CM field $E$ that is Galois and abelian over $\mathbb Q$, for every CM type. We credit the theorem of Colmez–Obus and derive an explicit finite Fourier formula for all its coefficients. This supplies a uniform infinite family of individual cases, including types that do not form one Galois orbit, rather than inferring an individual identity from one average.

Put $G=\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb Q)$, $|G|=2g$, and identify the embeddings with $G$. Let $c\in G$ be complex conjugation. For a type $\Phi\subset G$, define
\[
S_\Phi(\chi)=\sum_{\tau\in\Phi}\chi(\tau),\qquad
m_\Phi(\chi)=\frac{|S_\Phi(\chi)|^2}{2g},
\tag{1}
\]
where $\chi$ runs through the one-dimensional characters of the finite abelian group $G$. With the stable full-ring height and finite $L$-functions in the literal prompt, the resulting theorem is
\[
h^{\rm Falt}(E,\Phi)
=-\frac g2\log(2\pi)
-\sum_{\chi(c)=-1}\frac{|S_\Phi(\chi)|^2}{2g}
 \left(\frac{L'(0,\chi)}{L(0,\chi)}
                  +\frac12\log f_\chi\right).
\tag{2}
\]
The Artin conductor $f_\chi$ is that of the individual character; it is not replaced by the discriminant of $E$.

**External theorem and normalization.** Howard's Theorem3.3.2 explicitly attributes the individual abelian case to Colmez and Obus. Obus's Theorem3.9 removes the rational $\log2$ error in the earlier abelian result. These are established literature inputs, not new results proved here. Howard's Sections3.2–3.3 give exactly the stable-height normalization used in the prompt. In particular the finite trivial-character term is retained. No completed-$L$ archimedean factors are inserted into (2).

We prove the coefficient formula. Since $G$ is abelian, translation of the type does not alter its overlap function:
\[
A_{\rho\Phi}(\sigma)
=|\rho\Phi\cap\sigma\rho\Phi|
=|\Phi\cap\sigma\Phi|.
\]
Thus class averaging over the type orbit leaves $A_\Phi$ unchanged. Expanding its character inner product gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle A_\Phi,\chi\rangle
&=\frac1{|G|}\sum_{\sigma\in G}A_\Phi(\sigma)\overline{\chi(\sigma)}\\
&=\frac1{|G|}\sum_{\tau,\upsilon\in\Phi}
       \overline{\chi(\tau\upsilon^{-1})}
=\frac{|S_\Phi(\chi)|^2}{|G|}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves (1) without a conjectural period calculation.

The trivial character has $S_\Phi(\mathbf1)=g$, hence coefficient $g/2$. Every nontrivial even character has coefficient zero: because $G=\Phi\sqcup c\Phi$ and $\chi(c)=1$,
\[
0=\sum_{\tau\in G}\chi(\tau)=2S_\Phi(\chi).
\]
All remaining characters are odd. Substitution into the proved abelian Colmez formula gives (2), using $\zeta'(0)/\zeta(0)=\log(2\pi)$ and conductor1 for the trivial character. The expression is real: complex characters occur in conjugate pairs with equal squared-sum coefficients and conjugate logarithmic derivatives.

Two exact checks make the formula usable for any finite abelian type. All coefficients in (1) are nonnegative real algebraic numbers, and
\[
\sum_\chi m_\Phi(\chi)=g,\qquad
\sum_{\chi(c)=-1}m_\Phi(\chi)=g/2. \tag{3}
\]
The first identity follows either by evaluating the character expansion at the identity or from character orthogonality:
$\sum_\chi|S_\Phi(\chi)|^2=|G|\,|\Phi|$.
The second subtracts the trivial coefficient. Individual coefficients need not be rational for an arbitrary abelian group; the formula retains their actual algebraic character values.

For a multiquadratic CM field, $G$ has exponent2, so all character values are $\pm1$. The coefficients are then rational squares divided by $2g$, and (2) uses only the odd quadratic Dirichlet characters, equivalently imaginary quadratic subfields. This evaluates the entire coefficient system for every type in every such field by finite character sums.

As a check beyond the one-orbit case, take a biquadratic CM field and a type induced from one imaginary quadratic subfield of discriminant $D$. Here $g=2$. Its two embeddings lie in one coset of the corresponding index-two subgroup, so the associated odd character has $|S|=2$, coefficient1; the other odd character has $S=0$. Formula(2) becomes
\[
h^{\rm Falt}(E,\Phi)
=-\log(2\pi)-\frac{L'(0,\chi_D)}{L(0,\chi_D)}
                         -\frac12\log|D|.
\]
This is twice the matching CM elliptic height, consistent with the induced product type. The two biquadratic type orbits may select different imaginary quadratic subfields: equality on both comes from the individual abelian theorem, not merely their total average.

The earlier contribution's full text studies orbit residuals, two quartic examples and an elliptic normalization test. The present report supplies the missing established individual input for all abelian fields and a complete coefficient formula for every type, with the even-character, conductor and real-value checks. It makes no claim about arbitrary nonabelian CM fields, nonmaximal endomorphism orders, or a new proof of the general conjecture.
