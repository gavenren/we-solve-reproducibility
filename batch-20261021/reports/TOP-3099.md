# A quantitative simple-polytope theorem, with the nonsimple gap retained

The primary preprint by De Loera, Fang, Guo, Lu and Zheng, arXiv:2609.00397v1 (31August2026), establishes finiteness for every pair of target dimensions in the simple-polytope subclass. In the diagonal form of this prompt, its Corollary2.9 implies
\[
 f_s(k,k)\le\left\lceil k^3\,2^{k-1}\right\rceil\qquad(k\ge3).
\]
Here every input polytope is simple, but the simplex or combinatorial cube obtained is an actual$k$-face of the input. The statement is an attributed preprint theorem; no publication or independent formal verification is asserted.

The distinction between this and the archive's product example is substantial. The earlier complete attempt proves a sharp threshold only among Cartesian products of simplices, and mentions the new preprint from its abstract. The present report checks its full statement and relevant proof section for all simple polytopes, not just products. The preprint's TheoremB further yields
\[
f_s(k,k)\ge \max\{4,2(k-1)\}(k-1)+1.
\]
These bounds leave a substantial quantitative gap even within simple polytopes.

The source proof uses Nikulin's average face bounds and a local graph restriction forced by excluding simplex faces. More precisely, its Theorem2.5 bounds the average number of$i$-faces within$k$-faces by an explicit$U_i(d,k)$. Proposition2.6 combines absence of simplex$\ell$-faces with a lower vertex requirement; Corollary2.9 chooses the ambient dimension large enough that the average facet and vertex constraints force a cubical$k$-face. For$\ell,k\ge3$ its displayed safe bound is
\[
f_s(\ell,k)\le\tfrac12 k^2\ell\,2^k.
\]
Putting$\ell=k$ and rounding upward gives the threshold used above. This specialization avoids relying on a numerical extrapolation from a few dimensions. The source's proof uses an integer$m$ and$d=2m$; the report uses a ceiling in its final threshold to avoid treating an arbitrary real bound as an integer dimension.

The imported averaging and face-recognition lemmas are deep combinatorial inputs, credited rather than reconstructed from a vertex list. Combinatorial cube means its face lattice, so no equality of lengths or orthogonality is added. Simplicity is also indispensable: a general polytope need not be simple, and polar duality does not repair that omission while preserving the requested type of face. A simplex face in the dual corresponds to a coface condition in the original, not automatically the required simplex or cube face.

For$k=1$ any edge works. The source separately gives$f(2,2)=5$ for arbitrary polytopes. Its new all-$k$ theorem concerns$f_s$, not$f$; the full nonsimple question for prescribed$k\ge3$ remains outside the report. The existence of a good section, projection, triangulation cell or subset is never substituted for a face.
