# A reduction that retains the zero-waste integer subsequence

Bui's2025 Theorem1 proves that unrestricted optimal waste$W(x)$ is comparable, up to absolute multiplicative constants for all sufficiently large real$x$, to the optimum$W_{\rm good}(x)$ when every square has inclination at most$10^{-10}$ from the containing square. The inclination is taken modulo the symmetries of a square, in$[0,\pi/4]$. This is a small-angle restriction, not an axis-parallel restriction or a permission to discard tilted squares without paying for lost area.

The source proof uses a flow/cut construction and a surgery removing bad cells with additional waste bounded by a constant times the original waste. In particular the easy inclusion gives$W(x)\le W_{\rm good}(x)$, and the substantive direction is$W_{\rm good}(x)\le C W(x)$. Those are the imported theorem's two directions. Neither says$W(x)$ has a positive lower bound on every large real side length.

The integer issue can be quantified exactly without importing any asymptotic exponent. For an integer$m\ge1$ and$t\in[0,1)$, the grid packing yields
\[
0\le W(m+t)\le(m+t)^2-m^2=2mt+t^2.
\]
Because$N(x)$ is an integer and cannot exceed$x^2$, also
\[
W(x)\ge x^2-\lfloor x^2\rfloor.
\]
The upper bound shows, for every nonnegative sequence$t_m=o(m^{-1/2})$,
\[
W(m+t_m)=o(\sqrt m).
\]
It includes the exact zero-waste subsequence$t_m=0$. Consequently a statement such as$W(x)\ge c\sqrt x$ for every sufficiently large real$x$ is false regardless of how rotated squares are used.

The correct distinction is between$W\notin o(\sqrt x)$ and a pointwise$\Omega(\sqrt x)$ claim. The former only requires a subsequence along which$W(x)/\sqrt x$ stays bounded away from zero; it is compatible with other subsequences tending to zero. The primary paper carefully writes the former when discussing Roth–Vaughan. We do not independently certify that original lower-bound proof here, nor attach its exponent as a universal pointwise bound.

The multiplicative good-square theorem respects zeros: at an integer$m$, both classes contain the exact grid tiling and both wastes equal zero. It also transfers the near-integer upper bound just derived, since that packing is itself good. Thus the source reduction can simplify a future lower-bound argument only if the latter preserves the needed quantifiers on side lengths. Exact behavior between integers and the sharp global asymptotic description remain outside this report.
