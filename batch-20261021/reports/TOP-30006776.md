# Numerically trivial one-cycles on all abelian varieties and curve-dominated targets

Claim type: **Literature report**.

Numerical equivalence and smash equivalence coincide for **one-dimensional cycles on every abelian variety**, without a dimension bound. They also coincide for one-cycles on every smooth projective variety admitting a generically finite surjective morphism from a product of smooth projective curves. The source input is Sebastian [1, Theorems6 and9]; we give the precise transfer argument and distinguish the codimension and field scopes.

Over an algebraically closed field$k$, Sebastian’s Theorem6 proves that every numerically trivial rational one-cycle on$C_1\times\cdots\times C_d$ is smash-nilpotent. This is a codimension$d-1$ statement, not a statement about all cycle dimensions on that product. His Theorem9 proves the analogous one-cycle result on every abelian variety. The paper’s modified-diagonal relations and induction handle cycles that need not be algebraically trivial; the conclusion is consequently stronger than invoking Voevodsky’s theorem only on algebraically trivial cycles. We credit those long arguments rather than claiming that tensor nilpotence follows from composition nilpotence.

Here is the transfer in full. Let$f:Y\to X$ be a proper generically finite surjective morphism of degree$\delta>0$ between smooth projective varieties of dimension$d$, with$Y$ a product of curves. Let$\alpha\in\mathrm{CH}^{d-1}(X)_{\mathbb Q}$ be numerically trivial. The pullback$f^*\alpha$ is defined in the Chow ring of smooth varieties, even if$f$ is not flat. It is numerically trivial on$Y$: for every divisor$D$ there,
\[
 \deg_Y(f^*\alpha\cdot D)=\deg_X(\alpha\cdot f_*D)=0
\]
by the projection formula. Thus Theorem6 gives an$N$ with$(f^*\alpha)^{\boxtimes N}=0$. Push along$f^N:Y^N\to X^N$. Compatibility of exterior products with proper pushforward and $f_*f^*\alpha=\delta\alpha$ yields
\[
 0=(f^N)_*((f^*\alpha)^{\boxtimes N})
   =\delta^N\alpha^{\boxtimes N}.
\]
Rational coefficients permit division by$\delta^N$, proving the target. This uses an actual morphism and same-dimensional generic finiteness. We do not silently resolve an arbitrary rational map and assume the resolution remains a product of curves.

The abelian-variety theorem has a different useful reduction, explained in [1, §3]. The Jacobian of a curve is covered by a sufficiently large symmetric power, which is a projective bundle over the Jacobian. Pull a one-cycle back to that bundle, intersect with the needed power of its hyperplane class to restore dimension1, then pull to a product of curves by the symmetric quotient. Projection and the factor$m!$ recover the original class. Thus Theorem6 applies despite the larger dimension of that product. An abelian variety is a direct factor up to isogeny of a Jacobian; isogeny pull–push and the inclusion/projection of the factor transfer the one-cycle assertion. The hyperplane intersection is essential: simply pulling a one-cycle through a positive-relative-dimension morphism would produce the wrong cycle dimension.

Smash-nilpotence always implies numerical triviality. If$\alpha^{\boxtimes N}=0$ and$\beta$ is any complementary cycle, then
\[
 0=\deg(\alpha^{\boxtimes N}\cdot\beta^{\boxtimes N})
   =\deg(\alpha\cdot\beta)^N,
\]
so the rational intersection number is zero. Consequently the one-direction theorem really gives equality of the two equivalences in the stated classes.

The primary paper works over algebraically closed fields. For a variety and cycle defined over a general field, one may apply the algebraically closed theorem after base extension when its geometric hypothesis holds, then descend a finite rational-equivalence witness and use finite flat push–pull. That descent mechanism was already proved in prior A1; it is not presented as new here. No uniform exponent from the ambient dimension alone is claimed.

Prior A1 supplies abstract descent, curve-correspondence certificates and warnings about composition powers, but no unconditional theorem for all numerically trivial one-cycles on arbitrary-dimensional abelian varieties. The added content is the precisely checked published one-cycle theorem, its dimension-correct Jacobian mechanism and complete generically-finite transfer. Higher cycle dimensions on general abelian varieties remain outside this contribution.
