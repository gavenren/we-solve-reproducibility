# Original Pixton relations generate every genus-zero boundary-divisor relation

Claim type: **Partial result**.

There is an exact unbounded-marking partial result for the **closed** spaces in the prompt: for every$n\ge3$, every relation among polynomials in the boundary divisors of $\overline{\mathcal M}_{0,n}$ is a consequence of the original Pixton relation ideal and its standard forgetful/gluing closure. This is a statement about the entire boundary-divisor presentation, not merely a finite-degree numerical test. We do **not** identify the whole kernel for arbitrary formal $\kappa/\psi$-decorated strata in positive genus (or silently identify those formal presentations).

Use rational coefficients. For $2\le|S|\le n-2$, let$d_S=d_{S^c}$ denote the boundary divisor corresponding to the unordered split$S|S^c$. Form
\[
 B_n=\mathbb Q[d_S]/(d_Sd_T:\ S|S^c,T|T^c\text{ incompatible}).
\]
Two splits are incompatible if all four sets $S\cap T,S\cap T^c,S^c\cap T,S^c\cap T^c$ are nonempty. They cannot occur as edges of a common stable tree; consequently their product is already zero in the genus-zero strata-algebra multiplication (PPZ Section0.3). Thus$B_n$ has a natural map into that algebra and then to the Chow ring.

Keel's published presentation, *Intersection Theory of Moduli Space of Stable N-Pointed Curves of Genus Zero*, Trans.AMS330(1992),545–574, introductory item(6), proved in Section4.Theorem1, states that the map $B_n\to\operatorname{CH}^*(\overline{\mathcal M}_{0,n})_{\mathbb Q}$ is surjective, with kernel generated by the four-point relations
\[
 \sum_{i,j\in S;\ k,l\notin S}d_S
 -\sum_{i,k\in S;\ j,l\notin S}d_S, \tag{1}
\]
for distinct$i,j,k,l$, and their permutations. Each unordered split is counted once in the indicated orientation. It remains to put(1) inside the original Pixton ideal, rather than add it as an unrelated relation.

This is explicit on $\overline{\mathcal M}_{0,4}$. Write $a=d_{12|34}$, $b=d_{13|24}$, $c=d_{14|23}$. PPZ, *Relations on Mg,n via3-spin structures*, arXiv:1303.1043v3, Example3.5, gives among the original relations
\[
 R_{12}=5\kappa_1-5\psi_1-5\psi_2+7\psi_3+7\psi_4+5a-7b-7c,
\]
with$R_{34}$ obtained by exchanging the first and second pairs, and$R_{13},R_{24}$ by the corresponding relabeling. Direct addition cancels every cotangent asymmetry:
\[
 R_{12}+R_{34}=10\kappa_1+2\sum_i\psi_i+10a-14b-14c,
\]
\[
 R_{13}+R_{24}=10\kappa_1+2\sum_i\psi_i-14a+10b-14c.
\]
Their difference is exactly$24(a-b)$. Interchanging labels likewise yields$24(a-c)$. Since24 is invertible over$\mathbb Q$, both four-point boundary differences are original Pixton consequences. No assertion about integral relation generation is made.

Forget all but the four markings$i,j,k,l$. The pullback of the boundary point$12|34$ is the sum of all divisors separating$i,j$ from$k,l$, with multiplicity1; the analogous statement holds for$13|24$. Their difference is exactly(1). The original system's relation closure allows these pullbacks. To pass from linear generators to the whole kernel one uses its **ideal** closure, not just the vector space spanned by the initially displayed equations. This convention is explicit in PPZ Section3.5: the initial relations generate ideals, whose family is then closed under the specified pushforwards and pullbacks. Therefore every polynomial multiple of(1) is in the relation ideal. Keel's presentation proves the asserted completeness for$B_n$.

The relations are valid in Chow here: Keel's construction also identifies the Chow groups with the even cohomology groups and gives no odd cohomology. Thus PPZ's cohomological validity suffices in genus zero; one must not use that argument to claim the same Chow conclusion in arbitrary genus. Alternatively each four-point relation is directly the pullback of rational equivalence of points on $\overline{\mathcal M}_{0,4}\simeq\mathbb P^1$.

For$n=3$ there are no boundary divisors and$B_3=\mathbb Q$, so the claim is immediate. For$n=4$, the extraction above gives the complete boundary presentation with the zero degree-two products forced by dimension. In all$n$, boundary products represent every Chow class by Keel. This surjectivity does **not** by itself identify the kernel from all other formal decorated expressions in the strata algebra. The report therefore makes a precise presentation-level partial claim, leaving the full original closed-moduli question and any proposed large-genus counterexample unverified.
