# Corrected rational strong Atiyah theorem and its residual-group consequence

**Problem:** TOP-30000209

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** For all finite CW complexes whose torsion-free fundamental groups lie in Schick’s corrected class D, proves the exact passage from integer rational-matrix kernel dimensions to integer reduced cellular L2-Betti numbers; includes residually torsion-free elementary amenable groups and noncommutative free-product/direct-product constructions.

The literal question concerns integer \(L^2\)-Betti numbers for all finite CW complexes with torsion-free fundamental group. A substantial established class is provided by Schick's corrected strong Atiyah theorem. The correction matters: the paper's current arXiv text expressly removes claims affected by an older gap. The following uses Definition 1.10 and Theorem 1.11 of arXiv:math/0001101v4, not an unchecked version of a broader closure assertion.

Define \(\mathcal D\) to be the smallest nonempty class satisfying these conditions. It is closed under subgroups and under direct and inverse limits of directed systems. Further, if a torsion-free group \(G\) admits an epimorphism \(p:G\to A\) onto an elementary amenable group, and \(p^{-1}(E)\in\mathcal D\) for every finite subgroup \(E<A\), then \(G\in\mathcal D\). Here elementary amenable has its usual meaning: the class generated by finite and abelian groups using subgroups, quotients, extensions and directed unions. All groups in \(\mathcal D\) are torsion-free.

Schick's Theorem 1.11 says that the strong Atiyah conjecture over \(\mathbb QG\) holds for \(G\in\mathcal D\). In the torsion-free setting this says that every finite matrix over \(\mathbb QG\), acting on a finite sum of copies of \(\ell^2(G)\), has kernel of integer von Neumann dimension. Rectangular matrices cause no issue: for \(A:\ell^2(G)^u\to\ell^2(G)^v\), embed \(A\) as the off-diagonal block of the square map on \(u+v\) copies, acting as zero on the \(v\)-coordinate summand. Its kernel has dimension \(\dim_G\ker A+v\), so integrality for square matrices gives integrality for \(A\).

Here is the full passage to the problem's reduced homology, including the closure in its definition. Lift the cells of a finite \(X\), with \(\pi_1X=G\in\mathcal D\), so \(C_i^{(2)}=\ell^2(G)^{c_i}\) and \(d_i\) has entries in \(\mathbb ZG\subset\mathbb QG\). Put \(k_i=\dim_G\ker d_i\). Hilbert-module rank-nullity uses the closure of the image:
\[
 \dim_G\overline{\operatorname{im}d_{i+1}}
       =c_{i+1}-k_{i+1}.
\]
Since that closed image is contained in \(\ker d_i\), orthogonal decomposition yields
\[
 b_i^{(2)}(X)=k_i-\big(c_{i+1}-k_{i+1}\big)
             =k_i+k_{i+1}-c_{i+1}\in\mathbb Z.
\]
It is nonnegative because it is the dimension of a Hilbert module. No algebraic image is substituted for its closure, and no assumption that \(X\) itself is aspherical is needed.

The class is substantially noncommutative. Proposition 1.13 includes torsion-free groups in Linnell's class \(\mathcal C\), hence free groups, and proves that \(\mathcal D\) is closed under direct products and free products. Consequently arbitrary finite iterations of those operations on free groups satisfy the problem for every finite CW complex having the resulting fundamental group. The source's Example 1.14 explicitly treats
\(\mathbb Z*(F_2\times F_2)\); its role here is a group outside the preceding abelian examples, not a new computation claimed by this report.

One useful residual consequence can be checked directly from the definition. Every torsion-free elementary amenable group \(Q\) belongs to \(\mathcal D\): the trivial group lies in every nonempty subgroup-closed class, and apply the extension clause to the identity \(Q\to Q\), whose only finite subgroup is trivial. If \(G\) is residually torsion-free elementary amenable, the separating quotient maps embed \(G\) in a product of such groups \(Q_\alpha\). Finite products belong to \(\mathcal D\). The full product is the inverse limit of the finite-coordinate products with the coordinate projections, so it belongs to \(\mathcal D\), and subgroup closure then includes \(G\). The integrality proof above therefore applies to all such \(G\).

The theorem is rational-coefficient: it is sufficient because cellular matrices are integral, but it is not being promoted here to an arbitrary complex group-ring theorem. No claim for every torsion-free group follows from these closure properties. Nor are torsion denominator statements substituted for the prompt. The new contribution is the corrected noncommutative regime, a rigorous residual application and the exact reduced-homology deduction, with the deep matrix theorem credited to Schick.

References actually checked:
