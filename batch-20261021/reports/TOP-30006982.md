# Numerical MZV independence: the odd-value dimension mechanism and its limits

**Claim type: Literature report.** The literal $Z_k$ are spaces of actual real multiple zeta values, and the target is $\dim_{\mathbb Q}Z_k=d_k$ at each fixed weight. Brown's motivic basis gives numerical spanning, not numerical independence; the earlier contribution already explains that upper bound. This report reconstructs a substantial unconditional **numerical** lower-dimension mechanism in the depth-one odd sector, using Rivoal's primary theorem and its hypergeometric proof. It then derives quantitative independence after removing low odd weights or any fixed finite-dimensional space. These are credited literature and deductions, not a new proof of Zagier's conjecture.

Put
\[
V_N=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}\{1,\zeta(3),\zeta(5),\ldots,\zeta(2N+1)\},
\qquad D_N=\dim_{\mathbb Q}V_N.
\]
Rivoal [R, Théorème 1] proves the exact quantified statement
\[
\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists N_0(\varepsilon)\
\forall N>N_0(\varepsilon),\qquad
D_N\ge\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\log2}\log N.                    \tag{1}
\]
The logarithms are natural. The numbers in (1) are the numerical single zeta values, not motivic symbols. The odd restriction is essential: the span of even zeta values already has linear growth by Euler's evaluation and transcendence of $\pi$, so a logarithmic bound for all weights alone would miss the arithmetic content.

## 1. How the hypergeometric forms remove all even zeta values

Here is the parity mechanism underlying (1), reconstructed from [R, Lemma 1]. Fix an odd integer $a\ge3$ and $1\le r<a/2$. For a positive even integer $m$, define
\[
R_m(t)=m!^{\,a-2r}
 \frac{(t-rm+1)_{rm}(t+m+2)_{rm}}{(t+1)_{m+1}^{\,a}},
\qquad S_m=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}R_m(\ell),                  \tag{2}
\]
where $(u)_v=u(u+1)\cdots(u+v-1)$. The rational function decays faster than $t^{-1}$; its initial integer terms through $\ell=rm-1$ vanish, and subsequent terms are positive. Thus $S_m$ is convergent and strictly positive.

Its partial fractions are
\[
R_m(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{a}\sum_{j=0}^{m}
       \frac{c_{i,j,m}}{(t+j+1)^i},\qquad c_{i,j,m}\in\mathbb Q.
\]
The reflection $t\mapsto-t-m-2$ swaps the two numerator products and gives
\[
R_m(-t-m-2)=(-1)^{a(m+1)}R_m(t).
\]
Since $a$ is odd and $m$ even, coefficient comparison at the reflected poles gives
\[
c_{i,m-j,m}=(-1)^{i+1}c_{i,j,m}.
\]
Hence $\sum_jc_{i,j,m}=0$ whenever $i$ is even. Also $\sum_jc_{1,j,m}=0$, because it is the coefficient of $t^{-1}$ in a rational function decaying faster than $t^{-1}$.

Summing the partial fractions, with the $i=1$ terms grouped before taking their limit, therefore yields
\[
S_m=P_{0,m}+
 \sum_{\substack{3\le i\le a\\i\ {\rm odd}}}P_{i,m}\zeta(i),
\quad
P_{i,m}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}c_{i,j,m},\quad
P_{0,m}=-\sum_{i=1}^{a}\sum_{j=0}^{m}c_{i,j,m}H_j^{(i)}.      \tag{3}
\]
Here $H_0^{(i)}=0$. The divergent $\zeta(1)$ is never introduced as a number: its common harmonic divergence cancels. This reflection symmetry, rather than formal independence of weights, is what eliminates the even values from the linear forms.

## 2. Integer denominators, decay and the logarithmic constant

The difficult quantitative estimates are credited inputs from [R, Lemmas 2–5]. Write $q_m=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,m)$ and
$s_{r,a}=\lim_{m\to\infty}S_m^{1/m}$. Their conclusions used here are
\[
q_m^{\,a-i}P_{i,m}\in\mathbb Z\quad(0\le i\le a),             \tag{4}
\]
\[
\limsup_{m\to\infty}|P_{i,m}|^{1/m}
 \le B:=2^{a-2r}(2r+1)^{2r+1},                              \tag{5}
\]
\[
0<s_{r,a}\le
 \frac{(2r+1)^{2r+1}(ra+r)^{ra+r}(a-2r)^{a-2r}}
 {(ra+a-r)^{ra+a-r}}.                                      \tag{6}
\]
The positive integral representation in Lemma 2 supplies the decay analysis; Lemma 5 controls the denominators by differentiating products of rational functions with integral partial fractions. We do not replace these estimates by decimal or finite linear-relation experiments.

Multiply (3) at $m=2n$ by $q_{2n}^a$. By (4) the resulting form $L_n$ has integer coefficients. The prime number theorem gives $\log q_m=m+o(m)$. Therefore
\[
|L_n|=\alpha^{n+o(n)},\quad \alpha=(e^a s_{r,a})^2,\qquad
|p_{i,n}|\le\beta^{n+o(n)},\quad \beta=(e^a B)^2.
\]
For sufficiently large $a$ and the choice of $r$ below, $\alpha<1$. The linear-independence criterion of Nesterenko, stated in [R], gives
\[
\dim_{\mathbb Q}\operatorname{span}
 \{1,\zeta(3),\ldots,\zeta(a)\}
 \ge1-\frac{\log\alpha}{\log\beta}
 \ge\frac{f(a,r)}{g(a,r)},                                  \tag{7}
\]
where the exact expressions obtained by inserting (5)–(6) are
\[
\begin{aligned}
f(a,r)&=(a-2r)\log2+(ra+a-r)\log(ra+a-r)\\
 &\quad-(ra+r)\log(ra+r)-(a-2r)\log(a-2r),\\
g(a,r)&=a+(a-2r)\log2+(2r+1)\log(2r+1).
\end{aligned}
\]
The factor two from the even subsequence cancels; omitting denominator clearing would incorrectly improve the constant.

For completeness, the parameter optimization can be checked directly. Take
$r=\lfloor a/(\log a)^2\rfloor$ for large odd $a$. Put $A=r(a+1)$ and $d=a-2r$, so the two large arguments in $f$ are $A$ and $A+d$. Taylor's theorem for $x\log x$ gives
\[
(A+d)\log(A+d)-A\log A
=d(\log A+1)+O(d^2/A).
\]
Since $d=a-2r$, $\log A=\log a+\log r+O(1/a)$ and
$\log d=\log a+O(r/a)$, it follows that
\[
f(a,r)=a\log r+O(a+r\log r),\qquad
g(a,r)=(1+\log2)a+O(r\log r).
\]
Now $r\log r=o(a)$ and $\log r=\log a-2\log\log a+o(1)$, giving the ratio
$(1+o(1))\log a/(1+\log2)$. Its growth also ensures
$e^a s_{r,a}<1$ from (6), as required before applying the criterion. Taking $a=2N+1$ proves (1) with its “every sufficiently large $N$” quantifier. This reconstructs how an actual numerical lower bound arises from parity, integrality and exponential estimates together.

## 3. Quantitative numerical independence in tails and quotients

The theorem gives more than an unspecified infinite collection of irrational values. Let $E$ be any fixed finite-dimensional $\mathbb Q$-subspace of $\mathbb R$, of dimension $d_E$. For $1\le M<N$, set
\[
W_{M,N}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}
 \{\zeta(2j+1):M<j\le N\}.
\]
Since $V_N=V_M+W_{M,N}$ and $\dim V_M\le M+1$,
\[
\dim_{\mathbb Q}\frac{E+W_{M,N}}E
 \ge D_N-(M+1)-d_E.                                        \tag{8}
\]
Indeed the quotient's dimension is at least $\dim W_{M,N}-d_E$, while
$D_N\le\dim V_M+\dim W_{M,N}$. No disjointness of different numerical weight spaces is assumed.

A basis can be selected from the displayed generators of $W_{M,N}$. Thus whenever the right side of (8) is positive, there are at least its ceiling many distinct odd single zeta values, all above the cutoff $2M+1$, whose classes are linearly independent modulo $E$. For a fixed cutoff $M$ this number tends to infinity at least logarithmically.

There is also a growing-cutoff regime. Put $c=1/(1+\log2)$, choose $0<\kappa<c$, and let $M=\lfloor\kappa\log N\rfloor$. For every $0<\varepsilon<1-\kappa/c$ and all sufficiently large $N$, (1)–(8) imply
\[
\dim\frac{E+W_{M,N}}E
 \ge\bigl((1-\varepsilon)c-\kappa\bigr)\log N-1-d_E.          \tag{9}
\]
Thus logarithmically many independent numerical classes can be chosen even after discarding a logarithmically increasing initial block of odd weights and any fixed finite-dimensional exceptional span. The dimension loss in (8) is explicit; there is no unsupported assertion that the original theorem automatically applies to a shifted interval with the same constant.

Taking $E=\mathbb Q$ also shows that at least $D_N-1$ of the odd zeta generators must be irrational, since adjoining a rational value cannot enlarge the span containing 1. Hence infinitely many are irrational. This does not identify $\zeta(5)$ or any other prescribed unresolved individual value as irrational.

## 4. What this does and does not imply for fixed weights

The subspace $V_N$ lies in $Z_0+\sum_{j=1}^N Z_{2j+1}$. Equations (1), (8) and (9) concern its actual numerical depth-one odd sector. They do **not** give a lower bound $d_k$ at each fixed weight, assert a direct sum across weights, or establish numerical injectivity of the motivic period map.

A useful secondary diagnostic makes this boundary concrete. Put
$u=\zeta(3)^2/\zeta(2)^3$ and
\[
S_m=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}
 \{\zeta(2)^{3(m-j)}\zeta(3)^{2j}:0\le j\le m\}\subseteq Z_{6m}.
\]
The inclusion follows by multiplying absolutely convergent series and sorting order/equality patterns, which gives the weight-preserving stuffle product. Dividing a relation by $\zeta(2)^{3m}$ identifies it with a polynomial relation among $1,u,\ldots,u^m$. Consequently $\dim S_m=m+1$ if $u$ is transcendental, and $\dim S_m=\min(m+1,d)$ if $u$ is algebraic of degree $d$. This algebraic calculation is retained as a diagnostic, not the main significance of the report.

At weight six Brown's numerical spanning consequence, together with
\[
\zeta(2,2,2)=\pi^6/7!=3\zeta(6)/16,\qquad
\zeta(3,3)=(\zeta(3)^2-\zeta(6))/2,
\]
gives $Z_6=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}\{\zeta(6),\zeta(3)^2\}=S_1$.
The general odd-value dimension theorem does not decide whether these two same-weight numbers are independent, equivalently whether $u$ is irrational. Cumulative independence and fixed-weight independence are different problems.
