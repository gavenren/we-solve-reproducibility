# Removing the bigness hypothesis for every projective hyperbolic surface

Claim type: **Literature report**.

The conjecture in the prompt holds for **every smooth projective hyperbolic complex surface**, with no assumed bigness or curvature condition. The substantive extra step beyond the earlier manuscript is to force$K_X$ big by surface classification; the prior conditional ampleness argument alone did not do this. The classification and rational-curve theorem are credited deep inputs, so this is an exposition of a known surface case, not new priority or a solution for all compact Kähler manifolds.

First hyperbolicity excludes every nonconstant map from$\mathbb P^1$ or an elliptic curve. Restrict the first to$\mathbb C$; for the second compose with its universal cover$\mathbb C\to E$. It also excludes singular rational curves, since their normalizations give such maps. Thus$X$ has no exceptional$(-1)$-curves and is already a minimal surface. We use the classical Enriques classification in Beauville, *Surfaces algébriques complexes*, Astérisque54(1978), ChaptersVII–IX, to eliminate every possible Kodaira dimension except2.

If$\kappa(X)=-\infty$, the minimal surface is $\mathbb P^2$ or geometrically ruled, so it contains rational curves. If$\kappa(X)=1$, its elliptic fibration has a smooth general elliptic fiber in characteristic zero, which again gives a forbidden map. Multiple or singular special fibers do not remove the smooth general fiber.

For$\kappa(X)=0$, Beauville TheoremVIII.2 gives exactly four possibilities: K3, Enriques, abelian or bielliptic. An abelian surface has a nonconstant entire curve: compose a nonzero linear map$\mathbb C\to\mathbb C^2$ with its lattice quotient. A bielliptic surface has a finite étale cover by a product of elliptic curves, giving the same obstruction. An Enriques surface has a finite étale K3 double cover. Hyperbolicity pulls back to a finite cover, since an entire curve upstairs composes to an entire curve downstairs and a finite fiber cannot contain its nonconstant image.

A **projective** complex K3 surface contains a rational curve by the Bogomolov–Mumford theorem, recalled with a Mori–Mukai deformation proof as Theorem8 in Bogomolov–Hassett–Tschinkel, *Constructing rational curves on K3 surfaces*, Section3. This rules out both the K3 case and the Enriques cover. The projectivity qualifier matters: projective$X$ gives a projective finite cover, so this precise theorem applies. No unproved extension to nonprojective K3s is being invoked.

All cases$-\infty,0,1$ are impossible. Hence$\kappa(X)=2$, so$K_X$ is big. Also$K_X$ is nef: the projective cone theorem would otherwise produce a rational curve of negative canonical degree, excluded above. Thus$K_X^2>0$.

We include the last ampleness step to make the implication complete and to retain singular-curve precision. If an irreducible curve$C$ had$K_X\cdot C=0$, Hodge index would give$C^2<0$, since$K_X^2>0$ and the numerical class of$C$ is nonzero (it has positive degree against an ample divisor). Adjunction uses **arithmetic**, not geometric, genus:
\[
 2p_a(C)-2=C^2+K_X\cdot C=C^2<0.
\]
Since$p_a(C)\ge0$, it follows that$p_a(C)=0$ and$C^2=-2$. The normalization formula$p_a(C)=g(\widetilde C)+\sum\delta_x$ forces both geometric genus and every singularity correction to vanish. Therefore$C$ is a smooth rational curve, contradiction. We have proved$K_X\cdot C>0$ for every irreducible curve, as well as$K_X^2>0$. Nakai–Moishezon now gives$K_X$ ample.

This proof isolates the exact additional ingredients needed to close the prior surface gap. It uses classification to produce general type, and the projective K3 rational-curve theorem to exclude a genuine zero-Kodaira-dimension obstruction; merely asserting “hyperbolicity implies nef” would not suffice. It does not assume a metric of negative holomorphic sectional or Ricci curvature, nor infer projectivity of a general initially nonprojective Kähler surface. The latter is outside this scoped contribution, as is every higher dimension.
