# Profinite rigidity for punctured-torus bundles versus finite ambiguity in general

**Problem:** TOP-2806

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** The isometry conclusion follows when one manifold is an orientable hyperbolic once-punctured-torus bundle. General orientable finite-volume hyperbolic manifolds have a finite ambiguity theorem and norm/fibering invariants, which do not by themselves establish full rigidity.

The answer is affirmative on a substantial infinite family: assume \(M_1\) is an orientable finite-volume hyperbolic once-punctured-torus bundle. Take compact cores \(C_i\), which have the same fundamental groups as \(M_i\). Bridson–Reid–Wilton, Corollary B, identifies a compact 3-manifold with the same profinite completion as \(C_1\) with \(C_1\) itself. Applying it to \(C_2\) identifies the interiors; Mostow–Prasad rigidity then supplies the requested isometry, allowing orientation reversal.

The rank-two feature is essential to the cited proof. The bundle group is \(F_2\rtimes_\phi\mathbb Z\). Hyperbolicity gives \(b_1=1\). Profinite fibering results recover a bundle group with free kernel of rank two on the other side. Theorem A then recovers the monodromy through \(\operatorname{Out}(F_2)=\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb Z)\), and Corollary B performs the final topological identification. This is not a claim that profinite abelianization alone determines an integral matrix: the theorem uses the full finite-quotient information.

For comparison, Liu's published Theorem 1.1 proves profinite *almost* rigidity for finite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifold groups among finitely generated 3-manifold groups. Thus fixing a completion leaves only finitely many isomorphism types, not an asserted singleton. For orientable hyperbolic manifolds, Theorem 1.3 also recovers the integral first-cohomology lattice with its Thurston norm and fibered classes, up to the sign ambiguity specified there. The proof passes through regularity up to a profinite unit, virtual fibering and periodic Nielsen data.

The logical gap to the literal question can be made precise. Norm and fibering data are necessary compatibility conditions on a candidate pair. Finite ambiguity says that the set of compatible fundamental-group types is finite. Neither statement identifies all those types with one another. In particular no argument here deduces a common finite cover, equal volume or isometry for an arbitrary pair from those invariants; Liu explicitly separates these stronger issues from the results proved. The report establishes the bundle case by an actual rigidity theorem and records a general finiteness theorem with its quantifiers intact. It does not claim the arbitrary closed, cusped or nonorientable rigidity question is solved.
