# A beyond-half regime with its missing quantifiers intact

Claim type: **Literature report**.

The prompt requires all moduli up to $x^\theta$, an independently maximizing reduced residue class for each modulus, and a maximum over every height $y\le x$, for all fixed $\theta<1$. Known beyond-half estimates and prime-gap consequences must not be substituted for that statement.

A useful exact regime appears in Polymath's primary paper, arXiv:1407.4897v4. Write
\[
 \Delta(\alpha;a(q))=
 \sum_{n\equiv a\bmod q}\alpha(n)
 -\frac1{\varphi(q)}\sum_{(n,q)=1}\alpha(n).
\]
Its MPZ definition fixes positive parameters $\omega,\delta$, a set of primes $I\subset[1,x^\delta]$, their product $P_I$, and **one** reduced class $a\bmod P_I$. With $S_I$ the squarefree integers supported on $I$, the conclusion is
\[
 \sum_{\substack{q\le Q\\q\in S_I}}
  |\Delta(\Lambda\mathbf1_{[x,2x]};a(q))|
 \ll_{A,\omega,\delta} x(\log x)^{-A},
 \qquad Q\le x^{1/2+2\omega}.
 \tag{1}
\]
The bound is uniform in that chosen compatible class, which may vary with $x$. Theorem2.5 supplies (1) whenever
\[
 600\omega+180\delta<7.
\]
In particular $\omega=1/100$, $\delta=1/200$ gives the concrete unconditional regime
\[
 Q\le x^{0.52},\qquad p\mid q\Longrightarrow p\le x^{0.005}.
 \tag{2}
\]
The numerical check is exact: $6+0.9<7$. This is a genuine beyond-half theorem over an infinite class of moduli, credited to the source, not new progress proved here.

There are three separate restrictions in (1)–(2). The moduli are squarefree and smooth, rather than arbitrary. The classes for different $q$ are projections of one class modulo $P_I$. Finally the discrepancy is for a dyadic interval with its reduced-class average, rather than the prompt's height maximum and main term. The earlier contribution addresses how a complete family of endpoint estimates can recover the height maximum using exponent slack; it does not remove the other restrictions.

In particular
\[
 \sup_{a\bmod P_I}\sum_q|\Delta_q(a(q))|
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \sum_q\sup_{b\bmod q}|\Delta_q(b)|
\]
are different quantities. The first is bounded by the second, while the reverse inequality is not supplied by (1). An independent choice of maximizing classes need not be compatible when moduli share factors. Uniformity in a single parameter $a$ is therefore not the missing modulus-by-modulus maximum.

The same primary paper makes a second distinction that is often obscured. Its EH hypothesis concerns distribution of $\Lambda$; its **generalized EH** hypothesis concerns divisor-bounded convolutions $\alpha*\beta$ with specified support and Siegel–Walfisz conditions. Proposition2.7 proves GEH implies EH. The implication in the other direction is not established there. With $H_1=\liminf(p_{n+1}-p_n)$, the checked conclusions are
\[
 H_1\le12\quad\text{under EH for every fixed }\theta<1,
\]
and
\[
 H_1\le6\quad\text{under GEH for every fixed }\theta<1.
 \tag{3}
\]
The unconditional bound246 is also stated. These are sufficient hypotheses as used in the source; this report does not assert that the whole family of exponents is necessary.

For completeness, (3) has an exact finite-disjunction interpretation. Once $p_n>2$, consecutive prime gaps are even positive integers. A finite liminf at most12 implies infinitely many gaps among $2,4,6,8,10,12$: otherwise every sufficiently late gap would be at least14. One of these six values must therefore occur infinitely often. Under GEH the analogous list is $2,4,6$. Neither statement selects the gap2.

The number6 also has a structural explanation in the source's admissible-triple result. An admissible triple of integers cannot have diameter less than6. If it contains both parities it covers both classes modulo2. Otherwise all three have the same parity, and three distinct entries of diameter less than6 must, after translation, be $0,2,4$; these cover every residue modulo3. Conversely $(0,2,6)$ is admissible: it misses a class modulo2 and3, and three entries cannot cover all classes modulo any prime greater than3. Thus its diameter6 is minimal. This verifies the tuple arithmetic; it is not a proof of GEH or twin primes.

The added content beyond the earlier maximal-error and energy analysis is the fully quantified concrete smooth-modulus regime, the compatible-class distinction, and a source-checked EH/GEH consequence map. Neither a prime-gap theorem nor (2) proves the unrestricted averaged estimate in the literal prompt. That estimate remains unproved here.
