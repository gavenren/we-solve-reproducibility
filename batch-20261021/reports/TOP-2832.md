# Unordered Heegaard stabilization: explicit linear upper bounds and equal-genus lower families

**Problem:** TOP-2832

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** For the prompt's surface-isotopy convention allowing side interchange, Johnson's theorems give a universal common-stabilization upper bound floor(7g/2−1) for g≥1, and for every k>1 equal genus g=2k pairs requiring at least k−1 stabilizations each. These do not settle the proposed 2g upper bound.

Use the equivalence relation in the question: isotopy of the Heegaard surface, with no requirement to preserve the names of the complementary handlebodies. Write \(s(\Sigma,R)\) for the least common-stabilization genus.

Johnson's upper-bound Theorem 1 gives, for genera \(p,q\),
\[
 s(\Sigma,R)\le\left\lfloor \tfrac32p+2q-1\right\rfloor .
\]
The paper states that this works with either oriented or unoriented equivalence; it therefore applies to the prompt's weaker convention. Setting \(p=q=g\ge1\) gives
\[
 s(\Sigma,R)\le\lfloor7g/2-1\rfloor .
\]
Equivalently at most \(\lfloor5g/2-1\rfloor\) stabilizations of each suffice. These formulas are an established upper bound, not a proof of the requested \(s\le2g\). Genus zero is separate: a manifold with a genus-zero splitting is \(S^3\), and its splitting sphere is unique up to isotopy.

The lower-bound theorem supplies, for every integer \(k>1\), a closed orientable manifold with splittings \(A,B\) of respective genera \(2k-1,2k\) and
\[
 s(A,B)=3k-1.
\]
Crucially, that paper defines isotopic splittings by isotopy of the surfaces themselves. Its conclusion is not merely a lower bound for ordered handlebodies.

Here is the transfer to exactly the equal-genus setting of the question. Let \(A'\) be a one-fold stabilization of \(A\). Both \(A'\) and \(B\) have genus \(g=2k\). If \(C\) is a common stabilization of \(A'\) and \(B\), then by transitivity of stabilization it is also a common stabilization of \(A\) and \(B\). Hence
\[
 s(A',B)\ge3k-1=\tfrac32g-1.
\]
Each endpoint must therefore undergo at least \(k-1=g/2-1\) stabilizations before they can become isotopic. This is an infinite equal-genus family, not just a mismatch between minimal genera.

In contrast, proving that one **ordered** splitting needs genus \(2g\) to exchange its two sides does not produce two distinct surface-isotopy classes: the underlying surface is already identical. Such flip examples cannot be used as an unordered counterexample here.

The cited theorems leave a genuine gap between the lower family and the general upper estimate. Neither inequality decides whether every equal-genus pair has a common stabilization of genus at most \(2g\); that exact proposed bound is left unproved.
