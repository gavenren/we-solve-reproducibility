# Crown extremality at odd half-order and the balanced-even obstruction

**Claim type: Literature report.** The fixed-order crown comparison in the prompt holds for every **odd** \(q\ge5\). For every **even** \(q\ge6\), it also holds for every bipartite graph on \(2q\) vertices that admits a bipartition with unequal part sizes. The remaining balanced-even core is not settled by the results used here.

This is a precise literature resolution of broad parameter ranges, with explicit proofs handling reductions and word conventions. It is not a new proof of crown extremality for all orders.

## Exact primary bounds

Mozhui–Krishna, arXiv2506.01057v1, gives for a connected reduced bipartite graph with part sizes \(m\le n\):
\[
 \rho(G)\le1+\lceil m/2\rceil \tag{1}
\]
by Corollary1, and, if \(m\ge5\) is odd,
\[
 \rho(G)\le\lceil m/2\rceil \tag{2}
\]
by Theorem3. Reduced means vertices with identical neighborhoods have been removed until none remain. Their word constructions and parity threshold are the external input; no complete reconstruction of their lengthy words is claimed here.

Glen–Kitaev–Pyatkin, arXiv1609.00674v1, proves
\[
 \rho(H_{q,q})=\lceil q/2\rceil\quad(q\ge5). \tag{3}
\]
The threshold \(q\ge5\) is retained; their small crowns have separate values.

Before applying (1)–(2), we justify lifting from reduced connected graphs. A blanket assertion that false twins always preserve representation number would miss an exception at value one.

## Three elementary operations on uniform words

First, if a word \(w\) is \(k\)-uniform and represents \(G\), it can be promoted to \((k+1)\)-uniform. Let \(\pi\) list vertices in order of first occurrence in \(w\), and use \(\pi w\). For an adjacent pair, its alternating subword in \(w\) starts in the same order as in \(\pi\), so adjoining that initial pair preserves alternation. A nonadjacent pair already has equal consecutive letters in its old restriction; these remain. Repeating promotes to any larger uniformity.

Second, false twins can be expanded at any uniformity \(k\ge2\). Replace a vertex \(v\) by nonadjacent twins \(v_1,\ldots,v_t\) with its old neighborhood. In each occurrence of \(v\), replace it by a block containing each twin once. Use the order \(v_1\cdots v_t\) at the first occurrence and the reverse order at the second. In all later occurrences any fixed order may be used.

For any old other letter \(u\), the restriction to \(u,v_i\) has precisely the old \(u,v\) pattern, preserving adjacency. For two twins, their relative order reverses between the first two blocks, producing equal consecutive letters in their two-letter restriction. They are therefore nonadjacent, as required. Each new letter occurs exactly \(k\) times.

Third, if all components have representing words of a common uniformity \(k\ge2\), concatenate those words. Within a component the pair restrictions are unchanged. Across two components they have form \(u^kv^k\), which is not alternating, so no cross edge is introduced. Promotion allows unequal component uniformities to be made equal first.

Together these operations transfer any upper bound \(K\ge2\) on reduced components to the original graph. If a reduced component needs only one copy per letter, promote it to two before false-twin expansion. For example, reducing \(K_{2,2}\) to \(K_2\) changes representation number from two to one; our argument avoids claiming equality in that case. Isolated vertices can be combined by the same two-uniform concatenation rule.

## Odd half-order: every bipartite graph is covered

Let \(q=2t+1\ge5\), so \(t\ge2\), and let \(G\) have \(2q\) vertices. Every connected component, and then its reduced version, has a bipartition with smaller size \(m\le q\).

If \(m\le2t=q-1\), (1) gives
\[
 1+\lceil m/2\rceil\le t+1=\lceil q/2\rceil.
\]
If \(m=q=2t+1\), apply (2), giving the same bound \(t+1\). A component with no edge is handled by two-uniformity, below the target \(t+1\ge3\). Lift false twins, promote to \(t+1\) and concatenate the components. Thus
\[
 \rho(G)\le t+1=\rho(H_{q,q}).
\]
This proof covers arbitrary disconnected bipartite graphs and does not assume balanced components.

## Even half-order with an unequal bipartition

Let \(q=2t\ge6\), and suppose \(G\) admits a bipartition whose smaller side has size at most \(q-1=2t-1\). Every reduced component then has smaller part size \(m\le2t-1\).

If \(m\) is even, it is at most \(2t-2\), and (1) gives \(1+m/2\le t\). If \(m\ge5\) is odd, (2) gives \(\lceil m/2\rceil\le t\). For \(m\le4\), (1) gives at most three, which is at most \(t\); isolated components are again harmless. The word operations lift these bounds to
\[
 \rho(G)\le t=\rho(H_{q,q}).
\]

If instead a reduced connected component has equal even parts of size \(q\), (1) gives only \(q/2+1\), one larger than the crown target. Formula(2) does not apply. No rounding trick removes that extra copy, and no full result for this remaining case is asserted.

## Verification, novelty and remaining scope

The official intake lists no prior contribution for this handle. Both exact primary versions were freshly checked, including the odd-\(m\) lower threshold and the crown's \(q\ge5\) range. The parity cases and word operations above are complete; no finite word search or execution is claimed.

The main partial resolution is credited to the cited authors, as is their stronger neighborhood-inclusion analysis of some balanced-even subclasses, which is not needed here. The report does not extend the conclusion to every \(q\ge1\) by ignoring the small-order exceptions or settle the balanced-even core.
