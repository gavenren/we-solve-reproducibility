# Polynomial inner subsets in noncommutative Heisenberg boxes of arbitrary rank

Claim type: **Partial result**.

We prove the desired polynomial conclusion for genuinely **noncommutative** approximate groups in arbitrarily large dimension, with absolute constants. Work in the integral symplectic Heisenberg group
\[
 (x,y,z)(x',y',z')=(x+x',y+y',z+z'+x\cdot y'-y\cdot x'),
 \quad x,y\in\mathbb Z^r,\ z\in\mathbb Z.
\]
Associativity follows from bilinearity of the alternating form; the identity is0 and the inverse is$(-x,-y,-z)$. Let$L_i\ge1$ and $W\ge\sum_iL_i^2$ be integers, and set
\[
 A=\{(x,y,z): |x_i|,|y_i|\le L_i,\ |z|\le W\}.
\]
For **every**$K$ such that$A^2\subseteq XA$ with$|X|\le K$, there is a symmetric$S\subseteq A$ satisfying
\[
 S^8\subseteq A\subseteq A^4,
 \qquad |S|\ge\frac1{31}K^{-7}|A|. \tag{1}
\]
No dimension-dependent exponent is hidden in(1).

First these boxes really are approximate groups with an explicitly controlled parameter. In$A^2$, horizontal coordinates lie in$[-2L_i,2L_i]$ and the central coordinate has absolute value at most $2W+2\sum_iL_i^2\le4W$. For each horizontal coordinate choose a center$a_i\in\{-L_i,L_i\}$ so that its remainder lies in$[-L_i,L_i]$, and similarly choose$b_i$ for the$y$ coordinate. To write a point as$(a,b,c)(u,v,w)$, the needed central remainder is
\[
 w=z-c-a\cdot v+b\cdot u.
\]
Before choosing$c$, its absolute value is at most$4W+2\sum_iL_i^2\le6W$. The seven centers$c\in\{-6W,-4W,-2W,0,2W,4W,6W\}$, with intervals of radius$W$, cover that range. Therefore $A^2$ is covered by at most$7\cdot2^{2r}$ left translates of$A$. This checks the actual group law and the orientation of the covering; the central shear is not discarded.

We also need a lower bound on **any** permissible$K$, rather than only exhibiting a convenient large one. The projection of$A^2$ onto the horizontal coordinates is exactly the doubled integer box. For each horizontal sum choose two valid horizontal summands. Holding them fixed and varying the two central coordinates gives at least$2W+1$ distinct central values in that fiber (indeed$4W+1$ are available). Consequently
\[
 \frac{|A^2|}{|A|}\ge
 \prod_i\left(\frac{4L_i+1}{2L_i+1}\right)^2
 \ge(5/3)^{2r}.
\]
Since$|A^2|\le K|A|$, every actual covering parameter obeys$K\ge(5/3)^{2r}$. This is what turns a coordinate shrinkage cost into a polynomial in the given$K$.

Define$S$ by replacing each horizontal bound$L_i$ and the central bound$W$ by its floor after division by16. For a product of eight elements$s_j=(x_j,y_j,z_j)$, repeated use of the group law gives the exact central coordinate
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^8z_j+\sum_{1\le j<k\le8}(x_j\cdot y_k-y_j\cdot x_k).
\]
The first part has absolute value at most$W/2$. Each of the28 alternating terms has absolute value at most$2\sum_i(L_i/16)^2\le W/128$. The total is at most$(1/2+7/32)W=23W/32<W$. Horizontal sums have absolute value at most$L_i/2$. Thus$S^8\subseteq A$ exactly, including the integer rounding cases. Symmetry and the identity are immediate from the coordinate bounds.

For every integer$N\ge1$,
\[
 \frac{2\lfloor N/16\rfloor+1}{2N+1}\ge\frac1{31}.
\]
Write$N=16m+s$, $0\le s\le15$; the ratio is at least$(2m+1)/(32m+31)\ge1/31$. Hence$|S|/|A|\ge31^{-(2r+1)}$. Since$(5/3)^7>31$, the lower bound on$K$ gives$K^{-7}\le31^{-2r}$, proving(1). All constants are independent of$r$, the side lengths and$W$.

These groups have nonzero commutators: two unit horizontal generators in paired directions have commutator central coordinate2. The construction therefore adds a class not contained in the earlier abelian-box proof. It also proves the stronger containment$S^8\subseteq A$. The hypothesis$W\ge\sum_iL_i^2$ is essential to this proof: it absorbs the28 accumulated commutator terms. No structural theorem placing every approximate group into these boxes is asserted. BGT [1] provides the nilpotent approximate-group context; every estimate needed here is proved directly.
