# A verified near-linear lattice theorem for all few-vertex polytopes

Schymura, Wang and Xue's Theorem1.3, arXiv:2505.07369v2, proves that every full-dimensional$n$-polytope with at most$n+2$ vertices has
\[
\theta_L(P)\le Cn(\log n)^{1+\log_2 e}\qquad(n\ge2),
\]
with an absolute constant. One lattice supplies the covering. There is no central-symmetry, regularity or simplex-only restriction. This is a useful established subclass theorem, although its logarithmic power is larger than the prompt's desired first power.

The earlier two complete records respectively summarize an unverified universal one-fold$n\log n$ claim and a2026 multi-covering manuscript based on that construction. The present report does not treat either as an independently checked solution. Instead it gives a checked baseline and the precise geometric mechanism for an entire nonsymmetric few-vertex class.

Here is the density transfer, often a source of confusion in using inscribed bodies. If$C\subset P$ and$C+\Lambda$ covers space, then$P+\Lambda$ also covers, but its density is$|P|/\det\Lambda$, not$|C|/\det\Lambda$. Consequently
\[
\theta_L(P)\le\frac{|P|}{|C|}\theta_L(C).
\tag{1}
\]
Passing from an efficient inscribed cover without paying this volume ratio would give an invalid bound.

The source's Lemma4.2 builds an ordinary cylinder inside a polytope with exactly$n+2$ vertices, whose base has at most$(n-1)+2$ vertices and whose volume loss is at most$(1+1/(n-1))^{n-1}$. Whenever the initial body or a later$d$-dimensional base has only$d+1$ vertices, it is a simplex. It is affinely equivalent to an anti-blocking body, so the ordinary-cylinder step in the proof of Theorem1.1 applies with the same loss$(1+1/(d-1))^{d-1}$. An affine change within the base preserves the product with the existing interval factors. Thus the recursion handles both exactly$d+2$ vertices and simplex bases. Iterate to obtain a$k$-fold cylinder$C_k$, the product of a convex base and$k$ intervals, with
\[
|P|/|C_k|\le\prod_{i=n-k}^{n-1}(1+1/i)^i\le e^k.
\]
For$k>\log_2\log n+4$, Rogers' cylinder bound, stated as Lemma2.1 in the checked paper, gives$\theta_L(C_k)\le C_0\,2^k n$ for sufficiently large$n$; its explicit extra factor$(1+1/n)^n$ is absorbed into$C_0$. Choosing the least such integer$k$ yields
\[
2^k=O(\log n),\qquad e^k=O((\log n)^{\log_2 e}).
\]
Combining with(1) gives the asserted logarithmic exponent. Small dimensions are absorbed by increasing the absolute constant. The nontrivial cylinder-existence and covering lemmas are credited inputs; the volume and exponent bookkeeping is displayed in full.

The same primary paper distinguishes anti-blocking bodies, locally anti-blocking bodies and bodies with many reflection symmetries. Their theorems have different hypotheses and logarithmic losses; those categories are not merged here. In particular the few-vertex proof uses actual ordinary cylinders and recursive bases, not an assumption that every few-vertex polytope has coordinate reflections.

This is a lattice covering theorem in an unbounded space, not a finite covering of one copy of$P$ and not a union of independently shifted lattices. It proves no universal first-power logarithmic bound and no matching lower bound. Its added value is a checked primary proof pathway with full constants-in-exponents and scope, in a record otherwise containing broader unverified announcements.
