# The strict two-category Thomason theorem and the precise transfer gap beyond it

**Problem:** TOP-30007509

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** The requested proper combinatorial model structure and Quillen equivalence are established for strict 2-categories with the geometric nerve specified by Ara; exact transfer hypotheses are identified. No all-finite-n or strict-∞ extension is inferred, and the earlier announced universal proof is not certified.

The \(n=2\) instance of the prompt is a published theorem, with all four requested features present. In Ara's checked version, Theorem 3.6 gives a **proper combinatorial** model structure on small strict 2-categories. Theorem 3.7 gives the Quillen equivalence
\[
 c_2\operatorname{Sd}^{\,2}:\mathrm{sSet}
 \ \rightleftarrows\ 2\text{-}\mathrm{Cat}:
 \operatorname{Ex}^{\,2}N_2 .
\]
The existence theorem is credited to Ara–Maltsiniotis and the Quillen equivalence to Ara–Chiche–Maltsiniotis.

Here \(N_2\) is the geometric nerve of Definition 1.8. Its simplices are strict 2-functors from the specified 2-categorical simplices: objects \(0,\ldots,k\), with hom-categories given by suitable endpoint-containing subsets ordered by reverse inclusion. Keeping this convention fixed avoids silently substituting a nerve modelling \((\infty,1)\)-categories rather than the ordinary homotopy types in this problem. Weak equivalences are exactly the 2-functors whose \(N_2\)-images are weak homotopy equivalences of simplicial sets.

One can verify the weak-equivalence detection statement for the right adjoint explicitly. The natural transformation \(\mathrm{id}\to\operatorname{Ex}\) is an objectwise weak equivalence, so by two-out-of-three a simplicial map \(f\) is a weak equivalence if and only if \(\operatorname{Ex}^{\,2}f\) is. Thus using \(N_2\) or \(\operatorname{Ex}^{\,2}N_2\) gives the same class. Cofibrations are the weakly saturated class generated by
\[
 c_2\operatorname{Sd}^{\,2}
     (\partial\Delta^k\hookrightarrow\Delta^k),\qquad k\geq0 .
\]
They are not simply declared to be all objectwise inclusions.

The transfer issue explains why the result is substantive. For a candidate adjunction \(F\dashv U\), the small-object argument needs smallness and, crucially, that \(U\) send the relative \(F(J)\)-cell maps to weak equivalences, where \(J\) are the generating trivial cofibrations. Ara states this requirement explicitly in Lemma 4.2. Existence of the adjunction, nerve detection and local presentability alone do not prove it. Properness is a further theorem, not a formal consequence of calling a model category combinatorial.

Finally Corollary 3.9 supplies objectwise weak-equivalence unit and counit maps in the checked setting. This is stronger evidence than an assertion that the localized categories happen to be equivalent. The paper also warns that an older proposed two-category proof had serious errors; the credited replacement is the one used here. None of these statements, without checking the corresponding transfer and comparison conditions in each dimension, proves the universal finite-\(n\) or strict-\(\infty\) assertion in the prompt.
