# Exact density laws for the least quadratic nonresidue

Claim type: **Partial result** (classical ingredients; no novelty claim).

For an odd prime $p$, let $n_p$ be the least positive quadratic nonresidue. The literal problem asks for a bound at **every** prime. We prove a different, unconditional infinite-prime regime with exact densities. List the primes as $q_1=2,q_2=3,\ldots$. Then
\[
 \lim_{X\to\infty}
 \frac{\#\{p\le X:p\text{ odd prime},\ n_p=q_k\}}{\pi(X)}
 =2^{-k}.
 \tag{1}
\]
More generally, for every fixed real $Y\ge2$,
\[
 \lim_{X\to\infty}
 \frac{\#\{p\le X:n_p>Y\}}{\pi(X)}
 =2^{-\pi(Y)}.
 \tag{2}
\]
The finitely many primes at most$Y$ do not affect either limit.

First $n_p$ is prime. It is less than$p$, since some element of $\{1,\ldots,p-1\}$ is nonsquare. If a composite least nonresidue factored as $ab$ with $1<a,b<n_p$, both factors would be quadratic residues and their product would be one too, a contradiction. Thus checking all primes up to$Y$ is exactly equivalent to checking all integers up to$Y$.

We now prove the required finite-pattern independence, rather than assume that the quadratic characters behave randomly. Fix $k$ and prescribe arbitrary signs $\epsilon_j\in\{-1,1\}$. Set
\[
 M=8\prod_{j=2}^k q_j.
\]
Among reduced residue classes modulo$M$, exactly a fraction $2^{-k}$ satisfy
\[
 (q_j/p)=\epsilon_j\qquad(1\le j\le k)
 \tag{3}
\]
for primes $p\nmid M$. To see this, the four reduced classes modulo8 split equally according to $(2/p)=(-1)^{(p^2-1)/8}$. After fixing one such class, $p\bmod4$ is fixed. For every odd $q_j$, quadratic reciprocity expresses $(q_j/p)$ as $(p/q_j)$ times a fixed sign depending on that class modulo4. Exactly half of the $q_j-1$ nonzero classes modulo$q_j$ have either prescribed Legendre symbol. The Chinese remainder theorem makes these choices independent across the odd primes and the modulus8. The proportion is therefore
$(1/2)\prod_{j=2}^k(1/2)$, as asserted.

The prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions for this **fixed** modulus$M$ gives
$\pi(X;M,a)\sim\pi(X)/\varphi(M)$ for each reduced class. Summing the finitely many appropriate classes proves that (3) has relative natural density$2^{-k}$. The author-hosted Kedlaya notes, §4.4, provide the precise fixed-modulus theorem used here. No GRH or effective estimate uniform in$M$ is required.

To obtain (1), prescribe plus signs for $q_1,\ldots,q_{k-1}$ and a minus sign for$q_k$. Every smaller integer factors into the earlier primes, so this pattern is precisely $n_p=q_k$ apart from finitely many excluded primes. To obtain (2), prescribe plus signs at all $\pi(Y)$ primes up to$Y$. This also shows that every fixed tail has **positive** density, not merely infinitely many primes. In particular $n_p$ is unbounded along the primes, while each one of its possible prime values occurs infinitely often.

There is a surprisingly strong density-one consequence. Let $h$ be any real-valued function on primes with $h(p)\to\infty$, however slowly. Then
\[
 \frac{\#\{p\le X:n_p>h(p)\}}{\pi(X)}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{4}
\]
Fix$Y$. Eventually $h(p)\ge Y$, so, apart from finitely many exceptions,
$\{n_p>h(p)\}\subseteq\{n_p>Y\}$. Equation(2) bounds the limsup in(4) by $2^{-\pi(Y)}$. Only **after** taking that limsup do we let$Y\to\infty$, making the bound zero. This is not an application of PNT to a growing modulus.

Taking $h(p)=p^\varepsilon$ proves the desired subpower bound for a density-one set at each fixed$\varepsilon$. One may even take a single slowly growing$h$, such as $\log\log(p+3)$. Nevertheless (4) allows an infinite exceptional set of density zero. It therefore cannot give a constant $C_\varepsilon$ valid at every prime. The positive-density fixed tails in(2) are also consistent with the conjecture: a constant may depend on$\varepsilon$, and fixed$Y$ is eventually smaller than$p^\varepsilon$.

The earlier exploration gives Burgess-type worst-case information and a CRT/Dirichlet unboundedness argument; another record describes an unverified2026 zero-free-half-plane claim. The present addition is the exact joint pattern law, the geometric distribution of the index$k$, positive-density tails, and the full arbitrary-$h$ density-one deduction. No expectation limit for the unbounded random variable$n_p$ is inferred merely from these finite-cutoff limits. The all-prime exceptional set remains the unresolved part.
