# The simple loop conjecture for Sol targets, with a direct torus-bundle compression argument

**Problem:** TOP-2834

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** Zemke's published theorem proves the literal two-sided simple loop conjecture for closed Sol three-manifolds and its corollary for closed targets finitely covered by torus bundles. A self-contained orientable-domain torus-bundle argument is supplied; arbitrary three-manifold targets are not settled.

For a closed connected surface \(\Sigma\), a two-sided immersion \(f:\Sigma\to M\) satisfies
\[
 \rho_M\circ f_*=\rho_\Sigma
\]
for the orientation characters to \(\mathbb Z/2\). This condition is part of the literal problem. Zemke's Theorem 1 proves that if \(M\) is a closed Sol three-manifold and \(f_*\) is not injective, its kernel contains an essential simple closed curve. Corollary 2 extends the conclusion to closed targets finitely covered by torus bundles. These are credited published results, including the nonorientable surfaces and torus semi-bundles treated in that article.

Here is a direct proof of a substantial subcase which explains why a compression appears. Suppose the target itself is a torus bundle and the domain is the orientable closed surface \(\Sigma_g\). There is an exact sequence
\[
 1\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^2\longrightarrow\pi_1M
 \xrightarrow{p}\mathbb Z\longrightarrow1.
\]
Set \(\phi=p f_*\). For \(g\ge2\), choose a geometric symplectic basis \(a_i,b_i\) of \(\pi_1\Sigma_g\) so that \(\phi(a_i)=\phi(b_i)=0\) for \(i\ge2\).

To justify this basis choice, \(\phi\) factors through integral first homology. If nonzero, divide by the positive generator of its image to obtain a primitive integral covector. Its Poincaré dual is a primitive homology vector, represented by a nonseparating simple curve. Cut along that curve, choose an arc joining its two boundary copies to obtain its geometric dual, and extend by a symplectic basis of the complementary genus-\((g-1)\) surface. The resulting functional is supported on the first handle. If \(\phi=0\), any geometric symplectic basis works.

Now \(f_*(a_2)\) and \(f_*(b_2)\) lie in the abelian kernel \(\mathbb Z^2\), so \(f_*([a_2,b_2])=1\). The commutator is the boundary of an embedded one-holed torus. Its complement has genus \(g-1\) with one boundary circle, so this curve is essential when \(g\ge2\). This proves more than the conditional assertion in these genera: every such homomorphism kills an essential simple curve.

For \(g=1\), assume \(f_*\) is noninjective as in the conjecture. Its image is a finitely generated abelian subgroup of the torsion-free torus-bundle group, hence is free abelian of rank at most one. The kernel in \(\mathbb Z^2\) then contains a primitive nonzero vector: for rank one it is the kernel of a primitive linear functional, and for rank zero take a basis vector. A primitive vector is represented by an essential simple torus curve. For \(g=0\), the fundamental-group map is injective, so the conjecture's noninjectivity hypothesis does not arise.

This proof does not handle a general nonorientable domain or pass from an arbitrary finite cover by projecting simple curves. Those steps require the cited theorem. The unresolved part of the original question concerns targets beyond the stated geometric class, particularly the general hyperbolic setting; no conclusion for those targets is inferred from the abelian-fiber argument.
