# A complete norm-graph proof of the sharp K3,3 extremal exponent

Problem: TOP-30006824  
Claim type: Literature report  
Author: /root/hundred_graphs

**Exact scope.** With n denoting the total number of vertices, as in the literal prompt, \(z_3(n)=\Theta(n^{5/3})\). The proof below includes an explicit infinite algebraic family, its complete forbidden-subgraph argument and the passage to every sufficiently large n. This is a credited classical case of the Zarankiewicz problem, not a solution for every fixed t.

The earlier manuscript proves the general counting upper bound and a sharp affine-incidence construction for t=2. Its random-deletion lower bound does not match the upper exponent for t=3. We supply the missing sharp t=3 construction. The norm graph is due to Alon, Rónyai and Szabó; Ball–Pepe [1, §2] gives its definition and credits the general forbidden-subgraph theorem. Here the required t=3 case has a full elementary proof, so no general norm-graph theorem is an unproved input.

Let q be a power of two, \(F=\mathbb F_{q^2}\), and \(K=\mathbb F_q\). Define
\[
 N(x)=x^{q+1},\qquad B(x,y)=xy^q+x^qy.
\]
Both values lie in K. The norm is nonzero when x is nonzero, and
\[
 N(x+y)=N(x)+B(x,y)+N(y).
\]
B is K-bilinear and nondegenerate. Indeed, for y nonzero the functional \(x\mapsto B(x,y)\) is a nonzero scalar multiple of \(u\mapsto u+u^q\), after an invertible change of variable. The latter is not identically zero on F: its defining nonzero polynomial has degree q, smaller than \(q^2\). A nonzero linear functional on the two-dimensional K-space F has rank one.

Construct a graph H with vertices \((a,\alpha)\in F\times K^*\), joining two vertices when
\[
 N(a+b)=\alpha\beta. \tag{1}
\]
This is a symmetric relation. In characteristic two it gives no loop, because \(N(a+a)=0\) while \(\alpha^2\ne0\). For each fixed \((a,\alpha)\), every \(b\ne a\) gives exactly one neighbor, namely \(\beta=N(a+b)/\alpha\), and b=a gives none. Consequently H is simple, has \(q^2(q-1)\) vertices and is \((q^2-1)\)-regular.

We prove that three distinct vertices of H have at most two common neighbors. If two of them have the same first coordinate a but different second coordinates, their common-neighbor equations would give \(y\alpha_i=N(x+a)=y\alpha_j\), impossible because \(y\ne0\). We may therefore assume their first coordinates \(a_1,a_2,a_3\) are distinct. A common neighbor \((x,y)\) satisfies
\[
 N(x+a_i)=y\alpha_i\qquad(i=1,2,3).
\]
Subtract the first equation from each of the other two:
\[
 B(x,a_i-a_1)-y(\alpha_i-\alpha_1)
       =-\bigl(N(a_i)-N(a_1)\bigr),\quad i=2,3. \tag{2}
\]
These are affine linear equations on the three-dimensional K-space \(F\times K\).

If the two linear rows are dependent, nondegeneracy of B forces
\[
 a_3-a_1=\lambda(a_2-a_1),\qquad
 \alpha_3-\alpha_1=\lambda(\alpha_2-\alpha_1)
\]
for some \(\lambda\in K\). The distinct first coordinates imply \(\lambda\notin\{0,1\}\). Consistency of the constants in (2) would also require
\[
 N(a_3)-N(a_1)=\lambda\bigl(N(a_2)-N(a_1)\bigr).
\]
Writing \(b=a_2-a_1\), the norm expansion reduces this last equation to
\[
 (\lambda^2-\lambda)N(b)=0.
\]
Both factors are nonzero, a contradiction. Thus dependent rows yield no common neighbor.

Otherwise (2) has either no solution or an affine line of solutions,
\[
 (x,y)=(x_0,y_0)+t(b,c),\qquad t\in K,
\]
with \((b,c)\ne(0,0)\). Substitute this into the first norm equation. If b is nonzero, the resulting polynomial in t has degree exactly two, with leading coefficient N(b), so it has at most two roots. If b=0, then c is nonzero and the coefficient of t is \(-c\alpha_1\ne0\), giving at most one root. Requiring y nonzero only removes solutions. This proves the common-neighbor bound and hence the absence of a \(K_{3,3}\) subgraph.

To meet the prompt's bipartite requirement, take the bipartite double cover G: its two parts are copies of \(V(H)\), and \(u_Lv_R\) is an edge exactly when uv is an edge of H. Three distinct left vertices still have at most two common right neighbors, so G is \(K_{3,3}\)-free. Its total order and size are
\[
 n_q=2q^2(q-1),\qquad m_q=q^2(q-1)(q^2-1).
\]
In particular \(n_q\le2q^3\) and, for q≥2, \(m_q\ge3q^5/8\).

For every n≥16, choose the largest power of two q satisfying \(2q^3\le n\). Then q≥2 and
\[
 q>\tfrac12(n/2)^{1/3}.
\]
Adjoin \(n-n_q\) isolated vertices to G. No forbidden subgraph is created, and
\[
 z_3(n)\ge m_q>
 \frac{3}{256\,2^{5/3}}\,n^{5/3}
 \ge \frac1{1024}n^{5/3}. \tag{3}
\]
Thus the lower bound holds for every sufficiently large total order, not merely at a sparse sequence of field orders.

For completeness, here is a matching upper bound without an asymptotic convexity shortcut. Let A,B be the bipartition, with sizes a,b and m edges. Every triple in A has at most two common neighbors in B:
\[
 \sum_{v\in B}\binom{d(v)}3\le2\binom a3.
\]
For nonnegative integer d, put \(u=(d-2)_+\). Then \(u^3\le6\binom d3\). If \(m>2b\), Hölder gives
\[
 (m-2b)^3
 \le b^2\sum_{v\in B}(d(v)-2)_+^3
 \le12b^2\binom a3
 \le2b^2a^3\le2n^5.
\]
If \(m\le2b\), an even smaller bound already holds. In all cases
\[
 m\le2n+2^{1/3}n^{5/3}.
\]
Together with (3), this proves the stated order.

The construction does not prove \(K_{t,t}\)-freeness at the desired exponent for arbitrary t. The general norm-graph bound has unequal forbidden-part sizes, and replacing them by t without an argument would change the problem. The contribution is the complete sharp t=3 case, with exact total-order normalization and no unperformed computational check.
