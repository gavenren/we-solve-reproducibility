# Finite-group cancellation ranges for Wall's D₂ problem

**Problem:** TOP-30007511

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** For finite fundamental group G, χ(X)>1−def(G) gives a finite 2-complex simple-homotopy model; equality gives such a model after one S² stabilization. Every finite subgroup of SO(3) has the unstabilized D₂ property. No result for arbitrary groups or general cancellation of S² is claimed.

The prompt's hypotheses are precisely Wall's \(D_2\) conditions. Hambleton's Theorem A supplies an unstabilized answer on a quantitative finite-group range. Let \(G=\pi_1X\) be finite and define
\[
 \operatorname{def}(G)=
 \max_{\langle x_1,\ldots,x_d\mid R_1,\ldots,R_s\rangle}
 (d-s).
\]
Then
\[
 \chi(X)>1-\operatorname{def}(G)
 \Longrightarrow
 X\simeq_s K,\qquad \dim K\leq2,\ K\text{ finite}.                  \tag{1}
\]
At equality the proved assertion is instead \(X\vee S^2\simeq_s K\). The subscript denotes simple homotopy equivalence, which in particular is a homotopy equivalence.

The role of the all-coefficient cohomology condition can be seen directly. Put \(\Lambda=\mathbb ZG\) and use the universal-cover cellular chain complex. Since \(H_3(\widetilde X)=0\), the map \(\partial_3:C_3\to C_2\) is injective. Let \(B=\operatorname{im}\partial_3\). Regard \(\partial_3\), with codomain \(B\), as a 3-cocycle with coefficients in \(B\). Vanishing of \(H^3(X;B)\) supplies \(\varphi:C_2\to B\) with
\(\varphi\partial_3=\partial_3\).
Equivalently \(\varphi|_B=\mathrm{id}_B\), so
\[
 C_2=B\oplus P,\qquad P\oplus C_3\cong C_2.
\]
Thus \(P\) is stably free. This is the algebraic cancellation supplied by the hypotheses. It does not itself realize \(P\) as the 2-cells of a finite geometric model, and it does not cancel attached spheres geometrically.

The deep geometric step in Hambleton's theorem realizes suitable elementary automorphisms by simple homotopy equivalences and uses a spare \(S^2\) summand in the nonminimal Euler-characteristic range to cancel the stabilization. At the minimum \(1-\operatorname{def}(G)\), the same proof needs one additional sphere; erasing it would lose the essential distinction in the question.

Theorem B removes this difficulty for every finite subgroup of \(\mathrm{SO}(3)\): all their \(D_2\) complexes have a finite 2-dimensional simple-homotopy model. This includes the rotational tetrahedral, octahedral and icosahedral groups, as well as cyclic and rotational dihedral groups. It is not a statement about all finite groups, nor about binary polyhedral groups merely because they have related names.

These results give actual unstabilized conclusions on the stated scopes. They do not settle a minimal-Euler-characteristic \(D_2\) complex for a general finite group, or the arbitrary infinite-group case; a stable 2-dimensional model alone would not answer the literal question.
