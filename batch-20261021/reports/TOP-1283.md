# Rational approximation of Apéry's constant: a quantitative boundary

Claim type: **Literature report**.

The target is transcendence of the single number $\xi=\zeta(3)$, not simply irrationality. This report gives precise consequences of the established irrationality-measure theorem and distinguishes them from a recently claimed improvement. It proves no transcendence result.

For an irrational real number $\alpha$, define
\[
 \mu(\alpha)=\sup\{\nu:|\alpha-p/q|<q^{-\nu}
       \text{ for infinitely many coprime }p,q,\ q>0\}.
\]
Rhin and Viola's published work proves
\[
 \mu(\xi)<5.513891.
\]
This is a theorem about rational approximations, hence degree-one polynomials only. Their Theorem5.1 obtains a measure bound from three asymptotic constants satisfying $c_0>c_2$; the concluding parameter choice gives the displayed number. The optimized integral construction and its arithmetic divisibility estimates are credited to that paper, rather than reproduced here.

Here is a uniform consequence for every continued-fraction convergent $p_n/q_n$ of $\xi$. For every real $B>5.513891$, there is $n_0(B)$ such that, for $n\ge n_0(B)$,
\[
 a_{n+1}<q_n^{B-2},\qquad q_{n+1}\le 2q_n^{B-1}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, finiteness of the exceptional rational approximations gives
$|\xi-p_n/q_n|\ge q_n^{-B}$ eventually. The continued-fraction estimate
\[
 |\xi-p_n/q_n|<\frac1{a_{n+1}q_n^2}
\]
then proves the first assertion. From
$q_{n+1}=a_{n+1}q_n+q_{n-1}$, with $q_{n-1}\le q_n$ eventually, the second follows. The thresholds depend on $B$; no explicit threshold is asserted.

The same theorem controls a useful family of linear polynomials. For every $B>5.513891$, all sufficiently large $q$, and every integer $p$,
\[
 |q\xi-p|\ge q^{1-B}.
 \tag{2}
\]
For coprime pairs this follows directly from the definition. For noncoprime pairs write $p=dp'$, $q=dq'$. If $q'$ is large, the coprime lower bound yields
$d(q')^{1-B}=d^Bq^{1-B}\ge q^{1-B}$. For the finitely many small reduced denominators, the positive distances $|q'\xi-p'|$ have a positive minimum after restricting to the nearest integers; multiplication by $d$ eventually dominates $q^{1-B}$. Thus reduction of a fraction causes no hidden coprimality exception.

A September2026 preprint by **David Niedbala Giraudin**, arXiv:2609.26980v1, claims the improved bound $\mu(\xi)<5.5138800$ using the Rhin–Viola framework and a new large parameter tuple. Its full HTML, stated inputs, numerical constants and computational methodology were read. Its ancillary C verifier was **not run**, and the numerical integral/interval certification is not independently audited here. Consequently (1)–(2) use the published $5.513891$ bound. The newer value is reported as a preprint claim, not as a verified improvement established by this contribution.

Why does even a much stronger rational bound fail to settle the prompt? There is an exact obstruction to that inference. The algebraic irrational $\sqrt2$ satisfies
\[
 \mu(\sqrt2)=2.
 \tag{3}
\]
For the lower-error direction, if $p/q$ stays in a fixed bounded interval about $\sqrt2$, then
\[
 |\sqrt2-p/q|
 =\frac{|2q^2-p^2|}{q^2(\sqrt2+|p/q|)}
 \ge \frac{c}{q^2},
\]
because the numerator is a nonzero integer. Fractions outside that interval have a fixed positive error and cannot affect the exponent. Hence $\mu(\sqrt2)\le2$. Conversely the integers defined by
$p_n+q_n\sqrt2=(3+2\sqrt2)^n$ satisfy $p_n^2-2q_n^2=1$, $q_n\to\infty$, and
\[
 |p_n/q_n-\sqrt2|
 =\frac1{q_n^2(p_n/q_n+\sqrt2)}.
\]
For every $\nu<2$ this is eventually smaller than $q_n^{-\nu}$, proving the opposite inequality in (3).

Thus finite irrationality measure, and even the optimal exponent2, is compatible with algebraicity of degree2. A proof for $\zeta(3)$ must exclude polynomial relations of every degree; controlling only $q\zeta(3)-p$ does not supply such bounds. This does not say that rational-approximation methods can never contribute to a transcendence proof. It identifies the missing logical input.

The earlier contribution supplies Apéry recurrences, enclosures, a finite polynomial search, and a tensor-power obstruction. The present report adds quantitative infinite continued-fraction and linear-form consequences, a status-separated current-source check, and a complete counterexample to the inference from irrationality measure to transcendence. No finite polynomial enumeration is treated as universal evidence.
