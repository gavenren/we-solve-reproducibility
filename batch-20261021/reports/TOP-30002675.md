# All iterated Engel word maps are onto every projective unitary group

Claim type: **Partial result**.

For each $k\ge1$, define $E_1(x,y)=[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}$ and $E_{k+1}(x,y)=[E_k(x,y),y]$. These are nontrivial words of arbitrarily large length whose exponent sums in both variables vanish. We prove
\[
 E_k:\operatorname{PU}(n)^2\longrightarrow\operatorname{PU}(n)
 \quad\text{surjective for every }n\ge2\text{ and every }k\ge1. \tag{1}
\]
Thus the eventual-surjectivity question in the prompt holds for this infinite family with $n_0=2$, without relying on a nonzero abelianized exponent. The coefficient-equation branch and arbitrary words are not claimed here.

We first work in $\mathrm{SU}(n)$. Let $T$ be the diagonal determinant-one torus and let $P$ be the cyclic permutation matrix taking the standard basis cyclically. Multiply $P$ by a unit scalar $c$ with $c^n\det P=1$, and put $S=cP\in\mathrm{SU}(n)$. Conjugation by $S$ induces a cyclic permutation $\rho$ on the diagonal entries. For $t\in T$,
\[
 [t,S]=t\rho(t)^{-1}=:\Phi(t)\in T. \tag{2}
\]
This is a continuous homomorphism of the abelian torus. Its Lie algebra is
\[
 V=\{(u_1,\ldots,u_n)\in\mathbb R^n:\sum u_i=0\},
\]
and its differential is $D=I-\rho$. The fixed vectors of a full cyclic permutation have all coordinates equal; their intersection with $V$ is zero. Hence $D$ is an invertible linear map on the $(n-1)$-dimensional space $V$, and $D^k$ is invertible for every $k$.

This yields an explicit solution, not only a dimension argument. Given $z=\operatorname{diag}(e^{iv_1},\ldots,e^{iv_n})\in T$, choose real arguments whose sum is zero. This is possible because the initial sum is an integer multiple of $2\pi$, removable by changing one argument by that multiple. Solve the real linear system $D^ku=v$ in $V$, and put $t=\operatorname{diag}(e^{iu_1},\ldots,e^{iu_n})$. Iterating (2) gives
\[
 E_k(t,S)=\Phi^k(t)=\exp(iD^ku)=z. \tag{3}
\]
There is no requirement that $D$ be invertible over the integer lattice; real inversion followed by exponentiation is enough for surjectivity. The map has a finite kernel, which causes no obstruction to existence.

Every $g\in\mathrm{SU}(n)$ is conjugate to a diagonal $z\in T$ by the spectral theorem. A unitary diagonalizer can be multiplied by a scalar to have determinant1 without changing its conjugation action. If $g=qzq^{-1}$, conjugating both arguments in (3) produces $E_k(qtq^{-1},qSq^{-1})=g$, because evaluation of a word commutes with homomorphisms and simultaneous conjugation. Thus every Engel map is already onto $\mathrm{SU}(n)$. Every element of $\operatorname{PU}(n)=\mathrm U(n)/\mathrm U(1)$ has an $\mathrm{SU}(n)$ lift: rescale any unitary representative to determinant1. Passing the constructed solution through the quotient proves (1).

For completeness these words are indeed nontrivial in $F(x,y)$. Under the Magnus substitution $x\mapsto1+X$, $y\mapsto1+Y$ in noncommutative formal power series, the leading homogeneous term of $E_1-1$ is $XY-YX$. Inductively the leading term of $E_{k+1}-1$ is $L_kY-YL_k$, where $L_k$ is the leading term of $E_k-1$. The monomial $XY^k$ has coefficient1 in $L_k$; no monomial beginning with $Y$ can cancel it in the next commutator. Hence each leading term is nonzero, so the word cannot be the identity word. Only this elementary one-way use of the substitution is needed, not an injectivity theorem for the full Magnus map.

Gotô [1] is the classical source for surjectivity of the commutator map on compact connected semisimple groups. Our proof is the elementary cyclic-torus argument in typeA, with the repeated invertible differential handling all Engel depths. It is an exposition of a known mechanism, not a novelty claim. The new problem-specific scope is the exact uniform all-$n$, all-depth word family and its explicit construction. Earlier A1 discusses an unverified nonsingular coefficient-system claim, not this word-map branch; no claim from that manuscript is used.
