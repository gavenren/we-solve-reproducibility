# Exact geometric recurrences for the tangent-endpoint iteration

Orient a smooth strictly positively curved oval counterclockwise. Let$s$ be its arclength,$T$ its unit tangent,$N$ its inward unit normal, and$\kappa>0$ its curvature. The map in the prompt sends it to$F=\gamma+T$. We prove the exact one-step identities
\[
\kappa_F=\frac{\kappa_s+\kappa+\kappa^3}{(1+\kappa^2)^{3/2}},\qquad
A(F)=A(\gamma)+\pi,\qquad
L(F)\ge\sqrt{L(\gamma)^2+4\pi^2}.
\]
The area here is oriented area, even if the image ceases to be convex. Equality in the length inequality holds precisely for a circle. These give a strict monotonicity statement along any positively curved iteration, not a proof that indefinite convexity is impossible for every noncircle.

The Frenet identities$T_s=\kappa N$, $N_s=-\kappa T$ give
\[
F_s=T+\kappa N,\qquad F_{ss}=-\kappa^2T+(\kappa+\kappa_s)N.
\]
The speed is$\sqrt{1+\kappa^2}>0$, and$\det(F_s,F_{ss})=\kappa+\kappa_s+\kappa^3$, proving the curvature formula. The tangent angle of$F$ is$\theta+\arctan\kappa$, where$\theta$ is that of$\gamma$; it has turning number one. If the displayed numerator is everywhere positive, this angle increases strictly by$2\pi$, and the regular closed curve is a strictly convex embedded oval. One direct reason is that each direction has a unique tangent parameter and the projection onto any fixed direction has exactly one maximum and minimum; its tangent line at either extremum supports the entire curve. Thus its tangent half-planes describe the boundary of a strictly convex body. Conversely positive curvature of the image requires the same numerator to be positive. This criterion concerns positive curvature, not convex curves with allowed zero-curvature points.

Expand the oriented area integral:
\[
2A(F)=\int\det(\gamma+T,T+T_s)\,ds.
\]
The original term is$2A(\gamma)$. The integral of$\det(\gamma,T_s)$ is zero: integrate the derivative of$\det(\gamma,T)$ and use$\gamma_s=T$. Finally$\det(T,T_s)=\kappa$, whose integral is$2\pi$. This proves the area increment without a convexity assumption on$F$.

For length, apply the Euclidean triangle inequality to the vector integral of$(1,\kappa(s))$:
\[
L(F)=\int\sqrt{1+\kappa^2}\,ds\ge
\left\|\int(1,\kappa)\,ds\right\|=\sqrt{L(\gamma)^2+4\pi^2}.
\]
Equality requires all vectors$(1,\kappa(s))$ to have the same direction, hence constant$\kappa$; a closed positively curved constant-curvature oval is a circle. Define$D(\gamma)=L(\gamma)^2-4\pi A(\gamma)$. Combining the formulas gives$D(F)\ge D(\gamma)$, strictly for a noncircle. Along convex iterations,$A_j=A_0+j\pi$ and$L_j^2\ge L_0^2+4\pi^2j$, with strict defect increase whenever the current curve is not a circle. Growing defect alone yields no contradiction: no uniform upper bound on$D_j$ has been established.

The original source also records a linearized theorem. Here is a proof at the exact periodic regularity needed. Let$f$ be a smooth real function of period$P>0$, and suppose every$f_j=(I+D)^jf$ is nonnegative. Its mean$\mu$ is unchanged, since derivatives have zero integral. The normalized Fourier coefficients satisfy$|\widehat f_j(k)|\le\mu$. On the other hand
\[
\widehat f_j(k)=(1+2\pi i k/P)^j\widehat f(k).
\]
For$k\ne0$ the multiplier has modulus greater than one. Boundedness for all$j$ forces$\widehat f(k)=0$. Thus$f$ is constant and nonnegative; it is positive if the iterations are required positive. The proof uses all iterates, not a finite positivity test. It does not replace the nonlinear curvature recurrence by this linear operator.

The geometric recurrences supply computable necessary conditions and quantify the failure of the isoperimetric defect to decrease. The full nonlinear conjecture, especially possible sequences with increasing defect and convex images at every step, remains outside this result.
