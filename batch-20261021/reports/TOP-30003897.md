# Green’s conjecture on arbitrary K3 surfaces, including special members

Claim type: **Literature report**.

For every smooth nonhyperelliptic complex curve $C$ on a K3 surface, the standard Green conjecture is a theorem, with **no Picard-rank-one restriction and no requirement that $C$ be a general member**. The exact reference is Aprodu–Farkas, *Green's Conjecture for curves on arbitrary K3 surfaces*, arXiv:0911.5310v3, Theorem1.2 (published in Compositio Mathematica147(2011),839–851).

The source uses the equivalent quadratic-strand formulation
\[
 K_{a,2}(C,K_C)=0\quad(a<\operatorname{Cliff}(C)). \tag{1}
\]
For the positive integer $p$ in the literal prompt, canonical Koszul duality gives
\[
 K_{\ell,1}(C,K_C)^*\simeq K_{g-2-\ell,2}(C,K_C).
\]
Together with Green–Lazarsfeld nonvanishing, the theorem says that the last nonzero **positive** linear index is $g-\operatorname{Cliff}(C)-2$, if that number is positive. Thus for $p\ge1$ it yields exactly
\[
 [K_{\ell,1}=0\text{ for all }\ell\ge p]
 \Longleftrightarrow \operatorname{Cliff}(C)>g-p-2. \tag{2}
\]
We retain the earlier contribution's warning that the prompt does not specify the range of $p$: at $p=0$ a plane quartic has no positive linear syzygies and $K_{0,1}=0$, while its stated Clifford value is1 and $g-p-2=1$. Hence the literal equivalence with unrestricted $p=0$ is false. This report proves no altered p=0 assertion and does not claim to repair the official text silently.

The extra force of the theorem is the word **arbitrary**. Voisin's Picard-rank-one result establishes the generic-curve case, but upper semicontinuity of Koszul dimensions alone does not transfer vanishing from a general curve to a special one. Aprodu–Farkas construct and compare suitable curves in the same K3 linear system. In the range $k=\operatorname{gon}(C)\le(g+2)/2$, Theorem1.1 uses the Brill–Noether linear-growth route
\[
 \dim W^1_{k+n}(C')\le n,\quad 0\le n\le g-2k+2,
\]
for an appropriate comparison curve $C'$. The maximal-gonality boundary is combined with the cited Voisin/Hirschowitz–Ramanan results in Theorem1.2. Their Section5 separately handles higher Clifford dimension. It would be incorrect to claim that every curve on every K3 surface itself satisfies this linear-growth inequality.

For clarity, the geometric reason that comparison within one ample K3 linear system controls **all** its smooth members has a short algebraic proof. Let $L$ be ample on a K3 surface $S$, let $s\in H^0(S,L)$ cut out a smooth curve $C$, and put
\[
 R=\bigoplus_{q\ge0}H^0(S,L^q).
\]
Adjunction gives $L|_C=K_C$. The sequence
\[
 0\to L^{q-1}\xrightarrow{\cdot s} L^q\to K_C^q\to0
\]
is surjective on global sections for every $q\ge1$: for $q=1$ use $H^1(S,\mathcal O_S)=0$, and for $q\ge2$ use Kodaira vanishing and $K_S\simeq\mathcal O_S$. Its kernel is $sH^0(S,L^{q-1})$. Consequently the canonical section ring is $R/(s)$, and $H^0(C,K_C)=H^0(S,L)/\langle s\rangle$.

The element $s$ is a nonzerodivisor on $R$ since the surface is integral. If a minimal graded free resolution of $R$ over $\operatorname{Sym}H^0(S,L)$ is tensored with the quotient by this linear element, it stays exact: the two-term resolution of the quotient has no positive Tor against $R$ precisely because $s$ is a nonzerodivisor. The resulting resolution stays minimal, since its differentials still have positive degree. Thus its graded Betti numbers, and hence its Koszul groups, are those of $C$ over $\operatorname{Sym}H^0(C,K_C)$. This proves the required hyperplane transfer in this ample setting, without an invalid special-member semicontinuity argument.

The deep existence and Brill–Noether comparisons, the remaining nonample cases, and the nonvanishing theorem are credited literature inputs. The result addresses an unbounded-genus, arbitrary-Picard family of the literal problem. It neither proves Green for all curves nor asserts a positive-characteristic extension.
