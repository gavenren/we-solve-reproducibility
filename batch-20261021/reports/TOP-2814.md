# Published cusped existence and a 2026 quantitative preprint for all finite-volume manifolds

**Problem:** TOP-2814

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** Published infinitude for orientable cusped hyperbolic 3-manifolds, and a precisely attributed account of Han–Yang arXiv:2608.29855v2 Theorem 1.1, which claims density-one simple primitive geodesics for every finite-volume hyperbolic d-manifold, d≥3, including nonorientable manifolds. The general theorem is reported as a preprint, not independently certified or published.

The prompt's distinction between infinitely many homotopy classes and infinitely many embedded images is decisive. Kuhlmann's published Theorem 1.1 proves the desired infinitude for every orientable cusped finite-volume hyperbolic 3-manifold. Its construction uses closed axes spiralling around a cusp and approaching a simple cusp-to-cusp geodesic; the embeddedness argument is part of the theorem, rather than a consequence of infinitely many conjugacy classes.

There is also a recent primary preprint addressing the remaining general statement. Han–Yang, arXiv:2608.29855v2, Theorem 1.1, claims that, for every finite-volume hyperbolic \(d\)-manifold, \(d\geq3\), a number \(\delta_d>0\) satisfies
\[
 N_{\rm ns}(L)\ll_M e^{(d-1-\delta_d)L}.
\]
Here counts are primitive unoriented geometric images. Together with \(N(L)\asymp_M e^{(d-1)L}/L\), this gives
\[
 {N_{\rm ns}(L)\over N(L)}\ll_M L e^{-\delta_dL}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The preprint therefore claims a much stronger conclusion than infinitude, including closed and nonorientable examples. It is a September 2026 preprint in the checked version; this report does not promote it to an accepted journal theorem.

The final reduction can be checked explicitly from its two technical estimates. Put \(a=d-1\). Lemma 5.1 bounds geodesics entering cusp depth greater than \(H\) by \(O_M(e^{a(L-H)})\). Lemma 5.2 bounds shallow nonsimple geodesics by
\[
 O_M\!\left(e^{(a-\eta/2)L+2a(m_0+1)H}\right),
\]
where \(\eta>0\) and \(m_0\) arise from polynomial escape. Choosing
\[
 H={\eta L\over2a(2m_0+3)},\qquad
 \delta={\eta\over2(2m_0+3)}
\]
makes both exponents \(aL-\delta L\). The choice is admissible for sufficiently large \(L\). Subtraction from the prime-geodesic lower bound shows \(N_{\rm simp}(L)\to\infty\), and primitivity prevents multiple traversals from inflating the count.

The difficult input is the shallow estimate: a self-intersection forces a nonzero bounded-degree polynomial condition on deck transformations, followed by a uniform escape bound for products of lattice elements. Remark 5.3 explicitly handles orientation-reversing cosets. Simply projecting simple curves from an orientation cover would not suffice, because distinct lifted arcs can meet after projection. Thus the version and this particular extension matter. The published cusped theorem is an established answer on its scope; the claimed general resolution is accurately identified as depending on the cited recent preprint and its un-reproved technical lemmas.
