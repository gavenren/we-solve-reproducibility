# Common graphs with arbitrary chromatic number and connectivity

**Claim type: Literature report.** For every pair of positive integers $k,r$, there is a finite simple $k$-vertex-connected common graph $H$ with $\chi(H)\ge r$. This is Ko–Lee's Theorem1.1 and gives an established negative answer to the literal boundedness question. No new disproof priority is claimed. Fresh official metadata and the prompt list no earlier contribution.

We connect the checked theorem to the prompt's injective normalization, and prove the commonality-preserving operation used in its connectivity strengthening.

## The credited theorem

Ko and Lee, arXiv2207.09427v2 (13June2023), prove the existence assertion for each fixed $k,r$. The graph may depend on both parameters. Taking $r>B$ rules out every proposed absolute integer $B$. This does not say that every high-chromatic graph is common.

The paper uses graphon homomorphism densities. For a symmetric measurable $W:[0,1]^2\to[0,1]$, set
\[
t(H,W)=\int\prod_{uv\in E(H)}W(x_u,x_v)\,dx.
\]
Commonality is the inequality
\[
t(H,W)+t(H,1-W)\ge 2^{1-e(H)}
\]
for every such $W$. The constant agrees exactly with $W=1/2$.

## Conversion to injective copies

Fix $H$, with $h$ vertices and $e$ edges. For an $n$-vertex graph $G$, let $W_G$ be its usual step graphon, with zero on diagonal blocks. Then
\[
t(H,W_G)=\operatorname{hom}(H,G)/n^h.
\]
The graphon of the loopless complement differs from $1-W_G$ only on the $n$ diagonal blocks, whose total measure is $1/n$. A union bound over the $e$ edges of $H$ gives
\[
\left|t(H,1-W_G)-\operatorname{hom}(H,\overline G)/n^h\right|\le e/n.
\]

Every noninjective map has a colliding pair of domain vertices. Choosing that pair and the images of at most $h-1$ resulting classes bounds the number of noninjective maps by $\binom h2n^{h-1}$. Apply this in each color to obtain
\[
\frac{N_H(G)+N_H(\overline G)}{n^h}
\ge 2^{1-e}-\frac{e+2\binom h2}{n}.
\]
For every sequence with $n\to\infty$, the error vanishes, giving exactly the prompt's lower-limit inequality. The graph $H$ is fixed before taking this limit; no uniform error for growing $H$ is asserted.

## Independent-set books

Let $I$ be an independent set in $H$. A $q$-book consists of $q$ copies of $H$ with corresponding vertices in $I$ identified and all other vertices distinct. Its edge count is $qe$, because no edge is shared inside $I$.

Conditioning on coordinates in $I$ gives a nonnegative conditional integral $F_W$ for a single copy. The book density is $\int F_W^q$, so Jensen's inequality gives
\[
t(H_I^q,W)\ge t(H,W)^q.
\]
Applying this to both colors and convexity once more,
\[
t(H_I^q,W)+t(H_I^q,1-W)
\ge 2\left(\frac{t(H,W)+t(H,1-W)}2\right)^q
\ge2^{1-qe}.
\]
Hence books preserve commonality. They also preserve chromatic number: one copy supplies the lower bound, and a coloring of $H$ extends by coloring corresponding copies identically. Independence of $I$ is essential to this simple edge-count argument.

For a connected starting graph with at least one edge, Ko–Lee's Theorem2.2 gives the nontrivial connectivity amplification: iterating books over the independent classes of copies of its vertices produces arbitrarily high vertex connectivity for a suitable parameter. Their proof uses an auxiliary linear hypergraph and internally disjoint paths. This theorem and the underlying high-chromatic common-graph existence result are credited external inputs; convexity alone establishes neither. When $r=1$, one may start with a graph of chromatic number at least2, so the one-vertex connected-graph boundary causes no issue.

## What this does and does not resolve

The historical boundedness assertion is false by the checked theorem, even with prescribed vertex connectivity. The report gives its precise normalization and a reusable preservation argument. It does not give a classification of common graphs, a smallest-order formula, or simultaneous arbitrarily large girth. No program execution, formal proof assistant verification, or new priority is claimed.

Reference: [Sejin Ko and Joonkyung Lee, Theorems1.1 and2.2; Lemma2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.09427v2).
