# Raz–Tal distributions: the missing quantitative bridge to PH oracle hardness

**Exact scope.** Carefully credited Raz–Tal2018 Theorems1.1–1.3 and Corollaries1.4–1.5, with a full explanation of distributional-to-promise conversion and fixed-level PH circuit unrolling. This gives established oracle hardness only, not the literal unrelativized BQP noncontainment in PH.

The previous contribution develops exact easy subclasses of Forrelation. Here the distinct added scope is the quantitative theorem which actually produces the known hard black-box regime, including the conversion steps that must not be suppressed. We use the exact31May2018 ECCC TR18-107 primary version, not a claim of independent certification of the later journal paper's full proof.

**Checked source statements.** Raz–Tal Theorem1.1 constructs an explicit distribution D on2N Boolean signs. One quantum query and O(log N) computation distinguish D from the uniform distribution with advantage Omega(1/log N), whereas constant-depth circuits of quasipolynomial size have advantage at most polylog(N)/sqrt N. Advantage here is the absolute difference of acceptance probabilities averaged over the two input distributions; it is not a pointwise acceptance-gap guarantee on every input.

Theorem1.2 supplies a different amplified distribution D_1 on N_1 signs: a polylogarithmic-query/time quantum algorithm distinguishes it from uniform with near-perfect success, with error2^{-polylog(N_1)}, while the same form of small constant-depth-circuit distinguishing upper bound persists. Theorem1.3 gives a one-query amplified variant against a distribution denoted tilde U rather than simply asserting that all amplifications preserve uniformity. These are deep proved source results; their Gaussian/circuit lower-bound analysis is credited, not reproduced or replaced by a finite numerical test.

**From average behavior to a legitimate promise.** Let p(z) be the amplified algorithm's acceptance probability, labeled so its expected error under D_1 and uniform is at most delta in each case (changing constants from an equal-prior statement only changes delta by a constant). Define Y={z:p(z)>=2/3} and N={z:p(z)<=1/3}. The sets are disjoint, and the algorithm is a bounded-error quantum decider on Y union N. By Markov's inequality,
\[
 D_1(Y)\ge1-3\delta,\qquad U(N)\ge1-3\delta.
\]
A deterministic PH oracle predicate correct on every promised input would therefore accept D_1 with probability at least1-3delta and accept uniform with probability at most3delta. Its distinguishing advantage is at least1-6delta. This contradicts the source's vanishing circuit bound after the following unrolling. The unpromised middle set is left unspecified; it is not silently assigned answers that the quantum algorithm is claimed to decide.

**Why a fixed PH level gives the prohibited circuit.** Put a=log N_1. A fixed-level polynomial-hierarchy machine takes poly(a) time and uses a fixed number of existential/universal blocks of poly(a) witness bits. Expand an existential block as an OR over all its possible witnesses and a universal block as an AND. For fixed witnesses, a deterministic poly(a)-time query computation can be unfolded as a decision tree of depth poly(a); its accepting paths form a DNF with2^{poly(a)} gates and depth two. This accounts for adaptive queries as well as nonadaptive ones. Combining the fixed number of blocks yields constant depth and total size2^{poly(a)}=quasipoly(N_1). Fixed advice or already fixed external oracle answers merely specialize this circuit. Thus the source's distinguishing lower bound excludes every such fixed machine/level on sufficiently large address lengths.

The source records this bridge as Corollary1.4, Promise-BQP not contained in Promise-PH in the black-box model, and Corollary1.5 constructs one oracle O with BQP^O not contained in PH^O. The latter is a theorem about a single oracle defeating all fixed levels, not just a separate oracle for each level; the source appendix supplies the diagonalization. We checked the exact corollary and appendix rather than inferring that global quantifier from one finite circuit bound alone.

**Limits.** Explicit distribution in this theorem means a mathematically specified distribution of long black-box strings; it does not make every sampled string's values uniformly evaluable from a short circuit. The relevant efficient quantum time is polynomial in address length, not in a supplied full table length. Replacing the oracle by a short description would allow classical syntax access and requires a new lower bound. Likewise the Markov conversion above constructs a promise, not a polynomial-time test recognizing that promise. The original ordinary-language BQP-versus-PH question remains untouched by dropping neither qualifier. The result is an established substantial relativized frontier, with the specific quantitative bridge reconstructed here.
