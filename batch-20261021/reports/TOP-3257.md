# Tournament cycle packing: the exact conjectured threshold and the lower asymptotic barrier

**Claim type: Literature report.** Bang-Jensen, Bessy and Thomassé prove that every tournament with minimum outdegree at least \(2k-1\) contains \(k\) vertex-disjoint **directed triangles**, for every positive integer \(k\) [1]. They also prove that for every \(\varepsilon>0\), there is \(k_0(\varepsilon)\) such that minimum outdegree at least \((3/2+\varepsilon)k\) suffices for \(k\ge k_0(\varepsilon)\). These are substantial published tournament theorems, not new proofs in this report. They do not settle the full prompt for arbitrary digraphs.

A tournament has exactly one arc between every pair of distinct vertices. Its minimum outdegree is computed in the whole tournament; no minimum indegree condition is added to either theorem. “Disjoint” here means vertex-disjoint, and a directed triangle is in particular a simple directed cycle. Therefore the first theorem proves the prompt's conclusion throughout the entire tournament class, with a stronger conclusion on cycle lengths.

There are three quantifier distinctions. The first threshold is \(2k-1\) for **each** positive integer \(k\). The second threshold has a universal \(\varepsilon>0\) followed by a sufficiently-large-\(k\) requirement depending on that \(\varepsilon\). It does not give the endpoint \(3k/2\), a uniform finite exceptional list, or an improved threshold for general digraphs. The source's earlier bounded-minimum-indegree tournament results should not be substituted for its later theorem without that extra hypothesis.

Here is the complete lower-bound calculation explaining the coefficient \(3/2\). For every even \(k=2a\), take the cyclic regular tournament on
\[
 n=3k-1=6a-1
\]
vertices, with arcs \(i\to i+j\pmod n\) for \(1\le j\le(n-1)/2\). Exactly one of two nonzero opposite residues lies in this interval, so this is a tournament. Every vertex has outdegree
\[
 (n-1)/2=3a-1=\tfrac32 k-1.
\]
Every directed cycle uses at least3 vertices, since tournaments have neither loops nor digons. Consequently \(k\) vertex-disjoint directed cycles would use at least \(3k>n\) vertices and cannot exist. For any fixed \(c<3/2\), all sufficiently large even \(k\) satisfy \(3k/2-1\ge ck\). Thus **no coefficient smaller than \(3/2\)** can be a universal large-\(k\) sufficient minimum-outdegree coefficient for tournaments. This is a vertex-resource obstruction, independent of whether longer cycles are permitted.

The regular examples do not disprove the \(2k-1\) theorem: for \(k\ge2\), \(3k/2-1<2k-1\). Nor do they determine the exact additive constant at coefficient \(3/2\). In particular the \(\varepsilon\) in the asymptotic theorem cannot be dropped by appealing to these examples.

The earlier contribution treats symmetric digraphs by matching, bounded-circumference packing, and a circulant-induction obstruction. None supplies the all-tournament theorem or the distinction between the exact \(2k-1\) threshold and the sharp asymptotic coefficient. This report contributes a source-checked synthesis with the explicit unbounded obstruction and correct quantifier boundaries. No computational or full-paper proof verification is asserted.
