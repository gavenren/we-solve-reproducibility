# Balanced three-colorings: exact finite moment and the missing implication

**Claim type: Literature report.** For \(h=3s\), let \(Z_h\) count proper colorings of the labelled random \(h\)-lift of \(K_5\) with three named colors, each appearing exactly \(s\) times in every fiber. We derive the exact formula
\[
 \mathbb EZ_h=
 \left(\frac{(3s)!}{(s!)^3}\right)^5
 \left[
  \frac{(s!)^6}{(3s)!}
  \sum_{j=0}^{s}\frac1{(j!)^3((s-j)!)^3}
 \right]^{10}.
 \tag{1}
\]
It has exponential rate
\[
 \log\mathbb EZ_h
 =h\log(1024/243)+O(\log h).
 \tag{2}
\]
This is a specialization of the balanced-coloring moment framework of Nir and Pérez-Giménez [1], whose sharper asymptotic is stated below. A positive or exponentially growing first moment does **not** establish the single-value concentration asked by the prompt.

There are \(((3s)!/(s!)^3)^5\) balanced color assignments to the five labelled fibers. Fix one assignment. For a perfect matching between two fibers, let \(a_{ij}\) count matches from color \(i\) to color \(j\). All row and column sums equal \(s\). A proper matching has zero diagonal. Every such3-by3 table is uniquely
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}0&j&s-j\\s-j&0&j\\j&s-j&0\end{pmatrix}
 \quad(0\le j\le s).
\]
For a fixed table, partition each source color class among its destination colors, and then biject the selected preimages to the labelled vertices in each destination class. This gives
\[
 \frac{(s!)^6}{\prod_{i,j}a_{ij}!}
 =\frac{(s!)^6}{(j!)^3((s-j)!)^3}
\]
matching permutations. Dividing the sum by \((3s)!\) gives the bracket in(1), the probability \(q_h\) that this one matching respects the fixed coloring. The ten base-edge permutations are independent **for a fixed coloring**, so its success probability is \(q_h^{10}\). Linearity of expectation, which does not require independence between different colorings, proves(1).

To check the exponential rate without a saddle-point calculation, rewrite
\[
 q_h=\frac{(s!)^3}{(3s)!}\sum_{j=0}^{s}\binom sj^3.
\]
The largest binomial coefficient is at least \(2^s/(s+1)\), while each is at most \(2^s\). Hence
\[
 \frac{8^s}{(s+1)^3}
 \le\sum_j\binom sj^3\le(s+1)8^s.
\]
The elementary factorial estimate \(\log n!=n\log n-n+O(\log n)\) now yields
\(\log q_h=h\log(2/3)+O(\log h)\), and the logarithm of the number of assignments is \(5h\log3+O(\log h)\). Thus
\[
 5\log3+10\log(2/3)
 =10\log2-5\log3=\log(1024/243),
\]
proving(2).

For comparison, [1, Proposition2.2] gives the stronger credited asymptotic
\[
 \mathbb EZ_h\sim
 \frac{2^{20}}{9\sqrt3}(2\pi h)^{-5}
 \left(\frac{1024}{243}\right)^h
 \quad(h\to\infty,\ 3\mid h).
 \tag{3}
\]
The constant comes from its \(k=3,d=4,|V|=5,|E|=10\) specialization:
\(3^{15/2}(4/3)^{10}=2^{20}/(9\sqrt3)\). We do not independently reproduce the source's polynomial-prefactor analysis.

The same source's colorability Theorem1.2 requires
\[
 d<\ell_k=
 \frac{2(k-1)^3}{k(k-2)}\log(k-1).
\]
For three colors, \(\ell_3=(16/3)\log2<4\), whereas \(K_5\) has degree4. It therefore **does not** give3-colorability of these lifts. Proposition2.2 has no such upper-colorability restriction, so its first moment remains valid. Mixing these two sets of hypotheses would produce an erroneous solution.

Even abstractly, let \(c>0\) and let \(Y_h=\lfloor e^{2ch}\rfloor\) with probability \(e^{-ch}\), and0 otherwise. Then \(\mathbb EY_h\) grows like \(e^{ch}\), while \(\mathbb P(Y_h>0)=e^{-ch}\to0\). Thus(2) alone cannot show even a nonvanishing probability of3-colorability, much less probability tending to1. A suitable second-moment/conditioning argument or another structural method remains necessary. We make no statement deciding the validity of more recent external claimed solutions.

The earlier contribution proves the two-value window and cites the \(K_5-e\) theorem, but does not compute(1)–(3) or explain the exact degree threshold at which the balanced-moment upper-bound argument stops. Only the subsequence \(3\mid h\) is analyzed here; no extension to every residue class or concentration claim is implicit. No enumeration or program execution is claimed.
