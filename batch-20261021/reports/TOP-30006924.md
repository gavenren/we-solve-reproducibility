# A complete sofic-matrix proof with the omitted zero atom retained

Claim type: **Literature report**.

The determinant inequality holds for **every sofic group**, including groups not covered by the earlier residual-finite quotient argument. Elek–Szabó[1, Theorem5] prove this theorem. We give a complete finite-matrix version of the argument in the prompt's normalized spectral convention, explicitly identifying why exact finite quotients are unnecessary and retaining the omitted zero eigenspace.

A sofic approximation may be expressed as maps$\sigma_j:G\to\operatorname{Sym}(V_j)$such that$\sigma_j(1)=1$, multiplication defects$\sigma_j(g)\sigma_j(h)\ne\sigma_j(gh)$occur on$o(|V_j|)$points for each fixed$g,h$, and every fixed nonidentity$g$has$o(|V_j|)$fixed points. For a finitely generated sofic group one may take a sequence satisfying these conditions simultaneously on growing finite sets. For an arbitrary group and a fixed matrix, restrict to the finitely generated subgroup supporting that matrix: every word trace in the canonical group trace is unchanged. This reduction is enough, so no countability assumption is imposed on the final theorem.

Let$A=(\sum_g a_{uv,g}g)\in M_d(\mathbb ZG)$and write$P_j(g)$for the permutation matrix of$\sigma_j(g)$. Form the$d|V_j|$square integer matrix
\[
 A_j=(\sum_g a_{uv,g}P_j(g)),\qquad D_j=A_j^*A_j.
\]
The adjoint uses transpose, which matches inverses of the permutation matrices. Put$C=\sum_{u,v,g}|a_{uv,g}|$and choose$K\ge\max(e,C^2)$. Every summand is a coefficient times a coordinate-block isometry, so$\|A_j\|,\|A\|\le C$. All normalized spectral laws of$D_j$and$D=A^*A$lie in$[0,K]$.

For each fixed$k$, expand the normalized trace of$D_j^k$into finitely many coefficient-weighted permutation-word traces. Approximate multiplicativity implies that any fixed product of the$P_j(g)$and their inverses differs from$P_j(w)$on$o(|V_j|)$basis positions, where$w$is the corresponding group word. This follows inductively: multiplying permutations does not enlarge normalized Hamming distance, and$P_j(g)^{-1}$is close to$P_j(g^{-1})$by the defect for$gg^{-1}=1$. The normalized trace of a permutation matrix is its fixed-point proportion. It therefore tends to1if$w=1$and0otherwise. Hence
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{Tr}(D_j^k)}{d|V_j|}\longrightarrow
 (\tau_G\otimes\operatorname{tr}_d)(D^k). \tag{1}
\]
Uniform polynomial approximation on$[0,K]$then gives weak convergence$\mu_j\Rightarrow\mu$of the probability spectral measures. This is a moment argument; the approximate permutations are not claimed to define homomorphisms$G\to\operatorname{Sym}(V_j)$.

For any integer matrix$B$, the product of the nonzero eigenvalues of$B^*B$is a positive integer: up to sign it is the lowest nonzero coefficient of its monic integer characteristic polynomial. The empty product is1. Thus
\[
 I(\mu_j):=\int_{(0,K]}\log t\,d\mu_j(t)\ge0. \tag{2}
\]
The zero eigenvalues have been omitted, exactly as required by the prompt.

We give the needed limit step to avoid asserting false determinant convergence. From(2), the negative logarithmic part is at most$\log K$, and hence
\[
 \mu_j((0,\varepsilon])\le\frac{\log K}{|\log\varepsilon|}
 \quad(0<\varepsilon<1). \tag{3}
\]
For$\varepsilon$not an atom of$\mu$, weak convergence on$[0,\varepsilon]$and(3)give
\[
 \liminf_j\mu_j(\{0\})\ge\mu([0,\varepsilon])-\frac{\log K}{|\log\varepsilon|}.
\]
Let such$\varepsilon\downarrow0$. The opposite limsup follows from the closed-set inequality for$\{0\}$. Thus the zero masses converge. For fixed$\varepsilon$, the continuous truncated function$\log\max(t,\varepsilon)$has convergent integrals. Subtracting$\mu_j(\{0\})\log\varepsilon$removes only the zero atom and leaves a quantity at least$I(\mu_j)\ge0$. Passing to the limit preserves that inequality. Letting$\varepsilon\downarrow0$, monotone convergence for the truncated negative parts bounds their limit by$\log K$. This proves both integrability off zero and
\[
 \int_{(0,\infty)}\log t\,d\mu(t)\ge0.
\]
No invertibility or zero-kernel assumption has entered the proof. The source uses an unnormalized matrix trace; division by$d$and the positive factor$1/2$when passing from$A^*A$to$A$do not change the sign.

The earlier A1 proves the finite/residually-finite case and a zero-atom limit lemma. The added ingredient here is the full approximate-permutation construction(1), which extends that mechanism to all sofic groups and is credited to Elek–Szabó. The complete A2 summarizes an OpenAI claimed counterexample over some group; neither its manuscript nor Lean code has been verified here. Our theorem implies that **any actual counterexample of the literal kind must use a nonsofic group**. That is a conditional restriction, not a certification that the earlier claimed example exists or is nonsofic. Weak spectral convergence does not generally give equality of logarithmic integrals, and no such equality is asserted.
