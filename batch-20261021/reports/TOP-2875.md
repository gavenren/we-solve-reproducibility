# Why the published bounding rational homology spheres do not supply orientable fillings

**Problem:** TOP-2875

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** Ferrari–Kolpakov–Reid's published infinite families geometrically bound compact hyperbolic four-manifolds, but those constructions have nonorientable fillings. A rational-cohomology proof shows that their free-involution boundary-folding method cannot be made orientable for an odd-dimensional rational homology sphere. The orientation double cover supplies two boundary copies, not the single boundary demanded in the prompt.

The word “orientable” in the literal prompt excludes the most directly relevant published construction. Ferrari–Kolpakov–Reid's Theorem A produces infinitely many hyperbolic rational homology three-spheres geometrically bounding compact hyperbolic four-manifolds. Their introduction explicitly states that their constructions give **nonorientable** fillings. Thus this theorem does not answer the prompt as written.

The obstruction is structural, not a missing orientation choice. Their Lemma 3.1 starts with a geodesic embedding of \(Y\), cuts the ambient manifold, and, when two copies of \(Y\) occur, self-identifies one boundary copy by a fixed-point-free isometric involution \(\tau\). The remaining copy becomes the whole boundary. For an orientable result, this involution must reverse orientation.

Here is a self-contained reason such a \(\tau\) cannot exist when \(Y\) is an odd-dimensional rational homology sphere. If it did, \(Z=Y/\langle\tau\rangle\) would be a closed connected nonorientable manifold. For a finite free action, transfer identifies
\[
 H^*(Z;\mathbb Q)\cong H^*(Y;\mathbb Q)^{\langle\tau\rangle}.
\]
The only cohomology of \(Y\) is a copy of \(\mathbb Q\) in degrees \(0\) and \(3\). The action fixes degree zero and acts by \(-1\) in degree three. Therefore \(H^*(Z;\mathbb Q)\) would be \(\mathbb Q\) in degree zero and zero otherwise, giving \(\chi(Z)=1\). But every closed odd-dimensional manifold has Euler characteristic zero: its orientation cover has Euler characteristic zero by Poincaré duality, and Euler characteristic divides by the covering degree. This contradiction proves the obstruction. It is the content of the paper's Lemma 7.10 in the present dimension.

Nor does taking the orientation double cover of a constructed nonorientable filling fix the problem. Along an orientable boundary \(Y\), the orientation character of the filling restricts to that of \(Y\), since the outward normal line is trivial. This restriction is zero. Consequently the orientation cover has boundary \(Y\sqcup Y\), with two degree-one copies, rather than one connected copy. Its hyperbolic metric and total-geodesy survive the cover, but the single-boundary requirement fails.

The published construction therefore establishes a neighboring existence theorem and explains why a natural attempted upgrade fails. This is not a universal obstruction to orientable geometric fillings: it excludes this involution-folding route, not all possible hyperbolic four-manifolds. No claimed solution of the orientable existence problem follows from the cited result, and no unavailable computation or newer announced result is adopted.
