# An explicit spherical Pachner bound, including the size of intermediate triangulations

**Problem:** TOP-2826

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** For closed PL three-spheres in the standard face-pairing triangulation convention, Mijatović's explicit bound gives d_P(T1,T2)<F(t1)+F(t2), F(t)=6·10^6 t²2^(50000t²), with an explicit bound on intermediate tetrahedron counts. No universal three-manifold bound, polynomial bound, or lower bound is asserted.

Let \(T_i\) triangulate \(S^3\) with \(t_i\) tetrahedra. We use face-pairing triangulations: simplices need not be uniquely determined by their vertices. This is the convention explicitly allowed in the cited theorem, and includes the canonical two-tetrahedron sphere \(C\). The numerical statement below is made in that convention; it is not a bound for a variant requiring every intermediate object to be a strict abstract simplicial complex.

Put
\[
 F(t)=6\cdot10^6\,t^2\,2^{50000t^2}.
\]
Mijatović's Theorem 1.2 supplies a path \(T_i\to C\) of length \(m_i<F(t_i)\). This theorem is the substantial geometric input. Its proof uses normal and almost-normal spheres to control simplification of a three-sphere.

Reverse the path from \(T_2\) to \(C\) and concatenate. Pachner moves are reversible; the final identification with \(C\) is a combinatorial isomorphism, so these paths have the same middle endpoint up to the isomorphisms allowed in the problem. Consequently
\[
 d_P(T_1,T_2)\le m_1+m_2<F(t_1)+F(t_2).
\]
Taking the maximum over spherical endpoint triangulations proves the corresponding bound for \(P_{S^3}\).

There is also a useful guarantee about the space this certificate uses. A three-dimensional Pachner move changes tetrahedron number by \(3,1,-1\), or \(-3\). After \(j\) moves on the first path there are at most \(t_1+3j\) tetrahedra. Every triangulation on the reversed second path already occurred on its forward path, so reversal does not introduce a further increase. The concatenated certificate therefore has fewer than
\[
 \max\{t_1+3F(t_1),\ t_2+3F(t_2)\}
\]
tetrahedra at every stage. This is a proved size allowance, not the assumption that a small-size subgraph contains a connecting path.

For completeness, the elementary counting obstruction points in the other direction:
\[
 d_P(T_1,T_2)\ge\left\lceil |t_1-t_2|/3\right\rceil.
\]
Each move changes size by at most three. Starting with any spherical triangulation and repeatedly performing \(1\!-\!4\) moves gives endpoints of sizes \(t\) and \(t+3k\), and their distance is exactly \(k\): the displayed lower bound matches the constructed \(k\)-move path. Thus size change alone forces linear distances on an infinite family, while the cited upper estimate allows substantial temporary expansion.

This does not measure the hardest pairs of comparable endpoint size, establish sharp growth, or control arbitrary \(M\). In particular neither a finite census of small triangulations nor failure of a search with an imposed tetrahedron ceiling supplies a lower bound for unrestricted distance.

References actually checked:

- Aleksandar Mijatović, Simplifying triangulations of S³, arXiv:math/0008107v1 (15 August 2000), Theorem 1.2, introduction and §§2–6, https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0008107
