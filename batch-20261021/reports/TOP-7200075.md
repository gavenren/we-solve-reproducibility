# Existence in the convex subclass and a complete polygonal test family

Restrict the admissible regions to compact convex bodies. In this subclass the area infimum is attained. We also prove that for any fixed compact region, accommodating every rectifiable length-one worm is equivalent to accommodating every polygonal chain of positive length at most one. These are continuum reductions, not a computed optimal area and not a proof that an unrestricted optimal region can be chosen convex.

First, a disk of radius$1/2$ is universal: parametrize a worm by arclength on$[0,1]$ and center the disk at the half-arclength point. Every other point is within the length of the intervening subarc, at most$1/2$. Thus the convex infimum is at most$A_0=\pi/4$.

Every universal cover contains a rigid image of the boundary of an equilateral triangle of side$1/3$, traversed once. If it is convex it therefore contains that solid triangle and its indisk of radius$\rho=\sqrt3/18$. Move the triangle to a fixed position, with its incenter at the origin. For any$x$ of a convex cover at distance$R$ from the origin, the triangle with apex$x$ and base a diameter of this indisk perpendicular to$x$ lies in the cover and has area$\rho R$. A normalized cover of area at most$A_0+1$ is therefore contained in the common disk of radius$(A_0+1)/\rho$, while containing$B(0,\rho)$.

Take a minimizing sequence with these bounds. Its support functions are uniformly bounded and uniformly Lipschitz on the unit circle: $|h(u)-h(v)|\le R_0\|u-v\|$. A finite-net diagonal subsequence, or Arzelà–Ascoli, gives uniform convergence to the support function of a compact convex limit$K$, still between the same two disks. Equivalently the bodies converge in Hausdorff distance. Their areas converge: a Hausdorff error$\varepsilon$ and the common indisk imply
\[
K_j\subset K+\varepsilon B\subset(1+\varepsilon/\rho)K,
\quad
K\subset(1+\varepsilon/\rho)K_j,
\]
which squeeze their areas.

Universality is closed under this limit. For any single worm choose an accommodating rotation and translation into each$K_j$. Anchor one parameter point of the worm at zero before choosing these placements. Its translated anchor remains in the common bounded disk, and rotations form a compact circle, so a subsequence of placements converges. The limit worm image lies in$K$ by Hausdorff convergence and closedness. This subsequence may depend on the worm, which is legitimate because universality quantifies existence of a placement separately for each worm. Thus$K$ is universal and attains the convex infimum.

For the polygonal equivalence, a nonconstant polygonal chain of length$\ell\le1$ can be extended to total length one by repeatedly retracing an existing nonzero segment, with a final partial retracing. Its image is unchanged. Hence a universal worm cover accommodates the chain. Conversely approximate any arclength-parametrized worm by inscribed polygonal chains of length at most one. Uniform continuity gives Hausdorff convergence of these images to the worm. Accommodating placements into a fixed compact$K$ again have a convergent subsequence after anchoring, proving accommodation of the whole worm. A constant chain imposes no extra condition.

Finite lists of chain tests alone are not sufficient; the statement requires all chains, or an additional error-controlled dense family.
