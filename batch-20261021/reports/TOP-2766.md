# An explicit pants-translation volume constant for punctured-torus bundles

**Problem:** TOP-2766

**Claim type:** Partial result

**Exact scope:** For every orientation-preserving pseudo-Anosov mapping class of a once-punctured torus, V(φ)≥v3 τ_P(φ), where v3 is the volume of the regular ideal tetrahedron and P is the standard pants/Farey graph. This follows from Guéritaud–Futer's explicit syllable-volume theorem and a supplied translation-length estimate. No Weil–Petersson bound or higher-complexity uniform constant is claimed.

For the once-punctured torus, a pants decomposition consists of one essential curve. Slopes identify its pants graph with the Farey graph: vertices are \(\mathbb Q\cup\{\infty\}\), and two reduced slopes \(p/q,r/s\) are adjacent when \(|ps-qr|=1\).

First take a hyperbolic matrix of positive trace, conjugated to a positive cyclic word
\[
 \Omega=R^{a_1}L^{b_1}\cdots R^{a_n}L^{b_n},\qquad a_i,b_i\ge1,
\]
where \(R=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\) and
\(L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix}\). This standard normal form is the punctured-torus monodromy convention used in the cited paper. Its Theorem B.1 proves
\[
 V(\Omega)\ge2n v_3.
\]
This substantial imported geometric estimate counts alternating blocks, not the possibly much larger sum of the exponents.

We now compare with exactly the stable translation length asked for in the prompt. Let \(v=\infty\) and \(w=0\). They are adjacent; \(R\) fixes \(v\), and \(L\) fixes \(w\). For one block \(B=R^aL^b\),
\[
 d(v,Bv)=d(v,L^b v)\le d(v,w)+d(w,L^b v)=2 .
\]
The last equality uses \(L^b w=w\) and invariance of graph distance. For isometries \(g,h\),
\(d(v,ghv)\le d(v,gv)+d(v,hv)\). Applying this to all \(n\) blocks and then to \(m\) repetitions yields
\[
 d(v,\Omega^m v)\le2nm,\qquad \tau_{\mathcal P}(\Omega)\le2n.
\]
Together these inequalities imply the explicit constant
\[
 \boxed{\,V(\Omega)\ge v_3\,\tau_{\mathcal P}(\Omega)\,}.
\]

Conjugacy changes neither volume nor stable translation length. The mapping class group of the punctured torus is \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)\), and its central element \(-I\) acts trivially on slopes. If \(\phi\) has negative trace, pass to \(\phi^2\), which has positive trace and the normal form above. The mapping torus of \(\phi^2\) is the degree-two cyclic cover of that of \(\phi\), so \(V(\phi^2)=2V(\phi)\). Directly from the stable-length definition, \(\tau_{\mathcal P}(\phi^2)=2\tau_{\mathcal P}(\phi)\). Dividing the resulting inequality by two proves it for this case also.

The constant is effective and independent of monodromy exponents for this fixed surface. We do not claim it is optimal: the estimate \(\tau\le2n\) can be strict. No unspecified quasi-isometry constants are used to convert this into a Weil–Petersson assertion or a formula for general \((g,n)\); those parts of the question remain outside the proved scope.
