# A dense-graph theorem, with a sharp bipartite calibration

**Claim type: Literature report.** Hilton and Hind prove the total coloring conjecture for every finite simple graph \(G\) satisfying \(\Delta(G)\ge3|V(G)|/4\) [1]. That is, its vertices and edges can be colored with at most \(\Delta+2\) colors so that adjacent vertices, incident edges and an edge with either endpoint receive different colors. This published dense-graph theorem is a substantial external input. The complete bipartite argument below supplies a distinct sharp calibration and does not pretend to prove the dense theorem.

The hypothesis is on **maximum**, not minimum, degree; no regularity or parity hypothesis is inserted. It is not valid to replace it by \(\Delta\ge3|V|/4-O(1)\) without another result. It applies to connected and disconnected graphs as stated. The precise dense threshold is also explicitly restated in the primary research introduction of [2]. We do not infer a latest-best2026 threshold from either source.

Here is a self-contained proof for all bipartite graphs, with no density hypothesis. First a bipartite graph of maximum degree \(\Delta\) has a proper edge coloring with \(\Delta\) colors. To see this, pad its two sides with isolated vertices until both have the same size. While there are deficient vertices on both sides, add an edge between such a pair, allowing parallel added edges, until the graph is \(\Delta\)-regular. The total deficiency on each side is equal, so this process terminates in a regular bipartite multigraph.

In an \(r\)-regular bipartite multigraph with equally sized sides and \(r>0\), any subset \(X\) of one side sends \(r|X|\) edges into \(N(X)\), whose vertices receive at most \(r|N(X)|\) such edges. Thus \(|N(X)|\ge|X|\). Hall's theorem yields a perfect matching. Delete it and repeat for \(r-1\), producing a decomposition into \(\Delta\) matchings. Restricting to the original graph gives the edge coloring. Give the two vertex sides two additional colors. The result is a total \((\Delta+2)\)-coloring. For an edgeless graph the same bound is immediate, with empty sides allowed.

The additive2 is necessary for the unbounded family \(K_{n,n}\), \(n\ge1\). Suppose it had a total coloring with \(n+1\) colors; padding the palette permits this assumption even if fewer colors were used. At each vertex, its own color and the \(n\) incident edge colors are all distinct and exhaust the palette. Fix a color \(c\), and let \(a_c,b_c\) count the vertices of color \(c\) in the two shores. The number of edges of color \(c\), counted from the first shore, is \(n-a_c\), and from the second is \(n-b_c\). Hence \(a_c=b_c\). But every vertex of one shore is adjacent to every vertex of the other, so \(a_c b_c=0\). Therefore both counts vanish for every color, contradicting the presence of vertices. The bipartite upper bound proves
\[
 \chi''(K_{n,n})=n+2=\Delta+2.
\]

The preceding contribution handles trees and complete graphs and the elementary general \(2\Delta+1\) bound. The dense Hilton–Hind class is a distinct, order-unbounded structural theorem; the bipartite proof and palette-incidence obstruction explain why \(\Delta+1\) cannot replace the target even on highly symmetric graphs. The arbitrary sparse, nonbipartite case remains outside this report. No program execution is claimed.
