# Modulo orientations when the odd-degree vertices have stronger local connectivity

**Claim type: Literature report.** Fix \(k\ge1\) and put \(m=2k+1\). Let \(O\) be the odd-degree vertices of a finite loopless multigraph \(G\). If \(O=\varnothing\), or if every two distinct vertices of \(O\) have at least \(6k\) edge-disjoint connecting paths, then \(G\) has an orientation satisfying
\[
 d^-(v)-d^+(v)\equiv0\pmod m\qquad(v\in V(G)). \tag{1}
\]
The condition on \(O\) is equivalent to a bound of \(6k\) on cuts separating members of \(O\). It does not require every cut of \(G\) to have that size.

In particular this settles all prompt instances that are \(4k\)-edge-connected and whose odd-degree vertices meet this additional local hypothesis. It does not reduce the conjecture's global threshold to \(4k\) in general.

## Credited external inputs

Lovász–Thomassen–Wu–Zhang, *Nowhere-zero3-flows and modulo k-orientations*, JCTB103(2013),587–598, proves that every \((3a-3)\)-edge-connected graph has a modulo-\(a\) orientation for odd \(a\ge3\). Substituting \(a=2k+1\) gives the sufficient **\(6k\)** threshold. The exact numerical statement was checked in the primary author-institution abstract; a full author PDF retrieval failed, and no full-source-proof reading is claimed.

We also use Mader's local splitting theorem in the primary research restatement by Bernáth–Iwata–Király–Király–Szigeti, METR2006–62: a vertex of degree at least four incident with no bridge has a pair of incident edges that can be split off preserving all local edge connectivities among the other vertices.

The rest is the complete parity-compression and lifting argument below.

## Reduction to the odd-degree core

An Euler orientation handles each component with no odd-degree vertices: follow Euler circuits, giving integer imbalance zero. By the handshake identity, \(|O|\) is even. If it is nonempty, \(|O|\ge2\), and the local-connectivity hypothesis puts all vertices of \(O\) in one component. Work in that component; the other components have just been handled.

It has no bridge. A bridge splitting \(O\) would violate the \(6k\) bound. If a bridge does not split \(O\), one shore \(X\) has no odd-degree vertex; then
\[
 \sum_{v\in X}d(v)=2|E(G[X])|+1
\]
contradicts the even degree of every vertex in \(X\).

Now eliminate vertices outside the fixed terminal set \(O\). Each has even degree. Delete an isolated one; at degree two suppress its pair of incident edges; at degree at least four apply the local splitting theorem, lowering its degree by two. Discard loops when they arise. Degrees of other vertices are unchanged by a split before loop deletion, and deleting a loop changes degree by two. Thus every nonterminal remains even and every pairwise connectivity within \(O\) remains at least \(6k\). The bridge argument consequently applies again at every stage.

A degree-two suppression preserves the relevant local connectivities directly by replacing the two-edge segment in terminal paths. If both other endpoints coincide, the segment was only a closed excursion and its resulting loop can be removed. Components detached from \(O\) have only even-degree current vertices and can be processed by Euler circuits.

Eventually a multigraph on \(O\) remains. Its every nontrivial cut separates terminals, so it is \(6k\)-edge-connected. Apply the credited modulo-orientation theorem with \(a=2k+1\). Loops, if retained instead of discarded, could be oriented as balanced loops and do not affect the cut condition.

## Tracking all edges, not just a subgraph

To obtain an orientation of the **entire original graph**, retain edge resources throughout the splitting process. A current edge represents a trail of distinct original edges between its current endpoints. Distinct current edges represent disjoint original edge sets. Splitting concatenates two trails through the eliminated vertex. Each removed loop represents a closed trail; save it rather than forgetting its original edges. Any discarded even-degree component can be decomposed into closed trails of its current edges and then expanded likewise.

Orient each final current edge according to the modulo orientation of the core and lift its direction along its trail. At every internal visit of such a directed trail, one edge enters and one leaves, so its contribution to the integer imbalance is zero. At each terminal endpoint it contributes exactly the same unit as its final current edge.

Orient every saved closed trail cyclically. It contributes zero integer imbalance everywhere, including vertices visited more than once. These trails and the final open trails partition the original edge set: splitting only combines resources, and every discarded resource was saved. Consequently every original edge receives exactly one direction.

At each original odd-degree terminal, the total imbalance is the final core imbalance, hence zero modulo \(m\). At every eliminated vertex it is zero as an integer. Restoring the previously handled Eulerian components proves (1).

This is a fixed-unit orientation argument. No arbitrary edge weights or nowhere-zero weighted flow has been substituted for the prompt's unit contributions.

## Relation to earlier work and boundary

Math-1135-A1 proves a fixed-outdegree matching criterion, the all-even case and two-vertex examples. Its remaining obstruction is choosing compatible degree residues globally. The present result permits arbitrary odd-degree core structure subject to stronger terminal-local connectivity, while allowing smaller cuts in even-degree regions. It is a parity-compression deduction from the credited global theorem, rather than merely restating its \(6k\) sufficient threshold.

For example, an Eulerian graph can be attached at a single vertex of a core satisfying the condition; its small cuts do not affect the orientation construction. Conversely the general prompt permits cuts of size \(4k\) separating odd-degree vertices; the hypothesis used here may fail exactly there. No result is claimed for that remaining case.

No finite search or source-algorithm execution is used. The long external theorems are credited; the complete reduction establishes the precise additional scope.
