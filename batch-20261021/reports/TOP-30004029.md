# A precise local-to-global test, including the normalization issue

Fix a dimension$n\ge2$ and$0\le p<1$. Putterman's Theorem1.7, arXiv:1909.03729v2, identifies a condition equivalent to the global origin-symmetric$L_p$ Brunn–Minkowski inequality: for every pair of origin-symmetric convex bodies$K,L$,
\[
|K|\left(n(n-1)V(L[2],K[n-2])
+ (1-p)\int_{S^{n-1}}\frac{h_L^2}{h_K}\,dS_K\right)
\le n(n-p)V(L,K[n-1])^2.
\tag{*}
\]
The quantifier is all$K,L$ in that dimension, not only one ball or one smooth deformation direction. Mixed volumes and the surface-area integral are interpreted in their standard continuous convex-body sense. This is a credited global reduction, not a proof that$(*)$ holds for every$p$.

It is useful to reconcile the source's power-mean version with the literal prompt's geometric mean. For$p>0$, let$G_p(K,L;\lambda)$ be the intersection of halfspaces with upper values$((1-\lambda)h_K^p+\lambda h_L^p)^{1/p}$. The stronger version says
\[
|G_p|^{p/n}\ge(1-\lambda)|K|^{p/n}+\lambda|L|^{p/n}.
\tag{P}
\]
It implies the literal geometric-mean inequality by weighted arithmetic-geometric mean. Conversely suppose the literal inequality holds for every pair and every weight in the fixed dimension. Write$A=|K|$, $B=|L|$,
\[
s=((1-\lambda)A^{p/n}+\lambda B^{p/n})^{1/p},\quad
t=\lambda B^{p/n}/s^p.
\]
Set$K_0=A^{-1/n}K$, $L_0=B^{-1/n}L$. Directly in the halfspace definitions,
\[
G_p(K,L;\lambda)=s\,G_p(K_0,L_0;t).
\]
Both normalized bodies have unit volume, so the assumed geometric-mean inequality gives$|G_p(K_0,L_0;t)|\ge1$, proving$(P)$. At$p=0$ the logarithmic formulation already is the literal one. Thus no mismatch between the two formulations invalidates the theorem's use here.

The source's proof handles the nonsmooth obstruction in a specific way: work with strongly isomorphic polytopes and their varying support numbers, prove concavity on intervals of fixed combinatorial type, control the finitely many transitions, then approximate general symmetric bodies. The simpler smooth argument only applies when every intermediate Wulff body stays$C^2_+$; arbitrary interpolation does not preserve that condition. This is precisely why checking a second variation at selected inputs is insufficient.

It does not replace$(*)$ by its value at$K=L$, where it is equality and contains no information, or claim that a finite list of bodies verifies the all-pairs condition.
