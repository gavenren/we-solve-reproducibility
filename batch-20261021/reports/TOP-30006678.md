# Fixed-exponent lower bounds: the exact Kannan frontier and a closure distinction

Problem: TOP-30006678  
Claim type: Literature report  
Author: /root/hundred_algorithms

**Exact scope.** Precisely credited Kannan theorem: for every fixed k there is a language in Sigma2^P intersect Pi2^P with no O(n^k)-size Boolean circuits. Also explains the distinct fixed-class polynomial bound and the published bounded-query oracle translation. This does not place the lower-bound language in NP or give one language outside P/poly.

Write SIZE(n^k) for languages having circuits of size O(n^k); the multiplicative constant and finite exceptional lengths may depend on the language. The circuit basis is bounded fan-in AND, OR, NOT as in the prompt. Counting all input and constant nodes rather than only internal gates changes size by at most O(n) on this basis; by applying a theorem with a larger fixed exponent, the fixed-polynomial conclusions below survive that convention.

**The exact known result.** Kannan's1982 theorem gives
\[
 \forall k\ \exists L_k\in\Sigma_2^P\cap\Pi_2^P:
                         L_k\notin\operatorname{SIZE}(n^k). \tag{5}
\]
Here k is fixed before selecting the language and its polynomial-time alternating verifier. Kannan's primary1981 MIT report abstract and the1982 journal abstract both explicitly include the intersection. This is a substantial low-hierarchy circuit lower bound, unlike merely selecting hard truth tables in exponential time. Its deep alternating-complexity proof is a credited literature result, not a proof newly supplied here.

Several superficially similar statements are stronger than(5). One cannot swap the first two quantifiers to obtain a single L beating every exponent. One cannot replace Sigma2 intersect Pi2 by NP, since a second-level alternating verifier is not an NP witness verifier. Nor is a lower bound on the time needed to generate circuits a size lower bound: nonuniform circuits may be chosen without any efficient generator. The report imports none of these stronger statements.

**A useful class-wide distinction.** Compare
\[
 \mathcal C\subseteq P/poly
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \exists k\ \mathcal C\subseteq SIZE(n^k).                  \tag{6}
\]
The first lets every language of C have its own polynomial exponent. The second requires one exponent for the whole class, although hidden constants may vary. They are not interchangeable by the definition of P/poly. A complete language with arbitrary polynomial-time reductions does not automatically repair the difference: if reduction output length is n^a, composing its size bound with circuits of exponent k produces an exponent about ak, with a depending on the source language/reduction. Thus NP subset P/poly alone does not supply the second statement of(6) by this elementary completeness argument.

Fortnow, Santhanam and Williams, *Fixed-Polynomial Size Circuit Bounds*, Theorem10, establish a finer class-wide translation: for each fixed constant q (number-of-query exponent), existence of some k with NP subset SIZE(n^k) is equivalent to existence of some k' with P^{NP[n^q]} subset SIZE(n^{k'}). Their proof gives the forward simulation with exponent qk^2 in its stated circuit convention. We use only existence of a polynomial exponent, so changing the node-count convention or adding circuit addressing overhead does not affect the conclusion. The query bound is a fixed power of the original input length. This does not assert the same fixed exponent for unrestricted P^NP, whose machines may use arbitrarily large polynomial query counts and running-time powers.

The contrast is useful when auditing proposed improvements of(5): a theorem about every fixed exponent for a class, a theorem about one common polynomial bound for a class, and a theorem about a single language of superpolynomial size have different quantifier patterns. The bounded-query translation is a nontrivial theorem about those patterns, not permission to drop them.

**What remains missing for the literal target.** A negative answer requires one NP language outside every polynomial-size family. A positive answer requires every NP language to have its own such family. Neither(5) nor the bounded-query equivalence decides these alternatives. A self-contained finite logical analogy makes the first obstruction concrete: the collection of functions n^j, j=1,2,... has no common fixed-degree bound, while each member individually has a polynomial bound. The analogy illustrates the quantifier gap and does not model all properties of a complexity class. No current-status claim stronger than the two specifically checked literature theorems is made.
