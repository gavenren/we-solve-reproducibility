# Pair crossings: the logarithm-free bound and a width obstruction

**Claim type: Literature report.** Oriol Solé Pi proves
\[
 \operatorname{cr}(G)\le C_1\operatorname{pcr}(G)^{3/2},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{cw}(G)\le C_2
 \sqrt{\operatorname{pcr}(G)+\sum_v d(v)^2},
 \tag{1}
\]
for absolute constants, throughout the simple undirected graph class [1, Theorems1.1–1.2]. The first removes the logarithmic loss in the older bound recorded by the preceding attempt. We give a full consequence showing that sparse bounded-degree expanders already require quadratically many distinct crossing edge pairs. These bounds do not prove equality of the two crossing parameters.

The definitions in [1] permit curves with repeated crossings of a pair. Thus the pair parameter in(1) is the prompt's minimum over unrestricted drawings, not a minimum artificially restricted to good drawings. Adjacent edge crossings are not excluded from its definition. A good drawing may be obtained in a redrawing argument after the parameter is measured; one must not put that restriction into its definition.

The cutwidth of \(G\) is the minimum, over all vertex orders, of the maximum number of edges crossing an initial-segment cut. Suppose \(G\) has \(n\ge2\) vertices, maximum degree at most \(d\), and edge expansion \(h>0\), meaning
\[
 |\delta(X)|\ge h|X|
 \quad\text{whenever }1\le |X|\le n/2.
\]
In every vertex order, the first \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) vertices give a cut of at least \(h\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) edges. Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{cw}(G)\ge h\lfloor n/2\rfloor.
\]
Since \(\sum_vd(v)^2\le d^2 n\), the second inequality in(1) implies
\[
 \operatorname{pcr}(G)
 \ge \frac{h^2\lfloor n/2\rfloor^2}{C_2^2}-d^2n.
 \tag{2}
\]
This is an actual finite inequality with one fixed universal source constant. In any family with fixed positive \(h\) and bounded \(d\), it gives \(\operatorname{pcr}(G)=\Omega(n^2)\) once \(n\) is large enough. The trivial straight-line drawing upper bound \(\operatorname{cr}(G)\le\binom{|E|}{2}=O(n^2)\), together with \(\operatorname{pcr}\le\operatorname{cr}\), then gives both parameters of order \(n^2\). In particular allowing repeated crossings cannot make such expanders have only \(O(n)\) **pairs** crossing.

A second source result [1, Theorem1.5] is
\[
 \operatorname{cr}(G)
 \le C_3\log n\left(\operatorname{pcr}(G)+\sum_vd(v)^2\right)
 \quad(n\ge2).
 \tag{3}
\]
If \(\sum_vd(v)^2\le A\operatorname{pcr}(G)\) for a fixed \(A\),(3) gives
\(\operatorname{cr}(G)\le C_3(1+A)\log n\,\operatorname{pcr}(G)\).
For the expander families above the assumption holds eventually, but the direct \(O(n^2)\) upper bound actually improves this to a constant-factor comparison. Without the degree assumption, the additive squared-degree term cannot be discarded. For a star it is of quadratic order while both crossing parameters vanish.

At \(\operatorname{pcr}=0\), the graph has a drawing without crossings and is planar, so \(\operatorname{cr}=0\). For positive \(\operatorname{pcr}\), neither(1) nor(3) forces equality: their constants and superlinear/logarithmic losses remain. In particular numerical rounding at small values does not establish the original conjecture.

The source article is published in2025 and its primary full HTML was read, including exact theorem statements and the drawing conventions. The separator/redrawing proofs are credited rather than fully reproduced. The preceding attempt's cut-vertex additivity and older logarithmic bound do not contain(1) or the quantitative expansion obstruction(2). No drawing optimizer or crossing-count program is claimed.
