# Realizing PD₃ groups with a normal infinite cyclic subgroup

**Problem:** TOP-2836

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** Every finitely presented integral PD₃ group containing a normal Z is the fundamental group of a closed aspherical Seifert 3-manifold, including the source's nonorientable Seifert convention. No realization theorem without the normal-cyclic hypothesis is claimed.

A major realization case of the literal question is known:
\[
 G\text{ is integral }\mathrm{PD}_3,\quad
 \mathbb Z\cong N\triangleleft G
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 G\cong\pi_1(M)
\]
for a closed aspherical Seifert 3-manifold \(M\). The closed-manifold realization is Bowditch's Corollary 0.5. No orientability hypothesis is silently added: Section 15 explicitly uses a general Seifert convention accommodating the nonorientable case.

The normality condition is substantial. An arbitrary infinite-order element only supplies a subgroup \(\mathbb Z\); it does not give the quotient used in the proof. Let \(Q=G/N\). Bowditch's Theorem 15.1 uses the Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence and the duality/finiteness hypotheses to show that \(Q\) is a virtual closed-surface group. The difficult part is recognizing such virtual surface groups from their group-ring cohomology, including eliminating possible torsion-group pathologies. This is a credited deep recognition theorem.

Integral finite cohomological dimension makes \(G\) torsion-free: a finite-order element would give a finite cyclic subgroup with nonzero periodic cohomology in arbitrarily high degrees, contradicting restriction of a finite projective resolution. Bowditch's Lemma 15.3 then realizes the extension geometrically. After absorbing a finite normal subgroup of \(Q\) into the cyclic kernel, the quotient is an orbifold group; torsion-freeness ensures that inverse images of its finite subgroups are cyclic. The construction thickens the orbifold one-skeleton with its circle fibers and fills boundary tori with the required exceptional-fiber slopes. This produces the closed manifold and the exact fundamental group, rather than only a duality complex.

It remains to check the asphericity explicitly requested in the prompt. Pass to a finite-index torsion-free surface subgroup of the orbifold group and, if necessary, further to kill the action on the fiber's orientation. The corresponding finite manifold cover is a circle bundle over a closed aspherical surface. Its fiber and base have contractible universal covers; the homotopy exact sequence gives vanishing of every \(\pi_k\) for \(k\geq2\). Its simply connected universal cover is a CW manifold with all homotopy groups trivial, hence contractible by Whitehead's theorem. The original \(M\) has the same universal cover and is aspherical.

Thus no sphere-base or \(S^2\times\mathbb R\) exception is being hidden under the Seifert terminology. The cohomological orientation character agrees with that of the realizing aspherical manifold because it is the dualizing \(G\)-module; it need not be trivial. What remains outside this theorem is the realization of a general finitely presented integral \(\mathrm{PD}_3\) group without a normal infinite cyclic subgroup. Neither duality alone nor the existence of some cyclic subgroup supplies that missing hypothesis.
