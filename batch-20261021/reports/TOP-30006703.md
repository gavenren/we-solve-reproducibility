# Beyond ACC: quasipolynomial lower bounds with bottom threshold gates

Problem: TOP-30006703  
Claim type: Literature report  
Author: /root/hundred_algorithms

**Exact scope.** Williams arXiv1401.2444v1 Theorem1.3: NEXP has no nonuniform quasipolynomial-size ACC composed with a bottom layer of arbitrary threshold gates; the analogous ACC composed with symmetric gates is also stated. Exact counting-SAT/weak-AND-closure hypotheses are explained. This stronger restricted-class frontier is not NEXP outside unrestricted P/poly.

Fix circuit depth d and modulus m>1 independently of the input length. ACC uses unbounded-fan-in AND, OR, NOT and MOD_m gates; ACC composed with THR means that an ACC circuit receives outputs of bottom gates computing arbitrary linear threshold functions. Quasipolynomial size means n^{(log n)^c} for a fixed c, equivalently2^{(log n)^{O(1)}}. Neither depth, modulus nor the quasipolynomial exponent may vary arbitrarily with the input length.

The precise credited theorem is Ryan Williams, *New algorithms and lower bounds for circuits with linear threshold gates*, arXiv1401.2444v1, masthead10January2014, manuscript13January2014, Theorem1.3. It excludes nonuniform quasipolynomial-size ACC∘THR circuits for NEXP. The adjoining text states the ACC∘SYM analogue, where a bottom symmetric gate may compute an arbitrary function of the Hamming weight of its inputs. This goes beyond merely recalling the earlier ACC polynomial-size lower bound: the bottom layer is substantially richer, and the excluded size regime is quasipolynomial.

**Why ordinary closure cannot be presumed.** The source's Definition2.1 calls a circuit class typical when it admits efficient simulation of composition and unbounded AND, OR and negation. Its SAT-to-lower-bound Theorem2.4 assumes that stronger closure. ACC∘THR is not known to meet it: composing two such circuits gives ACC∘THR∘ACC∘THR, whose intermediate threshold layer has not disappeared. Treating this as ordinary ACC∘THR without a simulation theorem would be an unsupported step.

Theorem2.5 instead assumes only weak closure under AND of two circuits, as precisely defined in Definition2.2, and an algorithm for **counting** satisfying assignments. For every fixed c>=1, there must be epsilon>0 and an algorithm taking O(2^{n-n^epsilon}) time on n-input circuits of size n^{(log n)^c}. The theorem concludes that NEXP lacks quasipolynomial-size circuits of that class. The order of these parameters matters: a speedup only on one fixed size exponent is not the displayed premise.

The source supplies the stronger counting algorithm in Theorem1.2: for every fixed d,m there is epsilon>0 for ACC∘THR circuits of depth d and size2^{n^epsilon}, with time2^{n-n^epsilon}. Every fixed quasipolynomial bound eventually lies below that allowed size, so it supplies the counting premise after harmless parameter weakening. A top AND of two ACC∘THR circuits remains within that form after constant-depth/polynomial-size overhead. The deep algorithms and their lower-bound conversion are source theorems, not claimed newly proved or executed here.

**What is and is not being computed.** The source's all-input evaluation theorem runs in2^n poly(n) and prints2^n output values. Such output-linear evaluation is different from deciding SAT in2^{n-n^epsilon}; the latter saving is obtained by batching partial assignments and symmetric counting gates. One must not discard the2^n output length when citing the former as a subexponential algorithm. Similarly, an arbitrary threshold gate is a mathematical Boolean function with a finite integer-weight realization; the circuit lower-bound theorem concerns gates, not a promise that all enormous weight descriptions can be read in unit bit time. This report imports the source's nonuniform gate-count theorem and does not advertise a bit-time algorithm ignoring supplied coefficient encodings.

**Literal-target boundary.** ACC∘THR and ACC∘SYM remain constant-depth restricted architectures. An unrestricted polynomial-size Boolean circuit may have growing depth and arbitrary interleaving of gates. There is no polynomial-overhead conversion of every such circuit into either class in this argument. Hence a lower bound against these stronger restricted models does not refute NEXP subset P/poly. It does identify a concrete, substantial frontier beyond the previous fixed-exponent diagonalization and plain ACC headline, while retaining the decisive architectural restriction. No “latest known best” claim is needed or made.
