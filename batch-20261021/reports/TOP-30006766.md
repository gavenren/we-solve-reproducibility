# Unconditional simple zeros in every sufficiently large multiplicative window

Claim type: **Literature report**.

The literal target concerns every nontrivial zero, including zeros off the critical line. We report an established unconditional theorem and a quantitative consequence that guarantees many simple critical zeros in each sufficiently large window of a fixed multiplicative width. It supplies no all-zero conclusion.

Let $N(T)$ count all nontrivial zeros with $0<\Im\rho\le T$, **with multiplicity**, and let $S(T)$ count the simple zeros in that region whose real part is$1/2$. Bui–Conrey–Young, published Theorem1.1, prove
\[
 \liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{S(T)}{N(T)}\ge c,
 \qquad c=0.4058.
 \tag{1}
\]
Their separate constant$0.4105$ is for zeros on the line without the simplicity requirement. It must not be substituted for$c$. We read both the arXiv version and the actual published author-hosted PDF. This is an unconditional published baseline, not a claim that it is the latest record.

Here is a useful consequence valid at **every** sufficiently large scale. For each fixed $\lambda>1/c$,
\[
 \liminf_{T\to\infty}
 \frac{S(\lambda T)-S(T)}{T\log T/(2\pi)}
 \ge c\lambda-1>0.
 \tag{2}
\]
In particular, for every fixed $0<\eta<0.2174$ and all sufficiently large$T$,
\[
 \#\{\rho:\Re\rho=1/2,\ T<\Im\rho\le3T,\
                    \zeta'(\rho)\ne0\}
 \ge \eta\,\frac{T\log T}{2\pi}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Thus a long multiplicative window cannot remain devoid of simple zeros even though universal simplicity is unresolved here.

To prove(2), fix$\varepsilon>0$. The definition of liminf in(1) gives
$S(U)\ge(c-\varepsilon)N(U)$ for every sufficiently large$U$; it is not merely a subsequence assertion. Since $S(T)\le N(T)$,
\[
 S(\lambda T)-S(T)\ge(c-\varepsilon)N(\lambda T)-N(T).
\]
The standard Riemann–von Mangoldt asymptotic
$N(U)\sim U\log U/(2\pi)$ gives the lower limit at least
$(c-\varepsilon)\lambda-1$. Let$\varepsilon\downarrow0$. Taking$\lambda=3$ gives$3c-1=0.2174$ and then(3). Strict versus nonstrict height endpoints do not change the asymptotic; alternatively define all counts with the same half-open convention throughout.

The condition$\lambda>1/c$ is a limitation of this deduction, not a sharp assertion about actual simple-zero gaps. A cumulative lower bound at$T$ alone need not yield a positive lower bound in$(T,2T]$: its worst-case subtraction here gives$2c-1<0$. Improving that local conclusion requires additional information, rather than dropping a negative term or treating a liminf as an asymptotic equality.

Another rigorous consequence of(1) concerns possible repeated zeros. Let $M(T)$ count the occurrences, with multiplicity, of zeros of multiplicity at least2 anywhere in the strip up to height$T$. These are disjoint from the zeros counted by$S(T)$, so
\[
 M(T)\le N(T)-S(T),\qquad
 \limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{M(T)}{N(T)}\le0.5942.
 \tag{4}
\]
This deliberately gives an upper bound, not a claimed actual density. The remainder $N-S$ also includes simple off-line zeros, if any. A count of distinct zeros cannot silently replace$M$, since a multiplicity-$m$ zero contributes$m$ to$N$ but1 to a distinct count.

The theorem's mechanism uses a two-piece mollifier and an asymptotic mollified moment. In the published §2, taking the auxiliary differential polynomial to be linear is what yields the simple-zero proportion rather than merely the on-line proportion. Those analytic estimates and the numerical optimization in that paper are credited and were not rerun here.

The prior manuscript gives local reciprocal-integrability and Rouché criteria, an absolute multiplicity-defect test, and describes an RH-conditional proportion and a recent unaudited claim. The present report adds a published unconditional source check and the all-large-scale window conclusion(2)–(3). No recent manuscript claim is used as a premise. Even density one would permit finitely many or a sparse infinite sequence of repeated zeros; the positive bound in(1) is much weaker still. Neither RH nor universal simplicity follows from the result reported.
