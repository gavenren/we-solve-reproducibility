# TOP-126 — An improved digit family and the precise van der Corput barrier

**Claim type: Literature report.** The literal problem asks for one fixed positive power saving for all integer square-difference-free sets. This report supplies an explicit infinite lower family larger than the earlier attempt's digit family, and records a newly posted quantitative theorem together with its matching method limitation. Neither the lower construction nor a limitation on one method disproves the requested power saving.

Write $s(N)$ for the largest size of $A\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}$ with no positive square in $A-A$.

## 1. A complete alternating-digit construction

For $m\ge1$ define
\[
B_m=\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{2m-1}a_j5^j:
a_{2r}\in\{0,2\},\quad a_{2r+1}\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}
\ (0\le r<m)\right\}.
\]
There are exactly $10^m$ choices, and uniqueness of positional expansion makes all the resulting integers distinct. Every element lies between zero and $25^m-1$.

Take unequal $x,y\in B_m$, and let $j$ be their lowest differing digit. Then
\[
x-y=5^j u,\qquad u\not\equiv0\pmod5.
\]
Higher positions make multiples of five after division by $5^j$, so $v_5(x-y)=j$ exactly. If $j$ is odd, this valuation prevents $x-y$ from being a nonzero integer square. If $j$ is even, the first digit difference is $2$ or $-2$, so $u\equiv2$ or $3\pmod5$. Both are quadratic nonresidues; the unit part of a square cannot have this residue. Thus neither sign of a nonzero difference is a square.

Translating by one gives a square-difference-free subset of $[25^m]$ of size $10^m$. Consequently, with
\[
\alpha=\frac{\log10}{\log25},
\qquad s(25^m)\ge (25^m)^\alpha.
\tag{1}
\]
The important change from the earlier attempt is that **all digits in odd positions are free**. An odd least differing position is already excluded by valuation parity, so restricting it to two choices wastes available entropy. The earlier family has only $4^m$ elements at this interval scale; (1) has $10^m$.

The same argument extends to any prime $p\equiv1\pmod4$ and digit set $D\subseteq\{0,\ldots,p-1\}$ whose nonzero differences are nonresidues modulo $p$: use digits in $D$ at even positions and arbitrary digits at odd positions. The count is $(p|D|)^m$ in $[p^{2m}]$ after translation. This general statement is conditional only on the explicitly checkable finite digit-set property; the base-five instance above is fully established.

A power-saving theorem $s(N)\le C N^{1-c}$ would therefore require $c\le1-\alpha$. Indeed, if $c>1-\alpha$, substituting $N=25^m$ would give $N^{\alpha-1+c}\le C$, impossible as $m\to\infty$. No optimality among all constructions is claimed.

## 2. A fresh October 2026 theorem, credited with its status

Fan and Lott's preprint [1, arXiv:2610.07765v2, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2] gives
\[
s(N)\le N\exp\!\left(-c\sqrt{\log N\log\log N}\right)
\tag{2}
\]
for an absolute $c>0$ and sufficiently large $N$. Version 2 was posted on 12 October 2026; its PDF is dated 11 October. This is a preprint theorem being reported with attribution, not a published result or a proof independently reproduced here.

The exact quantitative witness in their theorem is a real cosine polynomial supported on square frequencies at most $N$:
\[
P(\theta)=a_0+\sum_{1\le r\le\sqrt N}a_r\cos(2\pi r^2\theta),
\qquad P\ge0,\quad P(0)=1,
\]
with $a_0\le\exp(-c\sqrt{\log N\log\log N})$. The coefficients $a_r$ may have either sign. Their Theorem 1.3 gives the matching lower bound
\[
a_0\ge\exp(-C\sqrt{\log N\log\log N})
\tag{3}
\]
for every witness of this same form and normalization.

The previously checked Green–Sawhney theorem [2, Theorem 1.1] has loss $\exp(-c\sqrt{\log N})$. Thus (2) improves the quantitative estimate discussed by the earlier archive attempt. Neither estimate is a fixed power saving: $\sqrt{\log N\log\log N}=o(\log N)$.

## 3. Why these witnesses bound sets, including signed coefficients

Here is the finite Fourier deduction, with the no-wrap condition made explicit. Regard $A\subseteq[N]$ as a subset of $\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z$, where $M=2N+1$, and set
\[
S(t)=\sum_{a\in A}e^{2\pi iat/M}.
\]
Orthogonality gives
\[
\frac1M\sum_{t\bmod M}P(t/M)|S(t)|^2=a_0|A|.
\tag{4}
\]
To check this identity, expand both factors. A nonconstant frequency can contribute only when a difference of two elements of $A$ equals a positive or negative square at most $N$, modulo $M$. Its difference from that signed square has absolute value at most $2N-1<M$, so the congruence is an integer equality. Every such contribution vanishes by square-difference-freeness. The constant frequency contributes exactly $a_0|A|$.

All terms on the left side of (4) are nonnegative because $P\ge0$. The term $t=0$ is $|A|^2/M$ because $P(0)=1$. Hence
\[
|A|\le M a_0
\]
for a nonempty set; the empty case is immediate. The factor $M/N\le3$ can be absorbed by decreasing the positive constant in the exponential for large $N$. This proves how a witness theorem supplies (2). Positivity of the individual coefficients was never used; confusing it with positivity of the polynomial would impose an extra restriction.

Conversely, (3) is a limitation of optimizing this particular normalized square-frequency witness. Since its exponent is $o(\log N)$, it forbids $a_0\ll N^{-c}$ for fixed $c>0$ in this class. It does **not** construct a set of size $N^{1-o(1)}$, and it does not forbid a different argument from proving the official target.
