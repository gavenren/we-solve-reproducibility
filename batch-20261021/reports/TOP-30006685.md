# A division-free logspace-uniform parallel algorithm for determinant

Problem: TOP-30006685  
Claim type: Partial result  
Author: /root/hundred_algorithms

**Exact scope.** For matrices over F₂, reconstruct polynomial-size logspace-uniform Boolean circuits of depth O(log²n) for characteristic polynomial, determinant and adjugate; nonsingular systems can consequently be solved in the same depth. For binary integer matrices, explicitly charged arithmetic yields polynomial-size polylogarithmic-depth uniform Boolean circuits. This significant linear-algebra subclass does not establish NC=P.

The construction is the classical Samuelson–Berkowitz method; its historical theorem is credited. We give a deliberately loose polynomial-size construction so no fast matrix-multiplication exponent or ring-division oracle is needed.

For A of order n write A=[[a,R],[S,M]], with M of order n−1. Let q(z)=det(zI−M)=Σ_{j=0}^{n−1}q_j z^{n−1−j}, q₀=1, and put q_j=0 outside that range. Cofactor expansion gives
\[
 p(z)=\det(zI-A)=(z-a)q(z)-R\operatorname{adj}(zI-M)S.
\]
The polynomial adjugate identity, or multiplication by zI−M and the characteristic identity, gives
adj(zI−M)=Σ_{k=0}^{n−2}z^{n−2−k}(M^k+q₁M^{k−1}+⋯+q_k I).
Consequently p_l, the coefficient of z^{n−l}, is
\[
 p_l=q_l-aq_{l-1}-\sum_{j=0}^{l-2}(RM^jS)q_{l-2-j}. \tag{1}
\]
The empty sum is zero; for n=1 this is p=(1,−a). These are polynomial identities with integer coefficients, so remain valid over every commutative ring; their proof does not divide by n!, a matrix pivot or a characteristic-dependent scalar.

Equation (1) is multiplication of the q coefficient vector by a rectangular lower-triangular Toeplitz matrix C whose first column is (1,−a,−RS,−RMS,…,−RM^{n−2}S). Repeat for every trailing principal submatrix. The characteristic coefficient vector is C₀C₁⋯C_{n−1}(1). Embed each rectangular matrix in the upper-left corner of an (n+1)×(n+1) zero matrix; the compatible coefficient coordinates ensure this padded product has the same result in its first n+1 coordinates.

All entries of all C_i are computable in parallel. For each needed power M^j, binary powering uses O(log(n+2)) matrix products of order at most n. With naive matrix multiplication each entry is a balanced sum of n pairwise products, of arithmetic depth O(log(n+2)) and polynomial size. Computing every required power separately is still polynomial total work and depth O(log²(n+2)). Next multiply the n padded C matrices in a balanced tree with the same depth bound. The determinant is (−1)^n times the last characteristic coefficient. Compute all cofactors by n² such determinant circuits simultaneously to get the adjugate, again with polynomial size and the same asymptotic depth.

Over F₂ the ring operations are constant-size bounded-fan-in Boolean circuits. A matrix is nonsingular iff its determinant bit is one, and then A^{-1}=adj(A). Multiplication by a supplied vector solves the unique linear system in O(log n) further depth. No rank formula for a singular matrix is inferred from its characteristic polynomial.

**Uniformity and bit model.** Every gate has a label comprising the trailing-submatrix index, matrix-power exponent and binary-power stage, product-tree position, row/column and balanced-sum position. These use O(log(n+2)) bits each; a fixed number of counters prints their polynomially many gates and predecessor labels in logarithmic workspace. Gate basis, matrix dimensions and the chosen naive powering schedules are fixed effective constructions, so this is logspace uniform, not an existential nonuniform balancing theorem.

For integer entries with at most L input bits in total, no intermediate integer is unbounded at unit cost. Powers have exponent at most n, so their entries have polynomial bit length. A crude expansion of an intermediate C-product as at most (n+1)^n products of at most n entries, each bounded by (nH)^{n+1} for H=max(1,max|A_ij|), gives O(n²(log H+log(n+2))) bits. This also bounds the simpler power/dot-product stages up to polynomial slack. Standard uniform binary addition and schoolbook multiplication circuits on B-bit integers have polynomial size and depth O(log²(B+2)); sign bits can be handled separately. A uniform family can reserve the larger B=O(n²(L+log(n+2))) bit width from the encoded dimensions and length alone; its wiring does not depend on inspecting H. Standard padded dense matrix encodings are used. Replacing the arithmetic gates therefore gives polynomial-size Boolean circuits of depth O(log²(n+2)log²(B+2)), polylogarithmic in the actual encoded input length. This is a conservative bit-depth bound, not a unit-cost real arithmetic claim.

The earlier reports parallelize affine update chains; their determinant difficulty is not eliminated just by multiplying a supplied sequence of matrices. Here the recursively defined characteristic coefficients are converted into an explicitly constructible balanced product without division, even in characteristic two. General Boolean circuit value is not reduced to this linear-algebra subclass, so no full NC=P consequence follows.
