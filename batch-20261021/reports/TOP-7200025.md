# Exact constants and the finite closed-square convention

Bereg–Dumitrescu–Jiang prove the following credited interval for the literal constant:
\[
\frac{9}{(\sqrt{46}+2)^2}\le c_\square\le
\frac14-\frac1{384}=\frac{95}{384}<\frac14.
\]
Their stronger numerical lower bound is not needed here: the displayed lower constant has a simple exact algebraic specification and an explicit algorithm. It improves the plain greedy$1/9$ guarantee without declaring the exact optimum known.

The lower bound is their Theorem1, algorithmA1, specialized to dimension two. Its parameter$\lambda$ satisfies
\[
9-\tfrac12(\sqrt\lambda-2)^2=\lambda,\qquad
\sqrt\lambda=(\sqrt{46}+2)/3.
\]
The algorithm sometimes takes two disjoint squares instead of the current largest square; its analysis charges the union removed in a round to at most$\lambda$ times the area selected. Summing those charges yields selected area at least union area divided by$\lambda$. This geometric charging theorem is the imported result.

There is a small but useful algebraic verification of the selected root. Writing$t=\sqrt\lambda$ gives$3t^2-4t-14=0$. The positive root is$(2+\sqrt{46})/3$, lying between$5/2$ and3, hence$\lambda$ lies in the required interval$[(5/2)^2,9]$. Squaring the wrong root or replacing$\lambda$ by an unproved decimal approximation could change the guaranteed constant.

The source's Theorem5 and Section6 construct a periodic arrangement with local selected-to-union ratio at most$190/768=95/384$. The extremal definition here permits only finite families. The source explicitly takes larger finite square patches: bulk area and selected weight are quadratic in patch size, while the boundary correction is linear, yielding ratios at most$95/384+o(1)$. The infimum is therefore at most$95/384$ even if no finite patch attains the limiting ratio. Rescaling such a patch to union area one changes every selected and union area by the same factor.

Closed boundaries are consistent with this transfer. In the source's upper construction touching squares count as intersecting; alternatively its small-enlargement limit enforces interior intersections. One cannot select all four unit squares meeting at a common vertex under the literal rule. The source lower selection also removes every square intersecting a selected one, so its output obeys closed-set disjointness.

The paper gives$O(n^2)$ geometric operations forA1 in dimension two. This is not automatically a constant-bit-cost bound. For rational coordinates and sides of full encoded length$L$, its finite arithmetic/comparison implementation has polynomial bit overhead; comparisons with the fixed algebraic$\lambda$ can be made exactly by sign tests and squaring with signs recorded.
