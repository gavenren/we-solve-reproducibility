# A terminating abstract-isomorphism algorithm on all right-angled inputs

Claim type: **Literature report**.

On the unbounded class of **right-angled Coxeter input pairs**, the positive semidecision procedure in the earlier attempt can be replaced by a genuine terminating decision algorithm. The decisive input is the published rigidity theorem of Radcliffe[1, Theorem5.4], not a presumption that abstract group isomorphisms preserve the displayed generators.

For a finite Coxeter matrix$M$ whose off-diagonal entries all belong to$\{2,\infty\}$, form the finite simple **commutation graph**$\Gamma_M$: its vertices are the generators and an edge means$m_{st}=2$. This edge convention is explicit because Coxeter diagrams often suppress precisely those edges. The Coxeter presentation becomes the graph product of one$C_2$at every vertex. Each vertex group is finite, nontrivial and directly indecomposable.

Radcliffe's theorem states that two finite-graph products of finite directly indecomposable vertex groups are isomorphic as groups only if their labeled graphs are isomorphic. It is a theorem about abstract group isomorphism, not merely an isomorphism that happens to match vertex groups in advance. Specializing all labels to$C_2$therefore gives
\[
 W(M)\cong W(N)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Gamma_M\cong\Gamma_N. \tag{1}
\]
The reverse implication also has a direct proof: a graph isomorphism sends each involutory generator to its matched generator, preserves every commutation relation, and its inverse defines an inverse homomorphism.

Here is a complete algorithm. First verify that both input matrices have the specified entries. This is the scope condition, not a rule returning a negative answer outside the class. Compare their vertex counts. If equal to$r$, enumerate all$r!$bijections, testing for every unordered vertex pair whether it is an edge on both sides. Accept on the first edge-preserving bijection; reject after the finite list is exhausted. For$r=0$the empty graph is the trivial group and the same convention works. Each test needs at most$\binom r2$comparisons, so even this elementary algorithm has a finite bound$O(r!r^2)$on Boolean adjacency tests. No claim of optimal graph-isomorphism complexity is needed.

Correctness in the positive case follows from the explicit generator maps. In the negative case the finite certificate says that every vertex bijection violates an adjacency; by the rigidity theorem this excludes **all**abstract group isomorphisms, including those represented by long words unrelated to the displayed generators. This is what the earlier dovetailed word search lacked: a terminating negative procedure on an infinite input class.

A coarse invariant cannot substitute for (1). Every right-angled input on$r$vertices has abelianization$(C_2)^r$, regardless of its edges. For instance the empty and complete graphs on$r\ge2$have the same abelianization but give respectively a free product of$r$copies of$C_2$and a finite elementary abelian group. The first is infinite: its retraction onto two factors contains the infinite dihedral group, with alternating reduced words of arbitrarily large length. The second has order$2^r$. Their inequivalence is detected by the adjacency test. More subtle equal-rank graphs need the full rigidity input rather than only a group-order distinction.

The graph-product theorem does not say that every automorphism sends each standard generator to a standard generator. For example partial conjugations can give other generator images. It says the **existence**of an abstract isomorphism forces the defining labeled graphs to match. Nor does the algorithm decide a pair when one supplied Coxeter matrix has a finite braid label greater than2, even if that group might admit some other presentation in the right-angled class. Recognizing that broader class from arbitrary presentations is not supplied here.

The earlier TOP-1192-A1 was read in full: it proves odd-edge abelianization, rank-two recognition and a positive semidecision procedure for arbitrary finite presentations. It does not prove the all-rank right-angled rigidity theorem or the terminating rejection algorithm above. The additional scope here is entirely credited to published graph-product rigidity; no new general Coxeter isomorphism algorithm is claimed.
