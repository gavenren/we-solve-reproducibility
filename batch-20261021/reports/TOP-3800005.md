# Real specializations of the all-field incidence theorem

Milojević, Sudakov and Tomon, arXiv:2401.06670v2, Theorem1.1 proves that$N$ points and$N$ hyperplanes over any field, with a$K_{s,s}$-free incidence graph, have
\[
 I\le C(s+d^3)N^{\,2-1/\lceil(d+1)/2\rceil}
\]
for an absolute implied constant in the displayed theorem. In particular it applies over$\mathbb R$. With$s=d$ and odd$d$, the exponent is$2-2/(d+1)$; for even$d$ it is$2-2/(d+2)$. The latter is weaker than the real-geometric exponent reported in the paper's introduction. This parity distinction must not be suppressed.

The paper's matching constructions are over fields allowed to depend on the parameters, with forbidden size$s=s(d)$ also allowed to depend on the dimension. Those quantifiers establish neither a real lower construction nor sharpness for the specific forbidden size$s=d$. Thus the theorem is a verified upper-bound contribution to the distinguished quantity, not its exact determination. The full piecewise unbalanced theorem is not needed here.

For comparison, the following explicit real construction supplies a lower bound without any field transfer. For an integer$q\ge1$, take
\[
 P=\{1,\ldots,q\}\times\{1,\ldots,2q^2\},\qquad
 \mathcal L=\{y=ax+b:1\le a\le q,\ 1\le b\le q^2\}.
\]
Each of the$q^3$ lines contains exactly$q$ listed points, because$2\le ax+b\le2q^2$ throughout the prescribed$x$ range. There are$2q^3$ points and$q^4$ incidences. Distinct affine lines cannot share two distinct points, so the incidence graph is$K_{2,2}$-free. Add$q^3$ distinct lines missing this finite point set to balance both sides at$N=2q^3$. Hence
\[
 F_2(N,N;2,2)\ge q^4=2^{-4/3}N^{4/3}
\]
on this infinite subsequence. Padding both parts by generic nonincident objects extends an order-$N^{4/3}$ lower bound to all sufficiently large$N$.

The same concrete incidence graph is realizable in every$d\ge2$: embed these points in the first two coordinates and replace each line by its product with$\mathbb R^{d-2}$. The resulting objects are distinct affine hyperplanes, and incidences with the embedded points are exactly unchanged. The graph remains$K_{2,2}$-free and hence$K_{d,d}$-free. This gives a real$N^{4/3}$ lower family in the distinguished problem in every dimension, although it does not match the higher-dimensional upper exponents.

No general-position hypothesis beyond the stated forbidden graph is added: the points in the lifted construction are deliberately coplanar. There are no earlier archive contributions listed.
