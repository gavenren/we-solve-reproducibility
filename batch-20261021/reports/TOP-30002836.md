# An explicit closure class of strongly local VOAs and a precise reconstruction criterion

Claim type: **Literature report**.

A large class answering both directions of the question is provided by Carpi–Kawahigashi–Longo–Weiner (CKLW), *From Vertex Operator Algebras to Conformal Nets and Back*, arXiv:1503.01260v4 (Memoirs AMS254(2018),no.1213). Define $\mathscr C$ to be the smallest class of simple unitary VOAs containing all unitary Virasoro VOAs and all simple affine VOAs at positive integral levels, and closed under finite tensor products and **unitary subalgebras in CKLW's sense**. Then every member of $\mathscr C$ is strongly local. This includes its unitary orbifold and coset subalgebras; the adjective unitary is not dropped from the closure operation.

Here are the precise theorem inputs and why they propagate through the entire class. CKLW Theorem8.3 says that a simple unitary VOA generated by $V_1\cup F$, with $F\subset V_2$ a family of quasiprimary, PCT-invariant Virasoro vectors, is strongly local. A unitary Virasoro VOA is generated by its conformal vector, and a simple unitary affine VOA is generated by $V_1$. These are Examples8.4 and8.7 respectively. The resulting nets are the Virasoro net and the corresponding positive-level loop-group net. Corollary8.2 states
\[
 A_{V\otimes W}=A_V\otimes A_W
\]
for simple unitary strongly local $V,W$. Theorem7.1 says every unitary subalgebra of such a $V$ is again strongly local. Structural induction on a finite expression using these two operations proves the assertion for every member of $\mathscr C$; this is an infinite closure class, not a list of checked models.

The analytic distinction is substantive. On the Hilbert completion $\mathcal H$, energy bounds have the form
\[
 \|a_n b\|\le M(1+|n|)^s\|(L_0+1)^k b\|
\]
on finite-energy vectors. Rapid decrease of Fourier coefficients then permits smearing against smooth functions and controls the operators on the smooth-energy domain. But algebraic locality as a formal identity, or commutation of unbounded operators on a common invariant core, does **not** by itself imply commutation of their spectral projections. Strong locality is the assertion that the generated von Neumann algebras for disjoint intervals commute. CKLW's generation criterion supplies that extra analytic step. It would therefore be invalid to apply formal VOA commutativity alone to all unitary VOAs and declare the first question solved.

Within this class the net retains the vertex-algebra information. Theorem9.2 identifies the Fredenhagen–Jörß smeared fields reconstructed from $A_V$ with the original smeared vertex operators, for every simple unitary strongly local $V$. Thus reconstructing an element of $\mathscr C$ from its associated net returns that VOA. Theorem7.5 further makes $W\mapsto A_W$ a one-to-one correspondence between unitary subalgebras of $V$ and Möbius covariant subnets of $A_V$, with their natural Hilbert-space restrictions. Hence taking a subnet of a net in this image does not lose the vertex-algebra description within the theorem's hypotheses.

There is also an exact conditional answer to the reverse question for nets not initially supplied by a VOA. Theorem9.3 assumes an irreducible conformal net $A$ generated by a family $F$ of quasiprimary vectors in the sense of its equation(134), invariant under PCT ($\theta F=F$). It requires energy bounds for every corresponding FJ field and requires
\[
 \dim\ker(L_0-n)<\infty\quad\text{for every integer }n\ge0.
\]
Under these hypotheses the finite-energy space admits a simple unitary strongly local VOA structure with $A_V=A$. Generation here means generation of each local von Neumann algebra by the prescribed interval-supported FJ fields, not merely density of a collection of state vectors. Finite multiplicities and the energy bounds are separate assumptions, not consequences of irreducibility.

The result supplies a concrete, operation-stable infinite family and a rigorous reverse criterion. The unrestricted claims remain outside its scope: no assertion that all unitary VOAs satisfy the needed analytic estimates, no assertion that every irreducible net meets the reconstruction hypotheses, and no tensor-category or complete-rationality theorem is inferred from strong locality alone.
