# The full planar theorem and an entropy-to-volume transfer

For arbitrary origin-symmetric planar convex bodies$K,L$ and$0<\lambda<1$, the literal logarithmic Wulff combination satisfies
\[
|W_\lambda|\ge |K|^{1-\lambda}|L|^\lambda.
\]
Böröczky, Lutwak, Yang and Zhang's Theorem1.3 proves this with equality exactly when$K,L$ are dilates or are parallelograms with parallel sides. This includes rotated, nonsmooth and nonunconditional planar inputs; a shared coordinate reflection structure is not required.

The theorem is established mathematics credited to its authors. The earlier exploratory attempt proves boxes, a shared-unconditional case, product closure and a disk second variation, but does not supply the full planar equality classification or the global entropy mechanism below. The other archive record summarizes an unverified all-dimensional claim; it is not used.

Write$d\overline V_K=h_K\,dS_K/(2|K|)$ for the cone-volume probability measure. The source's Theorem1.4 proves
\[
\int_{S^1}\log(h_L/h_K)\,d\overline V_K
\ge\tfrac12\log(|L|/|K|),
\]
with the same equality alternatives. It is a global inequality for every pair, not the nonpositivity of one second derivative at a disk. Here is a direct useful implication that also respects the Wulff definition.

Let$M=W_\lambda$. Its defining halfspaces imply
$h_M(u)\le h_K(u)^{1-\lambda}h_L(u)^\lambda$ for every$u$; equality is not assumed. Apply the entropy inequality with base$M$, first to$K$ and then to$L$, and add with weights$1-\lambda,\lambda$:
\[
\tfrac12\big((1-\lambda)\log|K|+\lambda\log|L|-\log|M|\big)
\le\int\log\frac{h_K^{1-\lambda}h_L^\lambda}{h_M}\,d\overline V_M.
\]
This inequality alone has the wrong direction to conclude the target. The additional contact property is essential: the surface-area measure of a Wulff shape is supported where its support function equals its prescribed positive continuous defining function. Thus the last integral is zero, and the desired lower bound follows.

For clarity, that contact property can be justified without assuming the prescribed function is itself a support function. At a boundary point with unique outer normal$u$, failure of the defining inequality to be active at$u$ would force the supporting boundary there to arise from a different active normal. For a unique normal that is impossible. Almost every boundary point has a unique normal with respect to surface area; at any active defining direction its value equals the support value. Equivalently it is the standard Wulff first-variation contact lemma used in the source. One must not replace the integral by an arbitrary nonnegative number and infer a lower bound.

The full equality classification is imported from the source's planar proof, not inferred from this implication alone. At$\lambda=0,1$ equality is automatic and those interior equality alternatives are not imposed.
