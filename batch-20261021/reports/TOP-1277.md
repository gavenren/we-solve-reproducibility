# TOP-1277 — Recovering the zero-ordinate information from the literal interval formulation

**Claim type: Conditional result.** Assuming exactly the positive-separation pair-correlation statement in the official prompt, the argument below proves that asymptotically all zeros are simple and on the critical line. The analytical input is a precisely credited unconditional averaged identity. The important additional step is to obtain the missing information at separation zero from that identity and positivity, rather than assuming a stronger version of the conjecture near zero.

Goldston, Lee, Schettler and Suriajaya [1, Theorem 1] establish the simple-and-critical conclusion from their cumulative PCC. This report does not claim that theorem as new. It supplies the passage from the archive's fixed intervals $0<a<b$ to the required horizontal-multiplicity conclusion. The earlier attempt correctly warns that the literal statement excludes zero; its Fourier perturbation and sufficient full-measure package do not make this passage.

## 1. Definitions and the credited unconditional input

As in the prompt, zeros are counted with multiplicities and $N(T)$ counts those with $0<\gamma\le T$. Put $L=\log T/(2\pi)$ and
\[
Q_T(u)=\#\{0<\gamma,\gamma'\le T:0<(\gamma'-\gamma)L\le u\}.
\]
The order of the two ordinates can be reversed without changing the positive-separation count. Let the distinct positive ordinates up to $T$ have total horizontal multiplicities $m_\gamma$, including every zero on that horizontal line and its analytic multiplicity, and put
\[
S_T=\sum_{\gamma\ {\rm distinct}}m_\gamma^2.
\]
Thus $N(T)=\sum m_\gamma$ and $S_T\ge N(T)$.

The unconditional formula [1, equation (6.2)] is
\[
S_T+\frac2\lambda\int_0^\lambda Q_T(u)\,du
=TL\left\{\lambda+\frac{\log(2+\lambda)}{\pi^2\lambda}
+O\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{\log(2+\lambda)}}{\lambda}\right)\right\}
\tag{1}
\]
for $0<\lambda\le\sqrt L$. It comes from their Propositions 1–2 and retains all same-ordinate pairs. We use it as an external theorem, not as an elementary identity newly proved here. It does not assume RH or PCC. Also $N(T)/(TL)\to1$ by the Riemann–von Mangoldt formula.

For fixed $\lambda$, let
\[
E(\lambda)=\frac{\log(2+\lambda)}{\pi^2\lambda}
+O\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{\log(2+\lambda)}}{\lambda}\right);
\]
the implied bound in (1) is absolute, and $E(\lambda)\to0$ as $\lambda\to\infty$. Every fixed $\lambda$ eventually satisfies the range of (1).

## 2. Use only intervals bounded away from zero

Write $g(u)=1-(\sin(\pi u)/(\pi u))^2$. The literal conjecture gives, for fixed $0<a<b$, the normalized pair count in $[a,b]$ tending to $\int_a^b g(u)\,du$.

It also implies that the normalized mass at a single fixed $a>0$ tends to zero. For any $0<\eta<a$, that mass is bounded above by the count in $[a-\eta,a+\eta]$. Take the limsup in $T$ and then let $\eta\downarrow0$; continuity of $g$ makes the limiting bound zero. We can therefore use either open or closed fixed positive endpoints.

Fix $0<\varepsilon<\lambda$. For $\varepsilon<u\le\lambda$ the literal assumption yields
\[
\frac{Q_T(u)-Q_T(\varepsilon)}{N(T)}
\longrightarrow\int_\varepsilon^u g(v)\,dv.
\tag{2}
\]
Because $Q_T$ is nonnegative and increasing,
\[
\int_0^\lambda\frac{Q_T(u)}{N(T)}\,du
\ge\int_\varepsilon^\lambda
\frac{Q_T(u)-Q_T(\varepsilon)}{N(T)}\,du.
\]
The integrand on the right is nonnegative. Fatou's lemma and (2) now give
\[
\liminf_{T\to\infty}\int_0^\lambda\frac{Q_T(u)}{N(T)}\,du
\ge\int_\varepsilon^\lambda(\lambda-v)g(v)\,dv.
\]
Letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ proves the lower bound
\[
\liminf_{T\to\infty}\int_0^\lambda\frac{Q_T(u)}{N(T)}\,du
\ge\int_0^\lambda(\lambda-v)g(v)\,dv.
\tag{3}
\]
No bound on $Q_T(\varepsilon)$ as $\varepsilon$ shrinks with $T$ has been assumed. Only a lower bound on the full integral is needed.

## 3. The averaged identity forces minimal horizontal multiplicity

Divide (1) by $N(T)$ and use (3). Positivity in (1) bounds $S_T/N(T)$ for fixed $\lambda$, so the harmless factor $TL/N(T)\to1$ is legitimate. We obtain
\[
\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{S_T}{N(T)}
\le
\lambda-\frac2\lambda\int_0^\lambda(\lambda-v)g(v)\,dv+E(\lambda).
\]
The constant part of $g$ cancels $\lambda$, leaving
\[
\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{S_T}{N(T)}
\le2\int_0^\lambda\left(1-\frac v\lambda\right)
\left(\frac{\sin\pi v}{\pi v}\right)^2\,dv+E(\lambda).
\tag{4}
\]
The squared sinc is integrable with integral $1/2$ over the positive half-line. For example this follows from Plancherel applied to the indicator of $[-1/2,1/2]$ with Fourier convention $e^{-2\pi iuv}$. Dominated convergence shows that the integral term in (4) tends to one as $\lambda\to\infty$. Since $E(\lambda)\to0$ and $S_T/N(T)\ge1$,
\[
S_T=(1+o(1))N(T).
\tag{5}
\]
The order of limits matters: first take $T\to\infty$ for each fixed $\lambda$ and $\varepsilon$, then $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, then $\lambda\to\infty$. There is no unjustified uniform PCC estimate at a moving endpoint.

## 4. From (5) to simple zeros on the critical line

The number of zero copies belonging to horizontal groups of size at least two is at most
\[
\sum_{m_\gamma\ge2}m_\gamma(m_\gamma-1)
=S_T-N(T)=o(N(T)).
\]
All remaining groups contain exactly one simple zero. That zero must lie on the critical line: otherwise the functional equation and conjugation produce a distinct zero $1-\overline\rho$ at the same positive ordinate, contradicting a group size of one. Therefore $(1-o(1))N(T)$ zeros are simultaneously simple and critical.

This does not prove that every zero is simple, or that every zero is critical. A density-zero exceptional set remains compatible with the conclusion. Nor is any nearest-neighbor gap distribution established.
