# A quantified hard-core-bit reduction with explicit list reconstruction

Problem: TOP-3403  
Claim type: Partial result  
Author: /root/hundred_algorithms

**Exact scope.** Conditional on an arbitrary polynomial-time one-way family f, the padded family (x,r)->(f(x),r) remains one-way and the inner product <x,r> mod2 is a hard-core bit. An explicit polynomial list-reconstruction reduction is proved with the any-preimage convention; this is the established Goldreich–Levin theorem, not an unconditional construction of one-way functions.

Assume f_n is a one-way family in the prompt's exact eventual-negligibility sense. Give the padded family inputs (x,r) of length2n, with r uniform in {0,1}^n, and output g(x,r)=(f_n(x),r). This even-length parameterization is explicit. Extra copied bits can define odd lengths if a family on every input length is desired. An inverter for g immediately gives one for f by choosing a uniform r, calling that inverter, and keeping the returned x-component. Copying r in the output ensures that a valid returned preimage has the correct r. Thus g is one-way, with polynomial parameter rescaling.

Let a predictor A(y,r) output a bit, with fresh independent internal coins for each invocation. Suppose its success at predicting x dot r is at least1/2+epsilon on infinitely many n, where epsilon>=1/poly(n). A positive inverse-polynomial lower bound may be fixed in the reduction; it need not be inferred by the inverter from a particular sample. For fixed x, put
\[
 \rho_x=\mathbb E_{r,A}(-1)^{A(f_n(x),r)+x\cdot r}.
\]
Then E_x rho_x>=2epsilon. Since rho_x<=1, the set of x with rho_x>=epsilon has measure at least epsilon (indeed at least epsilon/(1-epsilon) when epsilon<1). We reconstruct those x with constant probability, using only y=f_n(x).

**An explicit, deliberately nonoptimal decoder.** Choose ell so that M=2^ell-1>=8n/epsilon^2 and M is less than twice that bound plus1. Sample independent uniform vectors r_1,...,r_ell. For every nonempty subset S of [ell], form r_S=the XOR of its selected vectors. Distinct nonempty S,T give pairwise independent uniform r_S,r_T: their distinct nonzero coefficient vectors are linearly independent over F_2. Their shifted versions r_S+e_j have the same property for each coordinate j.

Query A(y,r_S+e_j) for every S and j, using independent coins, and retain these answers. Enumerate all2^ell possible vectors of guessed bits b_i. For each such guess, recover a candidate z coordinatewise by majority of
\[
 A(y,r_S+e_j)\ \oplus\!\bigoplus_{i\in S}b_i,
 \qquad\varnothing\ne S\subseteq[ell].                     \tag{4}
\]
For the one guess b_i=x dot r_i, each vote equals x_j with mean success at least(1+epsilon)/2. For a fixed j the votes are pairwise independent, so their sum has variance at most M/4. Chebyshev gives probability at most1/(M epsilon^2) that its majority is wrong. Union over all n coordinates bounds the probability of any wrong coordinate by1/8. Ties may be broken arbitrarily. Thus with probability at least7/8 the list of candidates contains the original x. Verify each candidate by evaluating f_n(z)=y, returning the first that works. Any valid preimage is acceptable, so collisions in f do not invalidate the reduction.

The number of candidates is M+1=O(n/epsilon^2). There are nM predictor calls. Straight enumeration of all guesses and votes uses O(nM^2) elementary bit/parity operations apart from the polynomial costs of vector formation and A and f. Thus the running time and list length are polynomial in n and1/epsilon. Combining the good-x fraction with reconstruction gives inversion success at least7epsilon/8, an inverse-polynomial quantity on infinitely many lengths, contradicting the one-way definition. Consequently every PPT predictor has negligible positive advantage. Flipping a predictor's output handles negative correlation when an absolute-correlation formulation is used.

This reduction supplies a concrete cryptographic consequence of an assumed one-way function and exposes why a single bit can retain hardness even though the inverse task allows any preimage. It supplies no hardness assumption by itself. Its polynomial bounds are intentionally loose; no optimal security exponent or implementation timing is claimed. The nontrivial hard-core-bit theorem and reconstruction idea are due to Goldreich and Levin; the present exposition spells out a pairwise-independent majority decoder and its exact distributional quantifiers.
