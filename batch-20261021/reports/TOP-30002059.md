# The defining-characteristic Quillen complex of GL_n: a uniform noncontractibility proof

Claim type: **Partial result**.

For every prime power $q=p^f$ and every $n\ge2$, set $G=\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb F_q)$. Then
\[
 |\mathcal A_p(G)|\simeq |\mathcal B_n(q)|,
 \qquad
 \widetilde\chi(\mathcal A_p(G))=(-1)^nq^{\binom n2}\ne0, \tag{1}
\]
where $\mathcal B_n(q)$ is the poset of nonzero proper subspaces of $\mathbb F_q^n$. Consequently the hypothesis of Quillen's assertion never holds in this entire trivial-$p$-core family. We prove(1), including the homotopy reduction and exact integer obstruction; it is a classical defining-characteristic case, not a new solution of the conjecture.

We use the finite-poset fiber lemma: an order map $f:X\to Y$ is a homotopy equivalence if every lower fiber $f^{-1}(Y_{\le y})$ is contractible. This is the poset form of Quillen's TheoremA, checked in Weibel IV.3.7. One elementary proof forms the mapping-cylinder poset $M=X\sqcup Y$ with $x\le y$ when $f(x)\le y$. The inclusion of $Y$ is a homotopy equivalence by the retraction extending$f$ and its pointwise comparison with identity. Delete the vertices of$Y$ from$M$ in increasing order. The lower link at the deleted vertex is precisely its contractible lower fiber; the full link is its join with the upper link, hence contractible. Coning off a contractible link preserves homotopy type. The inclusion of$X$ is therefore also a homotopy equivalence, proving the lemma. The pointwise-comparison homotopies here are the usual prism homotopies of order complexes.

Let $\mathcal S_p(H)$ denote all nontrivial $p$-subgroups of a finite group$H$. For a nontrivial $p$-group$P$, let $E=\Omega_1 Z(P)$, a nontrivial central elementary abelian subgroup. The maps
\[
 U\ \le UE\ \ge E\qquad(U\in\mathcal A_p(P))
\]
contract $\mathcal A_p(P)$: centrality ensures$UE$ remains elementary abelian. These are exactly the lower fibers of the inclusion $\mathcal A_p(H)\hookrightarrow\mathcal S_p(H)$, so the fiber lemma gives a homotopy equivalence for every$H$. If$H$ has a nontrivial normal $p$-subgroup$N$, the analogous zigzag $P\le PN\ge N$ contracts $\mathcal S_p(H)$. We use this on auxiliary groups only; it does not reverse the general conjecture.

Write $V=\mathbb F_q^n$. For nontrivial$P\le G$, the fixed space$V^P$ is nonzero: orbits of$P$ on the finite set$V$ have sizes powers of$p$, so $|V^P|\equiv|V|=0\pmod p$; the fixed identity implies at least$p$ fixed vectors. It is proper because the action of$G$ on$V$ is faithful. Thus
\[
 f:\mathcal S_p(G)\longrightarrow\mathcal B_n(q)^{\mathrm{op}},\qquad P\longmapsto V^P
\]
is an order map. For a nonzero proper subspace$W$, its lower fiber consists exactly of the nontrivial$p$-subgroups of the pointwise stabilizer$H_W$ of$W$. Choose $V=W\oplus W'$. Then
\[
 H_W=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}I&B\\0&C\end{pmatrix}: C\in\mathrm{GL}(W'),\ B\in\operatorname{Hom}(W',W)\right\}
\]
has the nontrivial normal elementary abelian$p$-subgroup of matrices with$C=I$. The preceding contraction makes every such fiber contractible. Hence$f$ is a homotopy equivalence. Reversing a poset reverses its chains but leaves the order complex isomorphic, establishing the first part of(1).

For completeness the building's Euler characteristic can be calculated without importing the Solomon–Tits wedge theorem. Let $\mu_n=\mu(0,V)$ be the Möbius function of its full subspace lattice. Expanding the Möbius recurrence as alternating chains shows $\mu_n=\widetilde\chi(\mathcal B_n(q))$. Since the number of$j$-subspaces is the Gaussian coefficient ${n\brack j}_q$,
\[
 \mu_0=1,\qquad \sum_{j=0}^n{n\brack j}_q\mu_j=0\quad(n>0).
\]
The finite Gaussian identity
\[
 \prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(1-q^it)
 =\sum_{j=0}^n(-1)^j q^{j(j-1)/2}{n\brack j}_q t^j
\]
follows inductively by multiplying the last factor and using the Gaussian recurrence. At$t=1$ the product is zero for$n>0$. Induction in the displayed Möbius recurrence therefore gives $\mu_n=(-1)^nq^{\binom n2}$, proving(1). Nonzero reduced Euler characteristic of this finite complex excludes contractibility and implies nonzero rational reduced homology in some degree.

Finally $O_p(G)=1$ directly. If a normal$p$-subgroup$N$ were nontrivial, its fixed space would be nonzero and $G$-invariant by normality. The natural$G$-module has no proper nonzero invariant subspace, since$G$ acts transitively on nonzero vectors. Hence$V^N=V$, forcing$N=1$ by faithfulness, contradiction. For$n=1$, the order of$G$ is prime to$p$ and the complex is empty, which is likewise not contractible under the prompt's convention.
