# An explicit pencil for first derivatives of products of linear forms

Let$\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_m$ be real homogeneous linear forms on$\mathbb R^d$, with$m\ge2$ and$\ell_i(e)>0$ at a direction$e$. Put$f=\prod_i\ell_i$ and$p=D_e f$. The closed hyperbolicity cone of$p$ at$e$ has an explicit homogeneous LMI of size$m-1$, in the original coordinates and without auxiliary variables. This proves direct spectrahedrality for an infinite family in arbitrarily many variables and degrees.

Normalize$a_i(x)=\ell_i(x)/\ell_i(e)$, so$a_i(e)=1$. Apart from the positive factor$\prod_i\ell_i(e)$,
\[
p(x)=e_{m-1}(a_1(x),\ldots,a_m(x)).
\]
Let$U$ be any real$m\times(m-1)$ matrix with orthonormal columns spanning$\mathbf1^\perp$, and define the linear pencil
\[
M(x)=U^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{diag}(a_1(x),\ldots,a_m(x))U.
\]
Then$M(e)=I$ and
\[
\det M(x)=\frac1m e_{m-1}(a(x)).
\tag{1}
\]
Indeed Cauchy–Binet expands the determinant into the products of$m-1$ diagonal entries weighted by squared maximal minors of$U$. Completing$U$ by$\mathbf1/\sqrt m$ gives an orthogonal matrix. Its cofactors show that every such squared minor is$1/m$, proving(1), even when some$a_i$ vanish.

Because$M(e)=I$, the polynomial$t\mapsto\det M(x+te)=\det(M(x)+tI)$ has only real zeros. Thus$p$ is hyperbolic at$e$ directly from its determinant, without a separate derivative-preservation theorem. In the connected component of$\det M\ne0$ containing$e$, no eigenvalue can pass through zero, so$M$ stays positive definite. Conversely$\{x:M(x)\succ0\}$ is convex, contains$e$, and is contained in$\det M\ne0$, so it is exactly that component. Its closure is$\{x:M(x)\succeq0\}$: for any positive-semidefinite$M(x)$, $x+\varepsilon e$ has positive-definite pencil and converges to$x$. Hence
\[
\Lambda_+(p,e)=\{x:M(x)\succeq0\}.
\]
The argument includes all singular boundary points and any lineality caused by the common kernel of the$\ell_i$; injectivity of$x\mapsto a(x)$ is not assumed.

For the special case$\ell_i(x)=x_i$, this is the familiar compressed-diagonal representation of$e_{m-1}$. Brändén's checked Theorem1.2 proves spectrahedrality for every elementary symmetric polynomial, a substantially larger known family, and credits Sanyal for the first-derivative case. The explicit derivation above is therefore a reconstruction and pullback formulation of known structured progress, not a novelty claim over that literature.

The complete earlier record describes unverified global nonspectrahedrality and lift claims. None of those is used. A determinant representation of this particular polynomial proves its cone has a direct pencil; it neither resolves all hyperbolicity cones nor confuses a projection of a spectrahedron with a spectrahedron in the original variables.
