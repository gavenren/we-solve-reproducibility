# Explicit high-surgery thresholds yielding Stein fillings

**Problem:** TOP-2839

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** The high rational surgery assertion is proved by credited theorems for all closed 3-braid L-space knots, their connected sums, all torus knots and their connected sums, and specified pretzels. Explicit thresholds are supplied for two twisted-torus families. No theorem for every knot is claimed.

Use the preferred longitude convention for \(S^3_r(K)\). Ding–Li–Wu give a substantially stronger conclusion than tightness on several broad knot classes: sufficiently large *rational* surgery bounds a Stein domain. In particular, put
\[
 K_{n,m}=T(3,3m+2;2,n-2),\quad
 K'_{n,m}=T(3,3m+1;2,n-2),\qquad n\geq2,\ m\geq1 .
\]
Their Theorem 1.13 supplies Stein-fillable contact structures for
\[
 r\geq 9m+4n-4\quad\text{on }K_{n,m},\qquad
 r\geq9m+4n-8\quad\text{on }K'_{n,m}.                  \tag{1}
\]
The same theorem treats connected sums of closed 3-braid L-space knots, with a sufficiently-large threshold depending on the knot. Theorems 1.15–1.16 treat all torus knots and their connected sums, and pretzels \(P(-2l-1,2m+1,2n+1)\) for positive \(l,m,n\).

The proof is not a claim that a large positive smooth surgery is automatically Legendrian surgery in the standard tight sphere. In Section 4 the authors change the smooth diagram, introduce a one-handle, and produce a Stein handlebody whose Legendrian framings satisfy the required inequalities. In their first twisted-torus case the auxiliary coefficients include \(-1/m\), \(-1/l\), and \(-1/r'\), where
\[
 r'=r-mu^2-l(u-1)^2 .
\]
Increasing \(r\) makes the final handle construction available. Negative rational contact surgeries are resolved into suitable Legendrian handle attachments. Proposition 4.2 combines the one-handle constructions for connected sums. These diagrammatic and Stein existence results are credited inputs, rather than an unprovided Kirby-calculus proof in this report.

Once a Stein filling exists, its boundary contact structure is tight. Thus (1) gives concrete integers \(n_K\) for the literal all-\(r\geq n_K\) quantifier on those families; it is not restricted to integral surgeries or merely to infinitely many slopes. The other family theorems also have that eventual quantifier, although no explicit numerical threshold is asserted here beyond the displayed source formulas.

The orientation and strength of the conclusion matter. A result about negative surgery on a knot, or about the orientation reverse of a surgery manifold, is not substituted for the positive-oriented surgery statement here. Likewise examples without symplectic fillings at smaller slopes do not disprove tightness. The universal eventual assertion for an arbitrary knot is not established by these family theorems; this report supplies actual positive-oriented high-surgery results and states their boundary.
