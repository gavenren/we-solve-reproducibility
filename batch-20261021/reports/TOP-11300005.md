# Simple-homotopy rigidity of all closed exact Lagrangians and the lens-space consequence

**Problem:** TOP-11300005

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** Reports Abouzaid–Kragh’s simple-homotopy-equivalence theorem for the projection of every closed connected embedded exact Lagrangian in a connected closed-base cotangent bundle, and derives their lens-space cotangent rigidity corollary, including exactness under an arbitrary symplectomorphism.

The ambient Hamiltonian-isotopy conclusion in the prompt remains stronger than the result reported here. Abouzaid and Kragh proved that, for a connected closed smooth base \(N\) and any closed connected embedded exact Lagrangian \(L\subset T^*N\), the restriction of cotangent projection
\[
 \pi|_L:L\longrightarrow N
\]
is a **simple homotopy equivalence** [Theorem 1]. This applies to every such \(L\), without assuming that its projection is a covering, an immersion, or a graph. It is a published major constraint on possible counterexamples to the nearby Lagrangian conjecture.

For precision, a homotopy equivalence of finite CW complexes is simple when its Whitehead torsion vanishes. Smooth compact manifolds admit finite CW models, and the assertion is about the canonical projection, not just the existence of some unrelated homotopy equivalence. Thus it identifies fundamental groups and all ordinary homotopy groups and integral homology groups, while adding the torsion condition. Homotopy equivalence alone would not supply the latter. The theorem is not a statement that the projection itself is a diffeomorphism.

The deep Floer-theoretic proof belongs to the cited authors. Their introduction describes the three crucial tasks: defining Floer-theoretic torsion, proving its vanishing after an appropriate large Hamiltonian deformation, and identifying it with the classical torsion. This report imports that theorem and does not purport to reconstruct those analytic steps from a graph argument.

One concrete consequence demonstrates the force of the torsion conclusion. Let \(N_1,N_2\) be smooth lens spaces of dimension at least three. Then their cotangent bundles are symplectomorphic if and only if \(N_1,N_2\) are diffeomorphic. This is the authors' Corollary 1.1. Here is the application, including a potential exactness issue.

Suppose \(\Phi:T^*N_1\to T^*N_2\) is a symplectomorphism, and let \(L=\Phi(N_1)\), where \(N_1\) denotes the zero section. The submanifold \(L\) is closed, embedded and Lagrangian. Although \(\Phi\) need not preserve the Liouville one-form or be an exact symplectomorphism, \(\lambda_2|_L\) is closed because
\(d(\lambda_2|_L)=(d\lambda_2)|_L=0\). Also \(L\) is diffeomorphic to \(N_1\), so \(H^1(L;\mathbb R)=0\): a lens space has finite first homology. De Rham theory now gives \(\lambda_2|_L=dh\). Thus \(L\) satisfies precisely the exactness hypothesis needed by Theorem 1.

It follows that \(L\to N_2\) is a simple homotopy equivalence. Together with \(L\cong N_1\), this gives simple homotopy equivalence between the lens spaces. The lens-space classification fact used in Corollary 1.1 says that this determines their diffeomorphism type, giving the forward implication. For the converse, a diffeomorphism \(a:N_1\to N_2\) has cotangent lift
\[
 (x,\xi)\longmapsto
 \big(a(x),(Da_x^{-1})^*\xi\big).
\]
Evaluation on tangent vectors shows that this lift pulls \(\lambda_2\) back to \(\lambda_1\), so it is a symplectomorphism. The classification step is specific to the cited lens-space corollary; simple homotopy equivalence of arbitrary closed manifolds is not being equated with diffeomorphism.

This supplies a global topological restriction even when the projection has singularities and many sheets. It is substantially broader than the exact graph or multisection arguments in the earlier exploration. Neither the theorem nor the lens-space consequence constructs a compactly supported ambient Hamiltonian isotopy sending an arbitrary \(L\) onto \(N\). In particular, zero torsion is not a sufficient replacement for the literal conjecture. The full Hamiltonian-isotopy question and the status of the announcement in the earlier summary are not resolved by this report.
