# Real length spectra determine commensurability for closed arithmetic hyperbolic three-manifolds

**Problem:** TOP-2815

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** For two closed orientable arithmetic hyperbolic three-manifolds, equality of the prompt's real length multisets implies commensurability by Chinburg–Hamilton–Long–Reid's stronger rational-length-spectrum theorem. A covering-space proof of the reverse rational-spectrum invariance is supplied. Nonarithmetic targets, arbitrary dimensions and arithmeticity detection are not claimed.

For a manifold \(M\), define the rational length set
\[
 \mathbb QL(M)=\{q\ell:\ q\in\mathbb Q,\ \ell
 \text{ is the length of a closed geodesic of }M\}.
\]
This is a union of rational rays. It is not the vector space obtained by taking sums of lengths.

Chinburg–Hamilton–Long–Reid's Theorem 1.1 proves that among arithmetic hyperbolic three-manifolds this set determines the commensurability class. Restricting to closed orientable arithmetic \(M_1,M_2\), the literal multiset equality immediately implies equality of the sets of real lengths, then equality of \(\mathbb QL\), hence commensurability. Neither the multiplicities nor holonomy rotation angles are required by this theorem. Its arithmetic reconstruction is the substantial credited input: real eigenvalue moduli encode sufficient field and quaternion-algebra information. We do not replace it by the easier but inapplicable argument “a real length determines a complex trace.”

The reverse implication at the rational-set level has an elementary covering proof. Let \(p:N\to M\) be a finite hyperbolic cover. A closed geodesic upstairs projects to a closed geodesic, possibly traversing its primitive representative several times, so its length is an integer multiple of a primitive length downstairs. This gives \(\mathbb QL(N)\subseteq\mathbb QL(M)\).

Conversely a primitive closed geodesic downstairs represents an element \(\gamma\) of the deck group of the universal cover. The permutation action of \(\gamma\) on the finite fiber of \(p\) has a finite cycle. Traversing the geodesic a number \(k\) of times equal to that cycle length lifts to a closed geodesic upstairs, of length \(k\ell\). Thus \(\ell\in\mathbb QL(N)\) and \(\mathbb QL(M)\subseteq\mathbb QL(N)\). A common finite cover proves rational-spectrum equality for any commensurable pair.

This last proof explains both the power and the limitation of rationalization. It forgets primitive multiplicities and allows passing to powers, exactly the changes introduced by finite covers. It does not prove that commensurable manifolds have equal original length multisets.

The argument therefore establishes a complete affirmative implication in the stated arithmetic closed three-dimensional regime. It does not establish that an arbitrary manifold sharing a spectrum with an arithmetic one is arithmetic, nor extend the arithmetic reconstruction to the nonarithmetic case. Those hypotheses cannot be removed by the elementary covering calculation.
