# Ryser extremals beyond the truncated-plane uniformities

**Claim type: Literature report.** For every prime power \(q\ge3\) and every integer \(t\ge1\), there is a \((q+2)\)-partite, \((q+2)\)-uniform hypergraph with \(\nu=t\) and \(\tau=(q+1)t\). The construction is credited to Abu-Khazneh, Barát, Pokrovskiy and Szabó, Theorem1.2 and Corollary1.4 of the author-hosted July2018 manuscript [1]. This does not prove Ryser's upper bound for arbitrary hypergraphs. It supplies equality examples at a different infinite sequence of uniformities from the preceding attempt's truncated-plane examples.

Put \(r=q+1\). Start with a projective plane of order \(q\), delete a point and its incident lines, and regard the remaining lines as an \(r\)-partite \(r\)-uniform hypergraph \(T\). Its sides have size \(q\), its \(q^2\) edges are pairwise intersecting, each vertex has degree \(q\), and two vertices in different sides belong to exactly one edge. Fix an edge \(S=\{s_1,\ldots,s_r\}\), with \(s_i\) in side \(V_i\).

**Cover lemma.** Every cover of \(T-S\) of size at most \(q\) is a whole side. Fewer than \(q\) vertices cover at most \(q(q-1)<q^2-1\) edges. If a set \(W\) of size \(q\ge3\) meets more than one side, choose three members \(w,x,y\) such that \(w\) lies in neither the side of \(x\) nor that of \(y\). The edges through \(w,x\) and through \(w,y\) produce at least two units of overcount in the sum of degrees of \(W\): if they coincide, that edge contains at least three selected vertices; otherwise there are two repeated incidences. Thus \(W\) covers at most \(q^2-2\) edges, too few for \(T-S\). A \(q\)-set in one side is that entire side. This proves the lemma.

Add a new side \(\{v_1,\ldots,v_r\}\). Form a mixed-size hypergraph \(H_0\) with the following edges:
\[
 \widehat E=E\cup\{v_i\}\quad(E\ne S,\ E\cap S=\{s_i\}),\qquad
 S,\qquad S-\{s_i\}+\{v_i\}\quad(1\le i\le r).
\]
It is \((r+1)\)-partite, with edge sizes \(r\) or \(r+1\). It is intersecting. Two enlarged edges intersect in their old vertices; each meets \(S\). The sets \(S-s_i+v_i\) pairwise intersect since \(r\ge4\). If \(E\cap S=\{s_j\}\), then \(\widehat E\) meets \(S-s_i+v_i\) at \(s_j\) when \(i\ne j\), and at \(v_i\) when \(i=j\).

The edge \(S\), as a vertex set, covers \(H_0\), so \(\tau(H_0)\le r\). Suppose a cover \(C\) has size at most \(r-1=q\). Replace each selected \(v_i\) by \(s_i\), obtaining \(C'\). Every \(\widehat E\) shows that \(C'\) covers \(T-S\), without increasing cardinality. The cover lemma forces \(C'=V_i\) for some \(i\). Equality of sizes precludes duplicates in the replacement. Consequently \(C\) is either \(V_i\) or \(V_i-s_i+v_i\). The first misses \(S-s_i+v_i\); the second misses \(S\). Both are impossible. Hence \(\tau(H_0)=r\).

To make all edges have size \(r+1\), give each size-\(r\) edge its own fresh vertex in its missing side. This preserves partiteness and pairwise intersections. It also preserves the cover number: any selected fresh vertex can be replaced by an old vertex of its unique incident edge, so a cover in the padded hypergraph yields a cover of \(H_0\) of no greater size. Conversely old covers still cover. The resulting \(H\) has \(\nu(H)=1\), \(\tau(H)=r\), and uniformity \(r+1=q+2\). Taking \(t\) vertex-disjoint copies makes both parameters additive, proving the stated scope.

For example \(q=5\) gives uniformity7 and cover number6, a parameter unavailable from the truncated-plane construction of uniformity7, since a projective plane of order6 does not exist. That nonexistence is background in [1], not needed for the construction proof. No assertion is made for every uniformity, or for every hypergraph of these uniformities.

The preceding attempts establish the elementary bound and ordinary truncated-plane extremals. The shifted construction, cover lemma and padding argument above supply the distinct contribution. No computational check is claimed.
