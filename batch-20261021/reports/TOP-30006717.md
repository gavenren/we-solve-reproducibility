# What coefficient-averaged Bateman–Horn proves, and what its probability means

Claim type: **Literature report**.

The prompt fixes a polynomial tuple before letting the input grow. A substantial known theorem instead averages over polynomial coefficients while their height grows. We state a precise version, then prove a probability-one consequence and identify why neither statement removes the fixed-polynomial exceptional case.

Use the notation of Browning–Sofos–Teräväinen, arXiv:2212.10373v2,21May2026. Fix degree$d\ge1$, a real$c$ with
\[
 0<c<\frac5{31d},
\]
and any logarithmic saving exponent$A\ge1$. A combinatorial coefficient cube$\mathcal C(H)$ consists of vectors in$[-H,H]^{d+1}\cap\mathbb Z^{d+1}$, with some coordinates fixed. The dimension must be at least2, and the constant coefficient must not be fixed to0. Put
\[
 S_f(x)=\prod_{p\le\exp(\sqrt{\log x})}
 \frac{1-N_p(f)/p}{1-1/p}.
\]
Theorem1.2 says that, for all but at most
$\#\mathcal C(H)/(\log H)^A$ degree-$d$ polynomials in the cube,
\[
 \sup_{H^c\le x\le2H^c}
 \left|\frac1x\sum_{n\le x}\Lambda(f(n))-S_f(x)\right|
 \le(\log H)^{-A}
 \tag{1}
\]
once$H$ is sufficiently large in terms of$d,A,c$. The source extends$\Lambda$ by $\Lambda(-m)=\Lambda(m)$ and $\Lambda(0)=0$; this convention is essential when coefficients of either sign occur. Its Theorem1.4 treats tuples using the analogous truncated joint product and an exceptional bound $(\#\mathcal C(H))^r/(\log H)^A$. The theorem is credited, not newly proved here. The checked v2 is a preprint; no journal publication is asserted.

Several details distinguish(1) from the target. It is a weighted estimate, including prime powers and negative arguments under the stated convention. Its product is truncated at a specified moving cutoff. The polynomial itself varies with$H$, and the available input window is tied to its coefficient height. In particular one cannot replace$S_f(x)$ by the infinite product uniformly merely because each **fixed** polynomial has a convergent singular product. A family-uniform tail estimate would be a further input.

Here is a fully rigorous interpretation of the probability language. For simplicity take the full cube in degree$d$, and let $H_j=2^j$. Choose a coefficient vector uniformly from this cube at each$j$, on any joint probability space with those uniform marginals. No independence is required. Choose, for example, $A=3$ in(1). The probability that the chosen degree-$d$ polynomial fails(1) is at most $(\log H_j)^{-3}$. A polynomial of lower degree has leading coefficient0, with probability $1/(2H_j+1)$, and may be included as an additional bad event. Therefore
\[
 \sum_j\Pr(\text{bad at }j)
 \le\sum_j\big((j\log2)^{-3}+(2^{j+1}+1)^{-1}\big)<\infty.
\]
For every$J$, the probability of at least one bad event with$j\ge J$ is at most the corresponding tail sum. That sum tends to zero. Hence, with probability1, only finitely many selected polynomials fail their respective finite-window estimates. This proves an almost-sure assertion directly by countable subadditivity.

The same argument can make the normalized error tend to zero along the selected sequence: apply the already fixed$A=3$ estimate, whose error $(j\log2)^{-3}$ vanishes. It does not require a new uniform theorem as$A$ varies. Similar summability works for a fixed tuple size using the source's tuple exceptional bound.

This is a statement about $(f_j,H_j,x)$ with $f_j$ changing. It is **not** an assertion that a single random integral polynomial satisfies Bateman–Horn at every sufficiently large input, nor that one fixed$f$ has infinitely many prime values. There is no uniform probability measure on all integral coefficient vectors implicit in this construction. Indeed, the probability of any one specified vector at stage$j$ is $(2H_j+1)^{-(d+1)}$; summability implies that vector is selected only finitely often almost surely. Repeated occurrence of one fixed polynomial is not what the construction supplies.

Nor does the exceptional bound exclude a particular polynomial from being exceptional at every relevant height. An exceptional set of cardinality at most$\#\mathcal C(H)/(\log H)^A$ can still contain any prescribed finite set for all sufficiently large$H$. A density-one family theorem and an every-fixed-tuple theorem have different quantifiers.

The earlier contributions prove fixed-cutoff CRT identities, weighted reformulations and a diagonal-cutoff normalization obstruction; they mention coefficient averages only as a scope warning. Here the added content is the exact current primary theorem, all its coefficient/window/sign restrictions, and a complete probability-one consequence with no independence assumption. The selected fixed admissible positive-leading-coefficient tuple, especially a specified nonlinear polynomial such as$n^2+1$, is not settled by this result.
