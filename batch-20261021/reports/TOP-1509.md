# A consequence of failure of the uniform Siegel-zero gap

Claim type: **Literature report**.

The target is an absolute positive lower bound for $(1-\beta)\log q$ at every real zero of every primitive real nonprincipal character of conductor$q\ge3$. We do not prove that bound. A published theorem gives a strong consequence if it fails: **every fixed positive even integer occurs infinitely often as a difference of two primes**. We spell out the scales and the prime-power removal needed for that implication.

Tao–Teräväinen define the quality of a real exceptional zero by
\[
 \beta=1-\frac1{\eta\log q},\qquad\eta\ge10.
\]
Their Corollary1.8(i), a consequence of Theorem1.6, gives for fixed distinct positive shifts$h_1,h_2$, and fixed$0<\varepsilon_0<1$,
\[
 \frac1x\sum_{n\le x}\Lambda(n+h_1)\Lambda(n+h_2)
 =\mathfrak S(h_1,h_2)+O((\log\eta)^{-1/20})
 \tag{1}
\]
uniformly when
\[
 q^{41/2+\varepsilon_0}\le x\le q^{\sqrt\eta}.
 \tag{2}
\]
This is a credited theorem conditional on the presence of the indicated zero, not a zero-existence assertion. The primary arXivv2 and its published journal metadata were checked. The theorem extends the older Heath-Brown mechanism; no original-paper reproof of that earlier work is claimed.

Suppose the selected uniform gap fails. There then exist zeros with
\[
 (1-\beta_j)\log q_j\to0,\qquad
 \eta_j=((1-\beta_j)\log q_j)^{-1}\to\infty.
 \tag{3}
\]
We may take$q_j\to\infty$. For bounded conductors there are only finitely many characters, and each of their $L$-functions is analytic and nonzero at1. A finite collection therefore has a common positive zero-free real neighborhood of1. Such a collection cannot furnish(3).

Set $\varepsilon_0=1/2$ and $x_j=q_j^{22}$. For all sufficiently large$j$, the upper condition in(2) holds because$\sqrt{\eta_j}\ge22$, and the lower condition is merely$q_j^{21}\le x_j$. Thus(1) has an error tending to zero at these scales for each fixed pair of shifts.

Fix a positive even$h$, and take$h_1=1$, $h_2=h+1$. The singular series is positive:
\[
 \mathfrak S_h
 =2\prod_{\ell>2}\left(1-\frac1{(\ell-1)^2}\right)
       \prod_{\substack{\ell\mid h\\\ell>2}}\frac{\ell-1}{\ell-2}>0.
 \tag{4}
\]
For $\ell\nmid h$ the two shifts forbid two distinct classes modulo$\ell$; for $\ell\mid h$ they forbid only one. At2, evenness of$h$ gives one forbidden class and local factor2. The remaining infinite product is positive because its logarithmic deficits are summable. Only finitely many factors are changed by divisors of$h$.

Equation(1) counts prime powers as well as primes. They are negligible here by a completely elementary estimate. The number of proper prime powers at most$x+h+1$ is
\[
 O(\sqrt x\log(2x)).
\]
Indeed for each exponent$k\ge2$ there are at most$(x+h+1)^{1/k}$ possible bases, and only$O(\log x)$ exponents; the deliberately coarse bound suffices. Hence the number of indices$n\le x$ for which either argument is a proper prime power has the same order. Each Mangoldt product is at most$O_h(\log^2(2x))$, so their total contribution is
\[
 O_h(\sqrt x\log^3(2x))=o(x).
\]
Zero or unit arguments cause no difficulty since the chosen shifts are positive.

Combining this with(1)–(4), along$x=x_j$ the sum restricted to actual prime pairs is $\mathfrak S_h x_j+o_h(x_j)$. Each such pair contributes at most$\log^2(x_j+h+1)$. Therefore, eventually,
\[
 \#\{n\le x_j:n+1,\ n+h+1\text{ prime}\}
 \ge \frac{\mathfrak S_h}{2}
       \frac{x_j}{\log^2(x_j+h+1)}.
 \tag{5}
\]
This tends to infinity. The same sequence$x_j$ is available for every fixed$h$, but the threshold in$j$ may depend on$h$; no uniformity for growing gaps is claimed. Odd gaps are excluded from this conclusion, as parity requires.

The exact contrapositive is useful for the prompt: **if there are only finitely many twin primes, then its uniform real-zero exclusion follows**. Otherwise failure of the exclusion gives(5) for$h=2$. This does not establish the premise, compute an exclusion constant, or derive zero-freeness from the truth of the twin-prime conjecture. The direction of implication cannot be reversed.

The earlier contributions examine derivative/positivity obstructions and describe2026 nonexistence claims and their varying verification status. Those recent proofs are not independently audited or used as premises here. The added content is a separately checked published exceptional-zero regime, its precise failure-of-uniformity interpretation, and the complete passage from Mangoldt correlations to actual prime pairs. It is a conditional consequence and boundary statement, not a new proof of uniform zero exclusion.
