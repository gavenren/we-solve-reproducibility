# Bass finite generation for all one-dimensional arithmetic schemes, including singular and nonreduced rings

Claim type: **Partial result**.

The assertion in the literal prompt holds for **every finite-type $\mathbb Z$-algebra$A$ of Krull dimension at most1**, whether reduced, irreducible or regular, and for every$n\ge0$:
\[
 G_n(A)=K'_n(A)\quad\text{is a finitely generated abelian group}. \tag{1}
\]
This is an established low-dimensional case, not a new unrestricted Bass theorem. The proof below explains why the coherent-module formulation genuinely covers singular and nilpotent arithmetic curves, rather than silently replacing them by regular ones.

The deep finite-generation input is Quillen's theorem for rings of integers of number fields and coordinate rings of smooth affine curves over finite fields, stated precisely in Weibel, *The K-book*, IV.Theorem6.9. We use also Quillen's coherent devissage and localization theorems, checked in V.Corollary4.2 and V.Section6.11. These author-hosted technical treatments are the sources actually read; the original arithmetic-group finite-generation proof is not reproduced or claimed to have been checked in full.

First, if$I$ is a nilpotent ideal of a Noetherian ring$A$, every finite module$M$ has a finite filtration
\[
 M\supset IM\supset I^2M\supset\cdots\supset I^eM=0
\]
with successive quotients annihilated by$I$. Devissage gives $G_n(A)\simeq G_n(A/I)$ for all$n\ge0$. This does not assert the corresponding invariance of projective-module$K$-theory on a singular ring. In our finite-type ring the nilradical is nilpotent, so we may replace$A$ by$A_{\mathrm{red}}$.

Every closed point of a finite-type$\mathbb Z$-scheme has a finite residue field. Indeed a residue field of characteristic zero would be a field finite type as a ring over$\mathbb Z$, impossible by the usual Zariski-lemma denominator argument; in positive characteristic Zariski's lemma makes it a finite extension of the prime field. A zero-dimensional closed subscheme has finitely many such points, and devissage reduces its$G_n$ to their direct sum. Quillen's finite-field calculation (IV.Corollary1.13) gives
\[
 K_0(\mathbb F_Q)=\mathbb Z,\quad
 K_{2i}(\mathbb F_Q)=0\ (i\ge1),\quad
 K_{2i-1}(\mathbb F_Q)=\mathbb Z/(Q^i-1)\ (i\ge1). \tag{2}
\]
In particular all these point groups are finitely generated. Equation(2) already proves(1) for zero-dimensional$A$.

For a reduced one-dimensional$X=\operatorname{Spec}A$, remove a finite closed set so that the remaining open$U$ is a disjoint union of regular affine pieces of its irreducible components (and any retained isolated finite-field points). This construction is legitimate: the ring is excellent, so the regular locus is open and contains all generic points; distinct one-dimensional components meet in a zero-dimensional closed set. On each component one may shrink further to a nonempty principal affine open; the discarded complement remains zero-dimensional and finite.

For a vertical component, its characteristic is$p>0$. Its function field has transcendence degree1 over a finite field, and its regular open is a smooth affine curve over a finite field, since finite fields are perfect. Its$G$-theory agrees with$K$-theory by regularity and is finitely generated by the input theorem.

For a horizontal domain$B$, finite type over$\mathbb Z$ with$\dim B=1$, dimension theory gives $B\otimes\mathbb Q=K$, a number field. There is a nonzero integer$d$ with
\[
 B[1/d]=\mathcal O_K[1/d]. \tag{3}
\]
To verify(3), first clear denominators of the finitely many generators of$B$ so they are integral over$\mathbb Z[1/d]$, obtaining $B\subset\mathcal O_K[1/d]$. Conversely each element of an integral basis of$\mathcal O_K$ belongs to $K=B\otimes\mathbb Q$, so after enlarging$d$ all these basis elements lie in$B[1/d]$. This gives the reverse inclusion. The complement of this open consists of finitely many finite-field points. Localization on $\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_K$ shows that $G_n(\mathcal O_K[1/d])$ is finitely generated: it is an extension of an image of the finitely generated$G_n(\mathcal O_K)$ by a subgroup of the finite direct sum of$G_{n-1}$ of the removed points. For$n=0$ localization simply ends with a surjection from$G_0(\mathcal O_K)$.

We have proved finite generation on all components of$U$. Let$Z=X\setminus U$ with its reduced structure. Localization gives
\[
 G_n(Z)\longrightarrow G_n(X)\longrightarrow G_n(U)
\]
exact for every$n\ge0$. The kernel of the second map is a quotient of the finitely generated group$G_n(Z)$, while its image is a subgroup of the finitely generated abelian group$G_n(U)$. Both are finitely generated, so their extension$G_n(X)$ is finitely generated. There is no need to assume the map to$G_n(U)$ is surjective in positive degree. This completes(1), including arbitrary nilpotent thickenings and singular intersections.

The restriction on Krull dimension is essential to this proof: higher-dimensional regular opens are not among the finite-generation inputs. Nor does coherent devissage prove the projective-module version for arbitrary singular$A$. For regular$A$, the agreement$G_n(A)=K_n(A)$ stated in the official prompt gives its projective-module conclusion as a corollary.
