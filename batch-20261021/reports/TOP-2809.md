# Spanning-surface certificates for the four-unit meridian bound

**Problem:** TOP-2809

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** The maximal-cusp bound holds for every adequate hyperbolic knot with crossing number c at least 6g_T−6, and for every adequate diagram with c at least three times its twist number. A general essential-spanning-surface certificate and its exact limitations are explained; no universal assertion for all knots is made.

Write \(\mu,\lambda\) for a meridian and preferred longitude on the maximal cusp torus. Burton–Kalfagianni's Theorem 4.1 gives the following useful certificate. If \(S_1,S_2\) are essential spanning surfaces and \(i=i(\partial S_1,\partial S_2)>0\), then
\[
 \ell(\mu)\leq {6(|\chi(S_1)|+|\chi(S_2)|)\over i}.                 \tag{1}
\]
Nonorientable spanning surfaces are permitted. Essentiality is an actual hypothesis: it supplies the pleated-surface cusp-length estimate used in their proof.

Here is the elementary slope calculation in that proof. Orient both boundary curves so their longitude coefficients are \(1\); their flat-torus vectors are \(v_1=\lambda+a\mu\) and \(v_2=\lambda+b\mu\). Then \(i=|a-b|\), so
\[
 i\ell(\mu)=|v_1-v_2|\leq |v_1|+|v_2|.
\]
The credited geometric input bounds this sum by \(6(|\chi(S_1)|+|\chi(S_2)|)\). In particular a pair with Euler-characteristic sum at most \(2i/3\) proves exactly the requested bound \(4\), on the same maximal cusp as in the prompt.

For an adequate diagram the all-\(A\) and all-\(B\) state surfaces are essential; adequacy means that neither state graph has a one-edge loop. The state counts give
\[
 |\chi(S_A)|+|\chi(S_B)|=c+2g_T-2,\qquad
 i(\partial S_A,\partial S_B)=2c .
\]
Thus (1) becomes
\[
 \ell(\mu)\leq 3+{6g_T-6\over c}.
\]
This proves the bound for the stated broad diagrammatic class. At fixed \(g_T\), any possible adequate counterexample must have \(c<6g_T-6\); there are only finitely many knot diagrams below this crossing cutoff. This finiteness statement uses no census calculation.

The same paper's Theorem 1.4 gives
\[
 \ell(\mu)\leq 3+{3t\over c}-{6\over c}<4\quad(c\geq3t),
\]
where \(t\) is the twist number of the adequate diagram. This is a second usable certificate directly from a diagram. Section 5 also gives a normal-surface algorithm for detecting appropriate essential spanning pairs. Failure to find such a pair does not show that the meridian is longer than four. The argument neither supplies a counterexample nor proves the bound for arbitrary nonadequate knots; that universal quantifier is the remaining part of the prompt.
