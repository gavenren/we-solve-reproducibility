# The prime-seven obstruction and the difference between V(4), powers and ring structures

**Problem:** TOP-30006095

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** Nave's published theorem rules out V(4) at p=7 in the precise BP-module sense and rules out a ring structure on V(3) there if it exists. The theorem's height/prime dependence and cofiber hypotheses are checked; existence at larger primes and the earlier p=1009 announcement are not certified.

At the prime \(7\), the answer is rigorously negative in the strongest usual Smith–Toda sense. Nave defines \(V(k)\) to be a finite spectrum with
\[
 BP_*V(k)\cong BP_* /(p,v_1,\ldots,v_k)
\]
as a \(BP_*\)-module. His published Theorem 1.3 states that for primes \(p\geq7\),
\[
 V((p+1)/2)\text{ does not exist}.
\]
Putting \(p=7\) gives exactly \(V(4)\), with no assumption that it is a ring spectrum. The same theorem says that \(V((p-1)/2)\), if it exists, is not a ring spectrum; at \(7\) this concerns \(V(3)\).

Two qualifications prevent incorrect extensions of this result. First the forbidden index varies with the prime. At \(p=11\) it is \(6\), not \(4\). Thus the theorem is not a proof that \(V(4)\) fails at all large primes. Second the usual periodicity theorem produces suitable powers of \(v_n\); quotienting by such powers is not the same module as the one in the displayed definition, where the exponent of every \(v_i\) is exactly one.

The cofiber issue is also subtle. For odd \(p\), Nave's Theorem 1.2 identifies the required \(BP\)-module condition with the appropriate exterior dual-Steenrod-comodule homology. From it the source obtains a necessary realization
\[
 \Sigma^{|v_k|}V(k-1)'\longrightarrow V(k-1)
 \longrightarrow V(k),
\]
whose first map induces multiplication by \(v_k\). The two models \(V(k-1)'\) and \(V(k-1)\) need not be equivalent. Consequently proving that a particular chosen \(V(k-1)\) lacks one self-map would not by itself exclude every possible \(V(k)\).

The published obstruction handles this stronger requirement using the Hopkins–Miller homotopy fixed point spectral sequence at height \(p-1\). Theorems 1.4–1.5 give incompatible permanence behavior with a map realizing the necessary \(v_k\)-multiplication when \(k=(p+1)/2\). The proof compares spectral sequences under the hypothetical realizing map; it does not apply an unjustified ring Leibniz rule to \(V(k-1)\). This distinction is especially important because the same theorem obstructs a ring structure.

The conclusion here is the exact prime-seven obstruction and a checked account of its scope. It does not construct \(V(4)\) at another prime, prove its nonexistence at every prime, or verify the earlier prime-1009 announcement. Those claims require their own complete construction or obstruction; they do not follow from either periodicity in some exponent or a list of formal declarations.
