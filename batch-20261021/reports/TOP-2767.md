# Closed arithmetic mapping tori: a new finiteness theorem and the distinction from monodromy classification

**Problem:** TOP-2767

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** The September2026 Cheetham-West–Lee–Miller preprint, arXiv:2609.28188v1, proves fixed-genus finiteness of cyclic commensurability classes of closed arithmetic hyperbolic surface bundles. This report identifies its trace-degree argument and explains why it neither means finitely many monodromies nor gives the literal classification algorithm. Preprint status and imported-proof boundaries are explicit.

A recent result changes the closed-fiber finiteness picture. The identified September 2026 preprint of Cheetham-West–Lee–Miller states and proves:

For every fixed \(g\ge2\), there are only finitely many cyclic commensurability classes of closed arithmetic hyperbolic surface bundles with fiber \(S_g\).

Here cyclic commensurability means existence of a common finite cyclic cover. This is Theorem 1.1 of arXiv:2609.28188v1. It is a **preprint theorem reported with credit**, not a claimed new proof or a published classification of all monodromies.

The new ingredient removes a trace-field-degree restriction. Section 2.4 recalls the Bowditch–Maclachlan–Reid finiteness theorem when both genus and invariant trace-field degree are bounded. The proof in §3 bounds that degree using useful simplicial hyperbolic surfaces: bounded-length pants curves and an arithmetic Margulis lemma yield embedded annular neighborhoods whose area lower bound grows with the trace degree. Gauss–Bonnet bounds the total available surface area by \(4\pi(g-1)\). The resulting contradiction for large degree enables the earlier finiteness theorem. These geometric and arithmetic steps are substantive imported results; they are not proved here from an abstract announcement.

There is an important quantifier distinction for the literal problem. Let \(\phi\) be any pseudo-Anosov with arithmetic mapping torus \(M_\phi\). For each \(m\ge1\), the mapping torus \(M_{\phi^m}\) is the pullback under the degree-\(m\) cover of the base circle. Hence
\[
 M_{\phi^m}\longrightarrow M_\phi,\qquad
 \operatorname{vol}(M_{\phi^m})=m\operatorname{vol}(M_\phi).
\]
Its holonomy is a finite-index subgroup of an arithmetic lattice, so remains arithmetic by the definition of commensurability. The powers are pseudo-Anosov and their dilatations are \(\lambda(\phi)^m\); since dilatation is a conjugacy invariant and \(\lambda(\phi)>1\), these are infinitely many distinct mapping-class conjugacy classes. Their manifolds also have distinct volumes and are pairwise nonisometric. All lie in one cyclic commensurability class.

Thus fixed-genus finiteness cannot mean a finite list of arithmetic mapping classes themselves. Any eventual criterion must accommodate unbounded powers, and potentially other fibrations in commensurable manifolds. Conversely, knowing that an unspecified quotient set is finite does not furnish representatives or a terminating membership test for a monodromy word.

The report therefore supplies a precise current theorem about the closed-fiber part and locates its limitation. It does not characterize every arithmetic monodromy, claim that commensurability equals fiber-preserving conjugacy of powers in every case, or settle the punctured and closed cases by a single unproved criterion.
