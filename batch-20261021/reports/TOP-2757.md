# A homogeneous length-two presentation using every positive half twist

**Problem:** TOP-2757

**Claim type:** Partial result

**Exact scope:** A complete group presentation on all isotopy classes of puncture-joining arcs, with the single geometric rule x_a x_b=x_{h_a(b)} x_a. Validity and completeness are proved using the classical Artin presentation. No embedding theorem or algorithmic normal form for the associated positive monoid is claimed.

Let \(\mathcal A\) be the set of isotopy classes, relative to the punctures and boundary, of the arcs in the prompt. Write \(a\cdot b=h_a(b)\): apply the positive half twist supported near \(a\) to \(b\), then take its isotopy class. This is explicit geometric data on arcs, including when they intersect many times.

Define a group
\[
 Q=\left\langle x_a\ (a\in\mathcal A)\ \middle|\
 x_a x_b=x_{a\cdot b}x_a\quad(a,b\in\mathcal A)\right\rangle .       \tag{1}
\]
Every defining relation is positive, homogeneous and of length two on each side. The rule is one uniform geometric operation; it is not a finite list of all pairs of arcs.

There is a well-defined homomorphism \(q:Q\to B_n\), \(x_a\mapsto h_a\), because conjugation carries a positive half twist to the positive half twist on the image arc:
\(h_a h_b h_a^{-1}=h_{h_a(b)}\).

We prove completeness. Choose the usual chain of adjacent arcs \(a_1,\ldots,a_{n-1}\). If \(|i-j|>1\), their supports are disjoint, so \(a_i\cdot a_j=a_j\); (1) gives \(x_{a_i}x_{a_j}=x_{a_j}x_{a_i}\).

For consecutive arcs write \(a=a_i,b=a_{i+1},c=h_a(b)\). The local three-puncture-disc isotopy gives \(h_b(c)=a\). Equivalently this follows from the classical three-strand Artin relation acting on the arcs; it is a geometric identity in the disk, not a relation assumed in \(Q\). Equation (1) gives
\[
 x_a x_b=x_c x_a,\qquad x_b x_c=x_a x_b.
\]
Therefore
\[
 x_bx_ax_b=x_bx_cx_a=x_ax_bx_a .
\]
The adjacent generators satisfy all Artin relations. The classical Artin presentation, as recalled in the checked primary source, thus defines a homomorphism \(s:B_n\to Q\) taking the standard generator to \(x_{a_i}\), and \(q s=\mathrm{id}\).

It remains to show \(s\) is surjective, since a right inverse alone would not prove (1) presents \(B_n\). Every arc is the image of an adjacent arc under a boundary-fixing puncture-permuting disk homeomorphism: map regular neighborhoods of the two arcs, then extend across their punctured complements. Write that homeomorphism as an Artin word \(w\). Relation (1) gives
\(x_{h_{a_i}(b)}=x_{a_i}x_bx_{a_i}^{-1}\);
applying the same formula to \(h_{a_i}^{-1}(b)\) gives the corresponding negative-generator formula. Induction along \(w\) yields
\[
 x_{w(a_j)}=s(w)x_{a_j}s(w)^{-1}.
\]
Every generator of \(Q\) therefore lies in \(\operatorname{im}s\). Thus \(s\) and \(q\) are inverse isomorphisms. For \(n=2\) the same transitivity argument leaves the unique arc class and gives \(Q\cong\mathbb Z=B_2\).

This proves a presentation on the literal full generator set. It does not assert that every equality of positive words follows without group cancellation in the positive monoid, or give a fast word algorithm. Those are stronger questions; the source's finite-band monoid theorem cannot automatically be transferred to this infinite presentation.
