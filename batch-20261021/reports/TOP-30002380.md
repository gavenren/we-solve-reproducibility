# Serre uniformity: a Frobenius certificate and exact isogeny-field degrees

Claim type: Literature report.

For every non-CM $E/\mathbb Q$ and every prime $p>37$, Furio–Lombardo's Theorem1.5 gives the exact dichotomy
\[
 \operatorname{im}\rho_{E,p}=\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_p)
 \quad\text{or is conjugate to }N_{\rm ns}(p),
 \tag{1}
\]
the full normalizer of a nonsplit Cartan. Their Theorem1.6 eliminates the former index-three proper subgroup alternative for primes larger than five. These are credited results from the actual v2, dated25November2025; the author's publication list identifies the paper as to appear in Algebra & Number Theory. This report derives two useful consequences of (1). It does not prove that the second alternative never occurs at large primes.

Choose a nonsquare $\epsilon\in\mathbb F_p^\times$. A nonsplit Cartan consists of
\[
 C=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&\epsilon b\\ b&a\end{pmatrix}:(a,b)\ne(0,0)\right\},
\]
and its normalizer is $C\cup JC$, $J=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$. The characteristic discriminant of a matrix in $C$ is $4\epsilon b^2$: it is zero for scalar matrices and a nonsquare otherwise. Every matrix in $JC$ has trace zero. Consequently a matrix with **nonzero trace and nonzero square discriminant** cannot belong to any conjugate of $N_{\rm ns}(p)$.

Let $\ell\ne p$ be a good-reduction prime for $E$, and put $a_\ell=\ell+1-|E(\mathbb F_\ell)|$. The standard good-Frobenius theorem gives characteristic polynomial $X^2-a_\ell X+\ell$ on $E[p]$. We obtain this exact certificate:
\[
 p>37,\quad a_\ell\not\equiv0\pmod p,\quad
 a_\ell^2-4\ell\text{ a nonzero square modulo }p
 \ \Longrightarrow\ \rho_{E,p}\text{ surjective}. \tag{2}
\]
Only one good prime is required once these conditions hold. Both nonzero conditions matter: trace zero is allowed in the outer coset, while a scalar Cartan element has zero discriminant. This differs from the earlier attempt's transvection criterion; no element of order $p$ or inertia calculation needs to be exhibited for (2). Frobenius compatibility is a standard arithmetic input, whereas the normalizer exclusion is the explicit matrix proof above.

There is also an exact consequence even when no such Frobenius has been found. Both groups in (1) act transitively on $\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_p)$, the set of lines in $E[p]$. For the Cartan this follows by identifying $\mathbb F_p^2$ with $\mathbb F_{p^2}$: multiplication by $\mathbb F_{p^2}^\times$ is transitive on nonzero vectors, hence on lines. The full general linear group is plainly transitive. Thus every line $L\subset E[p]$ has Galois orbit of size $p+1$.

In characteristic zero, a cyclic $p$-isogeny is defined over a field exactly when its order-$p$ kernel line is stable under that field's absolute Galois group. Let $F_L$ be the fixed field of the stabilizer of $L$ in $G_{\mathbb Q}$. Its degree is the orbit size, so
\[
 [F_L:\mathbb Q]=p+1.
\]
The kernel subgroup scheme descends to $F_L$ and the quotient isogeny exists there. Conversely if a number field $K$ supports this isogeny, then $G_K$ stabilizes $L$, hence $F_L\subset K$ (inside a chosen algebraic closure). Therefore
\[
 p+1\mid[K:\mathbb Q]. \tag{3}
\]
This argument does not require $K/\mathbb Q$ or $F_L/\mathbb Q$ to be Galois.

In particular, for **every** non-CM rational elliptic curve and every $p>37$, the minimal extension degree over which it admits a cyclic $p$-isogeny is exactly $p+1$. This is an all-curves, all-large-primes conclusion despite the unresolved image alternative. It matches the large-prime part of the primary paper's Theorem1.7; the orbit argument supplies its direct explanation. It must not be confused with defining a nonzero $p$-torsion point, which is a vector-stabilizer question and can require a larger degree.

The literal target still asks for one bound excluding the full nonsplit normalizer for all non-CM rational curves. Neither transitivity on lines nor the field degree (3) excludes that group. Likewise (2) does not supply a uniform theorem guaranteeing an appropriate good Frobenius for every curve and prime. A curve-dependent search threshold or height bound cannot be moved outside the quantifier over $E$. No full uniformity claim, new subgroup classification, or rerun of the primary paper's computations is made.
