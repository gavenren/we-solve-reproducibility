# Coxeter-manifold Singer vanishing, explicit middle Betti numbers and higher-dimensional edge degrees

**Problem:** TOP-30006819

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** Reports Davis–Okun Singer vanishing for closed aspherical Davis manifolds from finite flag PL spheres in dimensions 3 and 4, computes the dimension-four middle L2-Betti number from the flag-sphere f-vector, and reports b1 and b(n−1) vanishing in the same Coxeter construction for every n≥3.

There is a large cubical class in which the full Singer conclusion is established by Davis–Okun. Let \(L\) be a finite flag triangulation of the PL sphere \(S^{n-1}\). “Flag” means that every finite clique in the one-skeleton spans a simplex. Let
\[
 W_L=\langle s_v\ (v\in V(L))\mid s_v^2=1,\ 
 s_vs_w=s_ws_v\text{ whenever }\{v,w\}\in L\rangle
\]
be its right-angled Coxeter group. The Davis complex \(\Sigma_L\) is contractible and cubical, with vertex links \(L\); the group acts properly and cocompactly. For any torsion-free finite-index subgroup \(\Gamma<W_L\), the quotient \(M=\Sigma_L/\Gamma\) is a closed aspherical \(n\)-manifold.

Davis–Okun's Theorem 11.1.1, interpreted as in their §11.2, gives Singer vanishing for this construction when \(n\le4\). In particular all \(L^2\)-Betti numbers vanish for \(n=3\), while for \(n=4\) only degree two may be nonzero. This imports their deep vanishing theorem; contractibility of \(\Sigma_L\) by itself would not imply reduced \(L^2\)-acyclicity.

There is an explicit finite cover for every such \(L\). Abelianization gives
\[
 W_L\longrightarrow (\mathbb Z/2)^{V(L)},\qquad
 \Gamma_L=\ker(\text{this map}),
 \qquad [W_L:\Gamma_L]=2^v,\quad v=|V(L)|.
\]
As checked in §6.4.1, every finite subgroup of \(W_L\) is conjugate into a finite special subgroup generated by the vertices of a simplex. The abelianization map is injective on each such special subgroup. Hence its kernel contains no nonidentity element of finite order. It acts freely on \(\Sigma_L\); §6.4 realizes the quotient as a concrete finite cubical complex inside the \(v\)-cube. Thus this class does not require a hypothetical finite cover.

For \(n=4\) the sole possible middle invariant has a combinatorial formula. Write \(f_i\) for the number of \(i\)-simplices of \(L\), and \(d=[W_L:\Gamma]\). The cubical orbits are indexed by simplices including the empty simplex; a simplex with \(k\) vertices gives a \(k\)-cube with stabilizer of order \(2^k\). Therefore the source's orbifold Euler formula is
\[
 \chi(M)=d\left(1-\frac{f_0}{2}+\frac{f_1}{4}
                    -\frac{f_2}{8}+\frac{f_3}{16}\right).
\]
Finite-index scaling here is essential: using the parent Coxeter group without the factor \(d\) would compute a different normalization. Atiyah's Euler formula and the cited vanishing theorem now give
\[
 b_2^{(2)}(M)=\chi(M)
 =\frac d{16}\big(16-5f_0+f_1\big)\ge0.                 \tag{1}
\]
For completeness, \(f_2=2f_3\), by counting tetrahedron–triangle incidences in the closed triangulated three-manifold, and \(f_0-f_1+f_2-f_3=0\), since the sphere has Euler characteristic zero. Substitution gives the last expression. For the canonical commutator cover, \(d=2^{f_0}\). Equation (1) identifies precisely the surviving invariant and yields the flag-sphere inequality \(f_1\ge5f_0-16\), consistent with the source's Theorem 11.2.1.

A second published constraint survives beyond dimension four: Theorem 11.3.2 gives \(b_1^{(2)}(M)=b_{n-1}^{(2)}(M)=0\) for these sphere-nerve Coxeter manifolds in every \(n\ge3\), using finite-index scaling. It leaves the remaining interior degrees unresolved here when \(n\ge5\). This does not establish the universal Singer assertion for arbitrary closed aspherical manifolds, nor infer individual vanishing merely from the sign of an Euler characteristic. The new scope goes beyond the earlier product examples by allowing arbitrary finite flag PL sphere nerves and supplies exact normalization and a computable middle-degree formula.
