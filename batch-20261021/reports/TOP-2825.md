# Polynomial certificates for sphere recognition: positive witnesses and conditional negative witnesses

**Problem:** TOP-2825

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** Schleimer's NP theorem for triangulated S³ recognition is paired with Zentner's GRH-conditional finite-field certificate for non-sphericity of integer homology three-spheres. The input-encoding and verifier arguments are explicit. Neither deterministic polynomial time nor a general two-input homeomorphism bound is claimed.

Schleimer's Theorem 16.1 puts three-sphere recognition from a triangulation in NP. The certificate uses normal/almost-normal sphere coordinates and a controlled sequence of crushing and component-removal operations. Coordinates may be exponentially large as integers while having polynomial binary length. Checking the encoded surface and its effect is therefore different from explicitly writing out all its normal disks. The polynomial verifier is the substantial credited result.

There is a complementary conditional witness. Zentner's Theorem 9.4 says that a nontrivial integer homology three-sphere has an irreducible \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb C)\) representation. Under GRH, the finite-field reduction theorem stated as his Theorem 11.1 yields matrices over \(\mathbb F_p\) with noncommutative image and with \(\log p\) polynomially bounded by the presentation length. Theorem 11.2 formulates the resulting non-sphericity certificate for a Heegaard-diagram input.

The same certificate has the following transparent triangulation interpretation. A maximal tree in the one-skeleton gives generators from the remaining edges; the triangular two-faces give relators of bounded length, with total presentation length polynomial in the face-pairing description. Supply \(p\), a matrix \(A_i\) for every generator, and two generator indices \(i,j\) with
\[
 \det A_i=1,\quad r(A_1,\ldots,A_s)=I
 \ \text{for every relator }r,\qquad A_iA_j\ne A_jA_i .
\]
Modular arithmetic verifies these equalities in polynomial time in the input and certificate length. Primality can also be checked in polynomial time. The last inequality proves that the presented group is nontrivial, hence the manifold is not \(S^3\). If all generator pairs commuted, the image they generate would be abelian, so a noncommutative image always supplies such a pair.

For a closed connected orientable input which is not an integer homology sphere, integer homology itself distinguishes it from \(S^3\); boundary matrices and Smith reduction have polynomial bit complexity. Thus the difficult conditional negative branch is precisely the homology-sphere case. The number-theoretic hypothesis controls the **existence of short witnesses**, not the correctness of a particular matrix certificate once supplied.

These statements sharply separate three tasks: verifying a proposed positive certificate, verifying a proposed negative certificate, and finding an answer deterministically. Membership in NP, or even NP∩coNP under a hypothesis, does not imply a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm. Nor does recognition of the fixed target \(S^3\) solve homeomorphism with two variable inputs. The literal polynomial-time question and improved general bounds remain beyond this report.
