# A constructive two-spanning-tree certificate for Hamiltonian line graphs

**Claim type: Literature report.** If a finite simple graph \(H\) has at least three edges and contains two edge-disjoint spanning trees, then \(L(H)\) is Hamiltonian. In particular this holds when the root \(H\) is 4-edge-connected.

This is a classical supereulerian sufficient condition, associated with Jaeger's two-tree result. The useful contribution here is a complete parity-correction certificate, including its construction and conversion to the exact cyclic ordering asked for in the prompt. The hypothesis is explicitly an additional condition on the root, not a substitute for 4-connectivity of its line graph.

## The unique parity correction on a tree

Let \(T\) be a tree and let \(O\subseteq V(T)\) have even size. There is a unique subset \(J\subseteq E(T)\) whose odd-degree vertices are exactly \(O\). To construct it, include an edge \(e\) precisely when one component of \(T-e\) contains an odd number of vertices of \(O\). The two components have equal parity because \(|O|\) is even, so this prescription is unambiguous.

Root \(T\). For a nonroot vertex \(v\), the membership indicator of the edge to its parent is the parity of the number of \(O\)-vertices in its rooted subtree. The indicators of the edges to children are the corresponding child-subtree parities. Adding all incident indicators modulo two cancels the child subtrees, leaving \(1_{v\in O}\). At the root the same conclusion follows from the even total \(|O|\). Thus \(J\) has the required odd vertices.

For uniqueness, any edge subset with the specified odd set must meet the cut of each rooted subtree in the parity of its number of \(O\)-vertices, by the degree-sum identity modulo two. A tree has exactly one edge in this cut, forcing the displayed prescription. A postorder traversal computes all these parities in linear time.

## Two disjoint trees give a connected even spanning subgraph

Take edge-disjoint spanning trees \(T_1,T_2\) of \(H\). Let \(O\) be the odd-degree vertices of \(T_1\), an even set by the handshake identity. Construct its unique parity correction \(J\subseteq E(T_2)\).

Because the two trees are edge-disjoint,
\[
 F=(V(H),E(T_1)\cup J)
\]
is an ordinary subgraph, and
\[
 d_F(v)\equiv d_{T_1}(v)+d_J(v)\equiv0\pmod2
\]
at every vertex. It is connected and spanning because it contains \(T_1\). Thus it has an Euler tour using each of its edges once. This proof does not require the whole root graph to have even degrees, nor does it merely repeat its edges to obtain a multigraph tour.

The input pair of trees supplies a compact, verifiable certificate: check each is a spanning tree, check disjointness, compute the subtree parities and then traverse \(F\). No search over candidate even subgraphs is needed once the trees are supplied.

## Turning this certificate into the prompt's Hamilton cycle

Traverse the Euler tour of \(F\), initially listing only its edges. Every edge \(e\in E(H)\setminus E(F)\) has an endpoint in \(V(F)=V(H)\); choose one such endpoint and assign \(e\) to it. At one visit of the tour to each vertex \(v\), insert, in any order, all unused edges assigned to \(v\) between the incoming and outgoing tour edges.

Each original edge of \(H\) now occurs exactly once in the cyclic list. Within an inserted block, consecutive edges share \(v\); its first and last edges also share \(v\) with the adjacent Euler edges. Adjacencies elsewhere come from the Euler tour. Therefore the resulting cyclic ordering is a Hamilton cycle of \(L(H)\), whose vertices are the distinct edges of \(H\). The assumption of at least three edges ensures this is an ordinary graph cycle.

Given the tree pair and adjacency lists, the parity calculation, Euler traversal and insertion require \(O(|V(H)|+|E(H)|)\) operations. This complexity concerns conversion of an already supplied certificate, not a claimed linear-time tree-packing algorithm.

## Why 4-edge-connected roots are included

Use the Nash-Williams–Tutte partition theorem, checked as Theorem1.1 in DeVos–McDonald–Pivotto, arXiv1307.7621v2. If \(H\) is 4-edge-connected and its vertex set is partitioned into \(q\ge2\) nonempty parts, summing the part boundaries gives at least \(4q\). Each crossing edge is counted twice, so there are at least \(2q\ge2(q-1)\) crossing edges. The theorem therefore provides two edge-disjoint spanning trees, to which the construction applies.

## Exact relation to the earlier attempt and the open boundary

Math-1075-A1 proves the dominating Eulerian-subgraph characterization and correctly observes that arbitrary 4-connected line graphs need not have 4-edge-connected roots: a star can have a complete line graph. It does not show how to find a nontrivial spanning even certificate from a broad root packing condition.

Here the tree-parity lemma constructs that certificate from two disjoint spanning trees and proves the whole 4-edge-connected-root class. The star example remains valid and lies outside this root hypothesis. No converse of the two-tree criterion, and no implication from 4-connected \(L(H)\) to the existence of the two trees, is claimed. Thus the general line-graph conjecture is not resolved.

The two-tree supereulerian theorem is established literature, not claimed new. A checked primary publisher abstract, *Graphs without spanning closed trails*, attributes it to Jaeger(1979); the elementary construction needed here is proved above in full rather than imported from an unread original proof.
